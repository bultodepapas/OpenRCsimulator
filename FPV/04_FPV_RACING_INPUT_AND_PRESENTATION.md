# FPV, racing, input and presentation specification

**Examined:** OpenRC source `7bff78490f9af5c1664f47c8ba865a92b3430920`, 2026-11-11. This specification supports the proposed [master plan](01_FPV_MASTER_PLAN.md) and [engineering contracts](02_ENGINEERING_CONTRACTS.md). Camera/race/native-performance behavior and physical latency remain implementation/measurement work; numerical FOV calculations are included in the separately executed reference suite in [06](06_SOURCE_AUDIT_AND_EVIDENCE.md). The kernel remains the existing float64 RK4 vehicle-model architecture. Online racing and leaderboards are outside scope.

## 1. Camera: exact ownership and projection

**Observed:** `app/get-godot.sh` pins `4.8.beta2.official.cafc17a19`; the official beta release identifies engine commit `cafc17a192acf67903910a68aeb3c076c0907a4b`. `app/project.godot` specifies 240 Hz, at most 12 physics steps per render frame, `physics_jitter_fix=0`, Compatibility on the ordinary route, and Mobile on the supported native arm64 route. The 1280 × 720 project viewport is a default, not 1080p performance evidence. `Simulation.interpolated()` in `app/sim/simulation.gd` linearly interpolates the previous/current 64-bit position and shortest-path normalized quaternion; `main.gd` `_current_pose()` uses the engine interpolation fraction and, when held, uses fraction 1. `_render_pose()` supplies that same pose to both aircraft and `CameraRig.update()`. `Frames.ned_to_render`, `quat_to_render`, and `root_transform` in `app/render/frames.gd` define the render boundary. `CameraMath.vertical_fov()` already implements the formula below. These are fixed-wing implementation observations, not proof the prospective multirotor activity uses this path.

**Recommended implementation:** the new activity computes one detached interpolated render pose per frame and gives that exact pose to the four-rotor renderer and the FPV camera. The simulation retains its 64-bit authoritative state, event and collision geometry. Camera options, smoothing and rendering do not write into physics or score. The camera and any moving parent branch that this activity updates in `_process()` must not receive a second automatic interpolation. Avoid reading an already-interpolated child through another interpolator. This is especially relevant because Godot 4.5+ propagates a parent's interpolated local transform to children even when a child disables its own automatic interpolation. A top-level camera or explicitly noninterpolated branch is easy to reason about; establish the actual node configuration with a targeted test.

Let `p_N` be CG position in NED metres, `R_NB` the accepted/render-interpolated body-to-NED attitude, `r_B` the measured or estimated camera mount offset from CG in FRD metres, and `C(N,E,D)=(E,-D,-N)` the repository render conversion. For positive upward tilt `θ`, define in body axes

```
p_camera_world = C (p_N + R_NB r_B)
f_camera_world = C R_NB (cos θ, 0, -sin θ)
u_camera_world = C R_NB (-sin θ, 0, -cos θ)
```

Godot camera local `-Z` points forward and `+Y` up; construct an orthonormal basis from these vectors. Apply the rigid mount after the shared body interpolation, so roll remains fully body-coupled. Example: at level attitude, 40° tilt yields body forward `(0.7660, 0, -0.6428)` and up `(-0.6428, 0, -0.7660)`. At 90° body roll, the screen must roll with the craft; there is no hidden horizon lock. Mount offset is from the CG rather than an arbitrary imported model origin. Validate body/render conversion and an upright/inverted orientation, including positive tilt sign.

For undistorted rectilinear perspective, if the product control is **horizontal** FOV, with `KEEP_HEIGHT` set Godot `Camera3D.fov` to `2 atan(tan(hfov/2)/aspect)`, using the **actual content viewport's width/height**. At 120° horizontal this gives 88.5072° vertical at 16:9 and 104.8218° at 4:3. If 4:3 is fitted into a 1920 × 1080 frame without cropping or stretching, the rendered picture is 1440 × 1080 with 240-pixel bars each side; its camera must use 4:3, not the containing window's 16:9. Render only one active 3D view for this route, and measure any SubViewport overhead. A screen-resize or aspect switch recomputes the vertical value. Avoid `CameraAttributesPhysical` overriding the intended FOV or clip planes.

The existing 40° mount tilt and 120° horizontal FOV are **unvalidated proposed defaults**, not evidence of a particular product camera. Camera makers specify horizontal, vertical and diagonal angles separately and physical lenses may have significant nonlinear distortion; the RunCam Phoenix 2 SP example gives 4:3 H122°/V92° and 16:9 H122°/V68°, not pinhole relationships. Avoid treating their nominal diagonal angle as the simulator's horizontal FOV or claiming physical lens calibration. Distortion can be a separate optional visual feature. Retain player adjustment and disclose what stays fixed when aspect changes.

At 240 Hz, the custom interpolated picture is approximately one 4.167 ms simulation interval behind the ideal advancing simulation clock under normal catch-up, though its age relative to the latest *committed* tick varies with interpolation fraction. This is a scheduling consequence, not measured stick-to-photon latency. No extra smoothing or artificial video delay should be silently added; transport latency, physical actuator response and presentation latency remain distinct.

**Acceptance cases:**

- In a real renderer at 1920 × 1080, compare `project_ray_normal` at center and horizontal edges: half-angle is 60° for a nominal 120° horizontal pinhole FOV, within a tolerance established from float/render precision. Repeat in actual 1440 × 1080 content at 4:3 and after resizing. Check vertical angles against 44.2536° and 52.4109° respectively. Verify the bars and no stretched image.
- With θ=0° and 40°, known 90° roll, inverted attitude and a nonzero CG-to-camera offset, compare the displayed camera origin and axes to the equations; motion shows full roll. Check near prop, gate, grass/ground and far-field surfaces at representative camera planes. Move the near plane as far out as the view permits and check Z-fighting; do not assert a magic value qualifies every renderer/scene.
- At 30/60/144 rendered fps, feed the *same prerecorded physics-tick command tape* and compare accepted state/replay separately from frame-aligned camera transforms. Test first frame, pause, resume, crash and respawn; reset previous/current history to the new accepted pose on teleport so the camera does not sweep across the track. Godot's `reset_physics_interpolation()` is for its own interpolation; OpenRC's manual `previous` state also needs explicit reset. Physical camera latency and subjective comfort require a native display and pilot observation.

Primary sources (accessed 2026-11-11): official [Godot 4.8-beta2 release](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-builds/releases/tag/4.8-beta2), [Camera3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_camera3d.html), [CameraAttributesPhysical](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_cameraattributesphysical.html), [advanced physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/interpolation/advanced_physics_interpolation.html), [using interpolation and reset order](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/physics/interpolation/using_physics_interpolation.html), and [RunCam Phoenix 2 SP](https://shop.runcam.com/runcam-phoenix-2-sp/). `latest` documentation is unstable and may have moved past the pinned beta; confirm the referenced behavior on the pinned executable in implementation tests.

## 2. Controls, radio setup and latency

**Observed:** `FlightSession._input()` routes joystick events to `RcInput`; its `_physics_process()` polls the selected controller before the Simulation child steps. `RcInput.poll()` polls up to ten axes and establishes connection/sample evidence; see `app/sim/flight_session.gd` and `app/input/rc_input.gd`. `main.gd` disables input accumulation on that activity route. `ControllerProfile.normalize_axis` applies calibrated per-side min/center/max, inversion and clamping; `channel_value` applies gamepad deadzone/expo but absolute throttle is direct endpoint-normalized. Its legacy gamepad profile has expo and deadzone, so blindly piping already-conditioned `radio.sticks()` through an imported FPV rate curve can double-shape the result. `RcInput.armed` currently represents input readiness; it must not be treated as the drone motor-arm state. These are existing fixed-wing mechanisms, not proof of the proposed drone route.

**Recommended setup:** reuse the profile/device owner, calibration and fresh-evidence guards. For an unknown radio, have the user connect in USB joystick mode, select its actual enumerated device, move one control at a time to map roll/pitch/yaw/throttle, capture centers and both ends, invert as indicated, and visibly verify live bars and a 3D or axis-direction preview. Show mode, firmware/transport if supplied, selected device/profile and calibration. Test roll-right → positive FRD roll rate, stick-forward → nose-down, yaw-right → positive yaw and throttle-up → increasing 0–1 command. A dedicated simulator model on a radio should avoid double transmitter mixing/expo; if preconfigured shaping exists, disclose that exact input can't be unshaped from the received axis alone. EdgeTX's USB output is configurable: do not infer physical channel order from device name. Its manual says to switch RF modules off in USB joystick mode; confirm applicability for the user's radio.

Calibrate each centered axis using `x=(raw-center)/(max-center)` above center and `(raw-center)/(center-min)` below, then invert if configured and clamp to [-1,1]; normalize absolute throttle from endpoints to [0,1]. As a check, endpoints `(-0.9, 0.1, 0.8)` map the center to 0, raw 0.45 to +0.5 and raw -0.4 to -0.5 before inversion. For FPV rates, calibration and the selected deadzone feed one selected rates curve. Bypass gamepad cubic expo by default in the new pre-expo path. If composed gamepad/transmitter expo is deliberately offered, label and pin it and test the total curve; it is not identical to the imported firmware curve. Imported curve versions must match the complete curve, not only endpoint rates. Expose raw/normalized/commanded indications so setup mistakes and double shaping are diagnosable.

Acro center commands zero rotation rate, not level attitude. Angle training commands an attitude and is an explicit mode change. Simulated motor arming is a separate state machine: selected source valid, low throttle, arm OFF observed on the current connection followed by a new ON request. A held-high arm switch at launch, focus regain or reconnect cannot arm; show the reason. Disarm and crash remove drive regardless of Airmode. A missing device enters the hold/rearm policy. Follow the recovery transaction in [02](02_ENGINEERING_CONTRACTS.md): while physics is held, non-integrating checks stage fresh OFF→ON evidence; explicit Continue submits it to the first resumed tick. A centered rate-throttle source frozen above zero gets an explicit zero-throttle resume override or pad reset, and only the accepted resumed sample commits that override. A USB receiver can remain enumerated when an RF transmitter is off: a static axis is not proof of RF failure, so report wireless failsafe behavior per tested device. Test held switches, bounce, high throttle, disarm, pause/focus loss, unplug/replug and device ambiguity with both simulated inputs and a real radio.

The multirotor activity must initialize its intended global input-buffering policy explicitly, with shell/activity ownership and restoration defined. Direct launch must not depend on a previous fixed-wing activity having disabled accumulation. Test direct, Home and vehicle-switch routes on the pinned engine.

**Latency methodology:** the existing `app/input/latency_patch.gd` renders a marker driven by the sampled radio-axis threshold. `docs/TOOLS.md` and `docs/plans/controller-support.md` point to the F6 protocol/reducer. It is useful for **physical stick-to-marker** measurements, not displayed aircraft-response or actual RC air-link latency. Film a repeatable stick threshold and marker/display at fixed native resolution, refresh, vsync and preset; alternatively use a synchronized electrical threshold and photodiode at a declared screen location. Retain camera/photodiode timing resolution, exposure and scanout uncertainty, source video, calibration and raw trials. Compare ≥30 transitions in each applicable direction as the existing F6 plan requests; more are needed for a stable p95. Report median/p95, range, trial count, uncertainty, radio model/firmware, USB/receiver link, OS, engine SHA, host, GPU/driver and display model/mode. Do not sum overlapping manufacturer 'glass-to-glass' and end-to-end numbers.

Separately measure stick → first discernible FPV scene response, with a prescribed input step, maneuver, reference image/threshold and pilot-visible result; it includes sampled control, plant response, interpolation and scanout. The marker and scene measurement answer different questions. Instrument monotonic callback delivery → input sampled → accepted tick → frame submission as diagnostic segments, but do not call callback gaps USB poll rate, frame submission screen emission, or synthetic event injection a hardware measurement. With 240 Hz sampling, one tick is 4.167 ms; this is a conditional sampling granularity, not a total latency budget and not a guarantee that OS/device delivery occurs before the next tick. No actual FPV measurement is asserted here.

Primary sources (accessed 2026-11-11): [Godot Input buffering and accumulation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_input.html); [EdgeTX USB joystick](https://manual.edgetx.org/color-radios/model-settings/model-setup/usb-joystick); [EdgeTX joystick report and configured processing](https://manual.edgetx.org/edgetx-how-to/joystick-mapping-information-for-game-developers); [Betaflight setup: Acro and Angle semantics](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/getting-started/setup-guide). Firmware documentation does not establish compatibility or latency for an untested transmitter.

## 3. Race scoring and atomic publication

**Observed:** `Simulation.step()` constructs a candidate, runs its pure `VehicleModel.endpoint_transition`, validates it, commits `state`, `aux`, `modes` and `tick`, then emits `events_located` and `stepped` (`app/sim/simulation.gd`). A race listener that updates a separate score after those signals can expose mismatched physics/race ticks and cannot roll back with a rejected step. `VehicleModel` already supports bounded checkpoint-v2 layouts and pure endpoint callbacks. `step_policy` owns the full tick and cannot request kernel contact substeps; the policy must itself qualify compatible subdivisions/event handling. A bounded, detached `integration_receipt` reaches the endpoint callback only from an explicit opt-in policy; the kernel validates transport/shape, not physical correctness. `_endpoint_events()` concatenates policy and endpoint events instead of globally sorting them. Its event payload and receipt have a 4096-value aggregate budget and arrays are bounded to 256 elements; account for this before choosing path/event capacity.

**Recommended composition:** make a bounded race model wrapper with fixed race slots in the existing aux/modes and a pinned track/rule/layout identity. Its single endpoint transition combines final physical arm/crash state with a pure RaceDirector, using only detached previous/candidate frames, accepted-path receipt, immutable track data and accepted inputs. Preserve race slots in the sampled callback and `block_aux`, except the explicitly staged countdown/GO transition; the final state validator checks every slot and race/physics tick agreement. Validate the receipt against the final composed physical endpoint, including endpoint-owned corrections even when `body_filtered=false`. On an invalid/missing/incomplete receipt, cap overflow, impossible event or unrepresented body correction, require a qualified replacement path or refuse/mark the race unresolved; do not score an invented path. Restore/reset validates track, rules, race state and physics together. Verify all rejection points leave race, controller, clock and physics unchanged apart from documented fault/pause diagnostics. Use the event-local terminal cut latch in [02](02_ENGINEERING_CONTRACTS.md) so a located crash disables drive for later subintervals without resampling the PID. An observer receives the already-committed common result and cannot own authoritative scoring.

Policy receipts must describe only accepted, chronologically ordered path pieces, including the previous and candidate body positions, times, and one-sided states/velocities at physical discontinuities. Validate finiteness, interval, monotonicity, continuity except explicitly labelled jumps, endpoint match, bounded lengths and physical-event references; never sweep an abandoned RK stage, an impact jump, crash reset or pad teleport. The whole-tick policy must locate the applicable contact, rotor-stop and pack events and honor its contact refinement policy. Scoring uses CG as an explicit local-rule scoring point; collision uses separately qualified hull/rotor geometry and may happen before, at or after that crossing.

**Event ordering:** the director sorts all candidate physical terminal events and gate crossings by simulation time, then resolves a defined small tie window. A deterministic example policy groups events whose time is within 1 µs of the *earliest* event in the group; terminal crash takes precedence, remaining events use actual time followed by immutable gate index/event ID. This avoids a nontransitive 'times approximately equal' sort comparator. The 1 µs value is only a proposed rule-resolution convention and must be checked against actual event-location uncertainty; if roots cannot be distinguished at that precision, refine or mark timing unresolved instead of claiming it. A crossing earlier by more than the tie window and qualified uncertainty can remain in diagnostic lap progress; a crash within the same tie group wins and suppresses that crossing, and crossings after the crash are suppressed. The selected local rules still abort the whole attempt. Tie priority adjudicates scoring/publication order inside a group and does not reorder the physical transitions already applied by the policy. Never score the straight chord from pre-crash position to post-respawn position. A policy can return `events=[]` and supply its physical events in the validated receipt so the composite endpoint returns one ordered event list; verify no existing caller depends on policy events, otherwise add a narrowly scoped generic ordering seam with fleet parity. The kernel otherwise appends rather than globally sorts returned events.

**Swept rectangular gate:** each immutable gate has center `c`, unit normal `n` for the allowed direction, and orthonormal in-plane axes `u,v` with positive half-width `w` and half-height `h`. On each accepted continuous piece from `p0` at `t0` to `p1` at `t1`, compute `d0=n·(p0-c)` and `d1=n·(p1-c)`. The basic directed crossing requires `d0<0` and `d1>=0`; calculate `λ=-d0/(d1-d0)`, `q=p0+λ(p1-p0)` and `t_cross=t0+λ(t1-t0)`. For an exact straight piece, score only if `abs(u·(q-c))<w` and `abs(v·(q-c))<h`: aperture edge is outside by this local rule. For a qualified numerical path, use the located crossing point `q_hat` with projected root-position uncertainty bounds `epsilon_u,epsilon_v`, including state/path/root errors. Score only if `abs(u·(q_hat-c))+epsilon_u<w` and `abs(v·(q_hat-c))+epsilon_v<h`. It is definitively outside if either `abs(u·(q_hat-c))-epsilon_u>=w` or `abs(v·(q_hat-c))-epsilon_v>=h`; otherwise an uncertainty interval overlaps an edge, so refine or mark unresolved. A nominal chord point 0.1 mm inside a frame is insufficient if its in-plane error may exceed 0.1 mm. Gate plane starts, wrong-way travel and coplanar pieces do not score. At an exact plane endpoint, require positive normal velocity with qualified uncertainty from a located/dense accepted-path root or an explicit accepted policy split. Interpolating endpoint velocities alone does not establish that root state. A zero-normal-speed root is deliberately unscored/unresolved under this local rule; it is not necessarily a mathematical tangency (an odd cubic crossing can also have zero derivative). A collision in the same qualified tie group wins. With exact half-open endpoint convention, a crossing reported at the end of one piece is not reported again by the next beginning at `d0=0`; starting the race/respawn on a plane does not count. Retain enough state to apply the convention across checkpoints. Evaluate all gate candidates in the piece, process them in the deterministic event order, and accept only the next required gate; out-of-order gates do not advance it. A missed gate requires returning and passing it in the proper direction. Refine/flag numerically ambiguous tangencies; do not silently score an edge due to a numerical epsilon.

This algorithm is exact for the stated straight segment only. A path can cross and return between endpoints, so no finite set of endpoint signs proves no missed crossing without a path/dynamic bound. Supply accepted intermediate pieces and verify against an independently refined trajectory, including near-tangent/high-curvature cases, or use certified root bracketing with an acceleration/monotonicity bound. Mark a run unresolved if the declared refinement/cost limit is reached, rather than claiming exact lap timing. Tangency, repeated traversal, multiple successive gates in one tick, simultaneous crash/gate, gate beyond impact, and reset teleport must be separate cases. Race-replay checks use the same physical tick tape and include state/result/checkpoint equality.

**Numerical fixture:** let `n=(1,0,0)`, `u=(0,1,0)`, `v=(0,0,1)`, `c=(0,0,0)` in NED, with `w=h=0.75 m`, and `p0=(-0.05,0.25,0.4) m`, `p1=(0.1166666667,0.25,0.4) m`. At 40 m/s and `Δt=1/240 s`, λ≈0.3, crossing time=1.25 ms after the tick starts and the point `(0,0.25,0.4)` is inside. Using tick start underestimates by 1.25 ms; tick end overestimates by 2.9167 ms. Replacing the point's `u` coordinate with 0.75 m gives no score under the strict edge rule. Rotating all gate axes and positions by the same rigid rotation must preserve the outcome.

For a smooth normal acceleration bound `A`, linear-chord deviation on a segment of duration `h` is at most `A h²/8`; with `A=100 m/s²` and `h=1/240 s` this is 0.217 mm. If normal speed throughout the bracket is ≥1 m/s, the corresponding position-only timing bound is 0.217 ms, but at 40 m/s it is about 0.0054 ms. At half the piece duration the bound is 0.0543 ms at 1 m/s. This demonstrates why the existing straight-line ≤0.1 ms example alone does not establish curved-path accuracy near grazing crossings. The bound requires actual normal-acceleration and speed bounds, plus independent allowances for state, root and clock errors; these example values are not established limits of the simulator.

**Chosen first local rules:** [02, section 8](02_ENGINEERING_CONTRACTS.md) specifies a 720-tick drive-inhibited countdown, GO at the start of the next interval using fresh pre-inhibit throttle/source evidence, first-lap clock from GO, exactly three ordered valid laps, missed-gate retry within a lap, and crash/reset abort of the entire attempt. A high-throttle/invalid-source GO stays inhibited in staging and clears arm authorization. Pause freezes simulation time and marks practice metadata. Atomically retain finished and aborted terminal records, including completed lap slots and reason, in the bounded checkpointed session outbox before a reset replaces the attempt. Persist files asynchronously under 02 section 9: failed writes retain an explicitly unsaved record for bounded retry, and a full outbox refuses another replacement. Do not claim an in-memory queued result is durable before a file/checkpoint save. These are local simulator rules, not claimed FAI/MultiGP compliance. FAI publishes an editioned rulebook; MultiGP's UTT guide demonstrates one published missed-gate retry rule, but local simulation does not become compliant merely by adopting it.

Primary sources (accessed 2026-11-11): [FAI current drone rules index](https://www.fai.org/page/documents-and-rules), [MultiGP UTT](https://www.multigp.com/universal-time-trial-utt/), and the pinned repository files named above. These sources do not prescribe this proposed simulator algorithm.

## 4. Separate 1080p performance, pacing and latency

**Observed:** `ROADMAP.md` sets a 500 µs physics budget at 240 Hz on the accepted target machine, and Gate P requires per-aircraft/regime p50/p95 on an idle target. `Simulation.step_usec` is an exponentially smoothed overlay value, so it cannot provide raw per-tick p95/worst evidence. The drone plan proposes ≤500 µs p95 for a whole tick and ≤16.67 ms p95 render frame at 1080p; those figures are currently proposals. `docs/tools/graphics.md` says headless and Xvfb/llvmpipe do not qualify target GPU performance. Twelve catch-up ticks at 240 Hz correspond to 50 ms of simulated time; below roughly 20 presented frames/s for sustained operation the configured cap can make simulation run slower than wall time.

**Physics-cost acceptance:** on an idle, named target host and native exported build, record raw unsmoothed monotonic elapsed time around the full sampled session/FC + kernel + accepted event/score transaction, with a separate kernel-only number, per tick. Include normal free flight, high motor activity, low voltage, ground contact, gate/near-gate and located event cases; retain ticks per sample and distinguish instrument overhead. Report p50/p95/p99, maximum and number beyond the 4.167 ms fixed-step deadline, plus simulated-time/wall-time ratio, faults and catch-up counts. The proposed 500 µs p95 is 12% of one tick interval (0.500/4.167), leaving headroom for other work; it is not proof every tick is below deadline or that the whole frame meets 60 Hz. At 60 Hz about four 240 Hz ticks occur per frame; four 500 µs ticks consume 2 ms if all actually cost 500 µs, before rendering and other tasks. Measure batched catch-up directly rather than inferring batch p95 from individual p95 values. Record runs/length and identities before looking at the results.

**Frame pacing acceptance:** at the real 1920 × 1080 drawable/content resolution, named preset, FPV view, track, host, driver, renderer, refresh, vsync/VRR setting and recorded maneuver, capture a warmed sustained native run with the existing frame collector, reporting its precise sampling boundary. Gather p50/p95/p99, maximum, counts above 1.5× and 2.5× the measured refresh interval, raw timestamp series, no-draw frames and simulation-time/wall-time ratio; retain ≥60 seconds and separate startup/loading stalls. For an actual 59.94 Hz mode the refresh period is 16.683 ms, not 16.67 ms. Proposed screen for a declared fixed-refresh 60 Hz target: p95 ≤1.05 periods and p99 ≤1.10 periods, plus a separately reviewed hitch count; thresholds are engineering proposals and need calibration to the chosen measurement boundary and host. p95 ≤16.67 ms alone still permits about 5% of frames—roughly three every second at 60 fps—to exceed the nominal budget, and says nothing about their size or about lag. For uncapped/VRR configurations, specify an appropriate target and method instead of applying a fixed-vsync rule blindly. A `_process()` interval is a software frame proxy; use platform presentation telemetry or external display capture to establish physical presentation cadence.

**Latency acceptance:** carry out the separate physical stick-to-marker and stick-to-scene measurements in §2 on the same identified 1080p flight configuration. Pre-register a paired baseline/candidate and uncertainty; any allowed motion-to-photon limit is an explicit product decision rather than an invented industry number. No unexplained extra frame relative to the baseline beyond measurement uncertainty is a useful regression screen, but neither that screen nor frame p95 establishes absolute latency. Repeat at tested 30/60/144 render caps and under a documented temporary hitch; no wall-time-to-simulation-time claim follows without observing catch-up behavior. Pilot review complements instrumented evidence but does not replace it.

## 5. Firmware-style settings workspace

The 34 investigations in [07](07_BETAFLIGHT_INAV_SETTINGS_RESEARCH.md) identify useful settings and incompatible BF/INAV semantics. Implement the workspace described in [08](08_SETTINGS_SCHEMA_IMPORT_AND_TESTS.md): Receiver; Rates/throttle; Controller; Filters/timing; Motors/power; Modes/lifecycle; OSD/view; Profiles/logs. Show pending versus active settings, selected profile, effective simulator behavior and a specific reason for inactive capabilities. Raw firmware values remain source provenance when their law is not supported.

Do not interpret board alignment as camera tilt, VTX power as actual RF output, DShot rate as physical solver rate, or packed OSD positions as pixels. Rate/profile changes initially use the explicit held reset/apply workflow; opening the panel midair does not itself disarm the accepted vehicle. Ordinary Apply must not silently teleport or reset it. Use the distinctly labeled Reset to pad and apply action or a separate explicit pad reset, with the existing race-abort/outbox and rearm behavior. Validate preview/cancel, invalid import, first launch, Home/direct route and fresh export at native 1080p; pure presentation changes preserve physics/race results for the same accepted tape.
