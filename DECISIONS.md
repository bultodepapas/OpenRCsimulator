# Decisions

A small record of what we choose and why. The two guiding principles remain established. Every other decision is our current direction and can change as we learn. When an entry is replaced, mark it *superseded* and keep it, so its context remains visible. The route these decisions serve is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

| Date | Decision | Reasoning | Status | Revisit when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-10-05 | Build an RC airplane simulator. | This is the project's purpose. | Established principle | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Start with something very simple that works and shows a little airplane, then grow gradually. | Small working steps let us learn and shape the project through experience. | Established principle | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Use an Ultra Stick / Ugly Stick-style .60 nitro airplane as the first model. | The project owner prefers this aircraft family and propulsion direction as a concrete starting point. Begin with a simple representation and refine it gradually. | Chosen direction | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Use the **Hangar 9 Ultra Stick .60, conventional wing, tailwheel** as the reference variant for numbers. Keep the Big Stik .60 ARF as the alternate. | Its manual gives span, length, area, weight, CG and throws in one place, and matches the chosen name. Not mixing variants was a research finding. | *Superseded 2026-10-05* by Das Ugly Stik 60 (next row) | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Reference variant is **Das Ugly Stik 60** (Jensen kit, Phil Kraft design): span 60 in, wing area 723 in², length 52 in, .40–.61 engine, nose gear, classic red/white livery. The Hangar 9 Ultra Stick .60 and Big Stik .60 remain alternates for missing data. | The owner supplied plan images of this airplane; it is the original Ugly Stick. | Provisional | A plan scan or measurement contradicts these numbers, or the owner prefers another version (e.g. the RCM taildragger). |
| 2026-10-05 | First prototype runs **in the browser with Three.js, TypeScript and Vite**. | Zero install and easy sharing; quick edit/run loop; HTML panels for tuning; screenshots and headless tests give AI-assisted work concrete feedback. Three.js was one of the two researched candidates. | Provisional — **under bake-off** against Godot 4.7 until Gate 1 (see ROADMAP Phase B) | Gate 1 picks Godot, or the Gamepad API loses transmitter channels. |
| 2026-10-05 | Write **our own small flight-physics module**, with no renderer imports, testable headless in Node with Vitest. | No researched library models a 2-stroke glow RC airplane out of the box (JSBSim `FGPiston` is four-stroke only). A small model stays readable, and the boundary keeps it portable. | Provisional | We find ourselves rebuilding mature features (trim, gear, tables) at high cost. Then re-evaluate JSBSim. |
| 2026-10-05 | Physics uses **SI units**, a world frame with **NED axes** (north, east, down), body axes **FRD** (forward, right, down), and quaternion attitude. Conversion to the Three.js Y-up frame happens in **one** function at the render boundary. | Aerospace conventions match the sources (JSBSim, UMN, textbooks). The research repeatedly flagged unit and axis mix-ups as a main risk. | Provisional | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Fixed physics timestep** (start at 1/240 s) with render interpolation and a cap on catch-up steps. | *Fix Your Timestep* pattern; enables replay and convergence checks. | Provisional | The convergence test (`h`, `h/2`, `h/4`) suggests a different step or integrator. |
| 2026-10-05 | First flight model is a **linear stability-derivative model with a crude stall cap**. Its nondimensional derivatives are seeded from the UltraStick25e specimen and labeled `borrowed`; geometry and mass come from the .60 manual. | This is the smallest model that gives believable RC handling. The 25e is a geometrically similar Stick with inspectable data. Labels keep the borrowing honest. | Provisional | Playtesting or sensitivity tests show which effects matter (stall, propwash, ground effect…). |
| 2026-10-05 | Aircraft parameters live in a **data file** (JSON). Each value records its unit, source and evidence kind. | Recommended by the research (parameter manifest); keeps the model swappable and its provenance visible. | Provisional | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Default view is a **fixed ground pilot camera**. A chase camera exists for debugging. | This is how RC airplanes are actually flown; distant-aircraft readability is a core research question. | Provisional | Pilot observation suggests otherwise. |
| 2026-10-05 | Input starts with the **keyboard**, then the **browser Gamepad API with a raw channel inspector**. The sim applies no deadzone, expo or circular limit by default. | Detection, channel delivery and correct response are separate milestones; the radio already applies expo, rates and mixes. | Provisional | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Toolchain:** Node 24 LTS + npm; TypeScript 7.0 (`tsc --noEmit` for checking only); Vite 8; Vitest 5; Playwright for screenshots; Biome for lint and format. Exact versions pinned and lockfile committed. See [STACK.md](STACK.md). | Current stable releases; TS 7 checks fast, and Vite does not need its compiler. Biome avoids typescript-eslint, which does not support TS 7 until its API arrives in 7.1. | Provisional | TS 7.1 ships its API and we want type-aware lint rules. |
| 2026-10-05 | Render with three.js **`WebGLRenderer`** and built-in materials, with no custom shaders yet. Only `src/render/` imports three. | WebGL 2 runs everywhere; WebGPU is still missing on Firefox for Linux. Isolation limits three.js API churn to one folder. | Provisional | Upgrades start costing real time → try Babylon.js (backward-compatibility rule). WebGPU becomes universal and we need it → swap to `WebGPURenderer`. |
| 2026-10-05 | Physics state uses **float64 numbers only** (never `Float32Array`), through our own small vector/quaternion module. | Executed experiment: in float32, a 1e-4 m/s² acceleration is lost entirely, and 1e-3 is 8% wrong over 60 s at 240 Hz. gl-matrix defaults to Float32Array. | Provisional | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Replays and tests compare **within tolerance**, not bit-for-bit. All transcendental math goes through one module. | ECMAScript does not specify `Math.sin` and similar functions exactly, and engines differ. One module lets us swap in a deterministic implementation later. | Provisional | Multiplayer or shared replays need exact cross-browser determinism. |
| 2026-10-05 | No general physics-engine library (Rapier, Jolt) for now. | One airplane plus spring-damper gear does not need one. | Provisional | Scenery collisions become complex. |
| 2026-10-05 | Desktop distribution, when needed: installable **PWA** first, then **Electron**. Not Tauri. | Electron ships the same Chromium everywhere. Tauri's own docs list WebKitGTK rendering problems on Linux. | Provisional | Tauri's Linux webview issues are resolved, or download size becomes critical. |
| 2026-10-05 | **Bake-off:** build Stage 0 and Stage 1 in both three.js and Godot from one spec, then choose at Gate 1. The scoring criteria are fixed before the Godot results ([COMPARISON.md](prototypes/stage0/COMPARISON.md)). | Measuring is cheaper than arguing; fixing the criteria first avoids bias. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **No physics code before Gate 1.** | The physics language follows the platform (TypeScript vs GDScript/C++); writing it twice would be waste. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Known answers before unknown answers:** the integrator must pass exact-solution tests (free fall, torque-free spin, convergence order) before any aerodynamics. | Otherwise integrator bugs look like aircraft behavior. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Every roadmap step is small and has one objective proof (test, capture, trace or measurement). | Small reviewable steps; evidence instead of opinion, which matters with AI-written code. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **License: MIT** for the project's code and original content. Third-party data we bundle (e.g. UIUC airfoil data, "GPL'd data") keeps its own license, in its own folder with its notices. | The owner's choice; permissive like three.js and Godot. The repo was public with no license. | Chosen | — |

## Open (deliberately not decided yet)

- **Exact engine and propeller.** O.S. MAX-65AX and APC 12×6 Sport are reference candidates for Stage 5.
- Native/desktop packaging, mobile, VR, multiplayer, and a second aircraft.

The researched electric Ultra Stick 25e remains a comparison specimen. Only its nondimensional aerodynamic derivatives are borrowed, as labeled starting estimates. Its dimensions, mass properties and electric propulsion do not define the .60 nitro model.
