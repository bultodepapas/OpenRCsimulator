# Decisions

A small record of what we choose and why. The two guiding principles remain established. Every other decision is our current direction and can change as we learn. When an entry is replaced, mark it *superseded* and keep it, so its context remains visible. The route these decisions serve is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

| 2026-10-05 | Start with something very simple that works and shows a little airplane, then grow gradually. | Small working steps let us learn and shape the project through experience. | Established principle | — |

The Hangar 9 Ultra Stick .60 and Big Stik .60 remain alternates for missing data. | The owner supplied plan images of this airplane; it is the original Ugly Stick. | Provisional | A plan scan or measurement contradicts these numbers, or the owner prefers another version (e.g. the RCM taildragger). |
| 2026-10-05 | First prototype runs **in the browser with Three.js, TypeScript and Vite**. | Zero install and easy sharing; quick edit/run loop; HTML panels for tuning; screenshots and headless tests give AI-assisted work concrete feedback. | 2026-10-05 | Write **our own small flight-physics module**, with no renderer imports, testable headless in Node with Vitest. | No researched library models a 2-stroke glow RC airplane out of the box (JSBSim `FGPiston` is four-stroke only). | 2026-10-05 | Physics uses **SI units**, a world frame with **NED axes** (north, east, down), body axes **FRD** (forward, right, down), and quaternion attitude. Conversion to the Three.js Y-up frame happens in **one** function at the render boundary. | Aerospace conventions match the sources (JSBSim, UMN, textbooks). The research repeatedly flagged unit and axis mix-ups as a main risk. | Provisional | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Fixed physics timestep** (start at 1/240 s) with render interpolation and a cap on catch-up steps. | *Fix Your Timestep* pattern; enables replay and convergence checks. | Provisional | The convergence test (`h`, `h/2`, `h/4`) suggests a different step or integrator. |
| 2026-10-05 | First flight model is a **linear stability-derivative model with a crude stall cap**. Its nondimensional derivatives are seeded from the UltraStick25e specimen and labeled `borrowed`; geometry and mass come from the .60 manual. | This is the smallest model that gives believable RC handling. The 25e is a geometrically similar Stick with inspectable data. Labels keep the borrowing honest. | Provisional | Playtesting or sensitivity tests show which effects matter (stall, propwash, ground effect…). |
| 2026-10-05 | Aircraft parameters live in a **data file** (JSON). Each value records its unit, source and evidence kind. | Recommended by the research (parameter manifest); keeps the model swappable and its provenance visible. | Provisional | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Default view is a **fixed ground pilot camera**. A chase camera exists for debugging. | This is how RC airplanes are actually flown; distant-aircraft readability is a core research question. | Provisional | Pilot observation suggests otherwise. |
| 2026-10-05 | Input starts with the **keyboard**, then the **browser Gamepad API with a raw channel inspector**. The sim applies no deadzone, expo or circular limit by default. | Detection, channel delivery and correct response are separate milestones; the radio already applies expo, rates and mixes. | *Superseded 2026-10-05* — Godot input path below | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Toolchain:** Node 24 LTS + npm; TypeScript 7.0 (`tsc --noEmit` for checking only); Vite 8; Vitest 5; Playwright for screenshots; Biome for lint and format. Exact versions pinned and lockfile committed. See [STACK.md](STACK.md). | Current stable releases; TS 7 checks fast, and Vite does not need its compiler. Biome avoids typescript-eslint, which does not support TS 7 until its API arrives in 7.1. | *Superseded 2026-10-05* — applies only to the archived three.js build | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Render with three.js **`WebGLRenderer`** and built-in materials, with no custom shaders yet. Only `src/render/` imports three. | WebGL 2 runs everywhere; WebGPU is still missing on Firefox for Linux. Isolation limits three.js API churn to one folder. | *Superseded 2026-10-05* — Gate 1 chose Godot | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Physics state uses **float64 numbers only** (never `Float32Array`), through our own small vector/quaternion module. | Executed experiment: in float32, a 1e-4 m/s² acceleration is lost entirely, and 1e-3 is 8% wrong over 60 s at 240 Hz. gl-matrix defaults to Float32Array. | *Superseded 2026-10-05* — same principle, Godot form below | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Replays and tests compare **within tolerance**, not bit-for-bit. All transcendental math goes through one module. | ECMAScript does not specify `Math.sin` and similar functions exactly, and engines differ. One module lets us swap in a deterministic implementation later. | Provisional | Multiplayer or shared replays need exact cross-browser determinism. |
| 2026-10-05 | No general physics-engine library (Rapier, Jolt) for now. | One airplane plus spring-damper gear does not need one. | Provisional | Scenery collisions become complex. |
| 2026-10-05 | Desktop distribution, when needed: installable **PWA** first, then **Electron**. Not Tauri. | Electron ships the same Chromium everywhere. Tauri's own docs list WebKitGTK rendering problems on Linux. | *Superseded 2026-10-05* — Godot exports natively | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Bake-off:** build Stage 0 and Stage 1 in both three.js and Godot from one spec, then choose at Gate 1. The scoring criteria are fixed before the Godot results ([COMPARISON.md](prototypes/stage0/COMPARISON.md)). | Measuring is cheaper than arguing; fixing the criteria first avoids bias. | Done 2026-10-05: three.js 73, Godot 57 of 80 | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **No physics code before Gate 1.** | The physics language follows the platform (TypeScript vs GDScript/C++); writing it twice would be waste. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Known answers before unknown answers:** the integrator must pass exact-solution tests (free fall, torque-free spin, convergence order) before any aerodynamics. | Otherwise integrator bugs look like aircraft behavior. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | Every roadmap step is small and has one objective proof (test, capture, trace or measurement). | Small reviewable steps; evidence instead of opinion, which matters with AI-written code. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **License: MIT** for the project's code and original content. Third-party data we bundle (e.g. UIUC airfoil data, "GPL'd data") keeps its own license, in its own folder with its notices. | The owner's choice; permissive like three.js and Godot. The repo was public with no license. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **Gate 1: the simulator is built in Godot 4.7 (GDScript, Compatibility renderer), in `app/`.** The three.js build is archived in `prototypes/stage0/three/`. | Owner's decision. The scoring favored three.js (73 vs 57), mainly on float64 ergonomics. The owner preferred Godot for native desktop, SDL3 joystick input for real radios, and the editor. The float64 cost is handled by the rule below. | Chosen | Physics in GDScript proves too slow or too awkward (see the performance check in ROADMAP C3) → move the physics core to C++ GDExtension, or reconsider. |
| 2026-10-05 | **Physics stays in 64-bit GDScript `float`s.** Simulation code (`app/sim/`, `app/physics/`) never uses `Vector3`, `Basis`, `Quaternion` or `Transform3D` (32-bit in official builds); our own small float64 vector/quaternion helpers are used instead. **`app/test.sh` fails the build** if those types appear there. | The executed float32 experiment: small accelerations are lost in 32-bit storage. An automatic guard turns discipline into a check. | Chosen | A double-precision official Godot build appears, or the physics moves to C++. |
| 2026-10-05 | Physics is our own GDScript module, tested headless with `extends SceneTree` scripts (no add-on), run by `app/test.sh` locally and in CI. | The same known-answer approach as before, with tools proven in the bake-off (32 checks + e2e in seconds). | Chosen | Test volume outgrows plain scripts → evaluate GUT or gdUnit4. |
| 2026-10-05 | Input: keyboard now; then joysticks and radios through Godot's SDL3 input, **reading raw axes directly** (bypassing input actions and their default 0.5 deadzone). | RC transmitters already apply expo and rates; a deadzone would distort them. SDL3 on desktop reads devices without the browser's gesture gate. | Provisional | Hardware tests (ROADMAP F1) show missing axes or wrong mapping. |
| 2026-10-05 | Third-party plans, scans and CAD files live in a **gitignored `references/` folder**, local only. | Their licenses are unknown and the repo is public under MIT. Owner's decision; past commits still contain them (not purged). | Chosen | The owner decides to purge history, or a file's license is confirmed. |
| 2026-10-05 | **Physics data format `openrc-aircraft v1`** (`app/data/aircraft/`): every quantity is `{value, unit, kind, source}`. Mass and inertia come from a component inventory; the loader derives them and refuses invalid data. | Derived numbers cannot drift from their sources; provenance is visible per value; broken data fails loudly. | Chosen | — |
| 2026-10-05 | **CG = the Jensen plan's CG: 4.76 in aft of the LE (39 % chord).** Measured on the full-size oz1253 plan. ## Open (deliberately not decided yet)

- **Exact engine and propeller.** O.S. MAX-65AX and APC 12×6 Sport are reference candidates for Stage 5.
- Native/desktop packaging, mobile, VR, multiplayer, and a second aircraft.

The researched electric Ultra Stick 25e remains a comparison specimen. Only its nondimensional aerodynamic derivatives are borrowed, as labeled starting estimates. Its dimensions, mass properties and electric propulsion do not define the .60 nitro model.
