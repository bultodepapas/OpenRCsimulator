# OpenRC simulator MCP reliability plan

**Priority for 23 November 2026.** Make stalled requests explain which resource or receipt they are waiting for, then fix two misleading replies from the interactive tools. Both problems can lead an operator to repeat work that may already have run.

The debug tooling owner (PT1h-R11) owns the interactive contract in `tools/debug/` and `app/debug/`. Shared-host test admission (TQ) owns scheduling, receipts and recovery in `tools/testqueue/`. The findings below describe `openrc-sim` at audit revision `64b73545a`. The [fleet plan](mcp-agent-fleet.md) and [queue resilience plan](mcp-queue-resilience.md) own their respective implementation steps.

## Post-repair inventory, 25 November

The same Windows-pool records were read again on 25 November at about 08:40 Bogotá. The cohort has the 15 requests whose `request.json` changed on or after 23 November. At inspection their supervisors were 4 `FINISHED`, 5 `NEEDS_OPERATOR`, 4 `ERROR` and 2 `RUNNING`.

| Signal | 20–22 Nov | 23–25 Nov |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Completed rows: passed / test failed / infrastructure failed | — / 21 / 52 | 33 / 9 / 1 |
| Rows still waiting, refused for disk on every eligible host | — | 14 |
| Rows blocked on a local `SUBMISSION_UNCONFIRMED` receipt | — | 1 |

The 23 November cause typing worked: execution-stage infrastructure failures disappeared. The remaining stalls had two causes that the earlier repairs did not address.

- **Retained failures filled the remote disks.** On `linux` the queue store held 25 GB of a full 96 GB disk. Five `TEST_FAILED` jobs held 19.8 GB and five `INFRA_FAILED` jobs 4.15 GB. Each failure kept its extracted source and import cache (about 3 GB) and its input archive (about 1 GB); its evidence was under 5 MB. Passed jobs held 0.15 GB because `pool gc` already reclaimed them. Rows needed 2.95–4.72 GB, but only 0.8–2.6 GB was free on `linux`, `primis` and `angel`. Two freeze errors also reported too little space on `C:` for the snapshot.
- **A refused local submit reserved the Windows host indefinitely.** The batch froze a sparse worktree that was later deleted. The Windows authority refused the submit with "source must be an OpenRC checkout with the admitted adapter", although `checked` jobs execute the materialized snapshot. The pool kept the receipt `SUBMISSION_UNCONFIRMED`, and placement treated the host as busy. Receipts show the same refusal on 14, 15, 22 and 24 November, holding the host 61, 46 and 40 hours until manual reconciliation; the fourth was still holding it after 23 hours. Its ID appeared as the Windows `BUSY` reason in 17 batches.

Two operational findings need owner action. `angel` and `linux` still run `remote_agent.py` from `3f5718dc3` (20 November): the remote late-conflict wait and GPU-lane changes in `14aeac505` and `1c2de9e66` had not been deployed. `primis` failed at its SSH jump and `angela` was offline.

The Git history of `tools/testqueue/` shows the same pattern: 45 commits between 12 and 25 November, with audits on 14, 15, 20 and 23 November. Each round fixed its recorded incidents and verified them with fixtures. Storage retention stayed manual (17 November full disks; 101 GB reclaimed on 20 November) and remote deployment was never checked against `HEAD`.

**Repairs (TQ-04-R1, TQ-04-R8/TQ-05-F0.7).** An explicit local refusal is settled through the authority's tombstone: `recover` first, then `cancel_submission`. The row ends `REJECTED` with `LOCAL_SUBMIT_REFUSED` and the host is released. Status, recovery and a placement sweep settle overdue unconfirmed local receipts the same way. `checked` submissions no longer need the frozen worktree to exist. `pool gc` also reclaims reproducible inputs of finished failures and keeps run residue. Placement runs it, with a two-minute budget, on a remote host it refuses for disk. A remote `run` refused `BUSY` is deferred under its original ID. Compact status rounds stage durations. [Decisions](../DECISIONS.md) · [queue guide](../../tools/testqueue/README.md#operator-storage-and-reconciliation-tools)

An independent review found no duplicate-execution or unverified-pass path. Its two hazards (a sweep's host lock refusing another batch's `run`, and an unbounded sweep inside a placement lane) and the operator-reconciled reclaim case were fixed before deployment. Residual: a transient local error reply now ends `REJECTED` instead of reserving the host; the local fence uses two RPCs, so an acceptance between them is canceled (never duplicated); the Windows authority needs a restart to load the worktree change.

## What the records show

On 23 November at about 08:10 Bogotá time, we read the Windows pool's retained `mcp/<request_id>/` directories. The cohort comprises 119 requests whose `request.json` modification date fell on 20–22 November inclusive in Bogotá. Count each directory once by saved `supervisor.json.state`, then read `batch.json.result` and `jobs[]` for row-level evidence. The window includes multiple code revisions. The files are mutable, and the saved state alone cannot establish whether a process was still alive. See the [receipt and recovery contract](../../tools/testqueue/README.md#mcp-checked-batches).

| Last supervisor state | Requests | Meaning at inspection |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `FINISHED` | 81 | 39 batches passed and 42 failed. A failed batch can contain a test failure, infrastructure failure or timeout. |
| `NEEDS_OPERATOR` | 23 | Seven reported blocked rows after 900–908 s. Sixteen reported no progress after 21,605–39,788 s (6–11 hours). This ends observation. Accepted work can continue. |
| `ERROR` | 13 | All lacked a batch file. Nine explicitly reported a source change during freeze. The other four reported a missing file, a failed Git lookup, insufficient snapshot reserve and an invalid argument during verification, one each. |
| `RUNNING` / `SNAPSHOTTING` | 2 | One saved request in each state. Their eventual outcomes were unverified. |

**Admission and receipt recovery.** Within the 23 requests needing intervention, 15 listed `INSUFFICIENT_DISK` for at least one candidate host, 13 listed `BUSY`, and three listed `OFFLINE`. A request can have several reasons. Ten blocked rows in ten different requests recorded a missing `jobs/<id>.json`; all ten receipts were still absent at inspection. A missing receipt leaves dispatch uncertain. The submission counter records calls to the planner, including calls that find no eligible host. It cannot count engine launches. The batch runner deliberately resumes an interrupted submission by observing the original ID. [Batch state machine](../../tools/testqueue/pool_batch.py)

**One batch illustrates three different stopping points.** In `drs-low-end-focused-20261122`, one Linux row recorded a missing receipt after 49 submission calls. Another waited through 207 calls and reported 1,974,468,608 bytes free against 3,728,805,946 required. A macOS row reached the engine and timed out in import. Its retained `engine.log` ended at 34% reimport and its status recorded confirmed cleanup. The supervisor eventually reported 37,060 s without progress. The log identifies the stage reached. It does not establish a defect in the last asset named.

**Failure attribution.** Across the cohort, completed rows included 21 `TEST_FAILED`, five `TIMED_OUT` and 52 `INFRA_FAILED`. Of the 52, collection labelled 29 as execution, 19 as import and four as check. **26 of the 29 execution-labelled rows reported an external Godot process**. Seventeen recorded a refusal and nine explicitly said that preparation stopped before launch. Seven of the 19 imports timed out. Counting all 52 as failed tests would send maintainers to the wrong code.

**Log coverage.** All 119 `supervisor.log` files were empty. The useful evidence came from structured state, collection receipts and the retained engine log. The separate `watchdog-serve.log` contained 43 permission-denied lines and two connection resets while writing replies. Those entries lack a request ID and cannot establish which batch, if any, was affected.

**Queue protection bug, reproduced with synthetic receipts.** Two consecutive infrastructure failures on one host and source should trigger a 600 s cooldown. With the second attempt created 900 s ago and finished one second ago, `Pool._circuit` left the host eligible. It calculates elapsed time from receipt creation. An isolated run using the existing fixture confirmed this behavior. Its contribution to the recorded incidents is unknown. [Placement code](../../tools/testqueue/pool.py) · [circuit fixtures](../../tools/testqueue/test_pool_reconcile.py)

## Interactive failures reproduced on the audited revision

Both cases used the real JSON-RPC `tools/call` handler with a stub at `debugctl.result_of`. They establish reply behavior at the MCP boundary. Their frequency in live sessions is unknown. [Server](../../tools/debug/mcp_server.py) · [existing protocol fixtures](../../tools/debug/test_mcp_server.py) · [interactive contract](../../tools/debug/README.md#agents-and-mcp-pt1h-r11)

| Call | Injected sequence | Observed reply and impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sim_fly(ready=true)` | `fly` accepted, fresh state has no menu to close, `resume` refuses because a hold persists, `look` succeeds | `isError=false` with the original flight result. A caller can start measuring a flight whose resume was refused. The bridge already supplies the pause reason. |
| `sim_press` | `press` acknowledges the action once, subsequent `look` times out | `isError=true` with advice to call `sim_launch`. The action acknowledgement is lost. Repeating a toggle can reverse the change. Other actions using `_then_look` share this reply path. |

**Historical repairs to preserve.** On 7 November, `9db50cfc7` fixed malformed RPC input handling and propagation of a failed stop. On 15 November, `3d5c0a16c` separated transport intake from blocked work. The ordered simulator lane still queues `sim_stop` behind an in-flight launch. On 20 November, `f0558271c` added supervisor progress limits and watchdog recovery. At the audited revision the watchdog skipped `ERROR`, `NEEDS_OPERATOR` and requests older than six hours. The earlier 14–19 November audit found 37 unfinished requests among 115. Its definition and software differ from the cohort above. Three focused existing fixtures passed on the audited revision for malformed parameters, ping and cached status during a blocked launch, and observation of an unknown attempt without resubmission. After rollout, repeat the same three-day inventory with the deployed revision and host availability recorded. Compare requests needing intervention, wait duration by cause, missing original receipts and stage-specific failures. Inspect a sample of failed and successful batches against their engine evidence. Keep safety acceptance at zero duplicate executions and zero unverified passes. Use the [queue operating guide](../../tools/testqueue/README.md) for collection and recovery.
