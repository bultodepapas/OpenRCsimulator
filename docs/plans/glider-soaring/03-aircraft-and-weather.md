## 1. First aircraft and tow pair

Target a **MULTIPLEX EasyGlider 4 built as a pure glider**, with its optional aerotow installation, and a **FunCub matched to the pairing described in the EasyGlider manual**. This conventional 1.8 m trainer supports the requested solo and tow progression and has a documented control and launch description. Treat the pairing as a research starting point, not proof that either specific simulated aircraft, rope or controller is qualified.

The [EasyGlider 4 manual, 2017 version 1.0, kit #21 4332](https://www.multiplex-rc.de/userdata/filegallery/original/4934_214332-bedienungsanleitung-easyglider-4--de-en-fr-it-es.pdf) specifies a motorless option; for aero-tow it calls for glider nose #22 4350, coupling #72 3470 and a Tiny-S servo. It names the FunCub/EasyGlider combination and approximately 20 m of line. The cited manual does not supply the line's stiffness, damping, breaking force or an independently documented complete pure-glider mass. Its construction advice is not a validated simulator force threshold.

The manual states approximately **1.1 kg general takeoff mass**, without assigning that number to the pure-glider tow configuration. Do not infer that the motorless build weighs 1.1 kg, and do not subtract an assumed motor or flight battery. Weigh the complete selected build, including receiver battery, servos, nose, tow release, wiring and ballast; determine CG and inertia from the same installation. The manual reports approximately **40 dm² FAI area**, including wing and tailplane but excluding fuselage. This is **not** the wing-only aerodynamic reference area `S` for the solver or a stall-speed calculation. Measure the wing geometry and record the area convention.

An explicitly labeled generic engineering prototype may unblock software while physical measurements and redistribution rights are missing. Its data must remain estimates with provenance; it is not a calibrated EasyGlider replica. A named, physically representative release requires the evidence below and review of the actual model asset's distribution rights.

## 3. Data and controls admission

Before a physically representative named build is admitted, retain an evidence package for the exact assembled aircraft:

1. **Installation and balance:** total flying mass; each installed component's mass and position; CG, reference datum and full inertia tensor or a documented measurement/estimate; aircraft revision, battery and ballast. Record receiver and servo power separately from propulsion.
2. **Geometry and controls:** wing-only reference area, span, chord/planform, incidence/dihedral, tail surfaces and arms, aerodynamic/reference datums, contact or skid geometry; hinge geometry and servo travel, rate, polarity, differential and mixes. Check rendered hinges against the physical mapping.
3. **Flight behavior:** low-Reynolds lift/drag and stall evidence, a measured or provisionally estimated speed/sink polar, trim and launch observations, lateral/directional response, landing and control authority. Identify weather, density, mass, CG, instruments, uncertainty and methods for each test.
4. **Tow installations:** exact tug version and loaded mass, power, propeller, balance, performance and ground contact; both hook locations and release action; line length, mass, stiffness/damping model and source; independent evidence for any claimed weak-link strength. A manual's named pairing does not qualify a generic Extra or any existing simulator aircraft.
5. **Reproducibility:** stable source or build identity, observation dates, raw and reduced data, calibration, author, uncertainty and permission to retain/distribute the material. Use `manual`, `measured`, `borrowed`, `estimated` or `derived` as appropriate for each v2 quantity; an estimate remains an estimate after a passing test.

The current v2 contract requires an explicit propulsion kind. **Absent propulsion is a proposed capability extension**, not a currently supported empty, null, zero-thrust or motor-off workaround. Extend the registry, validators, physical consumers, controls, Workshop, persistence and presentation together before admitting this aircraft. Include receiver, battery, servo and release-actuator installation masses and require at least an explicit ideal receiver/servo/release supply. Finite-battery depletion and associated failures are optional, separately qualified capabilities. Unsupported combinations must refuse admission.

The reference implementation comes first in GDScript. The native backend must not inherit a hard requirement for a motor, propeller sample slot, shaft, propulsion wake, fuel draw or engine sound. Native support is a later capability and numerical parity qualification, not a prerequisite for a valid GDScript glider. Maintain legacy powered-aircraft behavior and keep all generated physics and geometry on their existing source pipelines.

### Surface behavior by configuration

For the EasyGlider 4, the manufacturer's “spoiler” function raises **both ailerons** and mixes down elevator; it is a spoileron/braking mix using the existing surfaces, not evidence of a separate blade spoiler. Its manual gives approximate starting travels of +20 mm for both raised ailerons and −3 mm elevator compensation; measure and calibrate the selected build. Bind left/right aileron, elevator and rudder controls to the actual installed surfaces, with rate, differential, saturation and mix applied to both aerodynamics and rendering. Glider release is a distinct actuator and command.

This braking function is **not currently supported by the normal conventional-aircraft control mapping**: [right/left ailerons receive opposite roll commands](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/physics/control_surfaces.gd#L23-L35), and the active contract retains three logical servo states. The independent four-position conversion is a fixture seam. Introduce a versioned symmetric-aileron/spoileron mix with input and achieved-actuator topology, saturation and matching aerodynamic/render bindings before offering braking. Do not relabel the existing flap law or a rendering-only animation as this capability. A development fixture that lacks this mix must declare braking unavailable and use a separately admitted approach/landing envelope.

Heron/Lentus flaps and butterfly/crow mixes are configuration-specific later capabilities. Allegro-Lite's RES spoiler is a separate surface and requires its own geometry, actuator, lift/drag and pitch response. Do not model every brake channel as a generic drag scalar or imply that the current aircraft contract already has a deployed-spoiler law. Tug throttle and glider brake use role-specific mappings and safe takeover; switching from tug to glider must not deploy a brake from a previously high throttle position. The [launch/control guide](01-launch-and-control.md) owns pickup behavior.

## 4. Airspeed, ground speed, and useful initial values

The vector relation is `air velocity = ground velocity − wind velocity`. Use local 3D wind at the appropriate physical location; [NASA's relative-velocity explanation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/relative-velocity/) distinguishes these quantities. Label true airspeed and ground speed, showing m/s and optionally km/h. At 9 m/s airspeed, a 3 m/s headwind gives 6 m/s ground speed along the same straight path; a 3 m/s tailwind gives 12 m/s. They convert to 32.4, 21.6 and 43.2 km/h respectively. Crosswind and vertical motion require vector calculation, not subtraction of speed magnitudes.

No selected pure EasyGlider build has a sourced stall, minimum-sink, best-glide, launch or tow speed in this research. Establish them for its actual mass, density, CG and control configuration. For level-flight intuition only, `Vs ≈ sqrt(2mg/(rho S CLmax))`, where `S` is aerodynamic wing reference area. In a **hypothetical** example of 1.10 kg, wing area 0.36 m², density 1.225 kg/m³ and `CLmax=1.1`, `Vs≈6.67 m/s` (24.0 km/h). Proposed release targets at 1.3–1.5 times that value are approximately 8.7–10.0 m/s (31.3–36.0 km/h). None is a measured EasyGlider number, universal launch rule, or substitute for a feasible hand-thrown ground-relative velocity.

An air start needs an unpowered trim with actual pitch, control settings and descent angle at the selected airspeed; convert to ground velocity by adding sampled wind once. A hand throw is authored in ground-relative velocity and evaluated against local wind; do not alter it after release to enforce a target. In steady uniform wind a downwind turn changes ground track, not the stall airspeed by itself. Gusts, gradients, load factor, control deflection and changing angle of attack can change the margin.

A coordinated, steady, level 30° bank at 10 m/s true airspeed has the **idealized air-relative** turn radius `V²/(g tan(bank)) ≈ 17.66 m`; the ground track drifts in uniform wind. The same simplified model gives `Vs(bank)=Vs(0)/sqrt(cos(bank))`, approximately 1.075 times the wings-level value at 30° and 1.19 at 45°. It assumes level coordinated flight and unchanged `CLmax`; it is not a measured turn, achievable trim, or speed recommendation for this glider. A motorless aircraft in still air must lose height to sustain its motion; increased bank generally changes drag and sink, making tight turns a tradeoff against the lift captured. The [FAA Glider Flying Handbook, 2024, chapter 10](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/glider_handbook/gfh_chapter_10.pdf) discusses this soaring tradeoff for full-size gliders; it is not RC aircraft validation data.

## 5. Weather recipes and admission

These five rows are **proposed finite tuning seeds**, not observed climates, validated difficulty, complete schema objects or named real-world forecasts. They use the existing kinematic field described by the wind reference. Use `openrc-weather v5` with `openrc-spatial-wind v2` and `openrc-thermal-field v1`; incompatible legacy uniform mean, gust and OU amplitudes are zero. Supply complete legal objects and validate all coupled fields; never infer that a partial table is loadable.

Choose the existing **standard reference atmosphere** at sea-level field elevation, which yields 1.225 kg/m³, rather than adding an invented free-form density field. For these proposed examples: flat weather datum 0 m; horizontal half-extents 2,500 m; domain ceiling 1,000 m; **world-epoch horizon** 3,600 s; no layers or finite-mode spatial turbulence; reference wind at 10 m; roughness 0.03 m, displacement 0 m, wall join 0.3 m, profile top 80 m and free-air blend thickness 20 m. Use the existing preset-builder rule for free-air speed: `U_free = U10 * ln(100/0.03) / ln(10/0.03)`, with the same horizontal direction and zero vertical component; its upper join ends at 100 m. Meteorological direction is **FROM**, clockwise from north, so the NED vector is `[-U_free*cos(direction), -U_free*sin(direction), 0]` with the angle converted to radians. The neutral log profile and blend vary with height, and the prescribed thermal drift need not equal local wind at all heights.

The current provider evaluates world time as simulation time plus epoch offset. A 3,600 s world-epoch horizon therefore leaves 3,600 s of admitted simulation time for Calm, 3,300 s for Wide first lift, 3,360 s for Drifting thermal and 3,420 s for each remaining row. The cell itself fades much earlier, as described below; the horizon does not renew it. All stage samples must stay within the admitted horizon.

**Tow compatibility baseline:** start pair/controller qualification with Calm trim only: no background wind, gust, layer, thermal or turbulence. This calm recipe is an initial admission target, not a claim that the FunCub pair or any controller has passed. The four thermal rows are initially **solo-only**. For any of them to be offered for either towing role, separately qualify the two-aircraft controller, start geometry, airspeed envelope, shared field and recovery in that actual wind/gust/thermal recipe. Passing calm does not admit stronger or gustier conditions. In any future paired scenario both actors use the same spatial field, epoch and position/time sampling; independent actor-only stochastic turbulence is not a substitute for a coherent pair environment. All four initial thermal seeds disable spatial turbulence; any later turbulence experiment is separate, explicitly modeled and qualified.

Each nonzero gust below is one **6 s spatial event** beginning at `epoch_offset + 20 s`, with the listed peak downwind horizontal increment and a 3 m ground transition; zero means no event. This is not a continuously repeating gust. Each thermal scenario has one cell, born at epoch 0, and no automatic replenishment. Start at age `life/2`, using `epoch_offset = life/2`; weather and aircraft clocks share that epoch. A new run starts at peak cell age and has only the remaining half of its lifetime. Reset restores the recipe, and pause freezes simulation-time weather.

The table's `N=0, E=0` center at simulator start is a **scenario-local template**, not a demand to put a thermal on the pilot or runway. Preflight chooses a valid world-space `Cstart=[N,E]` away from pilot/runway and obstacles, with visibility and terrain clearance appropriate to the field. Translate all field positions consistently and set `Cbirth = Cstart − drift × (life/2)`; never move the actor while silently leaving the weather at an old origin. A candidate `Cstart=[250,0] m` is only an example and must be checked against the selected actual field. Proposed solo air starts are 80 m above the flat datum, near the translated core, subject to actual terrain, visibility, trim and all wing/tail station bounds.

### What the current thermal parameters mean

For positive `P`, during the cell lifetime and between its bottom and top, the modeled vertical velocity is upward at `r<R`, zero at `r=R`, and includes compensating surrounding sink before its compact support fades out by `5R`. At the center, with cell age fraction `s` and flat-datum height `h`,

```text
w_up = P × sin²(πh/H) × 64s³(1−s)³,     0 ≤ h ≤ H,  0 ≤ s ≤ 1
```

Thus P is the center updraft **only at half-life and height H/2**, not the climb rate everywhere. At peak age with `H=200 m`, the height multiplier is about 0.0245 at 10 m, 0.0955 at 20 m and 1 at 100 m. With `P=1.8 m/s`, peak updraft at 10 m is only about 0.044 m/s. The current tall-cell law must not be advertised as a reliable rescue for a low hand toss: teach a short glide/landing first and use an air start or qualified tow for thermal practice. If accessible near-ground lift is required, change and validate the field law explicitly.

This is a prescribed, volume-divergence-free kinematic circulation; terrain, solar heating, cloud and humidity do not generate it. With a varying-density column it is not density-weighted mass conserving. Existing thermal weather is one cell born at 15 s, lifetime 360 s, center `[220,-120] m`, radius 35 m, top 200 m and peak 1.2 m/s; it does not regenerate. Ridge lift, terrain rotor and valley channeling are not implemented weather capabilities. Keep them unavailable until their own physical model and validation exist.

## 6. Reuse, provenance, and acceptance

Search first for a suitable, distributable asset and dataset before creating replacements. The [VJX developer repository](https://github.com/jxjo/VJX) provides slope-glider design material and its [license file](https://github.com/jxjo/VJX/blob/main/LICENSE.md) states CC BY-SA 4.0 unless otherwise noted. The [OpenSwift design repository](https://github.com/ahtly89/OpenSwift) states CC BY-SA 3.0 for its files. Neither is the selected first conventional aircraft; verify individual files, upstream components, attribution, change notices and license compatibility before incorporating anything. Pin the exact revision or content hash when acquired. Public access or a free download does not by itself permit redistribution. The Drela design pages and manufacturer manuals are primary references, but the reviewed pages do not establish a project-compatible license for copied geometry, photos, brand marks or manual figures. Do not import those assets without permission or a compatible license.

For any accepted asset, retain origin URL, author, exact version, file hashes, license, material changes, coordinate frame/units, processing and generated-output identities. Record separately which measurements, aerodynamic estimates and code generated the physical configuration; software analysis or copied geometry is not flight validation. Keep reference images and full manual text out of the repository when redistribution rights are unclear.

Verification should proceed in layers:

- **Data and controls:** reject incomplete or inconsistent motorless installations; check mass/CG/inertia derivation, wing reference area, source metadata, actual surface directions, spoileron mix, release and visual bindings. Compare reference and native paths only when both claim support.
- **Weather:** sample at center, `R`, `5R`, birth/death and `H/2`; verify the formula, pulse endpoints, translated support, epoch and reproducibility. Test station/domain rejection and shared pair sampling. No test in this plan has yet been run.
- **Flight and tow:** in calm, measure trim, sink, air/ground velocity, hand-release state, landing and pair energy; then test finite lift and qualified weather combinations. Capture source/config identity and complete replay. Simulator determinism, plausible pilot feel, and independent physical validation are distinct outcomes.
- **Qualification:** use measured RC flights and uncertainty, actual controller/field tests and native 1920×1080 visual evidence. Keep the named aircraft, rope and weather admission constrained to what was tested; report unknown speed or strength explicitly.
