# In-game graphics benchmark and automatic tuning

**Proposed for fixed-wing flight · 23 November 2026 · VQ-07h-A5.** A player picks a frame-rate target, watches a short flight in the simulator and can save the best-looking configuration that passed on that machine during the test. If the flight misses the target, the result says where and by how much.

*Can I have clearer wings, better-looking trees or more detailed ground and still fly smoothly at my chosen frame rate?* The first release answers that question from a departure, a distant bank and a landing approach.

## 1. Product decisions

| Decision | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | **Home → Settings → Graphics → Find recommended settings.** Offer the same optional action beside the first-launch hardware recommendation. In a paused flight, explain that the test runs from Home. Returning to Home follows the existing exit-flight flow. |
| Target | Ask **“What frame rate would you like in flight?”** Show **30, 60, 90, 120 and 144 FPS**. The highest selectable target in this version is 144. “Maximum” on a faster monitor would otherwise promise an unspecified objective. Suggest 30 on identified lightweight hardware and 60 elsewhere, bounded by the current display. The player chooses. |
| Starting point | Use the existing Automatic recommendation for the **active** GPU, renderer and pixel count, projected onto eligible visual controls. A UHD 630 starts at the readable minimum, with Simple trees and ground, birds and meadow off, and a projected aircraft shadow. A 3090 starts near its recommended quality. Measure the seed even if it matches an earlier recommendation. Keep locked fields at their actual values throughout. |
| Output | Optimize visual detail subject to meeting the target **in every required flight segment**. Offer the highest-ranked verified configuration explored within the time budget. Show the limiting segment, the achieved rate there and a short setting-by-setting diff. |
| Time | Design for **roughly 2–5 minutes**, with a **six-minute active-run ceiling** including loads, retries and final verification. Reserve time for verification before starting another trial. These are design budgets. Replace the estimate in shipping copy with observed ranges from the supported machines. |
| Saving | Trials are temporary. **Apply recommendation → Keep changes** saves through the existing Settings transaction. **Keep current settings**, **Cancel** and the existing 15-second Revert timeout restore the entry configuration. Remember the selected benchmark target on Keep. The target and the flight FPS limiter are separate preferences. [Player settings and recovery](../FIRST-LAUNCH.md) |

Read the active window's monitor, refresh, output pixels, cap and VSync at setup. Treat nominal fractional rates such as 59.94 and 143.86 Hz as 60 and 144 respectively for selection (within 1%). Show the measured refresh in Details. Mark higher targets unavailable with their reason. If refresh is unknown, allow a run and say so. With a saved 60 FPS cap and a 120 FPS request, offer **Use a 120 FPS limit** as an explicit change for test and Keep, or let the player select 60. Preserve VSync. Grade engine cadence on every device. A known binding driver limit gets an explanation. Actual screen presentation and VRR engagement stay unknown without independent evidence.

## 2. Art direction and the value of each upgrade

From the pilot box, the player must be able to follow the wing through a bank, see which way the aircraft is travelling against the trees, and tell how high it is above the runway on final. Give the field a recognizable treeline and the ground enough structure to judge motion. A dense meadow is a poor trade if the wing breaks into flickering pixels when it crosses the horizon.

| Priority | Visible improvement | First controls to compare | Acceptance cue in motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protected floor | Aircraft outline and markings, runway edge, obstacle silhouettes, near-ground aircraft cue and readable HUD | Keep physical obstacles visible with cheap representations. Keep the existing projected cue where applicable. Pin camera, assists, HUD scale and accessibility choices. Admit a minimum internal scale per renderer/output only after the moving-flight review below. | Pilot identifies bank direction crossing the treeline and distinguishes low pass from touchdown. A repeatable lost aircraft or invisible obstacle vetoes a rung at any FPS. |
| 1 · Edge and surface clarity | Stable thin wings, runway markings and oblique ground detail | Try a qualified Native scale step (e.g. 75 → 85%) and a supported MSAA step separately. Compare ground Simple → Full on a low approach. Assess runway filtering and mipmaps in asset review. A global texture-resolution or filtering slider needs its own implementation before it can be offered. | Wing stays continuous in a roll. Runway markings remain legible into the distance, without crawling turf or a smeared plane. |
| 2 · Treeline and landing depth | Coherent tree silhouettes, stable transitions, useful shadows | Simple → Full trees at the same density and physical layout. Compare one shadow-quality or distance step after keeping the landing cue. Admit rebuild-only changes only when a rebuilt trial and normal flight agree. | No distracting jump where the wing crosses trees. Contact cue tracks the plane during flare. Extra shadow distance earns its place only when visible from the pilot box. |
| 3 · Field richness | Fuller ground cover and better lighting detail around the approach | Try runway meadow Off → On, or one eligible lighting refinement on a workload where it is visible. Give cost and benefit to the specific scene. | Clear improvement in a matched approach or fly-by. Keep decoration from masking the runway or making orientation harder. |
| 4 · Ambient life | Birds and atmospheric decoration | Try birds Off → Few → Normal → Many only where the fixture actually draws them. Reflections, clouds and expensive effects enter later as independent, supported controls with an observable benefit and a measured cost. | Birds can add life in a wide view. A daytime clip cannot rank an unused night light, and a hidden effect earns no upgrade. |

This ordering is the starting hypothesis. Give each **adjacent setting change** an entry with setting/key, values, dependencies, supported renderer/provider, apply boundary, readback, affected scene, art rank and measured cost range. A rung enters the player build only after its visual effect and support have been checked. Measure its cost on each device class where possible. For example, a move from Simple to Full trees that yields the same cards at the pinned scene tier has no eligible upgrade on that route.

Rank legal upgrades by art priority, then the pilots' ordinal preference (clear improvement, slight improvement, tie). Within equal ranks try the lowest measured increase in the limiting leg's p95 first. An unknown device cost gets one ordinary trial behind known costs in the same rank. Skip a perceptual tie unless it reduces cost. To rank complete configurations, compare their retained, visible upgrades from highest to lowest rank. At the first differing rank prefer more of its approved upgrades. Break ties by the lower worst-leg p95, then fewer changed fields and stable setting ID. This gives the search a reproducible answer when expensive Full trees and inexpensive ground detail compete for the last few milliseconds.

**How to validate the ordering.** Reuse the project's blinded moving-visibility protocol. Capture equal-size A/B passes on the same physical display with identical aircraft, lighting, FOV, camera and command tape. Test sky, treeline and runway backgrounds at 20/50/100 m and the established far range. Include a head-on approach, a bank and a flare. Randomize A/B order, repeat with the order reversed and allow a tie. Ask “Which wing is down?”, “When did the wheels touch?” and separately “Which version would you choose to fly?” Record correctness, response time, preference and the specific artifact. Start with three RC pilots of mixed experience and two contrasting stock aircraft. Resolve any reproducible cue loss before promotion. Unclear preferences share a tier. Confirm shortlisted settings in live flight on the target display. These are practical release checks, not a population ranking. [Moving-visibility reference](../research/visual-quality-implementation/VQ-01d/README.md) · [Visual acceptance gates](global-performance-visual-quality.md#gates-to-freeze-in-vq-01c)

## 3. The flight the player watches

Use a versioned stock **Ugly Stik at Club Field** circuit with the production pilot camera. Start a disposable benchmark session from Home. Drive a fixed, validated tick-indexed command tape through the normal fixed-wing input boundary at 240 Hz. Pin the initial state, field, weather, lighting, visual seeds, camera path/FOV and zoom. Each candidate sees the same turns and the same part of the scenery. Package the fixture with the game. Reuse the project's checkpoint and replay contracts when constructing reproducible entries to the circuit. [Flight logging and exact replay](../FLIGHT-LOGGING.md)

| Leg | What the player sees | Why it can change the verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A · Runway departure | Ground fills the lower view. The aircraft lifts from its shadow and crosses runway markings. | Ground material, low-angle texture sampling, projected shadow and near-field props. |
| B · Distant bank | The aircraft crosses sky, enters the treeline and reverses its bank. | Small moving silhouette, aliasing, foliage transitions and visibility against a busy background. |
| C · Low return and flare | Broadside pass over the meadow, then a ground-heavy approach with trees and the contact cue in frame. | Combined vegetation, geometry and shading load. Protects against a pass obtained by averaging a cheap sky view with an expensive approach. |

The fixture specifies a checkpoint, pre-roll, first scored tick and last scored tick for each leg and window. Advance real physics for every scored tick. Verify tick boundaries, route completion and camera pose. Count genuine in-leg stalls. Time scene load, shader warmup, field rebuild and camera cuts separately. An out-of-route aircraft, missing segment or unintended fallback invalidates that trial. Preserve player aircraft, field, input mapping, camera preference, logbook, flight hours and recordings. The first result describes daylight fixed-wing flight at Club. Use the jet/Corsair and another field as held-out lab checks before widening the wording.

## 4. Measurement and search

### Define a pass before tuning

For target `F`, the frame budget `B = 1000 / F` is **33.33, 16.67, 11.11, 8.33 or 6.94 ms** in menu order. Sample adjacent monotonic main-loop frame intervals during verified active rendering, with draw progress checked independently. A process callback, GPU timer and physically displayed frame measure different things. Use the process-loop interval for the portable verdict and label it in Details. Collect fresh GPU timing and CPU attribution where available. Compatibility can use observed cadence and paired one-knob trials. Never count cached GPU polls as independent completed frames. Acquire and release the existing timing lease. [Frame diagnosis](../tools/graphics.md#frame-diagnosis) · [Stutter measurement contract](stutter-diagnostics.md)

| Stage | Default engineering contract, to calibrate in Phase 5 |
| --- | --- |
| Probe | Apply and acknowledge a candidate, wait for field readiness and pre-roll at least two seconds with the intended camera and draw progress. A pending rebuild or shader warmup at the scoring boundary invalidates the window. Score the same central **4 seconds of simulated flight per leg**. Screen on segment mean cadence, p95 and simulation pace using the final gate's corresponding limits. A short probe can reject a poor candidate or shortlist it. Only final verification can declare success. |
| Final | Restore normal flight presentation owners, limits and the intended dynamic-resolution policy. Run **two complete passes** of **10 scored seconds per leg**, with warmup before each leg. At 30 FPS that yields about 300 frames per leg per pass. Record both passes separately. Longer lab repeats quantify how often this quick result predicts an ordinary flight. |
| Metrics | Compute FPS as interval count / elapsed seconds. Record nearest-rank p50/p95/p99 in ms, maximum interval, fraction over budget and total excess time. Evaluate each leg and each repeat. Time a bad interval at full length. Record simulation elapsed / monotonic elapsed over the same tick window. |
| Initial final gate | For each leg in **both** repeats require mean FPS ≥ 0.98 × F, p95 ≤ 1.08 × B, p99 ≤ 1.5 × B and simulation/wall between 0.99 and 1.01. At 60 FPS retain the project limits of 18 ms and 25 ms. Flag >50 ms intervals and reject a reproducible hitch in the same manoeuvre. Two passing circuits verify the candidate. Two failing circuits give a miss. A split verdict is inconclusive. Display the actual rate even when the tolerance passes. |
| Validity | Check a complete tick window, a continuous interval sequence, actual effective settings, focus, viewport and draw progress. Record sample count and clock precision. A complete, slow flight is a miss. Missing observations, contamination or a changed workload give **inconclusive**. Preserve cold-start stalls in their own result. Never trim gameplay stalls to pass. |

Keep output and renderer fixed. Freeze dynamic scale at the intended fixed choice for comparison and suspend Home scaling and passive calibration for the benchmark viewport. Re-enable the real flight policy for final verification, including the player's provider, caps and overlays ordinarily active in flight. The **entire reachable scale range** must meet the B0.2 readability floor, even if a short flight only visits its ceiling. If the controller can go lower, offer a new explicitly authorized fixed-scale trial or mark that combination unqualified. Record the permitted and observed ranges separately, plus any fallback. A 144 FPS benchmark target does not create a 144 FPS dynamic-resolution mode. If the final path cannot be observed, withhold a verified recommendation for it. [Dynamic resolution](dynamic-resolution.md)

### Bounded candidate search

1. **Snapshot and filter.** Build the readable floor and Automatic seed from the allowed setting keys. Preserve all locked and unsupported fields. Mark equivalent *effective* candidates as one candidate. Read the effective state after application and after every deferred field rebuild.
2. **Test the seed.** If it passes the probe, make it the incumbent. If it misses, remove the least valuable costly step implicated in the slow leg. A proven GPU/pixel bottleneck permits scale or MSAA trials above the readability floor. For a CPU/scenery limit, try eligible draw or density reductions. With uncertain attribution, compare one isolated change and label the cause unknown.
3. **Find a viable starting point.** Allow one targeted downstep from a failing seed, then test the admissible floor if needed. When all probes miss, use the reserved final passes on the best observed admissible settings in the real flight policy. The fixed ceiling can miss while dynamic resolution reaches the target within its readable range. If both passes succeed, return that verified configuration immediately. If they miss, report the leg and rate for that configuration. If the operating policy cannot be tested, return inconclusive. A lower-target suggestion needs its own run before saving. If locks exclude a promising step, offer to include it on a new run.
4. **Spend remaining time on improvements.** From a passing incumbent, try one adjacent eligible change at a time in the art order above. Revert a miss and verify the restored state. If a high-value upgrade misses and the incumbent has a costly lower-ranked feature, allow one exchange within the six slots. For example, turn birds down one step, measure, then retry the clearer wing setting from that measured configuration. A trial that abandons a better verified result never replaces the incumbent. Otherwise try a cheaper independent improvement. Track every attempted effective configuration to prevent loops.
5. **Budget uncertainty.** Use up to **six probe slots** including the seed, floor when needed, and **one** repeat of an ambiguous probe. Treat a probe within 2% of the mean or p95 threshold, or one with unstable timing, as ambiguous. Reserve enough time for two final passes before issuing another probe. Learn per-leg load and rebuild durations from the elapsed run. Reach the deadline with no complete final result and return **inconclusive**.
6. **Verify and return.** Rank short-listed candidates by their accepted art improvements and measured cost. Verify the first choice in ordinary flight presentation. If it fails, verify the previous passing candidate if time remains. An unverified rollback is never presented as a success. Dispose of the test flight and restore entry settings while the result screen awaits the player's choice.

**Worked examples — illustrative, not measurements.** At 30 FPS on a UHD 630, suppose Simple trees, Simple ground and no birds pass. A clearer render scale passes, Full ground pushes approach p95 to 41 ms, and Few birds subsequently pass with Simple ground restored. Verify the clearer scale, Simple ground and Few birds together. At 120 FPS on a 3090, suppose its Automatic seed misses in the treeline with little GPU load. If one measured foliage reduction restores cadence, keep the aircraft clarity and test that combination on the final circuit. If the minimum permitted detail still reaches only 104 FPS there, show 104 FPS and offer a 90 FPS test.

## 5. Player flow, copy and recovery

| Screen or event | What the player sees and can do |
| --- | --- |
| Setup | Target buttons in FPS order. Show active GPU, renderer, output size/refresh, suggested start and the count of protected choices. **Settings included** expands the eligible list. Manual and ambiguous legacy choices start locked. Explicitly including one field grants permission for that run. New profiles can tune supported defaults. |
| Before Start | **“The simulator will fly the same circuit several times and try different graphics settings. The picture may change or pause briefly while scenery loads. Allow about 2–5 minutes. You can cancel at any time. Your settings are saved only when you keep the result.”** Buttons **Start benchmark** and **Back**. Show the selected target and any proposed cap change beside Start. |
| Running | Keep the aircraft unobstructed. A small panel at the screen edge shows **Testing trees · Distant bank**, **Target 60 FPS**, recent engine FPS and **Cancel**. Use named stages **Preparing → Testing → Verifying**, elapsed time and a remaining range once enough trials have run. Label shader warmup separately. Announce stage changes to assistive technology. Use text as well as color. Honor reduced motion, opaque panels, UI scale and reduced flashes. |
| Loss of focus / screen change | On focus loss stop sampling, discard the partial window and show **Resume test** or **Cancel** on return. Resume with warmup at the same leg if the configuration still matches. Minimize, resize, adapter/display/renderer changes invalidate affected windows. Restart setup when identity changes. Cancel and teardown remain available while preparing, between passes and during verification. |
| Target met | **“The simulator met your 60 FPS target. Approach was the slowest leg at 60 engine FPS.”** Distinguish engine FPS from displayed FPS on the result itself. Show a compact **Current → Recommended** diff and one reason per change. Offer **Apply recommendation**, **Keep current settings** and **Run again**. When all values already match, **Keep result** records the target and measurement without a quality change. |
| Target missed / inconclusive | **“The best tested settings averaged 48 engine FPS on approach. Your target was 60.”** Offer **Try 30 FPS** and **Keep current settings**. For interruption, timing failure, unsupported renderer or a binding cap, show the specific cause and a relevant retry action. Reserve verified saving for a complete passing result. |
| Apply, Keep and Revert | Apply the verified diff as one normal Settings preview. Keep persists only the included fields, target and matching result identity. Verify effective readback on the next normal field build for deferred options before release qualification. Revert, timeout, write failure and quit leave a recoverable prior configuration. After a failed save offer retry or discard. Support keyboard, mouse and controller at each step. |

### Persistence and handoff

- Store settings in the existing versioned display configuration. Extend its writer to stage, validate, durably replace and read back one complete generation. Keep the previous readable generation for recovery. Store compact benchmark metadata with the same transaction. Per-frame traces belong to opt-in local diagnostics. Block overlapping Settings previews or benchmark sessions. Compare the entry generation before commit so a newer edit cannot be overwritten.
- Track per-key provenance for hardware defaults, benchmark-kept choices and manual choices. A later manual Keep releases that field even when its numeric value equals a default. On old files, preserve unknown ownership and allow explicit inclusion in setup. A matching accepted result survives relaunch. When the hardware or workload changes, label the prior measurement **out of date** and offer a rerun. Preserve saved choices according to the display-store ownership rules. Restore graphics defaults retires the matching benchmark result through the same recovery transaction.
- Record schema and algorithm version, flight fixture/tape version, build/engine and actual physics backend, available GPU/driver identity, renderer/provider, target and pacing mode, field/aircraft/camera, output/drawable/internal sizes with source or **unknown**, effective knobs, per-leg metrics/counts, attempts, stop reason and winner. Compare workload-affecting identities before reuse. The UI and debug report consume the same result. Do not upload it as part of this feature.
