# OpenRC Simulator v0.1.0-rc2

The second alpha test build of an open-source RC airplane simulator (Godot 4.7, MIT). You fly a **Jensen Das Ugly Stik 60** with a .61 glow engine, from a fixed pilot position on the field, with a USB RC radio, a gamepad or the keyboard.

This release is about **what you see**. The flight model is the same as in rc1. The world around it is new: a physically based sky and haze, clouds, a sun shadow, and a grass field that fades into the distance.

> **Alpha build:** expect rough edges. It starts in the air (no takeoff or landing yet) and has no wind or menus. Feedback from RC pilots decides what comes next (see [What we need from you](#what-we-need-from-you)).

## Highlights

- **The sky shows which way is up.** It has a physically based gradient: a Hosek–Wilkie clear sky for a 45° sun, deep blue at the top and a pale horizon. It also has a real-size sun and fair-weather clouds that drift with simulation time.
- **Distance has depth.** Haze matches a clear day with 23 km visibility. The ground runs out to 20 km and fades into the horizon with no visible edge, from the pilot's eye or from 100 m up.
- **A sun shadow under the airplane.** The shadow is projected along the real sun direction, so its offset from the airplane tells you its height, as on a real field. Press **V** to switch between the sun shadow, a vertical "pilot aid" shadow, or no shadow.
- **Truer colours.** A measured tonemap (ACES, exposure 0.6) keeps the grass green and the sky blue, and the airplane readable against both.
- **Frame-time report for your PC.** Run the build with `-- --frametimes=frametimes.json --t=20`, then send us the file (see the [first-launch guide, §5](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/main/docs/FIRST-LAUNCH.md#5-measure-frame-times-for-the-landscape-work)).

## What's new since v0.1.0-rc1

**World and lighting** (landscape plan, steps L0–L4):
- **Sky:** a shader with a gradient, a 0.53° sun disc and halo, and built-in debanding.
  - We found that Godot's Compatibility renderer treats a sky shader's output as sRGB, not linear. The sky now encodes for it exactly, so the sky and the fog agree within one colour level.
- **Haze:** exponential fog from one source of truth, shared by the sky's horizon, the distant ground and every object.
- **Clouds:** a deterministic cloud layer, with no textures and no dependence on the rendering clock.
- **Sun:** light colour 5,200 K and the projected sun shadow.
  - Engine shadow maps stay off by default. In the Compatibility renderer they clip white surfaces, shift colours and double the draw calls ([godot#90259](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/90259)). For comparison, start the build with `-- --engine_shadows`.
- **Ground:** matte grass in world space, and the runway raised slightly so it never flickers at a distance.

**Airplane model** (model team):
- More detailed .61 engine and exhaust.
- New metal finishes: cast and machined aluminium, anodised gold.

**For testers and developers:**
- The frame-time logger (`--frametimes`) works in the release builds.
- Review views, render counters and determinism manifests are captured on every CI run. A visual comparison tool (FLIP) and an airplane-readability metric guard the look against regressions.
- Shaders may not use the rendering clock. Animation follows simulation time, so pausing, replays and captures stay exact.

- Linux or macOS: `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing`
- Windows PowerShell: `Get-FileHash .\openrc-simulator-*.zip`

**First launch.** The builds are not signed with a paid certificate, so each system warns once:
- **macOS 15 and later:** right-click → Open no longer works. Open the app once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → **Open Anyway**. Alternatively, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/OpenRC Simulator.app"`.
- **Windows:** in the SmartScreen warning, click More info → **Run anyway**. If Smart App Control is on, it blocks unsigned apps entirely.
- **Linux:** unzip, run `chmod +x openrc-simulator.x86_64`, then start it.

## How it was checked

- **Automated checks:** about 1,250, plus end-to-end tests on the real app with the keyboard and a simulated radio.
- **Flight checks:** flown handling against predictions, flight modes, stall and spin behaviour, and four golden flights that must replay exactly.
- **Captures:** 29 reference images that must repeat byte for byte, with pixel checks:
  - the sky gradient;
  - no banding;
  - the sun within 2 px of the light direction;
  - no seam at the 20 km horizon;
  - the shadow following the sun;
  - no clipped whites;
  - airplane readability.
- **Release builds:** made and checked in CI.
  - The exported Linux binary flies the trimmed-flight check.
  - The macOS app is universal and ad-hoc signed.
- **Determinism:** the landscape changes rendering only; the flight trace is bit-identical to rc1's.

## Known limitations

- **Handling is crisper than a real Stik.** Roll and pitch respond about 2× and 1.45× faster than a flight-identified Ultra Stick 120. Full rudder holds about 63° of sideslip.
- **A developed spin needs opposite rudder and forward stick to recover.** It does not recover hands-off.
- **No takeoff, landing or wind yet.** Ground contact is a crash, followed by an automatic restart after 1.5 s.
- **The horizon at eye level is a sharp line.** This is physically correct: the first ground beyond the horizon is about 1.4 km away. From the pilot's position the ground shadow helps mainly on close passes and landings.
- **Auto-zoom (Z) can push the ground out of the frame at altitude.**
- **Software-rendered numbers in our logs are not performance figures.** Your frame-time report is the real measure.

## What we need from you

This build is for **Gate 2: is it flyable, readable and fun?** Please:

1. **Fly the test flight:** three circuits, a loop, a roll, a stall and a spin recovery.
2. **Rate roll, pitch, yaw and throttle** from −2 (sluggish) to +2 (twitchy) compared with a real Stik.
3. **Check readability at a distance:** can you tell which way the airplane is facing at 100 m, with and without auto-zoom (Z)? Which shadow mode (V) helps you most?
4. **Send the frame-time report** (`frametimes.json`), your OS and your GPU.
5. **Report your radio:** model, USB mode, and whether calibration (K) was needed.

Open an issue or reply to the owner with your notes.
