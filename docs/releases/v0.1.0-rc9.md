# OpenRC Simulator v0.1.0-rc9

An experimental integration candidate with seven aircraft configurations, an Aircraft Workshop, flight overlays and expanded propulsion, scenery and crash behavior. Physical-model and pilot acceptance remain open.

## Changes since rc8

- **Aircraft:** Turbo Lumber Evolution now supports experimental electric flight, including airborne and runway starts. Its configuration retains fixed wheels, flaps up and receiver assists off. Two experimental Stik installations add separate MAX-55AX two-stroke and FS-64V four-stroke glow models with finite fuel, mixture controls and engine start/cutoff. The catalog also retains the original Stik, Extra 300S, P-51D and the renamed Ventage Jet.
- **Workshop:** create and save local experimental aircraft variants, compare their properties and launch isolated airborne trials. Control-travel adjustments cover the catalog; size, weight and virtual power adjustments are restricted to supported simple propeller configurations. Saved variants do not change shipped aircraft data.
- **Flight overlays:** configurable flight data, transmitter sticks, warnings, wind, telemetry and developer diagnostics, with metric/imperial/SI units. F2 cycles presets; F3 toggles diagnostics. Pause offers detailed overlay settings.
- **Camera views:** pilot, chase, orbit and close-up views, with view cycling, manual zoom and orbit controls. These presentation choices do not change the simulated flight.
- **Flight dynamics:** expanded normal-flight rates/expo, tail/downwash and pitch-response consistency checks, optional Stik ground effect, and continuously integrated turbine RPM for the Ventage Jet. These changes retain explicit estimated parameters and aircraft-specific validation limits.
- **Field and presentation:** expanded vegetation and lake integration, aircraft lighting and smoke, propeller rendering, and crash section/attachment behavior with propeller-strike feedback. Crash presentation does not establish physically calibrated structural damage.
- **Authoring and research:** stronger generated model/Workshop contracts and additional rotorcraft and RC vehicle research. Helicopters, drones and monster trucks are not new flyable catalog vehicles in this release.

## Packages and first flight

Expected package names after successful qualification:

| Platform | Archive |
| --- | --- |
| Windows x86_64 | `openrc-simulator-v0.1.0-rc9-windows-x86_64.zip` |
| Linux x86_64 | `openrc-simulator-v0.1.0-rc9-linux-x86_64.zip` |
| macOS Apple Silicon / Intel | `openrc-simulator-v0.1.0-rc9-macos-universal.zip` |

Extract the ZIP before launching and verify `SHA256SUMS`. Follow the [first-launch guide](../FIRST-LAUNCH.md). Existing preferences remain effective; choose the original Stik, calm weather and daylight for an initial comparison. A connected radio must see low throttle before arming. For the new glow installations, runway starts leave the engine stopped: F7 starts it, F6 cuts combustion and F8/F9 adjust mixture. See [glow controls and limits](../GLOW-ENGINES.md) and the [Workshop guide](../AIRCRAFT-WORKSHOP.md).

## Verification and limits

This is a prepared candidate, not a statement of completed publication. [Release evidence](../research/release-readiness/PT1-rc9/README.md) records the frozen source, checks and outstanding blockers. Publication requires the complete app, schema/generator, offline-tool and rendered-capture checks, followed by three-platform export and package checks. The numeric platform version remains `0.1.0`; the release tag supplies the full `v0.1.0-rc9` identity.

Native GUI/radio qualification, target-GPU performance, pilot readability and independent physical calibration remain separate gates. Glow engine/propeller and electric data include estimates; Timber uses a prescribed-voltage model and has no calibrated endurance claim. The turbine still uses prescribed fuel mass. Packages have no installer or trusted developer signing. Research kernels and software regressions do not qualify real aircraft or vehicles.
