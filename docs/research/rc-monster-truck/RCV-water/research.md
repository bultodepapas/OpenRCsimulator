# RC truck water and mud interaction research

**Date:** 2026-10-14  
**Status:** Pre-implementation investigation retained as dated evidence; current implementation and checks are in [README.md](README.md). Lake geometry is estimated, not surveyed. Present-tense findings below describe the inspected baseline.

## Finding

The RC activity already renders the shared lake and samples its bed as ordinary ground, but it has no truck-specific water force, buoyancy, depth-dependent tire response, or wheel spray. A truck can therefore retain tire support from the lake bed while visually underwater. The safest first pass is to reuse `LakeSurface` and the existing lake renderer, add memoryless water and mud terms to the truck's generalized dynamics at every RK4 stage in both native and reference kernels, and keep soil deformation and electrical damage out of scope.

## Existing interfaces and limits

- `app/data/fields/lake.json` defines lake `near_runway`, centered at N=110 m, E=125 m. The water datum is 0.45 m down. Its shoreline/bank polygons and bathymetry are estimates; the source explicitly says they are not surveyed. Bed depth grows from 0 m at shore to 0.8 m at 5.5 m inset and 5 m at 20 m inset, then remains at 5 m.
- `app/physics/lake_surface.gd` provides `sample_terrain_validated(lake, north, east)` (bed/bank region, ground height, signed shoreline and bank distances, bed depth), `water_mean_down(lake)`, `water_height_down(...)`, and `shore_distance_validated(...)`. Use the same normalized lake passed to the course. `water_height_down` includes the render waves; the mean datum is the better first force surface because the wave shader has no corresponding water-current model.
- `app/physics/ground_terrain.gd` composes the lake bed into ground sampling. `app/physics/ground_vehicle/airfield_course.gd` passes the lake into that sampler, but its material friction and rolling resistance still come from the field/track surface. No water-specific sample or force reaches `vehicle_dynamics.gd` today. `LakeSurface.sweep_probe` and `app/sim/lake_collision.gd` serve point-contact / terminal aircraft cases; they are not truck immersion models.
- `app/render/lake/lake_world.gd` and `lake_terrain.gd` already build the shared lake, bed, bank, and water mesh. `app/render/lake/water_effects.gd` has impact rings, plume, droplets, and splash audio, but also aircraft-root sinking. Reuse its visual effects through a truck-specific adapter and do not call its aircraft-sink behavior. `app/vehicles/ground/ground_effects.gd` already owns ground dust/grime. Splash audio is already shipped under `app/assets/audio/water/` with its license in `LICENSES.md`.
- `app/data/vehicles/ground/mt8_bisonte.json` estimates 4.8 kg sprung mass plus 0.18 kg per wheel, or 5.52 kg total, and derives 0.10 m tire radius from the supplied blockout. These are not measured values. The hull/collision probes are contact proxies, not a watertight displacement volume; the file contains no measured enclosed volume or tire construction.

## Bounded first physics pass

Use `LakeSurface.sample_terrain_validated` and the mean water datum at each RK4 stage. Add loads to the generic 14-state generalized RHS before the tangent-friction solve in both native and reference paths; avoid substep memory so stage, replay, and parity behavior remain deterministic.

- **Buoyancy:** Archimedes' principle gives F_b = rho_w * g * V_disp. In NED, upward buoyancy is negative down. Use only an explicit, conservative effective displaced volume for submerged solid geometry and the declared open-body/vented-wheel assumption; do not infer a sealed hull from the collision probes or count the entire tire envelope as sealed air. The estimated truck mass would require about 5.52 L of displaced fresh water to float; that is a useful sanity check, not a validated Bisonte float threshold. The IAPWS formulation is the authoritative source if temperature-dependent water density becomes necessary; 1000 kg/m³ is a rounded first-pass fresh-water value.
- **Translational drag:** A bounded quadratic form is F_d = -0.5 * rho_w * C_D * A_sub * |v_rel| * v_rel, evaluated at the submerged point and paired with tau = r cross F_d. Use a still-water reference initially. The drag equation is a standard fluid-drag form, but coefficient and projected submerged area are shape/flow dependent; NASA's coefficient guidance emphasizes declaring reference area and measuring the coefficient. Transferring the equation to this submerged truck is an engineering inference, and the effective C_D * A must remain an identified/tuned parameter until towing or deceleration data exist. Keep drag dissipative: F_d dot v_rel <= 0.
- **Wheel rotational drag:** If included in the first pass, apply a bounded torque only to immersed wheels, opposing wheel spin (for example tau_w = -k_w * |omega_w| * omega_w). Its coefficient is also unknown; keep it separate from motor/brake torque and fit it to wheel coast-down or loaded rotation measurements.
- **Shallow/deep transition:** Derive immersion from local mean water level, `bed_depth_m`, tire radius, and the available wheel/hull geometry. The current tire radius is 0.10 m, so the two near-shore points above span partial-to-full tire immersion. Let bed contact, buoyancy, and tire load determine traction continuously. Avoid a hard “deep water disables traction” depth threshold when the bed still supports the tires; a truck that loses ground support should do so through its force/contact balance. Do not infer electronics damage from immersion.
- **Mud-like bank:** Add a smooth, static shore-distance blend to the existing friction and rolling-resistance inputs within an explicitly authored bank strip. Keep it memoryless and describe coefficients as estimates; no measured mud friction data exist. Do not add rutting, sinkage, soil deformation, or permanent grime accumulation in this pass. Reece's soil–vehicle study explains why soft-soil mobility depends on pressure-sinkage, stress distribution, and slip-sinkage experiments; a simple material blend cannot substantiate those effects.
- **Visuals and sound:** Trigger existing splash/plume/ring effects from wheel immersion and speed, and reduce or replace dust with water/mud spray while the wheel is wet. Keep these effects render-only and use the existing licensed assets. The visual pass does not validate the force model.

## Calibration and evidence limits

The water datum, shoreline, bed profile, truck mass, tire radius, chassis datum, drag coefficient-area product, effective displaced volume, wheel drag coefficient, and mud friction/rolling values have different provenance. Lake geometry, vehicle mass, and most dynamics values are estimates; cite that provenance in configuration. Before claiming physical realism, measure vehicle mass and tire/hull displacement, characterize immersion and wheel support, measure wet/dry traction and rolling resistance on the chosen soil, and identify drag from controlled tow or coast-down runs. A runtime/parity test can establish determinism and sign/energy bounds, but cannot establish real-world accuracy.

It distinguishes low-speed viscous drag from the quadratic regime.
- NASA Glenn, [Drag Coefficient](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/drag-coefficient/): coefficient depends on shape, inclination, and flow; reference area must be declared and the coefficient is experimentally determined. Its displayed application is aircraft/rocket drag; applying it to this water case is an inference.
- IAPWS, [R6-95(2018), Formulation for Thermodynamic Properties of Ordinary Water](https://iapws.org/technical-guidance/release/IAPWS-95): official formulation for calculating water properties from temperature and density.
- A. R. Reece, [“Principles of Soil-Vehicle Mechanics”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1243/PIME_AUTO_1965_180_009_02), Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers: Automobile Division, 180(1), 45–66 (1965), DOI 10.1243/PIME_AUTO_1965_180_009_02.
