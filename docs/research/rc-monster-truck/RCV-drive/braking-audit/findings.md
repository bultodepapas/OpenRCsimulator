# Braking audit, 2026-10-14

This read-only audit probes the generated MT8 Bisonte after six seconds of full throttle on a flat surface (`μ = 0.85`, rolling value `1.0`), then applies full brake with no steering. The loaded drive model has 4.2 N·m per wheel, 100 rad/s torque fade, 2.0 N·m per wheel brake torque, tire friction scale 0.9, and 0.04 s slip relaxation. The audit changes only the brake-torque value for the partial-brake cases.

The launch ends with a small but repeatable heading seed: 0.003658 rad (0.210°), yaw rate 3.25×10⁻⁵ rad/s, and body lateral speed 6.75×10⁻⁶ m/s. Left/right wheel loads differ by less than 0.001 N at that point. I left the state as produced by the simulation.

The native-backed 1.2 and 1.5 N·m partial-brake runs yaw about 176° and 164° while remaining upright, and reach 4.3–5.0 m/s body-lateral speed. An earlier GDScript-reference screen at 0.8 and 1.0 N·m also approached 180° yaw. Body-forward velocity `u` becomes negative after the heading has turned; that value alone does not mean the truck tracked straight backward. At 2.0 N·m, a native-backed full-brake step stops with about 21° peak yaw. Ramping the same command from zero to full over 200 ms increases peak yaw to about 98° and increases lateral travel. The response is strongly dependent on speed and brake application in this model.

A separate native-backed speed screen shows that the partial-brake yaw is concentrated at the higher entry speeds. With brake inputs 0.4–0.75, peak yaw is 4.7–7.9° from 5.93 m/s, 94.1–142.0° from 7.91 m/s, and 163.8–180.0° from 9.89 m/s; full-brake peaks at those speeds are 1.0°, 3.3°, and 21.2°. The launches used fade settings of 60, 80, and 100 rad/s to set those entry speeds; the 9.89 m/s case uses the current 100 rad/s setting. The verified circuit run peaks at 5.858 m/s ([run data](../candidate-fast-signed-verified.json)), just below the lowest stress-test entry speed and below the two higher-speed cases. These are flat-surface handling observations, not a prediction of every circuit condition. See [speed_screen.gd](../../../../../research/rc-monster-truck/brake_audit/speed_screen.gd) and [speed-screen.log](speed-screen.log).

The partial-brake outcomes are nearly unchanged at 240 and 480 Hz. The earlier six-versus-24 sweep comparison was ineffective: its scripts changed the GDScript constant but ran trajectories through `step()` without selecting the GDScript reference backend. `step()` dispatches through `_evaluate_active()` and used the native backend in this run, whose six-sweep cap is compiled into the native library. The identical nominal-cap traces therefore do not establish 24-sweep convergence and are excluded from the handling conclusion.

The separate 1.5 N·m early-out spot check did bypass native: its manual RK4 driver calls `Vehicle._evaluate()` directly, comparing adaptive early-out with forced six sweeps in the GDScript reference solver. At 1.104 s the changes were 5.5×10⁻⁶ m/s in `u` and 1.3×10⁻⁶ rad in yaw. This is a reference-backend spot check only; it does not test native early-out sensitivity. I did not alter either backend's tolerance values. The 240/480 Hz agreement remains evidence of temporal consistency, but this audit does not establish sweep-count convergence for the active native solver or physical accuracy of the tire forces and yaw response.

The launch seed and resulting partial-brake yaw remain observed handling limits for this uncalibrated model. The reference-backend transient diagnostics show left/right tire-force asymmetry during braking; this audit did not record a matching native force breakdown. I found no confirmed equation defect and make no physical-validation claim. No runtime, generated vehicle, or solver files were edited.
