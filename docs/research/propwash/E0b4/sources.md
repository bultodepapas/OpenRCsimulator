# E0b4 — Propwash source audit

2026-10-07 · **Status: primary-source bounds verified; Stik calibration remains open.** This note supports E0b4 sensitivity work. It does not enable propwash on the production Stik.

## Selig: speed factor and meaning

Selig's AIAA 2010-7938 paper defines the downstream induced-velocity increment as

\[
w_{downstream}=k_w w,
\qquad
w=\tfrac12\left[-V_\infty+\sqrt{V_\infty^2+\frac{2T}{\rho A}}\right],
\qquad
m=\frac{V_{\infty,N}}{V_{disc,N}}.
\]

These are Eqs. 17, 19 and 20 on printed p. 6. Eq. 18 makes \(k_w\) constant at \(k_s\) below \(m_s\), linear between \(m_s\) and \(m_f\), then constant at \(k_f\). Fig. 5 on the same page plots typical, approximate values: \(k_s\approx0.8\) at \(m=0\), rising linearly to \(k_f\approx1.8\) by \(m\approx0.75\), then flat through \(m=1\). The paper does not tabulate these values or give exact transition knots. The figure is a reading from the plotted curve, not a propeller-specific measurement. Selig calls 2 the ideal isolated-propeller limit and says propeller/fuselage interaction lowers the downstream increment below it.

The text on pp. 5–6 says the static free-jet centreline speed at the tail is nearly the induced velocity at the disc; it also says a contracted normal-working slipstream can reach twice the disc-induced velocity after a few diameters. Those statements are centreline descriptions. They do not make \(k_w\) a tail-area mean. Eq. 21 (printed p. 7) adds \(k_w w\) to the component's local relative-flow vector before computing surface loads. Do not multiply all tail area by \(k_w^2\), or treat the figure as an area-mean dynamic-pressure ratio.

**Source:** M. S. Selig, “Modeling Propeller Aerodynamics and Slipstream Effects on Small UAVs in Realtime,” AIAA 2010-7938, 2010, [author-hosted PDF](https://m-selig.ae.illinois.edu/pubs/Selig-2010-AIAA-2010-7638-PropAeroSim.pdf).

## Khan and Nahon: measured static profiles and fitted model

Khan's McGill thesis, Chapter 5 §§5.5.1–5.5.2, describes a static bench survey of an Electrifly Powerflow 10×4.5 propeller (\(D=0.254\) m). It reports runs at 1,750, 5,425 and 6,425 rpm. The 5,425 rpm run supplied the fit: measured thrust was \(6.43\pm0.03\) N and \(C_T=0.1542\). The measurement grid spans \(x/D=0.08\) to 5.12, including 3.54 and 4.33, and radial positions 0 to 2.91 propeller radii at 10 mm spacing. Chapter 5 Fig. 5.8 (printed p. 120) compares fitted profiles with measurements at 1.58D and 4.33D for the 1,750 and 6,425 rpm runs.

The fitted efflux relations are \(V_0=1.46nD\sqrt{C_T}\), \(d_0=0.764D\), and \(R_0=0.74R_p\) (Eqs. 5.12–5.14, printed p. 115); Eq. 5.10 there defines \(R_{max,0}=0.67(R_0-R_h)\). In the second ZFE band, \(1.7\le\xi=(d_s-d_0)/D_0\le4.25\), Eqs. 5.18–5.20 (printed p. 118) give \(V_{s,max}=V_0(1.37-0.1529\xi)\), \(R_{max}=R_{max,0}(1.3-0.3059\xi)\), and

\[
V_s=V_{s,max}\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{r_s-R_{max}}{0.5176R_{max,0}+0.2295(d_s-d_0-R_0)}\right)^2\right].
\]

The source also has separate equations for the earlier ZFE band and for established flow; this E0b4 calculation uses only Eqs. 5.18–5.20. Fig. 5.7 (printed p. 117) shows measured-profile fits at 0.87D and 1.97D in the ZFE and 4.33D in the ZEF. The thesis's Figs. 5.6–5.7 (printed pp. 116–117) show the fitted velocity maximum and radial profile. The thesis notes that a one-term symmetric Gaussian cannot represent the measured asymmetry around the axis in the ZFE; it underpredicts near-axis speed there, with the discrepancy reducing farther downstream (Fig. 5.8 discussion, printed p. 120).

The equations provide a **model-derived Stik tail estimate**, not a measured Stik value. The E0b3b fixture's propeller-to-tail reference stations are 1.134176 m and 1.154176 m; with its 12 in (0.3048 m) propeller these are \(x/D=3.721\) and 3.787, or \(\xi=3.996\) and 4.085. Both are within the stated second-ZFE branch, which ends at \(x/D\approx3.909\). The survey grid includes 3.54D and 4.33D, but the latter belongs to the established-flow branch; it must not be treated as an endpoint in a linear interpolation of this ZFE equation. At the Stik stations, Eq. 5.18's peak is about 1.39 and 1.36 times the static disc-induced velocity \(w\); applying Eq. 5.20 at the axis gives nearly the same values. This calculation transfers the fit from a different 10×4.5 propeller to the Stik's normalized geometry and is neither measured Stik evidence nor a cross-sectional mean.

Eq. 5.20's radial width also depends on the propeller hub radius through \(R_{max,0}\). The Stik fixture contains the hub centre but no hub radius. [The reproducible donor-profile calculation](donor-profile.json), generated by [donor_profile.py](../../../../research/propwash/e0b4/donor_profile.py), therefore reports \(R_h/R_p=[0,0.1,0.2]\) as assumed sensitivity points and does not select a Stik value. Across these points, the Gaussian's area-mean \(V_s^2/w^2\) over the neutral horizontal tail is 0.916–0.974, versus centreline \(V_s^2/w^2\approx1.926–1.927\). For the fin, the area mean is 1.410–1.448, versus centreline 1.860. The normalized Gaussian area-mean/centreline-q ratios are about 0.475–0.506 for the horizontal tail and 0.758–0.779 for the fin. These results use midpoint integration at 128 and 256 samples per generated profile interval; they are geometry-weighted source-model calculations, not aerodynamic load predictions.

For a radial-shape comparison, the JSON also integrates the E0b3b smooth occupancy over the same neutral outlines. Its estimated static edge width gives mean occupancy 0.382 for the horizontal tail and 0.720 for the fin. This quantity weights area receiving a **uniform local wash increment**; it is not Khan's radial velocity or dynamic-pressure profile. The comparison illustrates why neither centreline speed nor smooth occupancy alone should be applied as a whole-surface \(q\) multiplier.

The test propeller, size, blade geometry, rpm (maximum 6,425), Reynolds number and thrust coefficient do not match the Stik's estimated 12×6, 11,149 rpm operating point. The velocity survey is static; it does not validate a Stik cruise endpoint. The hot-wire sampled at 1 Hz, with stated resolution 0.01 m/s over 0.2–5 m/s and 0.1 m/s over 5.1–25 m/s. The measured profiles were interpolated to 1 mm spacing for coefficient fitting. The thesis reports overall RMS errors of 0.6 m/s (13% of maximum speed) at 1,750 rpm and 2 m/s (12%) at 6,425 rpm. In the ZFE the fit underpredicts near-axis speed because it cannot reproduce the measured asymmetric profile; that discrepancy vanishes with axial distance (Fig. 5.8 discussion, printed pp. 120–121). Khan and Nahon's 2015 journal paper is the peer-reviewed follow-on; the thesis supplies the exact equation and experimental details reproduced here.

## E0b4 sensitivity interpretation

- Use \([0.8,1.8]\) as Selig's graph-read typical static-to-forward pair, with the transition \(m\approx0\) to \(m\approx0.75\). These are approximate generic source values, not calibrated Stik constants.
- The Khan fit provides a separate static centreline estimate around 1.36–1.39 at the Stik's normalized tail stations. Keep it distinct from Selig's generic \(k_s\approx0.8\); neither is an area mean, and their difference is useful model-form uncertainty.
- A sweep such as \(k_s=[0.8,1.1,1.4]\), \(k_f=[1.4,1.7,1.9]\) brackets the Selig graph and the Khan-derived static estimate. The middle values and bracket endpoints are sensitivity choices, not confidence limits. Keep \(k_f=2\) separate as an ideal isolated-propeller bound.
- Integrate a radial wake distribution over the actual polygonal tail geometry. The E0b3b edge occupancy (including its 0.15 fixture width) is a numerical load-transition regularizer, not Khan's Gaussian velocity profile or a measured wake edge. No source here calibrates that occupancy or the Stik's powered cruise derivatives.
