# Applied mathematics and numerical integration

The 240 Hz shared rigid-body kernel exhibits fourth-order convergence in independent free-fall, spinning-body and variable-mass benchmarks. The shipped Jensen Ugly Stik 60 applies the end-of-tick propeller RPM early, increasing static thrust impulse by 1.302% during a 0.25 s throttle step. Three additional defects affect authored airships. Admitted variants can produce 4.60 W at zero charge, return 12.33 times the incoming energy in a ground rebound, or silently distort the added mass of a nearly spherical hull. Each defect has a local correction.

## MATH-01 · Lagged propeller RPM arrives at the body one tick early

The Jensen definition has an idle speed of 2,800 rpm, a full static speed of 11,149 rpm and a 0.25 s time constant. Its engine defines neither a torque-balance shaft nor a separate glow installation. In the running-engine branch, the pre-step computes the exact endpoint RPM. The simulation installs the new auxiliary values before stage 1, and the legacy stage reads the same RPM at each subsequent evaluation. This persists through native integration. Relevant sources are `app/data/aircraft/jensen_ugly_stik_60.json:1511–1554`, `app/physics/propulsion.gd:62–70`, `app/sim/flight_session.gd:6537–6540`, `app/sim/simulation.gd:475–486`, and `research/native-kernel/src/nk_pre_step.hpp:241–254`, `nk_stage.hpp:269–275`, `nk_prop.hpp:105–118`.

At zero inflow, the authored coefficient law reduces to $F=C_T\rho D^4(n/60)^2$, with $C_T=0.113$, $D=0.3048$ m and ρ=1.225 kg/m³. For a held idle-to-full demand, $n(t)=a+b\exp(-t/\tau)$, where $a=11149$, $b=2800-a$. Exact integration of that squared exponential gives **3.04965681419 N·s** over the first 0.25 s. The experiment executes the actual native pre-step and propeller headers on this single static force element.

| RPM handoff frequency | Endpoint-held impulse (N·s) | Error | Stage-time RPM and RK weights (N·s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 120 Hz | 3.12906169427 | +2.604% | 3.04965682715 |
| **240 Hz** | **3.08935377463** | **+1.302%** | **3.04965681500** |
| 480 Hz | 3.06950392026 | +0.651% | 3.04965681424 |

The first 240 Hz tick receives 4.834% excess impulse. The endpoint RPM at 0.25 s is correct to numerical precision in all three runs. The pilot command is constant on the same 240 Hz tape. The table varies the experimental **RPM update** cadence to identify first-order integration of the load. In a separate experiment the 240 Hz RPM handoff stays fixed, and 120/240/480 Hz plant grids integrate the exact overlap of every held force interval. All three give 3.08935377463 N·s. Shorter body steps alone retain the timing bias. The effect on complete flight remains unmeasured.

**Native correction.** Carry initial RPM, target and time constant in immutable tick context. Evaluate the analytic exponential at each RK abscissa for propeller force, reaction torque, gyroscopic momentum and applicable wake inputs. Continue publishing the actual endpoint RPM. Preserve the model-specific switch-off policy and give the new sampled-input behavior a replay identity. The native and reference implementations both need the fixed-wing tick-context adapter. Risk is medium because accepted trajectories and old replay interpretation are affected. Use the static impulse fixture to require absolute error below $10^{-7}$ N·s at 240 Hz and fourth-order reduction across the three rates. Add throttle-down, steady RPM and shaft-coupled controls, qualified package parity, and matching-backend old/new replay.

## MATH-02 · Empty airship packs can keep supplying current

The airship derives voltage, current, motor speed demand and thrust from OCV and resistance. At SOC ≤ 0, only the SOC derivative becomes zero. The bus and motor demand still use the OCV at the first table point. Its session registers no depletion event. The battery permits independently authored cutoff voltage and empty OCV. Its own dynamics fixture already pairs 3.2 V/cell cutoff with 3.3 V/cell empty OCV. See `app/physics/airship/airship_dynamics.gd:187–221,236–245,588–597`, `app/physics/airship/airship_data.gd:162–170,555–560`, `app/sim/airship/session.gd:91–99,161–175,235–242`, `app/tests/test_airship_dynamics.gd:85–87`.

The subsystem run takes the stock blimp, sets cutoff to 3.2 V/cell and starts at an admitted 5% charge in its original 2.2 Ah pack. Neutral axes and full throttle command the three axial motors in proportions 1, 1 and 0.7. Their time constants coincide, so their dynamics reduce exactly to one speed and SOC. Vehicle acceleration and inflow do not enter the implemented battery/motor equations. At 240 Hz the first nonpositive boundary occurs at 201.45417 s. Indicated remaining charge freezes at −0.0009612 C, and the next 10 s draw **4.65730 C**. The 120/480 Hz controls draw 4.65730/4.65731 C over the same post-boundary duration. An independent bisection of the exhausted-state motor equilibrium gives **0.465726 A, 4.600925 W, and 0.907183 N total static thrust**. The actual production stage measured the same current, power and thrust at zero charge. Starting from a positive SOC of $10^{-8}$, one coupled 240 Hz step ended at −1.12507849 × 10⁻⁷ SOC while the stage still drew 0.465725857 A. The stock 3.3 V/cell cutoff gave zero drive and current from rest at zero SOC. The 201 s trajectory and 10 s charge integral use the independently reduced subsystem.

**Native correction.** Make remaining charge a unilateral resource with an explicit located transition. Continue the smooth powered law through trial states for localization, then set accepted charge to zero and disconnect motor drive and pack-side draw for the rest of the tick. Retain aerodynamic coast-down on the modeled rotor time scale. Use the reference event pattern in `app/physics/electric_battery_step.gd` and the pure C++ scalar/event boundary in `research/native-kernel/src/nk_tick.hpp` as patterns. The airship needs its own adapter and checkpoint/log identity. The aircraft-specific driver indices cannot be reused directly. Risk is medium and chiefly affects depletion/replay semantics. Require nonnegative accepted charge, one exhaustion event, bounded local root residual, zero pack current after disconnect, decaying shaft speed, and immutable dry-run/rollback before acceptance. Test both cutoff/OCV cases, an initially spinning motor, 32 initial event phases and all three plant rates against an independently integrated current trace.

## MATH-04 · Admitted airship contact stiffness can create rebound energy

Gear and hull accept stiffness up to $10^6$ N/m and damping up to $10^5$ N·s/m. The airship supplies neither a stiffness-dependent step count nor an admission test coupling those constants to effective mass. Its normal law computes a unilateral spring and a depth-ramped, clamped damper. The generic integrator therefore uses one RK4 step per 240 Hz tick. See `app/physics/airship/airship_data.gd:189–203`, `app/physics/airship/airship_dynamics.gd:403–416,474–491`, `app/sim/airship/session.gd:161–175`, `app/sim/simulation.gd:565–581`.

Use the project's analytical 2 × 1 m spheroid with its admitted zero-drag/added-mass configuration, coincident CG and buoyancy center, neutral loading, flat ground and one centered wheel. Centering the fixture wheel at $x=y=0$ removes pitch coupling. Its 0.2 m clearance below the envelope keeps the other witnesses away throughout the reference contact. At ρ=1.225 kg/m³ the heave mass is $1.225\pi/3$ kg. Start a 1 m/s descent at first wheel touch, with zero damping. The source law reduces to $\ddot x=-(k/m)\max(x,0)$. Its exact rebound speed is 1 m/s and contact duration is $\pi\sqrt{m/k}$, or **3.5582 ms** at $10^6$ N/m. At 240 Hz, $h\sqrt{k/m}=3.679$ exceeds the smooth oscillator's RK4 imaginary-axis stability radius $2\sqrt 2$. The unilateral benchmark directly determines the resulting energy.

| Plant rate, constant 240 Hz commands | $k=400$ N/m, energy out/in | $k=10^5$ N/m | $k=10^6$ N/m | Rebound speed at $10^6$ N/m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 120 Hz | 0.999875 | 0.796881 | 290.524461 | 17.04478 m/s |
| **240 Hz** | **1.000051** | **0.924436** | **12.328487** | **3.51119 m/s** |
| 480 Hz | 0.999966 | 0.987460 | 0.691213 | 0.83139 m/s |

The actual coupled GDScript stage and RK4 matched the 120, 240 and 480 Hz stiff-contact rebound speeds. The 240 Hz production result was 3.511194532 m/s and 12.32848704 times the incoming energy. At 3,840 Hz, equivalent to 16 pieces per 240 Hz tick for this held command, the stage returned 0.999921707 m/s and an energy ratio of 0.99984342. The compliant 400 N/m control returned 1.000025582 m/s at 240 Hz. The separate C++ reduction found a maximum absolute energy-ratio error of 0.005873 over 32 impact phases for the 16-piece case. The shipped pair has 450 N/m and 22 N·s/m per gear. The fixture tests the authored-data limits, with 400 N/m as a resolved control. A replay or finite endpoint alone would accept the 3.51 m/s rebound.

**Native correction.** Use the airship's established six-dimensional spatial-mass Cholesky factor to compute normal mobility $W=J M^{-1}J^T$ across simultaneously reachable contact points. Bound the fastest active stiffness and damping modes, locate contact onset/release where necessary, and supply deterministic native substeps through the model callback. Reject unsupported combinations when their accuracy budget would exceed the available piece count. The weighted mobility approach in `app/physics/normal_contact_admission.gd:76–113` is reusable after replacing its aircraft mass assumptions. Its current 0.1 frequency-product target would require 37 pieces for this extreme fixture, beyond the shared budget of 16. The 16-piece result quantifies the benefit of subdivision at this impact speed. A new admission threshold requires its own qualification. Risk is medium-high because simultaneous contacts and nonsmooth entry/exit change replay. Require ≤1% energy-ratio error over the 32 undamped phases at 240 Hz or explicit preflight refusal, contact-free parity, nonincreasing rebound energy within tolerance when damping is added, multiaxis/contact-set checks, and target-native cost near the ground.

## MATH-03 · Nearly spherical airship added mass loses its physical limit

The geometry helper admits volume-equivalent prolate spheroids with $0<b<a$. Its log formula for the axial coefficient subtracts nearly equal terms proportional to $e$, leaving a signal of order $e^3$. The transverse formula separately subtracts terms of order $1/e^2$. These operations lose precision before coefficients are multiplied into the 6×6 added-mass matrix. The current near-sphere test stops at $b/a=0.9999$. Relevant paths are `app/physics/airship/airship_data.gd:397–411,435–446` and `app/tests/test_airship_dynamics.gd:32–46,151–158,183–194`.

| Shape | Current axial/transverse $k_1,k_2$ | Independent 80-place reference |
|---|---|---|
| Stock 5 m blimp | 0.0571948649, 0.8973521321 | Same to $5\times10^{-16}$ |
| $b/a=1-10^{-10}$ | 0.435035307, 0.534739161 | 0.49999999994, 0.50000000003 |
| $b/a=1-10^{-12}$ | −1.399610955, −0.555802573 | 0.4999999999994, 0.5000000000003 |

The test fixture supplies length 2 m, diameter $2(1-\delta)$ m and its own analytic volume, preserving the actual conversion from volume to $b$. The stable reference evaluates $(\operatorname{atanh}e-e)/e^3=\sum_{j\ge0}e^{2j}/(2j+3)$ and β=1−α/2 at high precision. The actual loader and full model derivation admitted the $10^{-10}$ fixture with $k_1=0.43503530687$, $k_2=0.53473916082$ and a slightly negative rotational coefficient. The $10^{-12}$ document passed the loader and produced negative translational added mass. Final model derivation refused that fixture because its total spatial inertia failed Cholesky factorization. The extreme case therefore causes a false geometry rejection. The more moderate case silently changes acceleration. The stock blimp and the $10^{-4}$ near-sphere control both passed.

**Native correction.** Prepare coefficients once per configuration. For small eccentricity evaluate the series including a directly expanded rotation coefficient with leading term $e^4/6$. Choose the switch from an error bound. Use $e^2=(1-b/a)(1+b/a)$ near unity. Specify the analytic spherical endpoint explicitly if extending admission to $a=b$. Retain a float64 reference oracle for the existing airship route and share verified arithmetic with its eventual C++ preparation path. Risk is low. Sweep the catalog ratio, a logarithmic approach to unity and both sides of the series switch. Require $k_1,k_2\ge0$, $k_{rot}\ge0$, axial/transverse error below $10^{-10}$, a scaled bound on rotation and the existing force/added-mass and tensor-energy tests. This work has no dependency on a native airship flight port.
