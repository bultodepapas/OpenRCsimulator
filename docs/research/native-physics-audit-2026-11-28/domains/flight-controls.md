## CTRL-01 · An abrupt heading change enters the FPV roll command

**Evidence.** At 45° roll, zero yaw and centered sticks, use two normalized poses at pitch `acos(0.2) ± 10⁻⁸ rad` (about 78.463°). Their physical orientations differ by just 2×10⁻⁸ rad. The branch switches the reference heading from 0° to −45.58469°. Stock roll-rate demand changes from −225.629735 to −35.203124°/s. Both pitch demands are −400°/s. With equal previous setpoint memory, the stock 80 Hz low-pass still passes a **166.97658°/s** roll-demand jump on the next 240 Hz sample. At ±10⁻⁵ rad the raw jump is 190.42888°/s, and at ±10⁻⁸ it is 190.42661°/s. Zero bank removes the jump, and substituting the equivalent quaternion −q reproduces every result. These are admitted controller inputs and normalized flight attitudes. This is an algebraic component witness, without a claim about how often pilots encounter it.

The free-flight session and race wrapper install the same framed plant. The Angle loop chooses the nose azimuth when the horizontal nose projection exceeds 0.2 and the top-side azimuth below it. Those directions disagree when banked despite the nose having a valid horizontal projection on both sides of this threshold. The shortest-quaternion-error calculation then feeds a discontinuity in the *desired* attitude into the roll channel. The existing near-vertical heading assertion uses zero roll. Sources: `app/sim/multirotor/session.gd:284–320`, `app/physics/multirotor/controlled_plant.gd:307–321,341–347`, `app/physics/multirotor/flight_controller.gd:330–355,447–475`, `app/physics/math3d.gd:164–177`, `app/data/multirotors/openrc-fpv5-x1.json:235–254,341–346`, `app/tests/test_mr_controlled_plant.gd:193–220`.

**Native change and acceptance.** Implement a versioned thrust-direction attitude error for Angle mode. Centered sticks specify world vertical directly, which needs no inferred heading. For a tilted stick demand, define a continuous pilot-relative horizontal reference through near-vertical flight and an explicit tie rule at exact thrust-vector antipodes. If the chosen law stores a reference direction, include it in the sampled checkpoint and rejection rollback. PX4's reduced-attitude implementation gives a design precedent [1]. OpenRC's OSD also calls the existing heading helper (`app/vehicles/multirotor/osd_model.gd:175`), so the flight-law change must explicitly account for that separate compass consumer. Verify the seam in both directions at ±45° and ±80° banks, require <0.001°/s demand difference for the 2×10⁻⁸ rad fixture, exercise both quaternion signs and retain single-axis tilt, Acro, yaw-rate and inverted-recovery checks. Risk is the choice of recovery axis near an actual inverted attitude.

## CTRL-02 · The FPV integral cannot see the first torque limiter

**Evidence.** Use an already armed, source-ready Acro frame at full SOC, zero body rates, zero integral/previous yaw setpoint, throttle limit 1, 65% absolute collective, healthy rotors and the stock rate profile. A full yaw-stick step produces a filtered rate error of 10.25368794 rad/s. The raw yaw request is +0.92283191 N·m and the first limiter clips it to +0.35 N·m. The independent allocation solution for rotors 1–4 is 4.25147, 21.26222, 21.04295 and 4.47073 N, all comfortably inside the controller's [0.11614, 24.13121] N box. The allocator returns +0.35 N·m. OpenRC's anti-windup term is therefore zero within floating-point roundoff, and its integral grows **+0.00854474 N·m** in the same direction. Negative full yaw mirrors the result, and centered yaw gives zero. The stock integral bound is ±0.06 N·m. The proof establishes one sampled update with the production equations and geometry. It does not establish a full-flight overshoot.

The first clamp overwrites the unconstrained PID result. The subsequent tracking residual subtracts this already-clipped request from the static allocation prediction. An independent inverse-wrench solution is decisive here because production takes the exact in-box solution before calling any bounded optimizer. The existing full-throttle saturation test exercises the laboratory plant and checks bounded memory plus final settling; the invisible upstream clamp can satisfy both. Sources: `app/physics/multirotor/flight_controller.gd:358–401`, `app/physics/multirotor/allocator.gd:70–77,137–158`, `app/physics/multirotor/rates.gd:55–67`, `app/physics/multirotor/actuator_map.gd:47–74`, `app/data/multirotors/openrc-fpv5-x1.json:274–355`, `app/physics/multirotor/controlled_plant.gd:1636–1666`, `app/tests/test_mr_controlled_plant.gd:223–237`.

**Native change and acceptance.** Keep raw, torque-limited and statically allocated requests distinguishable. A conservative initial candidate blocks the rate-error contribution to the integral when that contribution drives the upstream clamp farther into saturation. Retain the existing allocator back-calculation, the integral clamp, low-collective freeze and disarm reset. On these two interior-allocation fixtures the proposed conditional law gives |ΔI| ≤10⁻¹² N·m. A competing combined tracking law gives −0.03919125 N·m for positive yaw using today's gain; this is a design comparison, not a preferred tuning value. Classical tracking and PX4's directional downstream saturation feedback support testing both limits explicitly [2, 3]. Add upstream-only, rotor-box-only, combined, unwind/reversal and unsaturated controls. Compare rise time, overshoot and settling on short full-stick-and-release tapes and rerun the existing sample-rate screen. Risk is lost disturbance rejection or an opposite-sign integral after a short feed-forward transient, especially on roll/pitch. The static allocator has no actuator measurement, so treating motor phase lag as a persistent torque shortfall would change the controller again.

**Voltage and available torque.** The existing electrical law explicitly gives the allocator open-circuit voltage at the sampled charge. For four equally driven healthy rotors on a static stand at frozen full SOC and full duty, its map predicts **24.13121 N/rotor**. Simultaneously satisfying the same steady motor/drag equations and the plant's four-motor discharging bus gives **17.11467 N/rotor at 20.24290 V**. At duty 0.2 those figures are 1.53753 and 1.52297 N. Setting pack resistance to zero recovers the map at both duties. This separate calculation quantifies a declared authority approximation with positive motor currents. It uses estimated pack resistance and provides no hardware accuracy claim. Do not substitute endpoint bus voltage or true damage state into a purported real controller without defining its sensor/estimator. Sources: `app/physics/multirotor/actuator_map.gd:1–9,47–74`, `app/physics/multirotor/controlled_plant.gd:341–347,407–429,438–465,576–615`, `app/data/multirotors/openrc-fpv5-x1.json:16–99,700–732,825–836`.

## CTRL-03 · Fixed-wing stage loads use the end-of-tick servo angle

**Evidence.** At 240 Hz, the stock Jensen Ugly Stik uses a 0.14 s neutral-to-full elevator travel time and 20° maximum throw. Starting from neutral, a full demand gives a **0.595238°** endpoint. Standard-flight aerodynamics receives that endpoint at every RK stage of the interval, including its beginning. The continuous trajectory with the same speed and endpoint has a mean of **0.297619°** over this first tick. The exposure is 0.00248016 versus 0.00124008 degree-seconds. A −1 command reverses both signs; zero gives zero. A +1% command arrives after 1.4 ms and produces a true 0.1664° mean against the 0.2° held value. The ratio measures deflection exposure; a nonlinear six-axis flight load requires its own experiment.

The normal pre-step advances all three axes before integration. The native helper reproduces the slew, and the native stage reads one set of deflections from the tick context. None of the aircraft JSON files in `app/data/aircraft` selects `servo_integration` on this baseline. A separate, explicitly selected interval law already samples the trajectory and partitions target arrivals. Its admission requires an explicit GDScript reference backend, a gearless conventional finite-OCV electric aircraft, base calm atmosphere and additional exclusions. It cannot currently carry the Jensen Stik's ordinary native route. Sources: `app/data/aircraft/jensen_ugly_stik_60.json:1027–1053`, `app/physics/aircraft_data.gd:2172–2173`, `app/sim/flight_session.gd:6369–6386,6483–6509,6540–6544,6894–6898`, `app/sim/simulation.gd:475–500`, `research/native-kernel/src/nk_pre_step.hpp:129–133,252–256`, `research/native-kernel/src/nk_stage.hpp:1–5,306–314`, `app/sim/servo_interval_flight.gd:27–59,123–132,147–170`.

**Native change and acceptance.** Implement the existing optional local-interval protocol in C++, starting in its published domain. Carry the start and target positions and exact arrival offsets; publish the endpoint only when the tick commits. Adopt one stock aircraft under a separate reviewed law after its gear, steering, wake, rigging and failure interactions have been covered. Preserve historical logs by solver identity. On the analytic fixtures require <10⁻¹⁰ normalized position error at stage times and <10⁻¹² normalized-position·s error in integrated exposure, including a mid-tick arrival, reversal, stationary axis and two coincident arrivals. Add a failure-induced jam, rejected tick and checkpoint restart before widening the domain. Wind or contact refinement alone cannot correct the endpoint hold. Hardware load and supply-dependent speed require measured installed arm/linkage geometry. Hitec's catalog defines its own servo speed under no load [4]. Risk is changing landing steering and phase margins in existing aircraft or invalidating recorded trajectories.

The mathematics audit also identified end-of-tick RPM held across a legacy Jensen step. Share the interval-clock, admission and replay infrastructure for those two corrections; servo motion follows a finite-rate target, whereas shaft RPM follows its own propulsion dynamics. Run independent servo-only and RPM-only switches before combining them.

## CTRL-04 · The helicopter applies new cyclic and tail pitch before the servos move

**Evidence.** The actual native trainer-electronics and tail-rotor headers produced the following results for the playable X-Cell model. Both arms of the comparison use the same admissible physical state: level attitude, zero body rates and air velocity, 167 rad/s main shaft speed, 4 m/s induced velocity, normal curves, direct cyclic, center collective, neutral optional bias and zero heading/integral memory. Each row is a separate same-time stage evaluation.

| Lateral / yaw stick | Achieved-by-model lateral cyclic | Achieved-by-model tail pitch | Tail component yaw moment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| −0.5 / −1 | −0.030 rad | 0.025 rad | +0.71505 N·m |
| 0 / 0 | 0.000 rad | 0.100 rad | +2.86019 N·m |
| +0.5 / +1 | +0.030 rad | 0.175 rad | +5.00534 N·m |

The shaft-stopped control returns zero tail moment for all three inputs. Main-rotor flapping and inflow already have continuous dynamics. The missing response lies between electronics output and blade-pitch inputs: three clipped 120° swash demands go straight into the inverse linkage; tail PI output after clipping goes straight into the tail law. The modeled tail component changes by 4.29029 N·m at a shared physical state. This is separate from gyro/head response and the net vehicle moment. The supported dry X-Cell can use the native stage; an attached lake excludes it. The HX-700's articulated inspection and laboratory-only body-rate candidate have separate identities and gates. Sources: `research/native-kernel/src/nk_helicopter_controls.hpp:99–105,130–149,154–173`, `research/native-kernel/src/nk_helicopter_stage.hpp:232–239,260–269`, `research/native-kernel/src/nk_helicopter_tail_rotor.hpp:107–129,158–198`, `app/sim/helicopter_flight_session.gd:219–255,263–271,353–367`, `app/physics/rotorcraft/electronics.gd:186–203,218–251`, `app/data/aircraft/xcell_60_research.json:407–600`, `docs/HELICOPTER.md:3–11,65`.

**Native change and acceptance.** Define four achieved actuator positions with independent swash and tail limits. Apply swash mixing and command travel limits, integrate the achieved horn positions, then reconstruct pitch from the achieved linkage. Feed achieved tail pitch to its aerodynamic law. Maintain helipad, hover trim, rate-controller saturation and zero-head-speed behavior with the new state. Start with a bounded velocity/lag law only after time-stamped command-to-horn or linkage measurements identify it. The HX-700 merits its own identified head, controller, pitch mapping and servo measurements. Mettler et al. independently identified swash actuators on an R-50 before fitting rapid head dynamics [5]. Its constants belong to that aircraft. Rotorflight illustrates why governor effort/spool response also needs separate measurement [6]; the stock X-Cell's proportional torque governor, drive limits and soft start are covered in HELI-04. Sources: `research/native-kernel/src/nk_helicopter_stage.hpp:31–77,268–280`, `app/physics/rotorcraft/lifecycle.gd:5–26`.

For a same-state reversal, achieved angle and actuator-induced tail-load jump at time zero must each be ≤10⁻¹² in their native units, with a positive rate bound and unchanged physical state. Fit neutral-to-edge, small-amplitude and reverse-direction commands to separate throws at two loads. On a held-out tape, pre-register timing and angular tolerances against the actual instrument uncertainty; a proposed engineering screen is ≤10% error in 10–90% rise time and ≤5% RMS of travel with the same trim. Establish yaw and cyclic closed-loop response separately before enabling the physical profile. More feedback updates per RK stage cannot make an instantaneous linkage physical. Risk is fitting actuator lag already absorbed in estimated flybar/control gains or moving a previously trimmed start away from equilibrium.

## Input ownership and reproduction

- The fixed-wing path can select transmitter-owned rates or explicit simulator rates. The gamepad's own expo is applied once and the simulated aircraft travel scaling is separate. Trims follow the rate map. The FPV path reads calibrated pre-expo sticks, applying the rescaled gamepad deadzone, then evaluates its own ACTUAL/BETAFLIGHT curve. The shipped ACTUAL mapping is 200°/s center slope and 670°/s full stick. Physical transmitter mixes can already shape the USB channel upstream. The solver has no direct observation of the pilot's mechanical stick or USB-to-display latency. See `app/sim/flight_session.gd:4976–5005,5064–5083,5122–5131`, `app/input/multirotor_input.gd:631–665`, `app/physics/multirotor/rates.gd:1–14,55–67`.
- The FPV controller runs once at the accepted 240 Hz boundary. It filters ideal body rate, differentiates measured rate, adds optional smoothed-setpoint acceleration feed-forward, and holds four motor commands through integration. Restore or refusal carries the PID memory, filters, source/arming latches, held commands and sample cursor with the body. A native port must retain the same outer clock when refining plant integration, including free-flight and race wrappers. The existing test separates plant-only refinement at 240 Hz from research controllers intentionally resampled at 960/1920 Hz. Re-run that separation with known inputs, a refused candidate and mid-tape checkpoints. See `app/physics/multirotor/flight_controller.gd:1–17,45–67,244–270,330–407`, `app/sim/simulation.gd:460–474`, `app/tests/test_mr_sample_rate.gd:1–8,171–205`. Helicopter pilot controls are held across the tick, while its existing PI and head states are evaluated continuously at RK stages.
- Run `python3 research/native-physics-audit-2026-11-28/probes/controls_probe.py . --native` from the repository root with `controls_probe.cpp` beside the script. The script reads the stock FPV JSON, solves the four wrench equations independently, checks both signs and the zero controls, then compiles and exercises three real native component headers in a temporary directory. The FPV values are independent source-equation calculations. The native binary tests components with the available GCC 14.2 toolchain. The native receipt requires GCC 13.3 for this Linux path; this host supplied neither that qualified binary nor GPU or test-pool evidence. The lead's 27 checked reference-engine suites passed 6,527 assertions, including the multirotor sample-rate suite. A platform-qualified native session, true whole-tick timing and measured aircraft response remain open. No production physics was changed.

## Primary references and applicability

This demonstrates an interface for downstream infeasibility; OpenRC also has the independent upstream software clamp proved above. [PX4 MulticopterRateControl.cpp](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/main/src/modules/mc_rate_control/MulticopterRateControl.cpp)

Installed surface speed requires linkage and hinge load, and the HS-311 is only an analogue for the Stik's estimated installation. [Hitec HS-311 specification](https://hitecrcd.co.jp/products/hs311/)
5. B. Mettler, M. B. Tischler and T. Kanade, *System Identification Modeling of a Model-Scale Helicopter* (2000), §5 Eq. 26. Their R-50 ground rig identified swash actuator dynamics independently before fitting the fast rotor in hover/cruise. The airframe and servos differ from both OpenRC profiles.
