# Combustion engines, propellers and turbine thrust

Baseline `8d4169f8febfd7d78a028eaa817414217a54f24b`, 28 November 2026. The P-51 and Corsair share the finite-gasoline route even though their raw aircraft definitions use `kind: glow_prop`. The finite-glow Stiks and Extra use separate engine and blade-element laws. The Avanti uses the finite JetCat P100-RX surrogate. The base Jensen Stik retains a prescribed-fuel RPM model. These distinctions matter for interpreting stopped-propeller behavior.

## PROP-01. The wind representation changes gasoline propeller inflow in physically identical air

`OpenRCNativeKernel::tick_stage` calls `stage_evaluate` at `research/native-kernel/src/nk_kernel.cpp:3117–3122`. In `nk_stage.hpp:292–318`, spatial weather supplies explicit propeller-point velocity. For other weather the `local_hub` flag is `m.electric || m.glow.ready`. Finite gasoline falls through `nk_prop.hpp:81–95` to datum velocity. The spatial sampler adds rigid rotation even when every station reads identical wind (`nk_wake_spatial_flow.hpp:115–140`). The selected vector drives both the aerodynamic propeller wrench (`nk_stage.hpp:332–348`) and shaft torque (`nk_liquid_stage.hpp:119–129`, `nk_liquid_propulsion.hpp:139–143`). The GDScript mirror explicitly preserves the fallback in `app/physics/propulsion.gd:585–619`. This is a shared modeling inconsistency, so reference/native agreement alone cannot expose it.

For any rigid hub, `v_hub,air = v_O,air + ω × r_OH` in uniform wind. The probe sets zero ambient wind, datum airspeed `[25,0,0]` m/s, 4,500 rpm, density 1.225 kg/m³ and pitch rate +2 rad/s. It evaluates the production uniform fallback and constant-valued spatial sampler with the stock propeller tables and offsets. Spatial minus uniform gives the following results. Both differences are exactly zero at zero body rate.

| Aircraft | Hub velocity difference in FRD, m/s | Body force difference in FRD, N | Body moment difference about datum, N·m |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-51 | (−0.141552, 0, −1.441570) | (+1.06719, +0.01381, +0.97409) | (−0.07306, −0.78025, +0.62004) |
| Corsair | (−0.124950, 0, −1.213052) | (+0.65265, +0.01967, +0.95008) | (−0.00503, −0.62212, +0.97375) |

Reuse the existing native point-velocity and wrench routines for every supported offset gasoline hub. Supply the same point velocity to the shaft, applied loads, propeller-source thrust/torque used by the wake, and stopped-drive check (`nk_stage.hpp:341–348,399–402`, `nk_liquid_propulsion.hpp:160–163`). Preserve ambient wake-gradient terms separately from the propeller's rotational point velocity. Match the reference, start/trim and replay identity rules. Do not add `ω × r` a second time to an explicit spatial sample. Expected benefit is removal of the measured representation dependence during rotation. No complete-aircraft maneuver error or speedup is inferred. The fallback needs a few scalar operations and no additional wind queries.

**Acceptance.** Compare identical constant fields, positive and negative angular velocities about all axes, zero and nonzero offsets, and a nonuniform-field control. Require velocity agreement within 1e−12 m/s and isolated wrenches within 1e−9 N or N·m for this scale. Test shaft derivatives and wake targets with the same sample. Preserve existing no-rotation results and qualify same-backend replay and reference/native parity for both stock aircraft.

## PROP-02. The gasoline fuel model subtracts indicated idle power from brake output

`app/physics/aircraft_data.gd:1934–1954` defines shaft `idle_power` as the power admitted at closed throttle. The loader copies it directly into the fuel adapter at `aircraft_data.gd:1851–1860`. The shaft then subtracts friction (`research/native-kernel/src/nk_liquid_propulsion.hpp:80–94`). The finite stage passes the resulting **brake** power, `max(0,Q_engine × 2πN/60)`, to the fuel law at lines 185–187. Lines 111–117 compute `ṁ_idle + max(P_brake − P_idle,0) × BSFC / 3,600,000`. The reference uses the same operations in `app/sim/liquid_fuel_flight.gd:92–115` and `app/physics/liquid_fuel.gd:142–157`. The watt-to-kg/s conversion is correct. The mixed reference leaves a band of delivered brake power above actual modeled idle that has no incremental fuel consumption.

| Aircraft | Authored idle rpm | Indicated idle W | Friction loss W | Brake idle W | Fuel at brake idle +500 W, current / consistently brake-referenced proxy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P-51 | 1,400 | 225.4453 | 102.0389 | 123.4064 | 0.48698 / 0.53800 kg/h |
| Corsair | 900 | 181.1099 | 122.9149 | 58.1950 | 0.47654 / 0.53800 kg/h |

The independent idle-loss oracle is `(Q₀ + Q₁ N_idle/1000) × 2πN_idle/60`. The actual stock shaft can deliver a 50 W increment at fixed idle rpm with a small throttle increase. Both current fuel laws give 0.288 kg/h there. The same estimated 0.5 kg/kWh slope and 0.288 kg/h idle flow anchored to brake idle instead give 0.313 kg/h. These differences quantify the model-domain mismatch. Physical consumption remains unmeasured on both installations.

Prepare the brake reference once at the authored static idle, reference atmosphere, and zero throttle using the common torque model. Pass the explicitly named value through the native adapter. Preserve the idle flow, zero-fuel cut and power floor. Version the fuel-law identity and retain the previous law where required for recorded flights. This changes depletion time, CG and inertia downstream without changing shaft torque. The stage still performs the same number of fuel-law operations. **Acceptance:** the anchored law must give the specified idle flow at modeled brake idle, add 0.025 kg/h for +50 W, produce 0.538 kg/h at +500 W, and consume zero with the source off. Test both aircraft, 240/480 Hz fuel integrals, exhaustion localization, fixed-datum mass and rollback. Existing formula-only tests in `app/tests/test_liquid_fuel.gd:74–79` use the supplied threshold as an input and cannot independently verify its origin.

## PROP-03. Stopped table-driven propellers lose all axial drag and air-driven breakaway torque

`research/native-kernel/src/nk_prop.hpp:105–129,215–232` returns zero below 1 rpm and evaluates `J=max(U,0)/(nD)` above it. `prop_coefficient` at lines 33–48 bounds the P-51 and Corsair negative table tails. At fixed forward airspeed, their thrust and torque approach zero as `n²`. At fixed sufficiently small nonzero speed, each force also stops responding to further increases in forward airspeed once the table tail is reached. The finite gasoline shaft uses that torque for engine-off dynamics and static friction at `nk_liquid_propulsion.hpp:99–108,145–170`. GDScript mirrors it in `app/physics/propulsion.gd:437–446,652–677`. Mechanical body-driven breakaway remains possible. The limitation concerns aerodynamic drive from axial flow at rest.

At 0 rpm and 40 m/s axial inflow both gasoline props produce exactly zero thrust, torque and scalar shaft acceleration with the source off. At 100 rpm the P-51 produces the same −0.06643 N and −0.006538 N·m at both 20 and 40 m/s. At 3,000 rpm and 30 m/s it correctly enters its authored windmilling range, −28.36 N and −2.341 N·m, which drives +689.1 rpm/s after friction on the scalar shaft. At 4,500 rpm the P-51 gives the same +194.42 N at −20 m/s as at zero inflow. The loaded forward quadrant already works. The present stopped/glide component cannot reproduce the measured physical existence of locked-blade drag, or determine a real breakaway speed. This evidence does not quantify total airframe drag.

Retain measured or accepted forward tables and add an identified off-design representation that is nonsingular when `n=0`. One option uses advance angle `atan2(U,ΩR)` and a scale proportional to `ρ[(ΩR)²+U²]`. Another uses a bounded blend to the existing passive blade-element model with installation-specific geometry. Keep supported rotation sense explicit, use measured static friction, and distinguish a locked prop from a free rotor. Identify stopped drag and windmill torque on the actual prop/engine and check whether the airframe drag calibration already absorbs some propeller drag. The expected improvement is realistic dead-stick/tailslide propeller loading. A small lookup can keep stage cost near existing tables. A 12-annulus implementation needs target profiling.

**Acceptance.** Preserve forward stock table knots to 1e−9 relative scale, ensure finite force and torque as `N→0±` for supported shaft states, verify static drag at positive inflow, and check axial passivity `QΩ−TU≥−1e−8 W` on the test grid. Compare cut, free windmill, locked-rotor and restart/breakaway cases on held-out runs, within instrument uncertainty. No numeric breakaway speed or measured drag magnitude follows from the current inputs.

## PROP-04. The turbine meters the same fuel on acceleration and deceleration at the same RPM

`nk_stage.hpp:317–324` calls `liquid_turbine_stage`. `research/native-kernel/src/nk_liquid_propulsion.hpp:193–225,282–291` calculates an ECU demand, bounds spool rate by separate acceleration/deceleration schedules, and reads fuel consumption only from the current RPM. The rate reaches the live store at `nk_liquid_stage.hpp:143–158`. The reference goes through `app/sim/liquid_fuel_flight.gd:80–88,114–121`, `flight_session.gd:5425` and `app/physics/turbine.gd:91–99,188–190`. The stock fuel map and governor are at `app/data/aircraft/sebart_avanti_s_a200.json:2257,2401`. This behavior is an explicit empirical approximation in the turbine plan.

At 110,000 rpm, zero intake airspeed and 1.225 kg/m³, the native stage returns 32.20092 N and 2.459 g/s at both command endpoints. Spool rate changes from −66,000 to +44,000 rpm/s. With the authored rotor inertia, `IΩΩ̇` is −2.787 kW versus +1.858 kW. This arithmetic exposes what the surrogate cannot distinguish in fuel delivery. It does not establish unphysical creation of energy, a missing torque of that size on the airframe, or the magnitude of a real fuel error. The exhaust thermal state and angular-momentum flux are unobserved.

As a reproducible model baseline, a scalar RK4 step from stable idle to full throttle crosses 10%/90% of the installed static thrust excursion at 1.41725/3.32211 s and uses 20.9722 g in six seconds. Full-to-idle gives 0.05749/1.14310 s and 8.4658 g. Between 240 and 480 Hz, the largest crossing shift is 2.6 µs and the largest six-second burn difference is below 2 µg. These are model traces. Published P100 tests used different ECU command endpoints and bandwidth, with load-cell thrust and ECU telemetry.

Fit a small delivered-fuel transient correction or metering state to synchronized P100 throttle/RPM/fuel/thrust traces, conditioning on inlet conditions where data permit. Keep the historical non-BL variant, its independently measured endpoints and the existing rate-limited governor as the baseline. Compare the extra state against a rate-dependent map on whole held-out up/down reversals. Existing pressure/temperature inputs can be extended through the turbine adapter if a corrected map is identifiable. Expected benefit is better fuel and possibly thrust history under rapid commands. One lookup or scalar ODE per stage is feasible, but its cost and benefit depend on the selected law. **Acceptance:** static reference endpoints must remain within the manufacturer’s stated tolerance, installed losses must be independently labeled, and terminal source-off fuel must be zero. Proposed initial held-out targets are six-second consumption within the larger of 5% and twice measurement uncertainty and 10%/90% crossing time within the larger of 0.2 s and twice timing uncertainty. Record failed fits and retain the simpler model if measurements cannot discriminate. Qualify replay, depletion, cut/restart policy and native timing separately.

## Coverage and negative findings

- **Torque, power and oxygen.** The gasoline shaft subtracts friction after its dry-charge correction. The native glow source evaluates 2T opportunities at `N/60` and 4T at `N/120`, then uses `P=Q×2πN/60` for both (`nk_glow_source.hpp:126–173`). The two-stroke separates delivered from trapped charge, the four-stroke includes pumping and valve losses, and premix drain includes unburned delivery. At full admission, nominal mixture and full firing, the stock MAX-55AX gives 1,250.348 W, 0.74625 N·m and 266.667 opportunities/s at 16,000 rpm. FS-64V gives 838.469 W, 0.72789 N·m and 91.667 opportunities/s at 11,000 rpm. Test dry-density ratio 0.85 scales indicated power to 0.85 in both, while mechanical losses persist. The code checks fuel, chemical, indicated and brake power ordering and premix mass closure. The ratings are fitted anchors, so this is a dimensional and accounting check. The 61FX curve, full throttle maps, real mixture and throttle response still need installed data.
- **Glow feed, propeller and thermal response.** Servo, opening and fuel transport are separate continuous states. Actual mixture affects firing under rich/lean and thermal limits. Head temperature sees ambient and cooling speed (`nk_glow_lifecycle.hpp:152–220`, `nk_glow_stage.hpp:130–150,214–252`). Carburetor capacity uses a fixed 288.15 K in the restriction normalization (`nk_glow_lifecycle.hpp:95–120`), an ambient-dependence approximation. All six stock glow propeller installations passed a 2,001-point axial sweep each at 10,000 rpm, `J=0…2`, density 1.225 kg/m³. Minimum positive-thrust `QΩ/P_ideal` ranged from 1.0831 to 1.3385. At 0 rpm, 20 m/s, the stock MAX-55AX blade gives −1.457 N and −0.1373 N·m and the FS-64V blade gives −2.549 N and −0.2282 N·m. Reverse shaft breakaway remains a declared finite-glow refusal. The sweep tests axial energy consistency, not bench accuracy.
- **Fuel mass, CG and inertia.** The finite liquid adapter holds unusable residue, computes live mass/CG/tensor before each stage, transfers moments to the live CG once and applies a fixed-datum derivative (`app/sim/liquid_fuel_flight.gd:39–47,66–120,129–135`, `nk_liquid_mass.hpp:115–167,171–222`, `nk_liquid_stage.hpp:114–118`). Glow clips main and line stores separately (`nk_glow_mass.hpp:17–39`). No additional `ṁv` thrust or `Īω` torque is justified for this declared co-moving efflux law. Tank slosh, outflow arm/velocity and installed tank metrology remain separate identification work.
- **Jet pressure and captured momentum.** Static gross thrust is constructed from the 0.92 installation factor, tabulated static thrust and air flow. At full speed the resulting force is 92 N versus the bare nominal 100 N. The full signed inlet velocity enters the capture term at the intake moment arm (`nk_liquid_propulsion.hpp:203–217,252–269`). The general balance is `(ṁ_air+ṁ_fuel)V_exit−ṁ_air V_in+(p_exit−p_ambient)A_exit`. At the catalog maximum, fuel/air mass flow is about 2.26%. That ratio is not a missing-thrust correction because static effective exit velocity already fits measured bench thrust. Density scales air flow, while the stock fuel and spool schedules do not separately respond to pressure, temperature, differing corrected RPM, or ECU history. Off-axis capture, off-design nozzle pressure, cold rundown/windmilling and casing torque need installation data. Ordinary shutdown intentionally drops combustion loads while rotor momentum persists. No validated nozzle or measured exhaust-swirl law is present.

## Reproduction and scope

The two companion files are `propulsion_probe.py` and `propulsion_probe.cpp`. Run `python3 research/native-physics-audit-2026-11-28/probes/propulsion_probe.py .` from the repository root with the files adjacent. The driver reads the stock aircraft/engine JSON, builds the C++17 component probe against `research/native-kernel/src` with `-O1 -fno-fast-math -ffp-contract=off`, checks independent kinematic, mass, power and fuel assertions and prints numbers as JSON. The executable is temporary. The wind test uses a constant-valued native provider, stock hub geometry and stock propeller coefficients. The other aerodynamic stations see the same constant wind and do not enter the reported isolated wrench. The scalar turbine integrator is an independent driver of the production schedule and fuel functions. No Godot stage ABI, full aircraft trajectory, measured performance or pilot response was exercised here. The lead's separate 27-suite reference baseline passed 6,527 checks including `glow_flight_physics` and `turbine_continuous`. This host used GCC 14.2, distinct from the pinned 13.3, and had no installed GDExtension, GPU or test pool for production qualification.
