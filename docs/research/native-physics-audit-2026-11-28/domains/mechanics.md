## MECH-01. Static tire friction changes with compass heading

**P2, confirmed native component defect, reference path shares the branch.** The north-facing tire solver treats a motionless vehicle as force-balanced. A one-millimetre extra front-left suspension displacement in the otherwise static stock MT8 on level, uniform road (μ = 0.85, all controls neutral) produces the following actual header outputs. Rotating vehicle and road 90° together preserves each body-frame state, gravity, surface normal and tire friction coefficient.

| Observable | North | East |
|---|---:|---:|
| Front-left lateral tire force | 0 N | 5.380361 N |
| Body roll acceleration | 1.875412 rad/s² | 0.592691 rad/s² |
| Friction iterations | 0 | 96 |

The result also occurs in a normal terrain initialization scenario. The `make_terrain_state` equations fit travel to a road with a local, flat-topped 1 mm pad under the front-left wheel. Rotating this complete scene changes front-left force from 0 to 3.068001 N and roll acceleration from 2.552995 to 0.388779 rad/s². The component exercised the fitted state and samples, without running the Godot initializer. In the undisturbed, level equilibrium both headings give lateral loads below 5 × 10⁻¹⁴ N.

The shortcut at `research/rc-monster-truck/native/src/ground_vehicle_kernel.hpp:413–424,805–815,914–938` requires the identity quaternion up to sign, neutral inputs and zero rates. It ignores stored suspension travel and the unconstrained acceleration. The reference has the same shortcut in `app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_dynamics.gd:1186–1195,1308–1328,2149–2161`. Supported terrain placement is at lines 418–465, and the production session stages at `app/sim/ground_vehicle/session.gd:2255–2273`. A stationary patch can require friction to constrain its *acceleration*, even at zero slip velocity. Extending the shortcut to all headings alone would propagate the north-facing error.

**Native repair and acceptance.** Remove the shortcut as a correctness baseline. If retaining an optimization, admit it after assembling all active loads and tire rows, only when zero tire forces already satisfy the declared tangent residual (including bias) within the solver’s tolerance. Preserve the existing flat equilibrium and mirrored reference behavior. Compare body-frame forces and acceleration at 0°, 37°, 90° and 180° on uniform and rotated uneven terrain, with symmetric support, the 1 mm displacement, asymmetric preload, and a small external force. For this regression require rotational equivalence within 1 × 10⁻⁵ N and 1 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s² and compare against forced full-solve results. Expect consistent launch/settling responses. Estimate 2–4 engineer-days, medium risk to equilibrium traces and quiescent tick cost. Requalify the native constructor, parity and replay.

## MECH-02. Ground tire constraints exclude simultaneous scenery wall loads

**P2, confirmed split-solve defect.** In the shared road adapter, wet and existing external generalized forces enter before the tire solve. Wall loads receive a second unconstrained mass solve and are added afterward. The underlying native equations therefore see different applied forces depending on which route supplied them. This affects stock-electric normal driving near course/scenery walls at the simulation stage. The nitro composite calls the shared chassis kernel, but only the stock electric configuration was numerically probed.

Two MT8 right tires each penetrate a flat vertical wall by 0.5 mm on a level high-grip road. The static wall load is 6.5 N toward the left, from 6,500 N/m per tire. The four road tires carry 54.132708 N in total. A source-equivalent wall projection and the unmodified native factorization were used for the shipped ordering. The comparison passes the **identical** generalized force through the existing native external-force input.

| At this stage | Current wall-after-tire order | Same force before tires |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sum of lateral road tire forces | 0 N | 3.974471 N |
| Largest absolute lateral acceleration of a road contact patch | 2.639596 m/s² | 7.66 × 10⁻⁷ m/s² |

Free suspension and rotational motion account for the remaining imbalance in the pre-solve case. The ground force need not equal 6.5 N. At zero road friction both variants give 2.639596 m/s². With a 1 mm wall clearance both give approximately 2.24 × 10⁻¹⁴ m/s². At half brake, which forces friction iteration even at north, the current result stays 2.639596 m/s² and the pre-solve result is 0.000310 m/s². This separates the defect from MECH-01.

**Route and cause.** `app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_dynamics.gd:721–759,813–861,865–926`; native `research/rc-monster-truck/native/src/ground_vehicle_kernel.hpp:644–705,1019–1034`. With a sticking-patch Jacobian J, the shipped update adds Δa = M⁻¹f_wall *after* constraining the acceleration, leaving a tangent residual JM⁻¹f_wall. The current road labels, wall bins and supported scenery world are constructed in `app/physics/ground_vehicle/airfield_course.gd:148–175,263–291`.

**Native repair and acceptance.** Move the bounded wall sphere/AABB force assembly into the native stage before the existing tire/brake projection. Pass validated deterministic wall rows across the binding, reuse the stage mass factor and preserve per-wall normal load, spring energy, friction work and damage telemetry exactly once. Follow the same ordering in the reference. Check the cited fixture, zero-friction, clear-wall and half-brake controls. Require agreement with the same force delivered externally within 10⁻⁵ m/s² for the neutral fixture and the same finite-iteration tolerance for the brake control. Check the 240 Hz tick plus actual wall substeps, slip dissipation, simultaneous wet/wall contact, and both vehicle types through their installed backend. Benefit is physically consistent simultaneous tire contact. Estimate 4–7 engineer-days, medium risk to wall trajectories and telemetry.

## MECH-03. Rotational padding can hide entry into a scenery box

**P2, confirmed geometric defect, extended to a stock 26-point hull by source-based oracle.** The narrow phase expands the box by the angular chord error, then applies the true initial-overlap rejection policy to a point inside that *expanded* box. A point just outside the real face can therefore cross the true face without generating an entry. The broad phase admits the witness. The physics route calls this GDScript narrow phase after native reach filtering whenever the world may overlap.

Use the stock Jensen Ugly Stik inventory and all 26 authored crash-hull points. Approach a broad horizontal-normal box face along body X at 2.4 m/s, with 1.5° roll about X in a 1/240 s tick. Each point’s coordinate along the contact normal is independent of roll. The spinner begins 20 µm clear, travels 10 mm, and crosses at fraction 0.002. The next-forward probe is 36.8 mm behind the spinner. The full-hull radius is 0.905097348 m, giving 77.542886 µm padding, 57.542886 µm above initial clearance. This is well inside the rejection region. The Python branch reproduction reports zero entries for the full hull. The no-roll control reports one at 0.0019999. An outward path stays clear, and a 1 mm initial gap produces an entry. Adjacent 1.48°/1.50° roll increments bracket a shared 76.515901 µm clearance: the prior tick is genuinely clear; the following tick enters at 0.007651590 and is missed. Earlier checked-engine tests used synthetic probes and an authored box at a different revision. No stock flight tape or current Godot execution of this expanded witness was collected.

**Owner and native repair.** `app/physics/aircraft_data.gd:560–568,593–603,2517–2524`; `app/sim/flight_session.gd:4207–4222`; `app/sim/scenery_collision.gd:125–166,470–520,696–699`. Use conservative padding to bound candidates only. In the ambiguous band separate the exact signed distance at the true start from the padded chord, and refine using the actual interpolated probe path to find earliest inward crossing, with a bound against uncovered grazes. Keep true initial overlap/separation policy, both signs of a face, and deterministic ordering. Implement native float64 narrow-phase code behind the existing geometry boundary, with a matched reference oracle and versioned collision/replay identity. Acceptance requires both directions on axis-aligned and rotated boxes, the stock spinner within 10⁻⁶ tick fraction of analytic entry, clear/outgoing controls, a true pre-existing overlap and a grazing rotating off-axis probe. Benefit is accurate accepted collision timing. Estimate 4–7 engineer-days including native entrypoint and parity, medium risk to existing intentionally conservative contact timing.

## MECH-04. Distant terrain can reject an airborne vertical truck

**P3, confirmed on the stock MT8 native component.** Put the chassis 10 m above flat ground, wheels straight, zero controls and velocities. An exact +90° or −90° pitch returns `Tire forward axis is degenerate on the sampled surface`. At 89.999999999° the projected length is 1.74534 × 10⁻¹¹ and the same error occurs. At 89.999999°, 90.000001°, 0°, 45° and 180° the kernel accepts the state and reports airborne with zero tire load. This is a narrow numerical contact-frame singularity in a physically valid airborne pose. Its frequency in ordinary flips is unknown.

`research/rc-monster-truck/native/src/ground_vehicle_kernel.hpp:853–869,879–916` and reference `app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_dynamics.gd:1245–1260,1269–1310` project body forward onto the tangent plane and reject lengths ≤ 10⁻¹⁰, before determining whether a friction row is needed. The session converts a failed stage to refusal through `app/sim/ground_vehicle/session.gd:505–514,2255–2273`. Gravity, free suspension and wheel spin have valid dynamics there. A level launch is a setup convenience. The solver also handles jumps, rollover and arbitrary finite orientation during a tick.

**Native repair and acceptance.** Keep airborne propulsion, brakes and rotor momentum active. Give inactive tires defined finite telemetry when the horizontal rolling direction is unavailable. For loaded tires at singular orientations, use the axle and ground normal to construct a rolling tangent where available, plus a documented deterministic fallback when wheel axle and ground normal also align. Limit changes away from the singular region and mirror the reference. Test ±90°, nearby controls, banked road with a rotated rig, in-air brake/drive, active contact separately, a complete accepted stage/tick and restored-state continuation. In the zero-load fixture require finite derivatives, zero road force and airborne status for every test orientation. Estimate 2–3 engineer-days, low-to-medium risk to slip telemetry and rolling-frame symmetry.

## Cleared and deliberately simplified

- **Rigid-body frames and inertia.** `app/physics/rigid_body.gd:1–20,42–67,90–137` and the native ground mass assembly at `ground_vehicle_kernel.hpp:322–368` use world north/east/down, body forward/right/down, body-to-world quaternion, radians per second, kg·m² and symmetric matrix entries. Rotating NED gravity into body, body-frame transport −ω×v, and angular momentum ω×Jω have the expected signs. The stock MT8 diagonal moments (0.067, 0.160, 0.115) kg·m² satisfy mechanical triangle inequalities, and the ground loader checks positive definiteness and chassis-to-CG offsets. This is a conventions/property check, with no measured mass-property qualification.
- **Airship.** `app/physics/airship/airship_data.gd:87–89,245–312`, `airship_dynamics.gd:83–97,251–286`, `app/sim/airship/session.gd:147–175`. Stock accepts only a full, taut constant-volume envelope, freezes density at release, accounts for helium once in the rigid mass, applies ρVg at the center of buoyancy and gravity at the center of mass, and adds apparent inertia in the acceleration solve. At illustrative ρ=1.225 kg/m³ and g=9.80665 m/s², V=2.502001 m³ gives 30.056904 N gross buoyancy. Stock helium mass is 0.422838 kg, ballast is 0.433846 kg and default 0.080 kg heaviness gives 0.784532 N net downward. A moving ballonet, gas heat/leak/pressure and time-varying added mass are deliberately outside this law. Ground and water envelope contact are conservative witness policies, with terminal water entry and appendage-spacing limitations (`app/sim/airship/session.gd:309–325,352–381,420–455`). No ballonet/drag constants proposed without corresponding vehicle evidence.
- **Truck water.** `app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_environment.gd:5–19,45–48,64–113` bounds immersion to the water column by subtracting spherical-cap fractions. Four 0.00035 m³ body cells plus four 0.00030 m³ wheel cells provide up to 25.4463 N buoyancy at the illustrative gravity above. This is below the stock MT8's 54.1327 N weight. Drag power terms use −k|v|³ and −c|ω|³ with nonnegative wet fractions. Open-truck displacement, fixed cell locations and still-water drag coefficients are declared estimates. Native bathymetry support does not turn the separately dispatched wet loads into a native hydrodynamic law.
- **Scenery conversion for cars.** `app/physics/ground_vehicle/scenery_walls.gd:1–19,117–170` intentionally converts rotated objects to perimeter boxes and capsules to columns; the column corner overreach is (√2−1)R. This is separate from the aircraft box solver's missed physical face. Existing coverage at `app/tests/test_rcv_scenery_collision.gd:125–157` checks the conversion. Wall and full-application CPU cost require target testing.

## Primary engineering references

1. MuJoCo's forward dynamics places the combined applied force in the unconstrained acceleration used to solve the contact constraints. This supplies the governing check for MECH-01/02; OpenRC keeps its own relaxed tire solver and passivity projection. [MuJoCo computation, primal problem](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.8.0/computation/)
2. Project Chrono defines the contact system with an actual tire/terrain contact, terrain normal and rolling direction. Its disc-terrain methods do not validate OpenRC's spherical tire dimensions or singular fallback. [Chrono 10 ChTire](https://api.projectchrono.org/10.0.0/classchrono_1_1vehicle_1_1_ch_tire.html)
3. Wang, Ferguson, Schneider, Jiang, Attene and Panozzo cover degeneracy, false negatives, conservative timing and inclusion-based continuous collision detection. Their mesh primitive algorithms motivate bounded rejection and independent geometry oracles; they do not directly implement rotating stock hull witnesses. [Authors' paper and benchmarks](https://continuous-collision-detection.github.io/tight_inclusion/)
4. David Eberly’s solid capsule line query clips the wall and both spherical caps to the appropriate axial ranges. Its roots provide independent geometry; OpenRC retains its own approach and response policies. [Geometric Tools IntrLine3Capsule3](https://www.geometrictools.com/GTE/Mathematics/IntrLine3Capsule3.h)
5. MIT Ocean Engineering derives linear superposition of oblique waves using directional wave vectors, amplitude, frequency and phase. This supports the independent encounter-frequency oracle. It supplies no measurement of the simulator's lake, shoreline fade or impact force. [MIT Marine Hydrodynamics, Lecture 20](https://web.mit.edu/fluids-modules/www/potential_flows/Lectures/lec20/lecture20.pdf)
6. Thor Fossen gives the six-degree-of-freedom model with separate rigid and added inertia, momentum transport and hydrostatic loads. Its structural checks transfer to the blimp within the declared constant-property assumptions. Marine coefficients do not calibrate this airship. [Fossen's model](https://www.fossen.biz/html/marineCraftModel.html)
