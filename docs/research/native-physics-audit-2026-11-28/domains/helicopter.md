## HELI-01 · Tilted thrust and shaft work

**Independent disposition:** deliberately documented simplification with a bounded model-accounting residual. `docs/research/helicopter/HELI-05/README.md:19,24–29,44–52,100` explicitly preserves shaft/body inflow, omits in-plane hub forces and treats the exact tip-path-plane vector as a kinematic extension requiring separate qualification. The 167.8731 W residual below is relative to the stipulated thrust-only disc closure. In both tilted cases shaft power minus body translational work remains positive (663.8135 and 999.5596 W). This fixture does not prove open-system energy creation. Shaft-frame induced velocity, empirical profile loss and omitted in-plane loads must be reconciled when selecting a new law.

**Location.** `research/native-kernel/src/nk_helicopter_main_rotor.hpp:131–156,202–221`, `heli_main_rotor` and `heli_main_stage`. The rotor uses body-shaft inflow, while its returned thrust follows the flap-defined tip-path plane. Its model gives shaft power `QΩ = T(vi − U_z) + P0`. The translational work actually delivered to the body at zero angular rate is `F·U`. A uniform actuator disc with this thrust-only force and profile loss should close `QΩ − F·U − Tvi − P0 = 0` at steady state. NDARC separates induced, profile and propulsive power and defines hub force work from the force/velocity dot product [2].

**Native counterexample.** Hold the X-Cell head at 167 rad/s in 15 m/s forward air, zero body rate, far above ground. Solve oblique momentum for 80.41453 N, giving `vi=1.156212 m/s`, `θ=0.059751 rad`, and `μ=0.11590`. Solve cyclic individually for each tilt within the authored ±0.06 rad travel. All runs have zero flap and wake derivatives to printed precision. Profile power is 738.7103 W and induced power is 92.9763 W.

| Longitudinal flap | Native shaft power | Actual thrust work | Closure residual |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| −8° | 831.6865 W | +167.8731 W | −167.8731 W |
| 0° | 831.6865 W | 0 W | 0 W |
| +8° | 831.6865 W | −167.8731 W | +167.8731 W |

The measurable result is a component accounting discrepancy, not an observed aircraft power error. Gavrilets deliberately omitted small in-plane hub loads and fitted effective fuselage drag [1]. That simplification can partly absorb errors in a forward trim. On this paired stand the fuselage and tail receive identical velocities and wakes (`nk_helicopter_aero.hpp:152–161`), so neither supplies the sign-changing 335.746 W difference. Even projecting a shaft-aligned induced velocity onto the tilted normal changes `Tvi` equally at ±8°. No transient flap energy or body rotation is needed for the counterexample. The probe separately confirmed the drivetrain's instantaneous angular-work identity.

**Native change and acceptance.** Version the law. Compute one normalized disc vector and resolve the stage hub velocity into normal and in-plane components with consistent signed thrust. Derive torque from induced, profile and propulsive work in that frame. An alternative closure may retain the empirical thrust law and explicitly include its emitted hub-force work; choose between them against held-out X-Cell trim and descending-flight data. Account for the original drag lumping to avoid charging in-plane drag twice. Proposed analytic stand residual ≤10⁻⁸ W on both signs of flap/thrust, including a zero-tilt legacy control. Compare thrust, torque, rotor RPM and trim in forward flight and autorotation entry. Expect corrected coupling of translational work to rotor torque; its flight-level size is unmeasured. Estimated 3–6 engineering days before flight-data qualification. Risk is altered trim, yaw load and recorded trajectories. Runtime cost is a few scalar projections at each native stage.

## HELI-02 · Moving, tilted and inverted wakes near terrain

**Location.** `nk_helicopter_stage.hpp:220–229,260–266` samples the local terrain below the translated hub, discards both slopes and computes vertical `h/R`. `nk_helicopter_main_rotor.hpp:210–221` gates the correction using only the sign of thrust times its world-down projection. `nk_helicopter_inflow.hpp:177–185,202–215` multiplies the target by a normalized Hayden height curve. The research API explicitly expects caller-established along-wake clearance (`docs/research/helicopter/HELI-04/README.md:18–23`). Thus the local hub position is already correct. Wake incidence, forward-speed attenuation and ground slope are missing at the caller.

**Native counterexample.** Over a flat plane at `h/R=1.1`, the IGE/OGE induced-velocity **ratio** is 0.88781836 at `V/vh=0, 0.75, 1.5, 3, 4`. Absolute induced velocity still changes with forward speed. At `V=3vh=12.5121 m/s`, the ideal OGE wake gives `cos ε=0.109772`, hence `h/(R cos ε)=10.02075`. NDARC's effective-distance model switches off at 3R. The native correction still reduces the target by 11.22%. A separate tilt test gives 0.88781836 on the approach to an edge-on disc and 1 at and past the sign boundary. This is a finite target jump; the integrated wake state remains continuous. A two-bladed model-rotor experiment at one radius height measured different flow fields for horizontal and 15° inclined ground [3]. Its data do not validate the trainer's sloping-field behavior.

**Native change and acceptance.** For the flat/smooth local surface use the existing height and derivatives from `nk_terrain.hpp:334–347`. Resolve signed total wake motion against the terrain normal, derive distance to the wake/ground intersection, and apply a documented distance- and speed-dependent correction. Preserve the source's bounded small-height behavior, positive/negative-thrust symmetry and no downstream ground influence for wake directed away. The flat-plane formula in [2] is a defensible first step. A bounded multi-point terrain query near an edge requires separate qualification; a local-plane model cannot reproduce partial-disc recirculation or water. Independently tabulate the flat, tilted, lateral and signed fixtures. At 3vh above, require factor 1 under the chosen NDARC effective-distance rule, and convergence to 1 on both sides of grazing incidence. Check hover and takeoff/landing power on held-out data. Estimated 2–4 days for flat/local-plane treatment. Main risk is double-counting translation or introducing a threshold jump. A scalar projection uses existing terrain-stage data and needs no additional terrain call on a planar patch.

## HELI-03 · Sideflow and high-advance-ratio flap response

**Location and result.** `nk_helicopter_main_rotor.hpp:30–33,109–138,166–175` applies `sign(μx)·16Kμ²/[(1−μ²/2)(8μ+aσ)]` to the flap response from axial flow. The sign extension to backward flight is an explicit project interpretation (`docs/research/helicopter/HELI-05/README.md:42`). With the native X-Cell at nominal speed, `U=(−10⁻⁸,14,5), (0,14,5), (+10⁻⁸,14,5) m/s` gives longitudinal flap rates `−0.01077645, 0, +0.01077645 rad/s`. The limiting difference is 1.235°/s in the derivative at `μ≈0.108`. A lateral velocity of 14 m/s persists as the tiny longitudinal perturbation changes sign.

The same formula has a separate, much larger extrapolation hazard. At 20 m/s crossflow, 1 m/s axial flow, `Ω=16.700000` rad/s is accepted by startup, `16.700001` is refused, and `18.24793518` rad/s is accepted with `ȧ₁=431.28246 rad/s`, just above the `μ=√2` pole. The repository already tests the reference's large derivative and refusal at `app/tests/test_heli_rotor_domain.gd:108–126`. This is an unresolved low-speed model envelope, outside the research paper's `μ<0.15` range. It is distinct from a failure observed in nominal flight.

**Native change and acceptance.** Resolve the axial correction in the direction of the in-plane velocity with a continuous longitudinal projection, e.g. `μx/μ`, with its analytic zero-crossflow limit and appropriate lateral harmonic verified against a rotor-axis transformation. Preserve axial-forward and axial-backward anchors. A heuristic sign smoother would introduce an arbitrary scale. For a power-off extension, select a bounded dimensional blade-element law before the pole or issue a documented preflight/envelope refusal until that alternative is qualified. Sweep headings through ±90° in both axial-flow directions, halve perturbation until the left/right derivative gap tends to zero, and retain the pure longitudinal source fixtures. Require a finite rate bound supplied by head/rotor data throughout each newly admitted transition, plus 240/480/960 Hz convergence. Expected gain is continuous response through sideways descent and explicit low-RPM authority. Estimated 2–4 days for the directional correction, plus 5–10 for a high-crossflow dimensional extension. The latter depends on blade-polar/head data and overlaps HELI-04; estimates are not additive.

## HELI-04 · Autorotation, governor, freewheel and tail authority

**Status and evidence.** The one-speed proxy is deliberately labelled in `docs/HELICOPTER.md`. `nk_helicopter_stage.hpp:31–77,268–280` computes engine speed as a fixed multiple of main speed, applies a bounded proportional governor with explicit torque/power caps, and removes drive input on hold. The 12 s estimated startup ramp is at lines 207–209. The model has one independent shaft speed and no engagement state. `research/helicopter/xcell/derive.py:47–50,78–83` supplies estimated drive values, 8.3 engine/main and 4.66 tail/main gearing. Engine spin inertia is absent. The high-speed signed `CQ` can transfer energy from the air. In the component probe descent at 20 m/s with `Ω=167, vi=1, θ=0.05` produces `Qmain=−19.93945 N·m`. This is a prescribed snapshot, not a sustained autorotation. At 5 rad/s the startup law gives the same `T=0.075792 N` and `Qmain=+0.019003 N·m` for axial speeds −20, 0 and +20 m/s. Its induced torque is nonnegative by construction (`nk_helicopter_main_rotor.hpp:36–54,106–120`). The stopped law has zero loads. A held, fixed-body rotor in this branch cannot extract positive main-rotor shaft work from axial airflow. Tail speed remains geared to main, and the low-speed tail also clamps induced torque at zero (`nk_helicopter_tail_rotor.hpp:90–95,120–140`).

**Native change and acceptance.** Add a separately identified physical profile with input-shaft speed/inertia, a unilateral clutch and installation-specific tail drive. Express clutch torque and slip on one gear-referred coordinate. In an ideal overrun, rotor-side speed minus referred input speed is nonnegative, transmitted driving torque is nonnegative, and their product is zero. A slipping engagement must dissipate `Qclutch·(Ωdriver−Ωdriven)≥0`; integrate equality/engagement and stop events under the accepted tick owner. Add physics-informed low-speed signed sectional torque with measured chord/polars and an explicit parked-blade policy. The FAA gives generic freewheel mechanics [5]. X-Cell's manufacturer offered a constant-tail-drive autorotation unit, so exact tail topology requires the installed variant [6]. Preserve physical main/tail angular momenta in each mode and do not retain a disengaged engine's reflected inertia.

Engineering gates include zero overrun torque, modeled tail/main RPM ratio for the documented topology, nonnegative engagement dissipation, total rotational momentum conserved across an externally torque-free engagement and convergence of the coupled energy ledger. In an ideal rigid engagement use the analytic weighted final speed and energy loss; permit <10⁻⁸ relative stand error. Also record hold entry, coast, equilibrium descent, flare, bailout and restart with **independently measured** main/engine/tail RPM; size physical tolerances from sensor uncertainty before tuning. No universal autorotation sink rate follows from the FAA example. Estimated 10–20 days after the hardware topology and inertia data exist. Additional native states and event branches need a worst-case benchmark. Benefit is physically meaningful power-off response and tail authority; real performance remains unmeasured.

Estimated 4–8 days for numerical integration and qualification, with flight-data acquisition scheduled separately. Cost concentrates in the event neighborhoods; whole-tick tails require measurement.

## Coverage retained

- The scalar momentum target uses `vh=√(|T|/(2ρA))`, normal/windmill branches, signed reflection and dimensional residual-wake decay. The main rotor is azimuth-averaged blade-element/momentum with a hard `CT` cap and fixed profile drag. The head has two first-harmonic, flybar-lumped flap states. These implemented approximations do not identify dynamic stall, reverse-flow loads, blade elasticity, full recirculation, spatial wake harmonics or an HX-700 flybarless head. Higher-order states should be justified with identified responses and timing evidence.
- The native generalized torque uses `Qdrive−Qmain−nQtail`. Aerodynamic reaction moments and signed rotor momentum enter the coupled body equations. For supplied X-Cell axes, body inertias and reflected `J=0.0798 kg·m²`, the direct native stand gave `dE/dt=−91.531 W` versus `M·ω+QΩ=−91.531 W`, residual `2.84×10⁻¹⁴ W`. This distinguishes internally consistent angular coupling from HELI-01's atmospheric-work accounting. Physical tail inertia, engine axis and locked-body inventory still need evidence before a richer drivetrain.
- The tail uses the published local thrust-slope construction, finite blockage, yaw-force arm, signed shaft moment and same-stage main wake. Its positive trim scalar solve has a bounded residual. Gavrilets' tail approximation drops the `−h_tail q` forward component of full `U+ω×r`, retains pitch rate through vertical velocity, and linearizes tail thrust about a positive operating point (`nk_helicopter_tail_rotor.hpp:114–122,163–185`). The repository explicitly records these choices (`docs/research/helicopter/HELI-06/README.md`). Transient sideways tail VRS and wake obstruction need measurement; the existing cap alone cannot predict them.

## Primary sources and their limits

1. **Gavrilets, Mettler and Feron, Dynamic Model for a Miniature Aerobatic Helicopter**, especially §§4.1, 4.2, 4.6 and the conclusion. Measured X-Cell inertias and recorded maneuver responses support a low-order reference, its flap/torque equations, and the `μ<0.15` validation envelope. The paper's lumped drag, controller and source airframe differ from the generated trainer and cannot identify the electric HX-700. [Author paper](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~bwgordon/mit_heli_report.pdf)
2. **Johnson, NDARC – NASA Design and Analysis of Rotorcraft, Theory**, ch. 12, especially §§12-3.4, 12-4.1.2, 12-5 and 12-6. It gives force/velocity work, induced/profile/propulsive power, and a wake-normal projection with effective ground distance. These are design-analysis relations and a planar-ground engineering baseline. They supply analytical checks, not X-Cell validation. [NASA technical publication](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250010468/downloads/Johnson%20TP-202510468.pdf)
3. **Platzer, Milluzzo and Rauleder, Investigation on Hovering Rotors over Inclined Ground Planes**, European Rotorcraft Forum, 2018. The PIV and CFD comparison used a two-bladed 0.8 m-diameter rotor one radius over parallel and 15° inclined surfaces. It establishes measurable sensitivity to inclination; different rotor geometry and the limited test conditions preclude transferring its absolute force coefficients. [Original experiment](https://dspace-erf.nlr.nl/server/api/core/bitstreams/bb3ec185-4a96-4066-9fcf-ba9ee7194e69/content)
4. **Johnson, Model for Vortex Ring State Influence on Rotorcraft Flight Dynamics**, NASA/TP-2005-213477, §§3–4 and Tables 3–4. Wind-tunnel and flight data underpin the steady mean-inflow extension and vertical-velocity-drop comparison. The report does not identify the simulator's scalar transient lag, instability setting or a universal RC sink-rate boundary. [NASA report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060024029)
5. **FAA, Helicopter Flying Handbook, ch. 4, Freewheeling Unit and Antitorque System.** The source describes speed-dependent overrun, loss of engine coupling and common transmission-driven tails. It supplies mechanical topology and sign checks; it provides no model-specific gearing, friction, blade polar or engine inertia. [FAA handbook chapter](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/helicopter_flying_handbook/hfh_ch04.pdf)
6. **Miniature Aircraft, Part #0550, Constant Tail Rotor Drive Autorotation Unit, assembly instructions.** A primary manufacturer sheet preserved by the Vintage Radio Helicopters archive describes a split-gear upgrade that keeps the tail engaged as the main rotor overruns the driver. It establishes the importance of a variant-specific topology. The exact parts fitted to the MIT test specimen remain unverified. [Manufacturer sheet archive](https://www.vrhc.co.uk/images/RC_Helicopters/USA/Miniature_Aircraft/X-Cell/Instruction_upgrades.pdf)
