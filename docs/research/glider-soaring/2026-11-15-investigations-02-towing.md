# Glider investigation cards GI-07…GI-12: towing and numerics

**Date:** 15 November 2026. **Source baseline:** `ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f`; existing plan branch `49dce1db4fce7e907c38367cdfdbe1fc0338876a`. **Status:** the source investigations are complete; the proposed implementation, simulator, bench and flight experiments are not performed. The [master plan](../../plans/glider-soaring.md), [launch/control guide](../../plans/glider-soaring/01-launch-and-control.md), [physics guide](../../plans/glider-soaring/02-physics-and-tow.md), [acceptance guide](../../plans/glider-soaring/04-acceptance-and-delivery.md) and [dated source review](2026-11-15-source-review.md) own the current proposal. References below are primary experiments, reports, authors' documentation or research papers; their test articles and assumptions limit transfer to RC.

At the inspected baseline, a session has one `VehicleModel` and a float64 fixed-tick transaction; a proposed pair must use shared same-stage states and held sampled controller outputs, not vehicle-specific kernel branches. First conservation fixtures use fixed mass, fixed CG and rigid hooks. Variable-mass/fixed-datum kinematics and co-moving efflux require their own proof; simply replacing the CG sample is insufficient. The selected EG4/FunCub installations and rope are not measured in this investigation.

## GI-07 — Identify the installed towline, not one nominal spring

**Question.** Can a tension-only Kelvin–Voigt law predict this installed RC line over its admitted load, rate and history, including take-up and release?

**Evidence and finding.** Liu et al.'s 2014 cyclic experiments [S1] observed nonlinear stiffness changes with mean load, amplitude and cycle count in large synthetic mooring ropes. Honda's 1978 experiments [S2] measured hysteresis and fracture under cyclic loading for nylon, polyester and vinylon twines. These are directly observed material effects, but neither specimen nor load scale identifies the proposed towline. One static extension does not independently determine a stiffness and a rate-dependent damping coefficient; conditioning, terminations and damage can change the result. The massless line cannot resolve a localized weak-link tension wave.

**Plan change — SG-08/09/14/16.** Identify rope material, construction, length, knots or splices, hooks and weak-link installation, plus batch, conditioning, temperature and prior use. Record synchronized tensile force and gauge extension at multiple mean loads, amplitudes, rates and cycle counts. Capture full loading/unloading loops, residual extension, release response and uncertainty. Fit `k` to appropriate local slopes and `c` against independent rate/loop-work information only in a declared taut range; check that predicted dissipated work and observed loop orientation agree. Use separate specimens/rig and documented conditions for any breaking-force evidence; a new rope's nominal rating is not an installed weak-link law. Leave physical break unadmitted if evidence is missing.

**Discriminating experiment.** Reserve entire loading histories as held-out data, including revisiting the same elongation and rate after different histories. Compare force residual, loop work, permanent set, peak timing and uncertainty against the one-`k`, one-`c` law and a separately versioned memory-capable alternative. Reject the simple law outside its predeclared error range rather than tuning it independently per case. **Priority:** P1. **Uncertainty:** actual EG4/FunCub line, connector properties and repeatability remain unknown.

## GI-08 — Hook moments and the joint tow envelope

**Question.** For the actual pair, where do hook placement, tow angle and line tension leave sufficient pitch/yaw control and tug climb authority?

**Evidence and finding.** NACA's 1943 wind-tunnel study [S3] varied tow geometry and dihedral and also examined a free-flying tow model. It used **twin parallel lines**, so its stability results are not single-line RC coefficients. The FAA's 2024 glider handbook [S4] describes how a below-CG hook can produce nose-up pitch as tension grows and how out-of-position towing can affect the tug. Its examples are full scale and cannot set RC distances, airspeeds or bank limits.

**Plan change — SG-03/08/09/10/11/16.** Measure both hook locations in each body's fixed-datum frame, CG/inertia for the installed build, control throw/rate, drag polar, available tug power and safe physical speed envelopes. Resolve each attachment force and `r × F` moment at the same stage. For a forward pull at a glider hook below its CG, the pitch moment can be nose-up; an elevated glider pulling on a rear tug hook can create a nose-down moment. The actual signs and leverage depend on full 3-D geometry. A shared airspeed interval is necessary but does not by itself prove a climb: solve force/moment balance with towing drag, load factor, actuator saturation and available excess power.

**Discriminating experiment.** Sweep plausible measured hook positions, line angles, mass/CG, speed and thrust in an offline joint trim; perturb yaw and pitch on either side of each equilibrium, then integrate the pair with bounded controls. Record required surface deflection, margin to saturation/stall, both moments, tension and recovery direction. Compare with independent measured or instrumented tow behavior before promoting the selected envelope. **Priority:** P1. **Uncertainty:** source-specific control derivatives, hook loads and valid coupled trim are not yet established.

## GI-09 — Same-stage coupling versus lagged co-simulation

**Question.** What errors appear if each aircraft uses stale line forces or commits its own events, even when both seem stable individually?

**Evidence and finding.** González et al. [S5] and Sadjina and Pedersen [S6] show that discrete exchange between coupled mechanical subsystems can introduce artificial energy. This is a general co-simulation result, not a measured OpenRC defect. As a diagnostic illustration, two unit masses joined by a unit spring with unit extension and zero initial speed have energy 0.5. One **explicit-Euler** step using held initial forces leaves positions unchanged and gives velocities `±h`, so energy becomes `0.5 + h²`. This is an analytic counterexample to assuming action/reaction alone guarantees discrete energy conservation, not an execution of the current RK4 kernel.

**Plan change — SG-07/08/15.** Compare an intentionally partitioned, delayed-force baseline to a composite stage evaluator at identical commands and weather. Log each hook's force, material-point velocity, moment and work; independently close both-body kinetic/rotational energy plus spring storage and physical damping. Check linear/angular momentum and line-power residual separately. A same-stage RK method can still have truncation or event errors; it does not become exact by design. Sample controllers from one accepted pair frame and hold their outputs during stages, while each stage recomputes both body and line dynamics from that stage's candidate state. A release or failed second-body stage must commit or roll back the whole pair and all controller/event state.

**Discriminating experiment.** Use the analytic oscillator first, then unequal mass, off-CG rotating hooks, slack re-engagement and a release between boundaries. Run each at successively smaller fixed steps, checking energy/work residual, event time and both-body state against an independent reference. Compare partitioned energy drift and convergence, including a one-sided release mutation. **Priority:** P1. **Uncertainty:** actual pair eigenmodes and production cost remain unmeasured.

## GI-10 — Select a solver for a unilateral, potentially stiff line

**Question.** Which integration and event method meets accuracy and tick-cost limits over the smallest defensible line/mass envelope?

**Evidence and finding.** Anitescu, Potra and Stewart's 1999 work [S7] develops time-stepping methods for nonsmooth rigid-body contact using impulses and complementarity; it is not a prequalified rope implementation. The SciPy 1.11.3 solver documentation [S8] states that endpoint sign-change event searches can miss multiple crossings within a step. A stiffer or lighter line/body combination increases frequency; high damping can also create a fast decaying mode. The ideal RK4 oscillator stability limit is not a peak-load, event-detection or accuracy guarantee.

**Plan change — SG-07/08/12/17.** Compare: (1) same-stage explicit RK4 with bounded subdivisions and located unilateral transitions; (2) an implicit compliant formulation with iteration/residual and failure bounds; and (3) a rigid unilateral constraint with explicit velocity/impulse and complementarity policy. The third is a **different physical law**, not simply an infinitely stiff spring with unchanged release energy. Any selected method must compose contact and release events, preserve pair rollback and secondary attitude validity, and fit the kernel's current `step_policy`/substep restriction or a qualified generic SF extension. No silent stiffness reduction, force cap or unchecked solver failure.

**Discriminating experiment.** Declare independent absolute/relative tolerances before runs; use a closed-form taut oscillator and a separately converged small-step compliant oracle with independently bracketed mode changes. Compare `h`, `h/2`, `h/4`, `h/8` across mass, offset, stiffness, damping and closure-speed boundaries. Retain peak and integrated tension, energy residual, event count/order/time, pair motion and p95/max cost. Include a within-step threshold excursion that returns below threshold. Nonsmooth convergence need not be monotonic or fourth order; if references disagree or event bounds are inconclusive, refuse that case. **Priority:** P1. **Uncertainty:** the measured material envelope and target-host budget are open.

## GI-11 — Swept pair and line clearance during events

**Question.** How can the activity prevent missed aircraft crossings and misleading line pass-through without pretending a slack massless rope has a known shape?

**Evidence and finding.** Wang et al.'s 2021 continuous-collision benchmark [S9] exposes tradeoffs among false negatives, conservative false positives and cost for its tested primitives and trajectories. Xavier's 1997 swept-volume work [S10] distinguishes translational sweeps from approximations involving rotation. Neither source validates OpenRC's aircraft hulls, curved RK trajectories, propellers or rope contact.

**Plan change — SG-07/08/12/14/17.** Define physical aircraft collision proxies, station bounds and swept motion between accepted states; use a conservative broad phase plus a verified narrow phase that accounts for rotation and its trajectory/interpolation error. A point-sampled endpoint check cannot qualify T13. Before scored paired towing, an intersecting airframe must produce a bounded incident/terminal or refused transaction, never a successful tow; full crash mechanics remain separate. The first rope law has no sag, mass or collision. If checking an attached taut straight segment against terrain, propeller or aircraft, bound the **swept segment** over the entire interval and define endpoint/hook exemptions. For an assumed maximum physical arc length `L_max`, every possible slack-line point `x` satisfies `|x − x_g| + |x − x_t| <= L_max`; the resulting ellipsoid is a conservative enclosure, not a solved sag shape. Sweep that bound and exclude installed hooks; it may cause many false positives. If the slack path or a candidate intersection cannot be bounded, reject or conservatively terminate the affected case and state the limitation; do not depict or score an unmodeled rope passage as a demonstrated clear path.

**Discriminating experiment.** Construct between-tick wingtip crossings with clear endpoints, rotating thin surfaces, grazing/tangent contact, initial overlap, fast closure and simultaneous release/contact. Include taut-segment sweeps across terrain/propeller and slack-path ambiguity. Compare reported time intervals against an independent analytic or conservative high-precision geometry oracle; record false negatives, false positives, refusal and complete-pair rollback, then measure cost under the real 1080p activity. **Priority:** P1 release gate. **Uncertainty:** actual proxy accuracy, feasible rope scope and performance are unknown.

## GI-12 — Reachable tow guidance and bounded aborts

**Question.** Can the tug and glider controllers maintain a reachable tow state under actuator limits and execute a distinct, recorded release or abort?

**Evidence and finding.** The 2025 e-Genius study [S11] reports full-size flight tests of an automatically guided tug towing a glider, including response to line disturbances; collision avoidance remained future work. NASA's 2002 formation-autopilot work [S12] flight-tested relative station-keeping for two F/A-18 aircraft **without a towline**. These demonstrate useful controller-test methods, not an autonomous RC follower, collision guarantee or transferable gains.

**Plan change — SG-09/10/11/14/16.** Build the initial controller around air-relative speed, attitude and actual actuator states, plus accepted pair geometry, closure and modeled line tension. Limit requested climb, turn, surface travel and rate to a jointly attainable configuration; use anti-windup and distinguish slack recovery, separation and tug recovery. If simulation-truth tension or other unavailable real sensor data are used, mark them as idealized assistance. Controllers sample the same detached accepted pair frame at the declared cadence; checkpoint their integrators, timers, held commands, mode and ownership, and do not read future RK stages or write positions. Keep release independent from ownership transfer so a refused transfer does not remove an available emergency release.

**Discriminating experiment.** Identify feasible equilibrium and transition bounds first, then compare straight tow, left/right turns, gust entry, slack take-up, glider high/low excursion, actuator saturation and tug power loss. At each boundary record actual versus commanded flight path, line power/tension, speed and control margins, saturation time, minimum separation and the ordered abort/release outcome. Include both human-role variants and replay from immediately before an intervention; require completion or explicit refusal under predeclared tolerances. Hold out weather and mass cases. **Priority:** P1. **Uncertainty:** actual hardware/controller envelope and safe recovery space require independent measurement.
