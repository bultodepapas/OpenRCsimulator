# M5-ATM-1 source evidence

**Status:** source review recorded 2026-10-09. **Step:** M5-ATM-1.

| Source | Supported fact used here | Scope and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NOAA/NASA/USAF, [U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19770009539/downloads/19770009539.pdf) | Standard atmosphere is an idealized steady profile; the lower atmosphere uses the familiar tropospheric lapse-rate model. Primary source for the ISA baseline. | Not real weather at a particular field. |
| NASA, [NDARC Theory Manual, §4–5 Environment and Atmosphere](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/Publications/files/NDARCTheory_v1_6_938.pdf) | Gives `R = p0/(rho0·T0)`, the tropospheric pressure equation, `h_g = r·h/(r+h)`, and density-altitude inversion. Here the adopted standard values imply `Rd = 287.052874247 J/(kg·K)`; code rounds to 287.05287. | The manual uses standard atmosphere models and equations, not a local weather-column solve. The returned density altitude is geometric after converting the inversion result. |
| NASA Ames/AIAA, [Comparison of Fuel Consumption of Descent Trajectories](https://aviationsystems.arc.nasa.gov/publications/2012/AIAA-2012-4818.pdf) | Independently states the conventional specific gas constant for dry air as 287.05287 J/(kg·K). | Confirms a conventional engineering value; it is not a universal exact gas constant. Rounded 287.05 differs by about 10 ppm. |
| Buck, [New Equations for Computing Vapor Pressure and Enhancement Factor](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0450(1981)020%3C1527:NEFCVP%3E2.0.CO;2); UCAR EOL, [Water Vapor Pressure Formulations](https://www.eol.ucar.edu/data-software/conventions-and-standards/water-vapor-pressure-formulations) | The updated Buck liquid-water equation uses `611.21·exp((18.678 − t/234.5)·t/(257.14+t))` hPa. UCAR lists separate liquid-water and ice saturation curves and notes limited direct measurements for supercooled water. | Runtime explicitly defines RH relative to liquid water for the full −20 to 45 °C input envelope. An RH value relative to ice needs conversion. Subzero results carry greater empirical uncertainty. The input bounds are simulator choices, not a Buck validity claim. |
| Picard et al., [Revised formula for the density of moist air (CIPM-2007)](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/calibrations/CIPM-2007.pdf) | Independent equation of state, gas constant `8.314472 J/(mol·K)`, dry-air molar mass `28.96546 g/mol` (the 400 µmol/mol CO₂ reference), water molar mass `18.01528 g/mol`, enhancement and compressibility equations. Also supplies a validity interval. | NIST recommends the CIPM equation for 600–1100 hPa and 15–27 °C. The app implements a simpler ideal partial-pressure mixture; it is compared only at an in-range point and does not claim CIPM accuracy outside that interval. |
| NOAA/NWS, [Pressure Altitude and Density Altitude spreadsheet guide, TM-215](https://www.weather.gov/media/wrh/online_publications/TMs/TM-215.pdf) | Relates field elevation and altimeter setting to pressure altitude and gives the temperature correction used for density altitude. | Aviation operational approximation; the app instead reports density altitude by inverting its ISA density equation. |
| NACA, [TR-426: The Effect of Humidity on Engine Power at Altitude](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19930091500.pdf) | Historical engine tests found water vapor to act as an inert diluent and reported indicated power proportional to mass-rate oxygen consumption under the tested conditions. | Supports using dry-air partial pressure as a first-order combustion charge proxy. It does not establish a modern RC engine torque curve or manufacturer altitude map. |

## Independent numeric checks

[`../kernel/reference_check.py`](../kernel/reference_check.py) calculates the standard-atmosphere and CIPM-2007 reference values independently in Python; it imports no app code. The checked output is [`../kernel/reference_results.json`](../kernel/reference_results.json). At 20 °C, 101325 Pa, 50% RH, the simple runtime equation differs from CIPM-2007 by about −0.0384%, within the step's 0.05% comparison threshold. This is one point inside CIPM's recommended range, not a bound on error across the app's broader input envelope.
