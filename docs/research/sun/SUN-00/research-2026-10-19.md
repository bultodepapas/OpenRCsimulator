# Sun disc and pilot dazzle: research notes (2026-10-19)

Dated research snapshot for [SUN-00](README.md), gathered by web search on 2026-10-19 and kept verbatim apart from
script paths. Every claim carries a tag and its URL. The README states what the implementation adopted; this file is
evidence, not operating instructions.

Scope: a physically based sun disc for the sky shader and a dazzle overlay for the
human-eye pilot view, plus lens effects for chase and orbit cameras. Target engine is
Godot 4.7.2 using the Compatibility (GLES3) renderer.

Labels used below:
- **[primary]**: read in the original paper, standard or source code.
- **[secondary]**: a reliable summary or citation, but the original was not read.
- **[inference]**: my own calculation or reasoning. Scripts are at
  [`research/sun/sun-00/calc.py`](../../../../research/sun/sun-00/calc.py)
  and [`calc2.py`](../../../../research/sun/sun-00/calc2.py).
- **[unverified]**: recalled from memory and not confirmed this session. Do not ship
  without checking.

## 1. Disability glare and veiling luminance

### 1.1 Equations

**CIE 146:2002 family (Vos & van den Berg)**, θ in degrees, A = age in years, L in cd/m²,
E in lux at the eye [primary: TNO abstract https://repository.tno.nl/SingleDoc?docId=8955;
structure confirmed in a CIE excerpt via search]:

1. **Age-adjusted Stiles–Holladay**, 1° < θ < 30°: `L_veil/E = 10·(1 + (A/70)^4)/θ²`,
   "albeit with great individual spread". The unadjusted original is "only valid between
   3° and 30°" per a 1999 Vos talk [secondary].
2. **Small-angle**, 0.1°–30°: commonly quoted as `10/θ³ + (5/θ²)·[1+(A/62.5)^4]`
   (the general equation with p = 0) [unverified].
3. **General Disability Glare equation**, 0.1°–100° [primary, eq. 2 of Bará & Bao-Varela
   2023, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09103]:

   ```
   Ψ(θ) = L_eq/E_gl = 10/θ³ + (5/θ² + 0.1·p/θ)·[1 + (A/62.5)^4] + 2.5e-3·p      [sr⁻¹]
   ```

   p = 0 dark, 0.5 brown, 1.0 blue-green eyes; matters only above ~20–30°. Ψ is
   "normalized such that its integral across the visual field is unity". Below ~6′, each
   person's aberrations dominate. Field form (their eq. 5):
   `L(α) = L_f(α) + ∫ Ψ(angle(α, α′))·L_f(α′)·cos z′ dΩ′` [primary]. The "complete"
   0°–100° CIE equation (9.2e6/[1+(θ/0.0046)²]^1.5 …) could not be confirmed [unverified].

**Ijspeert, van den Berg & Spekreijse 1993** (Vision Res. 33:15–20): full-angle four-term
PSF with age, pupil and pigmentation; clean coefficients in the MPL-2.0 `raytools` code
(pigmentation 0.16 blue, 0.106 brown, 0.056 dark brown) [primary code:
https://raytraverse.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/raytools/evaluate/hvsgsm.html].

**Vos 1984 photopic PSF**, as used by Spencer et al. 1995 [primary, reproduced in Yoshida
et al. 2008 eqs. 3–6, https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/yoshida08bgi.pdf]:

```
PSF(θ) = 0.384 f0 + 0.478 f1 + 0.138 f2      (θ in degrees)
f0 = 2.61e6·exp(−(θ/0.02)²);  f1 = 20.91/(θ + 0.02)³;  f2 = 72.37/(θ + 0.02)²
```

Numerically it integrates to 0.9996 over the sphere, so it is a normalised sr⁻¹ PSF
usable directly as Ψ; 26.5% of the energy lies beyond 0.1° [inference].

**Ward Larson, Rushmeier & Piatko 1997** [primary:
https://graphics.cs.yale.edu/sites/default/files/1997tvcg_hdr_tonemapping.pdf]:
`L_a = 0.913·L_f + (K/π)∬_{θ>θf} L cosθ sinθ/θ² dθ dφ`, K = 0.0096 (θ in rad),
θ_f = 0.5°; per pixel `L_pv = 0.913·L_p + L_v` (Holladay: glare acts as an added veil).
K/π in radians equals 10·E/θ² in degrees [inference].

### 1.2 Numbers for the sun

Inputs: E_sun = 100 klx normal to the eye, A = 40, p = 0.5 [inference, calc.py].

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5° | 103 | 44 | 108 | 1.0e7 | 1.2e6 |
| 1° | 15.9 | 11.1 | 19.0 | 1.6e6 | 2.9e5 |
| 2° | 2.74 | 2.77 | 3.66 | 2.7e5 | 7.4e4 |

The KS91 sky column uses k = 0.172 (Mauna Kea V band) with the sun at the zenith. That
value is low for a lowland site; real clear skies are often 2–4× brighter far from the
sun, since multiple scattering and ground albedo are ignored.

Fraction of eye-scattered light beyond a given angle (CIE general equation, p = 0.5)
[inference, calc2.py]:

| Beyond | Age 20 | Age 40 | Age 70 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1° | 0.27 | 0.28 | 0.38 |
| 1° | 0.076 | 0.084 | 0.15 |
| 5° | 0.045 | 0.050 | 0.096 |

So roughly 5% of the sun's corneal flux ends up as veil more than 5° from it.

### 1.3 Applying the PSF per pixel

- **The sun is an almost-point source of known direction**, so no image convolution is
  needed: compute `cosθ = dot(view_ray, sun_dir)` in a fullscreen pass and add
  `L_veil = Ψ(θ)·E_sun·V_sun` (V_sun = visible disc fraction). Inside ~1°, use an offline
  1D log-θ lookup of Ψ convolved with the limb-darkened disc; clamp θ ≥ 0.1° [inference].
- **Spencer et al. 1995**: glare = flare (lenticular halo and ciliary corona, from
  diffraction) + bloom (scatter in cornea, lens, retina); PSF convolution makes sources
  look brighter [secondary: https://history.siggraph.org/?p=118751].
- **Ritschel et al. 2009, "Temporal Glare"** (CGF 28(2):183–192) [secondary, Ulm seminar
  summary]: aperture images (pupil, lens fibres and particles, vitreous, eyelashes,
  blink) × Fresnel term → FFT → spectral blur → FFT convolution → tone map. Lens fibres
  give the halo (~40% of forward scatter), lens particles the corona needles; hippus
  makes glare pulse. Dynamic glare looked brighter; static glare was preferred.
- **Pupil and hippus** (Frisvad) [primary: https://people.compute.dtu.dk/jerf/code/hippus/]:
  `p = p_max − c·(1 + tanh[0.4·(ln(L_v·π·10⁻¹) + 0.5)])`, p_max = 9 mm, c = 2.5 (Moon &
  Spencer: 7.9 mm, 3); `h(t,p) = p + noise(t/p)·(p_max/p)·sqrt(1 − p/p_max)` with
  three-octave sparse convolution noise.
- **Yoshida et al. 2008** [primary]: the glare illusion raises perceived luminance by
  **20–35%**; a Gaussian (σ = 0.34°) is as strong as the Vos PSF and cheaper; some kernels
  make Mach bands. On an LDR display the eye-shaped veil therefore serves contrast-loss
  realism, not extra brightness [inference].

### 1.4 Contrast threshold and the angle at which the plane is lost

**Threshold model.** Crumey 2014 (MNRAS 442:2600) refits Blackwell 1946 (JOSA 36:624;
about 450,000 trials; uniform discs 0.595′–360′; unlimited viewing time)
[primary: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.4209]. A in sr, B in cd/m²:

- Ricco (small-target) branch: `C_low = R/A`, with photopic `R = (r3·B^-1/4 + r4)²`,
  r3 = 1.772e-4 and r4 = 7.167e-5.
- Large-target branch: `C∞_phot = 2.720e-3` (Weber).
- Combined: `C = ((R/A)^q + C∞^q)^(1/q)`, with q = 1.146 − 0.0885·log10 B for
  B ≥ 3.40 cd/m².
- Blackwell's brightest field was 3426 cd/m², so anything near the sun is an
  extrapolation.

**Worked case.** Plane: about 0.4 m² projected, underside in shadow, L_t ≈ 2500 cd/m².
Background: sky + veil from §1.2. Effective contrast is
`C_eff = |L_t − L_sky| / (L_sky + L_veil)` [inference]:

| Range | 1° | 2° | 5° | 10° | 20° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C_eff (all ranges) | 0.15 | 0.21 | 0.24 | 0.25 | 0.27 |
| C_eff / C_th at 150 m | 37 | 53 | 66 | 70 | 75 |
| C_eff / C_th at 300 m | 24 | 35 | 44 | 46 | 49 |

**Result:** static disability glare plus Blackwell thresholds predict the silhouette
stays visible down to about 1° from the sun. That contradicts field experience, so the
steady-state veil is not what blinds the pilot [inference].

Supporting evidence:

- Cones keep obeying Weber's law even when nearly all pigment is bleached [secondary:
  Nature 265:243, via search snippet].
- Hawkins 1987, cited by the ATSB see-and-avoid report (Hobbs 1991, §2.3.3): "When the
  glare source is 5 degrees from the line of sight, visual effectiveness is reduced by
  84 per cent." It also says glare half as intense as the general illumination at 40°
  reduces effectiveness by 42% [primary report text:
  https://www.airproxboard.org.uk/media/i12novz5/aus-safety-board-limitations-of-see-and-avoid.pdf].
- Driver studies place sun-glare effects within about 19° (age 40) to 25° (age 60), with
  glare "generally absent beyond 25°" [secondary: BMJ Open 2019 literature review,
  https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/9/8/e028350].

**What actually makes the pilot lose the plane** [inference, consistent with the sources
above]:

1. **Transient photostress.** When the gaze or the plane crosses within about 1–2° of the
   sun, its 0.53° image lands near the fovea. Retinal illuminance is about 8e9 td with a
   2.5 mm pupil, far above the 2e4 td half-bleach level, so the patch bleaches almost
   instantly. The after-image then masks the plane for seconds (§2).
2. **Adaptation transients.** Retinal illuminance goes from about 1e4 td on open sky to
   1e5–1e7 td within 1–5° of the sun. Weber adaptation needs time, and the eyelids and
   pupil react (squint, blink, aversion).
3. **The plane becomes a dark silhouette.** Colour and shading cues for attitude are lost,
   and the veil drains saturation. This matches the RC wording "lose orientation", not
   just "lose sight" (§4).
4. **Geometric coincidence.** A 1.5 m span at 160 m subtends 0.54°, so the model can cover
   the sun entirely.

**Suggested design envelope** [inference, tunable]:

| Zone | Angle from sun | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Hard loss | ≤ 3° | Any fixation within 3° triggers a foveal after-image. |
| Degraded | 3–10° | Contrast is reduced by L_sky/(L_sky + L_veil): 0.16 at 1°, 0.25 at 3°, 0.30 at 5°, 0.44 at 10°, 0.67 at 20° (KS91 sky). |
| Mild veil | 10–25° | Comfort and saturation effects only. |

## 2. Photostress, after-images and flash blindness

### 2.1 Retinal dose and the after-image threshold

Ho, Ghanbari & Diver 2011 (ASME J. Sol. Energy Eng. 133:031021)
[primary: https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/167/2025/03/Methods_Ho_2011.pdf]:

- Retinal image diameter: `d_r = f·ω`, with ω the subtended angle in rad and
  f ≈ 0.017 m.
- Retinal irradiance: `E_r = E_c·(d_p²/d_r²)·τ`, with pupil d_p = 2 mm (daylight) and
  ocular transmission τ = 0.5.
- **Sun:** E_c = 0.1 W/cm², ω = 0.0094 rad, giving **E_r ≈ 8 W/cm²**.
- After-image threshold (lowest E_r that gave ≥1 s of after-image):
  `E_r,flash = 3.59e-5 / ω^1.77` W/cm².
- Retinal burn: `E_r,burn = 0.118/ω` for ω < 0.118 rad, otherwise 1 W/cm². These assume
  a 0.15 s blink-response exposure.
- "A brief direct viewing of the sun (0.15 s) has a high potential for producing
  after-image effects."
- For the sun, E_r,flash = 0.139 W/cm², so the sun is about **58×** over the after-image
  threshold [inference].
- The same equations appear in the SGHAT manual
  [primary: https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/167/2022/03/SGHAT_Technical_Reference-v6.pdf].
- For the scattered light around the disc image, scale E_r by L_veil/L_disc and use the
  halo's angular size as ω [inference]:
  - 0.5° from the sun: about 1.1× threshold.
  - 1°: about 0.65×.
  - 2°: about 0.4×.
  - 5°: about 0.2×.
  So the after-image is the disc plus a roughly 1° halo. Glances that stay ≥2° from the
  sun make little after-image.

### 2.2 Recovery times

- **Clinical photostress test** (10 s bleach): normal recovery < 30 s [secondary: EyeWiki
  https://eyewiki.org/Photostress_Recovery_Test]; 15–30 s (Kanski, via Wikipedia); with a
  15 s exposure, normal maximum 35 s up to age 50 and 70 s over 70 (Bartlett, Davies &
  Eperjesi); aged 70+: median 80 s without AMD, 119.5 s early AMD, 198 s late AMD (Brandl
  et al. 2023, https://pred.uni-regensburg.de/id/eprint/59108/) [all secondary].
  Margrain & Thomson 2002 exists but its numbers were not retrieved.
- **Flash blindness** [secondary: DTIC/NAP via search]: log recovery time is about linear
  in log(retinal illuminance × duration), roughly 20–130 s for 9e5–3e7 td·s; it levels
  off at very high doses (maximal bleaching), grows with flash size and duration, shrinks
  on brighter backgrounds; individual means vary about 2×.
- **Reidenbach (Cologne), laser and LED dazzle** [secondary: SPIE/LIA abstracts]: foveal
  after-images up to **300 s**; red HeNe fit `t_AI ≈ 50.6·ln(P·t/µJ) − 13.4` s; green LED
  acuity recovery `t_VA ≈ 3.7·ln(E/µJ) − 16.2` s; after-image size nearly constant for
  ~12 min while acuity "is recovered within 30 up to 60 seconds". Design consequence:
  acuity masking with a ~5–30 s time constant, faint visible after-image with a 1–5 min
  tail [inference].
- **Pigment kinetics** [secondary]: cone half-bleach 4.3 log td (2e4 td; Rushton & Henry
  1968 via https://psychtoolbox.org/docs/ComputePhotopigmentBleaching); recovery after
  near-total bleach has a time constant of about 1.5 min (ERG study, via search).

### 2.3 After-image appearance

- A bright flash leaves a **positive** after-image for about 1 s, then a **negative** one
  "often with fleeting colors". The positive phase is receptor after-discharge; the
  negative phase is pigment bleaching [secondary, physiology overview via search].
- Hue is complementary to the stimulus. A dazzling white source often gives a **purple**
  after-image; green gives red [secondary].
- The "flight of colours" (hue drift) is mostly seen with eyes closed or in the dark
  [primary: Ritschel & Eisemann 2012].
- After-images blur progressively over time (Brindley 1962) [primary: Ritschel &
  Eisemann].
- A yellowish sun after-image on blue sky reads as a dark blue-violet or purple blob
  [inference].

### 2.4 Real-time model to copy

Ritschel & Eisemann, "A Computational Model of Afterimages" (CGF 31(2), EG 2012)
[primary: TU Delft PDF]:

- Per pixel and per LMS cone, track the active-opsin fraction r:
  `ṙ = c_a·L·(1 − r) − c_d·r`, with c_a = 0.3 and c_d = 1.0·c_a, "an average over three
  test subjects". L is effective radiance derived from trolands (Stiles–Crawford
  weighted), but its scaling for these constants is not stated: treat L as normalised and
  re-tune [inference].
- Integrate one explicit step per frame and clamp r to [0, 1].
- Diffuse r with a Gaussian of σ = N_σ/√f per frame (f = fps), matching Brindley's blur
  after 1 s.
- Map bleaching to an opponent colour space (oRGB) and composite the opposite colour
  over the LDR image.
- Optional hue rotation per step for the flight of colours.
- Ran at 300 Mpixel/s in fp16 on a GTX 480.
- Saccade and micro-saccade jitter is applied to the input.

**Port to Godot Compatibility** [inference]:

- Run the integration in a small SubViewport (for example 256×144, RGBA8 or RGB10_A2)
  that reads its previous frame as a texture (ping-pong). This needs no compute and no
  float buffers.
- Store r in [0, 1] (8 bits is coarse; use a dithered update).
- The pilot's eye tracks the plane and the camera follows the gaze, so the after-image is
  **retina-fixed = screen-fixed** in pilot view. Feed the sun's screen position each
  frame; when the camera sweeps past the sun this naturally draws a smeared streak.
- Do **not** reproject the after-image buffer into world space.

## 3. Sun disc appearance

### 3.1 Size and luminance

- **Angular diameter:** 31.6′–32.7′ over the year, mean about 0.533°. LIGHTX_SIZE in a
  Godot sky shader is "about .0087 radians (0.5 degrees)"
  [secondary/standard; Godot docs primary]. Solid angle Ω = π·(0.2666°)² = 6.80e-5 sr
  [inference].
- **Disc luminance:** about 1.6e9 cd/m² at noon, about 6e5 cd/m² at the horizon, average
  clear sky about 8e3 cd/m² [secondary: https://schorsch.com/en/kbase/glossary/luminance.html].
  The AMS glossary gives about 470,000 lambert at the zenith, which is 1.5e9 cd/m²
  [secondary].
- **Consistency check:** 100 klx / 6.80e-5 sr gives a mean of 1.47e9 cd/m². With
  limb darkening (mean/centre = 0.80), the disc centre is about 1.84e9 cd/m²
  [inference].
- **Illuminance:**
  - Solar illuminance constant: 128 klx outside the atmosphere [secondary].
  - Clear-day direct sun: 80–100 klx at sea level (about 93 lm/W × 900–1050 W/m²)
    [secondary].
  - "Up to 100 kilolux on the Earth's surface" [secondary, Wikipedia-derived].

### 3.2 Limb darkening

- **Power law (best for shaders).** Hestroffer & Magnan 1998 (A&A 333:338):
  `I(μ)/I(1) = μ^α`, α(λ) = −0.023 + 0.292/λ[µm], fitted for 416–1099 nm to Pierce &
  Slaughter 1977 and Neckel & Labs 1994 data, with a discontinuity near 390 nm
  [secondary: quoted in arXiv 2407.21583 §3.1].
  - α = 0.656 at 430 nm, 0.573 at 490 nm, 0.508 at 550 nm, 0.440 at 630 nm [inference].
  - μ = cos(heliocentric angle) = sqrt(1 − (r/R)²).
- **FlightGear HDR `oursun.frag` uses exactly this law** with u = 1 and
  α = (0.429, 0.502, 0.575, 0.643) at 630/560/490/430 nm [primary code:
  https://gitlab.com/flightgear/fgdata/-/raw/next/Shaders/HDR/oursun.frag; fgdata is GPL,
  so reuse the equation, not the file]. For RGB use roughly α ≈ (0.44, 0.50, 0.60)
  [inference].
- **Quadratic law (Allen/Cox 2000 via Wikipedia):**
  `I/I(0) = 0.30 + 0.93·μ − 0.23·μ²` at about 550 nm. The edge is 30% of the centre and
  the disc mean is 80.5% of the centre [secondary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limb_darkening].
- **Linear law:** the best area-weighted fit to μ^0.508 is u = 0.636. That supports the
  suggested u ≈ 0.6 at 550 nm [inference].
- **Neckel & Labs 1994** (Sol. Phys. 153:91): 5th-order polynomials at 30 continuum
  wavelengths (303–1099 nm), fitted for μ ≥ 0.12 [secondary]. One coefficient set at
  640.97 nm (μ⁰…μ⁵): 0.33644, 1.30590, −1.79238, 2.45040, −1.89979, 0.59943 [primary
  code: HAO lmbdrk_sbsp.pro]. Neckel 2005 (Sol. Phys. 229:13) gives analytic λ-functions;
  paywalled.
- **Colour:** limb darkening is stronger in blue, so the limb looks slightly redder
  [inference from α(λ)]. On an 8-bit display the disc saturates to white anyway. Show limb
  darkening only when the sun is attenuated (haze, near the horizon, thin cloud) or
  through the exposure the eye model would apply. Never let a full-brightness disc show
  its interior gradient in the pilot view [inference].

### 3.3 Sky near the sun: aureole

**Krisciunas & Schaefer 1991** (PASP 103:1033) [primary scan:
https://cta-redmine.irap.omp.eu/attachments/download/3619/1991PASP__103_1033K.pdf]:
`f_R(ρ) = 2.27e5·[1.06 + cos²ρ]`; `f_M(ρ) = 10^(6.15 − ρ/40)` for ~10–80°; and below 10°:
"the data in King 1971 show that equation (18) is not valid. Schaefer 1991 has shown that
f_M(ρ) = 6.2 × 10⁷ ρ⁻² ... This small angle scattering forms the aureole" (ρ in degrees).
It was validated against **Russian daytime sun** data (Pyaskovskaya-Fesenkova 1957),
with no solar/lunar difference; accuracy 8–23%. Sky brightness:
`B[nL] = f(ρ)·I*·10^(−0.4·k·X_src)·(1 − 10^(−0.4·k·X))`, `I* = 10^(−0.4(m + 16.57))`,
m_sun = −26.74 (1.17e4 fc ≈ 126 klx), 1 nL = 3.183e-6 cd/m². Calibrated:
`L_aureole(ρ) ≈ 2.9e5·ρ⁻² cd/m²` for k = 0.17, sun near zenith [inference].

**Suitability verdict** [inference]: **yes** for the 1–10° aureole shape (it is literally
the aureole term). Regularise the core, e.g. `6.2e7/(ρ² + ρ0²)` with ρ0 ≈ 0.25–0.3°;
join `10^(6.15−ρ/40)` at 10° (within 2×); scale by (1 − 10^(−0.4kX)) for turbidity. Do
**not** use it as the eye veil; that is the separate Ψ(θ)·E term.

**A Henyey–Greenstein lobe alone cannot make an aureole** [inference]: HG g = 0.8 is
nearly flat inside (1−g) rad ≈ 11° (relative 45 at 0°, 44.6 at 1°, 36.4 at 5°, 22.1 at
10°: a 2× fall from 1° to 10°, versus 100× for ρ⁻²). **Sky3D** uses exactly HG g = 0.8
(`atm_sun_partial_mie_phase = (0.36, 1.64, 1.6)`) [primary code]. Add a ρ⁻² term or a
second lobe with g ≈ 0.98–0.99.

**CIE 2003 general sky** (beyond ~10°) [primary code: colour-science, BSD-3-Clause]:
indicatrix `f(χ) = 1 + c·[e^(dχ) − e^(dπ/2)] + e·cos²χ`, gradation `φ(Z) = 1 + a·e^(b/cosZ)`.

| Type | a | b | c | d | e |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | −1 | −0.55 | 10 | −3.0 | 0.45 |
| 12 | −1 | −0.32 | 10 | −3.0 | 0.45 |

**Buie et al. 2003 sunshape** (Solar Energy 74:113) [unverified recollection; paywalled]:
`φ = cos(0.326θ)/cos(0.308θ)` for θ ≤ 4.65 mrad, else `e^κ·θ^γ`, with
`κ = 0.9·ln(13.5χ)·χ^-0.3`, `γ = 2.2·ln(0.52χ)·χ^0.43 − 0.1`, χ = circumsolar ratio (clear
sky ~0.01–0.05). A patent example gives κ = 2.1158, γ = −2.2997 [secondary]; γ ≈ −2.3
agrees with the ρ⁻² aureole.

### 3.4 Refraction flattening of the setting sun

Formulas [primary via Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_refraction]:

- Bennett 1982: `R = cot(h_a + 7.31/(h_a + 4.4))` arcmin, h_a = apparent altitude in
  degrees. Within 0.07′ of Garfinkel from zenith to horizon.
- Sæmundsson: `R = 1.02·cot(h + 10.3/(h + 5.11))` arcmin, h = true altitude.
- Both are for 101.0 kPa and 10 °C. Scale by `(P/101)·(283/(273+T))`.
- Horizon refraction is about 34′ nominal, 35.4′ at 10 °C and 1013 hPa. The setting sun
  is "flattened by about 5′ (about 1/6 of its apparent diameter)".

Computed vertical/horizontal ratio [inference, calc.py]:

| Lower limb (apparent altitude) | Vertical diameter | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | 26.9′ | 0.84 |
| 1° | 28.7′ | 0.90 |
| 2° | 29.8′ | 0.93 |
| 5° | 31.3′ | 0.98 |

Implement by mapping each disc sample's true altitude through R(h) [inference].

## 4. RC pilot practice

- **Field orientation.** AMA's "Suggested Flying Site Specifications" (2022) has no sun
  rule: AMA "does not have specific requirements for the site layout" [primary PDF].
  - Model Aviation, July 2001: "The sun rises and sets east–west. It is not good to have a
    runway north–south. Half the day the sun is right in your eyes." and "If you fly
    mostly in the morning, and you fly facing west, then the sun is mostly at your back"
    [primary: https://library.modelaviation.com/article/ama-news-299].
  - BMFA F3N report (2024-08-13): "Normally the flightline is facing directly north"; the
    pilot requested "a perspex disc on a pole which can be positioned so that it directly
    covers the sun" [primary: https://bmfa.org/?p=7272].
  - MAAC club rules: with no wind, take off "east or west but away from the sun"; one
    field provides three "sun discs" for low sun [secondary: club PDFs via search].
- **Loss of orientation.** HobbyKing: flying into the sun, you'll "lose your orientation
  and crash your plane" [secondary, page 403]. RC Airplane World lists "Flying across the
  sun." as a disorientation cause [primary:
  https://www.rc-airplane-world.com/rc-airplane-disorientation]. A 2015 RC pilot was
  "disorientated by the glare of the sun" and ditched [secondary: PetaPixel].
- **Sunglasses.** Model Aviation, Dec 1995: "The best all-around color for sunglasses for
  model flying is a light brown with no perceptible added color tint"; keep shades light,
  since dark lenses dim the model too [primary:
  https://library.modelaviation.com/article/now-youre-talking-6]. FAA brochure:
  "Polarized sunglasses are not recommended because of their possible interaction with
  displays or other materials in the cockpit environment"; lenses passing 15–30% of
  visible light, 20% typical [secondary, brochure 403]. Polarized lenses can black out a
  landscape-held LCD (DJI RC Pro reports) [secondary], which applies to LCD transmitters
  [inference].
- **Caps and visors** are folk practice (no authoritative quote found); the sun disc on a
  pole is the competition equivalent.
- **Sim features suggested** [inference]: a cap-brim occluder clipping Ψ for sources
  ~25–35° above the gaze; a neutral-density "sunglasses" factor ≈ 0.2 on scene and veil
  (contrast unchanged, after-image dose 5× lower); a "sun disc" field prop.

## 6. Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility facts

Godot 4.7.2-stable was released 2026-08-18 [primary: GitHub API]. Source paths below are
under `drivers/gles3/` at that tag [primary].

- **Render target format** (`storage/texture_storage.cpp`): opaque viewports use
  **GL_RGB10_A2**, `transparent_bg` viewports RGBA8, the HDR-2D path RGBA16F (docs: HDR
  2D unsupported in Compatibility). The docs' "RGBA8" summary is a simplification.
- **Glow** (`rasterizer_scene_gles3.cpp`, `effects/glow.cpp`, `shaders/effects/glow.glsl`):
  - On an RGB10_A2 target with glow on: "we can fake a little HDR to do glow..." and
    `luminance_multiplier = 0.25`, so values up to **4.0** reach the glow filter;
    otherwise everything clamps at 1.0.
  - Filter: `smoothstep(hdr_threshold, hdr_threshold + hdr_scale, max(rgb))`, capped by
    `glow_hdr_luminance_cap`. Dual-filter (Bjørge 2015) with **4 fixed levels**, each half
    size (min 4 px). Largest blur ≈ 1/16 screen: far too narrow for a 10–20° veil
    [inference].
  - Glow moves tonemapping into a post pass; otherwise it runs inside each material.
  - Docs: Compatibility glow "uses a different implementation with some properties being
    unavailable and hidden from the inspector"; bicubic upscale has no effect [primary:
    https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html].
- **Auto exposure** is "only available when using the Forward+ renderer" [primary, same
  page], so eye adaptation must be scripted.
- **`hint_screen_texture` / `hint_depth_texture` in spatial shaders** are supported
  [primary: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html].
  In the engine: after the opaque pass **and the sky**, one `glBlitFramebuffer` copies
  colour and/or depth+stencil, only if some material uses them; once per viewport per
  frame; **no mipmaps** (so `textureLod` blur does nothing in 3D). Such materials are
  forced into the **transparent** pass. Cost is one fullscreen blit per buffer
  [inference]. `hint_normal_roughness_texture` is Forward+ only.
- **Depth** [primary:
  https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/advanced_postprocessing.html]:
  Compatibility NDC z is [−1, 1], so `ndc = vec3(SCREEN_UV, depth)*2.0 - 1.0` (Forward+:
  `vec3(SCREEN_UV*2.0-1.0, depth)`); branch on `CURRENT_RENDERER ==
  RENDERER_COMPATIBILITY`; depth is "24-bit without reverse Z".
- **Fullscreen quad (4.3+)** [primary, same page]: `POSITION = vec4(VERTEX.xy, 1.0, 1.0);`
  with `render_mode unshaded, fog_disabled;`; parent it to the camera or set a huge
  `extra_cull_margin` to avoid CPU frustum culling. The page says nothing about
  `depth_test_disabled`, and no Compatibility bug for it was found. MSAA makes depth
  reads "bleed" at edges [secondary forum].
- **Sky shader** [primary docs]: `LIGHTX_SIZE` (angular diameter, rad), `LIGHTX_ENERGY`,
  `LIGHTX_DIRECTION`, `EYEDIR`, `AT_CUBEMAP_PASS` (skip disc and aureole maths there).
- **Canvas screen texture** (`rasterizer_canvas_gles3.cpp`): one back-buffer copy, with
  mipmaps generated when the material's screen-texture filter needs them. A CanvasLayer
  overlay sees the tonemapped LDR frame.

## 7. Recommendations for implementation

1. **Analytic eye veil, no convolution.** Per pixel `L_veil = Ψ(θ)·E_sun·V_sun`, with the
   CIE general equation (θ ≥ 0.1°, defaults A = 40, p = 0.5) and a 1D log-θ lookup of Ψ
   convolved with the limb-darkened disc inside ~1°. E_sun ≈ 100 klx at noon times
   atmospheric transmittance; V_sun from a CPU raycast or a few depth samples in the disc.
   Pilot view only; draw as a fullscreen additive pass (canvas ColorRect, or a
   camera-child spatial quad when depth occlusion is needed).
2. **Add the veil before tonemapping, with scripted eye adaptation.** Adapted luminance at
   the fixation `L_a = L_scene(centre) + L_veil(θ_fix)`, smoothed asymmetrically
   (FlightGear: 3.0/s dark→light, 1.0/s light→dark); exposure ∝ key(L_a)/L_a (Krawczyk).
   Do not use Godot glow for the veil (4 narrow levels, 4× headroom).
3. **Plane-visibility gate.** `C_eff = C·L_b/(L_b + L_veil)`, with C ≈ −0.5 to −0.9 for the
   shadowed silhouette; contrast factors with the KS91 sky are ×0.16 at 1°, ×0.25 at 3°,
   ×0.30 at 5°, ×0.44 at 10°, ×0.67 at 20°. Use it to fade colour and shading cues
   (silhouette mode), not to hide the model; hiding comes from item 4.
4. **Photostress after-image buffer (pilot view only).** Ritschel–Eisemann kinetics
   `ṙ = c_a·L·(1−r) − c_d·r` in a low-res, screen-fixed ping-pong SubViewport. Inject the
   sun disc image (58× over the after-image threshold) plus its ~1° halo. Mask contrast
   where r is high (acuity recovery about 5–30 s); draw a faint complementary
   (purple/blue-violet) negative image with a 1–5 min tail, after a ~1 s positive phase,
   blurring over time.
5. **Sun disc.** Power-law limb darkening, α_RGB ≈ (0.44, 0.50, 0.60) from
   α = −0.023 + 0.292/λ[µm]; diameter 0.533°; centre ≈ 1.84e9 cd/m² (mean 1.47e9) before
   extinction; ~1 px antialiased edge; vertical flattening by Bennett/Sæmundsson (0.84 at
   the horizon).
6. **Aureole in the sky shader.** `L_aur(ρ) = K·6.2e7/(ρ² + ρ0²)`, ρ in degrees, ρ0 ≈ 0.27°,
   K including (1 − 10^(−0.4kX)); ≈ 2.9e5 cd/m² at 1° for k = 0.17. Join the existing
   Rayleigh/Mie sky at ρ ≥ 10°. Keep it separate from the eye veil: the aureole exists
   in every view, the veil only in the eye view.
7. **Chase and orbit cameras.** No eye veil, no after-images. Lens flare from the CC0
   TranquilMarmot canvas_item shader (`blend_add`, no screen texture) with fractional
   depth-disc occlusion (Unity-style) and optional starburst; glow threshold near 1.0 on
   the 4× headroom.
8. **Options.** Cap-brim occluder (cut Ψ for sources more than ~30° above the gaze);
   sunglasses (scale scene and after-image dose by 0.2); age slider ((A/62.5)⁴ term); a
   "glare strength" setting (IL-2 shows over-large glare reads as fake).
9. **Evidence gates.** Unit-test Ψ(1°) = 15.9 and Ψ(10°) = 0.0755 (A = 40, p = 0.5), the
   Vos84 integral ≈ 1.0, the limb value I(μ = 0.5)/I(1) = 0.703 at 550 nm, and the
   horizon flattening ratio 0.84 ± 0.02.
