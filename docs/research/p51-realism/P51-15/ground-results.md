At every rate, all five cases completed with finite states, no crash and no simulation fault. Reset and the constructed three-contact ground fixture succeeded. The probe exercises `FlightSession` ticks, inputs, crash handling, reset and checkpoint paths. Ground distance sums horizontal movement across steps with contact at either endpoint. For landing, a separate gross touchdown-to-final along-runway displacement is derived from the existing end displacement and first touchdown: `along_runway_displacement − 30 − 14.5/tan(4°) − touchdown_along_runway`. The 30 m / 14.5 m / 4° terms are the frozen probe's initial approach geometry; this endpoint displacement includes the airborne rebound segment. Cross-track is measured against the threshold northing for the entire case, including flight; the takeoff values below are not runway-clearance bounds.

## Comparison bands and rate sensitivity

The P51-17 plan calls for 1% steady-state comparison, 5% smooth-transient comparison, and individual reporting of discrete or near-stall event changes. Candidate-minus-baseline changes are below 5% for takeoff liftoff time, location and speed, and for the initial landing time, location, speed, sink and derived gross touchdown-to-final displacement at both rates. Gross landing displacement changes 139.821→139.007 m (−0.58%) at 240 Hz and 138.845→141.173 m (+1.68%) at 480 Hz. Two contact-path aggregates exceed 5%: rudder-free takeoff distance increases 5.11% at 240 Hz and 5.20% at 480 Hz (51.515→54.149 m; 51.490→54.168 m); landing-case contact-only distance changes −2.17% at 240 Hz and +6.21% at 480 Hz (73.075→71.488 m; 71.611→76.056 m). These differences reflect the measured model outputs; they are not evidence that contact physics has been corrected.

Both parked cases' distance, speed and heading-drift comparisons stay within 1% between baseline and candidate at each rate. For rate sensitivity, takeoff liftoff time, location and speed vary by at most 0.31% between 240 and 480 Hz within each revision. The parked distances and speeds are also effectively unchanged across rates. First-touchdown timing is identical at both rates; touchdown distance, total speed and sink differ by at most 0.61% across rates. The derived gross touchdown-to-final displacement changes 139.821→138.845 m (−0.70%) in baseline and 139.007→141.173 m (+1.56%) in candidate, within 5%. The contact-only path metric does exceed that rate band in the candidate (71.488→76.056 m, +6.39%); the baseline changes 73.075→71.611 m (−2.00%). Because it counts only steps with contact at either endpoint, it omits much of the airborne rebound displacement. The 140 m value is net along-runway displacement from first contact to the final sample, not an arc-length integral. This identifies a limitation in the contact-only metric; it does not establish a change or fix in contact physics. Keep both measures visible and assess touchdown events separately.

Late landing final ground speed is below 0.01 m/s in all runs, so percentage comparisons of that near-zero residual are not useful steady-state acceptance measures. Final heading change is +3.061°→+2.085° at 240 Hz and +1.746°→+2.232° at 480 Hz; with the rebound recorded, this endpoint is reported as a trajectory difference in degrees rather than forced into the smooth-event percentage band. No physical band was widened to accommodate these outputs.

## Lifecycle and scope

All four reports have `numerics_ok`, `cases_completed` and `completed` true. Each reset returns at tick 0 with no crash or fault. Checkpoint replay matches exactly after 240 ticks with maximum state delta 0: 1.0 s at 240 Hz and 0.5 s at 480 Hz. The deliberate hull-contact crash is detected; the session restarts after exactly 360 ticks at 240 Hz or 720 ticks at 480 Hz, matching the 1.5 s hold, and the restart state matches exactly at tick 0 (maximum delta 0).

The P-51 production runway-start path still does not support this aircraft: the shared route is restricted to the Ugly Stik and requires gear stiction. This probe instead constructs and settles a three-point pose using the P-51 gear geometry. Contact count represents the configured spring-gear model; it does not measure tire or oleo behavior. These outputs are model predictions only, not physical validation, and do not prove player-selectable runway launch, real directional control, braking, or landing safety.
