## Findings

### C1 — An unreported raw surface axis can become a non-neutral control after throttle-only arming

**Status:** reproduced with injected readings through the production reader; device incidence and native hardware behavior unverified. **Priority:** investigate before claiming radio startup/focus behavior is qualified. **Scope:** raw radio and non-gamepad reader paths, including aircraft that consume them; not the verified standard-gamepad path.

[`app/input/rc_input.gd:235-262`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/rc_input.gd#L235-L262) records a fresh motion event for each axis, but only a fresh LOW throttle event establishes radio arming evidence. `:317-322` polls and stores all ten values without establishing whether an individual raw axis has ever reported, `:376-393` arms from the throttle evidence plus the current throttle poll, and `:448-458` emits roll, pitch and yaw without a corresponding `_seen` check. [`app/tests/test_rc_axis_history.gd:49-53`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/tests/test_rc_axis_history.gd#L49-L53) explicitly treats a LOW throttle event alone as sufficient to arm. Poll trust for a recognized, focused, generation-verified standard gamepad is a different path ([`app/input/controller_service.gd:191-202`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/controller_service.gd#L191-L202); [`app/input/rc_input.gd:329-353`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/rc_input.gd#L329-L353)).

Concrete injected sequence: a valid configured radio has roll min `-1`, center `0.2`, max `1` and a throttle on axis 2. Its actual roll stick is at its calibrated center `0.2`, but the raw backend reports startup `0.0` because that axis has not emitted an event. Axis 2 emits `-1` and the current poll agrees it is LOW. The radio arms although `_seen` contains no roll event. `ControllerProfile._normalize_centered`, [`app/input/controller_profile.gd:591-594`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/controller_profile.gd#L591-L594), turns the polled zero into `(0 - 0.2) / (0.2 - (-1)) = -1/6`; `sticks()` emits that roll. At reset or focus recovery this can introduce a control bias or conceal an actual held deflection until the driver reports it. A backend that always returns current trustworthy raw values is not affected by this particular mechanism. Existing repository research records an engine/raw-device zero-until-moved concern at `docs/research/menu-investigations/12-identidad-joypads-sdl3.md:16`; it is not proof that any particular present OS, device or package exhibits it.

**Recommendation:** explicitly define per-axis readiness/trust for raw devices on connection, profile change and focus/suspension recovery. One possible policy is to require new physical evidence for all mapped axes before authorizing their commands; a centered fallback alone cannot tell whether the unseen stick is actually held away. Preserve the existing trusted standard-gamepad path and ensure users can complete readiness with devices that do not emit stationary events. If a fallback is chosen, surface it clearly, fail safely, and document the usability tradeoff rather than silently treating an unverified zero as fresh. Do not assume stale history under an old mapping establishes fresh evidence for a new one.

**Discriminating test:** the supplied probe checked the values above: armed `true`, roll event seen `false`, and returned roll `-0.166666666666667`. Add a session-level regression covering initial selection, reconnect with a reused handle, focus loss/recovery, changed mapping, an unseen off-center calibration, an actually deflected stick, and verified standard gamepads. Exercise keyboard fallback and explicit Continue. Separately verify real radios and drivers; a synthetic event test cannot establish them.

### C2 — Repeated polling can turn one bad hardware report into a retained calibration endpoint

**Status:** reproduced with injected polls through the production calibration class; device incidence unmeasured. **Priority:** calibration robustness, conditional on a burst or transient that the backend keeps returning across polls.

[`app/input/rc_calibration.gd:36-40`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/rc_calibration.gd#L36-L40) requires three consecutive poll samples for endpoint support and five for stable rest. `:151-179` appends a reading for each `sample` call, sorts the recent poll values and permanently updates `_lo`/`_hi` when the three-poll window is stable. It does not know whether the three readings came from three independent hardware reports. `:221-253` accepts the resulting extrema after the specified sweep and center checks. This limitation is acknowledged in the source comment: sample counts do not guarantee a duration or independence.

Concrete sequence after the roll held-direction prompt: true usable raw endpoints are `-0.8` and `+0.8`, and true center is `0`. A single bad hardware report of `+1.0`, returned by the backend on three consecutive polls, is treated as a supported maximum and stored. The user then supplies `-0.8` and returns to `0`. The measured span is `1.8`, both sides of center exceed the 20% requirement, and the stored roll channel becomes `min=-0.8, center=0, max=1.0`. True `+0.8` now maps to `+0.8`, a 20% loss of commanded positive full travel; a clean paired sequence maps it to `+1.0`. Repeated calls at 240 Hz, including several catch-up callbacks after one input report, need not represent distinct device reports.

**Recommendation:** investigate bounded-duration and report-freshness handling or another robust estimator at the calibration owner. Do not presume a device timestamp exists or equate a fixed number of polls with independent device reports. Rejecting all isolated extrema blindly may harm low-report-rate devices; retain explicit endpoint/center checks and verify realistic update rates, deliberate holds, jitter and bias. No arbitrary replacement duration or estimator is justified by this source review.

**Discriminating test:** the probe passed paired clean and injected sequences through the actual calibration class: endpoint `0.8` produced command `1.0` in the clean run, while endpoint `1.0` produced command `0.8` after the injected repeated report. Add a fixture that models device report cadence separately from physics poll cadence, including catch-up polls, endpoint holds, legitimate reduced range, a noisy center, swapped polarity and a transient burst; then repeat with physical transmitter logs on native OS packages.

### Timing hypotheses checked and not established

The project requests 240 ticks/s and at most 12 physics steps per rendered frame ([`app/project.godot:46-49`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/project.godot#L46-L49)), accepting slow motion under overload. [`app/main.gd:1458,1487-1494`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/main.gd#L1458) advances propeller phase using the process callback's `delta` and the latest accepted RPM. **Do not equate that `delta` with measured wall-frame time.** Godot's [MainLoop documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_mainloop.html#class-mainloop-private-method-process) says process delta is capped by the product of time scale and maximum physics steps divided by the physics tick rate; at default scale and the project settings this is 0.05 s. The earlier supposition that a 10 FPS wall interval necessarily delivers `delta = 0.1` was therefore wrong. At a stable 10 FPS, 12 accepted ticks and the documented process-delta cap both correspond to 0.05 simulated seconds. A mismatch may still exist under a different configuration, an irregular timeline, changing RPM or a special test route, but this review does not establish a defect. Record actual accepted ticks, process delta, wall timestamps and prop phase on the pinned engine before proposing a change.

Similarly, [`app/audio/flight_audio.gd:271-280,307-315`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/audio/flight_audio.gd#L271-L280) supplies accepted source velocity, while [`app/audio/soundscape.gd:392-406`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/audio/soundscape.gd#L392-L406) uses it only when `0 < delta < 0.2`; an injected `delta >= 0.2` makes both velocity inputs zero and the calculation at [`app/audio/soundscape_model.gd:5-9`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/audio/soundscape_model.gd#L5-L9) returns unity. With the documented default process cap, a normal overload alone does not establish that such a callback is reachable. Treat it as a conditional API behavior requiring a legitimate caller or changed-engine configuration, not a demonstrated 5 FPS audio defect. Neither rendered timing nor audio perception was measured here.

## Good-enemy assessment: variants, coefficients and prior audits

Do not delete either stock Stik merely because two entries share a visual airframe or the defaults are imperfect. The basic aircraft is the existing prescribed-fuel/prescribed-RPM reference, while the electric 6S is an explicitly experimental conversion with its own inventory and finite-pack normal flight ([`app/app_state/aircraft_catalog.gd:23-33,68-78`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/app_state/aircraft_catalog.gd#L23-L33); `docs/UGLY-STIK-PLAN.md:13-24`). Three additional finite-glow IDs are intentionally hidden from new stock selection but retain technical and saved-workflow compatibility ([`app/app_state/aircraft_catalog.gd:34-67`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/app_state/aircraft_catalog.gd#L34-L67); `docs/UGLY-STIK-PLAN.md:24`). A simpler, reversible response to an uncertain configuration is accurate labeling or an explicit experiment, not removal of IDs, data, saved selections or comparison baselines. Product removal would require its own user decision and migration impact assessment.

Nor does poor subjective feel identify a correct substitute for a coefficient. The Stik's control throws, servo time and expo are estimated or borrowed from a different aircraft ([`app/data/aircraft/jensen_ugly_stik_60.json:1027-1090`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/data/aircraft/jensen_ugly_stik_60.json#L1027-L1090)); geometry alone cannot validate response. First establish the actual installation, device calibration, real and simulated rate/expo/trim, CG/mass/inertia, motor/prop, wind and matched maneuvers. Preserve baseline `.orfl`, check complete exact replay, change one candidate and replay the same tape (`docs/FLIGHT-LOGGING.md:266-288`). Compare separately against independent measured flights and a blinded/structured pilot judgment. These measures distinguish a control-path problem from a physical-model deficiency; software consistency and more detailed propulsion do not independently prove realism. The documented accepted elevator-moment discrepancy is an explicit software tolerance, not proof of the true coefficient (`docs/UGLY-STIK-PLAN.md:53-57`).

Prior external controller reports are historical leads, not current findings by default. Their quad CTRL-01 described decoding throttle before radio readiness; current [`app/input/multirotor_input.gd:283-311`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/multirotor_input.gd#L283-L311) gates all decoded inputs on `radio.armed`, and [`app/vehicles/multirotor/multirotor_scene.gd:232-236`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/vehicles/multirotor/multirotor_scene.gd#L232-L236) calls a focus invalidation path ([`app/input/multirotor_input.gd:243-253`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/multirotor_input.gd#L243-L253)). Historical airship CTRL-04/05 described paused sampling and ownership gaps; current [`app/vehicles/airship/airship_scene.gd:321-362,527-547,619-630`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/vehicles/airship/airship_scene.gd#L321-L362) routes managed selection, trusted gamepad confirmation and paused polling, and [`app/input/airship_input.gd:83-111`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/airship_input.gd#L83-L111) preserves physical controls for the resume gate. These are static rechecks of the previously described branches, not a new full validation of quad or airship. The shared raw surface-axis issue C1 can still affect consumers of the common reader and should be checked at each activity seam.

## Performance opportunities — UNMEASURED

1. **Input polling:** [`app/input/rc_input.gd:317-322,339-344`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f/app/input/rc_input.gd#L317-L322) calls the axis reader ten times and, when a button reader is bound, the button reader 128 times per physics callback: 138 calls/tick, or 33,120 callable invocations per second at 240 callbacks/s, before AUX-specific reads.
