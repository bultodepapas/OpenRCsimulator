# R17 — Ground contact, terrain and landing statistics

**Decision: prototype/measure first.** Treat physical contact, aircraft geometry and the statistics classification as separate qualification layers. A reviewed statistics row can be a small software improvement, but its successful classification does not validate a real landing.

## Current implementation

`GroundWheels.frame` transforms each authored wheel contact to world coordinates, samples local terrain and normal, calculates compression/normal velocity and applies a nonnegative spring-damper load (`app/physics/ground_wheels.gd:36–88`). Rolling/side forces, relative wheel speed and terrain factors have their own laws; `GroundContact.loads` also retains a legacy path (`app/physics/ground_contact.gd:1–16,80–171`). `GroundTerrain` samples the field and optional lake/track composition. Ground effect is a separate aerodynamic calculation; per-contact terrain sampling does not itself validate the aerodynamic height reference or a flare (`app/physics/ground_effect.gd`; [physics audit](../../physics-and-aerodynamics.md)). Preserve the current selected ground path and its declared domain when comparing.

`FixedWingAdapter.sample` is an observer, deriving physical support from actual gear compression and other accepted contact facts; `Profiles.resolve` separately requires a reviewed aircraft identity, geometry and role map to set `landing_available` (`app/statistics/fixed_wing_adapter.gd:61–133`; `app/statistics/profiles.gd:149–205`). The detector uses time windows and control/stop conditions, with unsupported or interrupted episodes potentially **unknown**, not automatically failed (`app/statistics/detector.gd:317–406`; `app/statistics/rules.gd:9–26`). The reducer keeps unknown counts out of the success-plus-failure landing-rate denominator (`app/statistics/reducer.gd:201–235`).

The [Electric 6S integration follow-up](../../follow-up-01/electric-integration-plan.md) derives dedicated main and nose contact roles and points for both ideal and finite-pack sources. Its pinned-engine probe reported 30 checks, zero failures, no checked diagnostics, including loader/contact-map validation and **synthetic** detector observations. It did not install the row, independently certify role meanings, exercise real adapter clearance, or qualify UI, actual landing or hardware. The basic-Stik statistics row has different CG-relative positions and fails the existing geometry tolerance; do not alias it or relax the validator just to gain coverage.

## Applicable outside evidence

The [JSBSim reference manual](https://jsbsim-team.github.io/jsbsim-reference-manual/user/concepts/frames-of-reference/) distinguishes structural-frame contact coordinates from CG-relative forward/right/down body coordinates (living manual, IN-S18). Its [ground-reaction diagram](https://jsbsim-team.github.io/jsbsim-reference-manual/formulation/equations-of-motion/) shows individual wheels can differ in weight-on-wheels (IN-S19). These are comparison conventions, not interchangeable coordinates or an oracle for this simulator. A [FlightGear developer discussion](https://forum.flightgear.org/viewtopic.php?f=49&t=21803) (January 2014, IN-S20) recommends animating visible gear compression rather than moving the physical contact point merely for appearance; it warns that contact location affects handling. It is a useful implementation experience, not evidence of this model's geometry.

Daniels' [NASA landing-gear study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19960049759) (1996, IN-S21) compares a nonlinear A-6 main-gear model with static/dynamic drop-carriage and shaker data, including friction and tire behavior. [Fasanella et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19780003109.pdf) (1977, IN-S22) use distinct static and level/inclined drop tests to study a light-aircraft gear. These establish measurement approaches and possible nonlinearities; neither provides an RC-Stik gear coefficient or its landing-performance target.

## Integration and acceptance

For the **statistics-only** candidate, independently review the main/nose roles and exact accepted datum-to-body points for both source hashes. Retain fail-closed checks for altered/missing/duplicate contacts, span, identity or bindings. Exercise actual adapter samples on both sides of the geometric clearance threshold; then test sustained takeoff, stable touchdown, full stop, touch-and-go, bounce/rearm, off-axis or gear-up incident, terminal crash, and unreviewed geometry. Verify lifecycle, saved/reloaded history, counters, rates and unknowns. Preserve historical unknowns; present coverage without retrospectively awarding successes.

For **physical behavior**, measure installed mass/CG, gear geometry and static stance; characterize force versus compression and repeated release/drop responses within an appropriate controlled test, then compare withheld sink speeds, ground speed, crosswind/steering and surface conditions. Record contact timing, stroke, wheel speed if available, vertical/side acceleration and uncertainty. Independently test terrain slope/roughness and near-ground flare with controlled airspeed, attitude and power; refine time step and check force/energy/continuity over contact transitions. Do not call a detector regression or a matching render a physical gear validation. Conversely, missing statistics coverage does not prove faulty contact physics.

**Disposition: prototype/measure first.** A dedicated Electric row is mechanically prepared and worth an independent adapter/lifecycle implementation, while physical coefficients and flare changes require separate measurements.
