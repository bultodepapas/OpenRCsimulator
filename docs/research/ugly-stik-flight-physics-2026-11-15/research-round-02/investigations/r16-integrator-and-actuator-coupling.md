# R16 — Integrator stages, actuators, wake and propulsion

**Decision: retain the established hybrid tick contract; prototype an explicitly versioned coupled alternative only if convergence and target-hardware measurements justify it.** No source here establishes a present numerical defect or a measurable feel improvement.

## The implemented hybrid is not one uniform approximation

`app/sim/vehicle_model.gd:1–9,38–52` distinguishes sampled auxiliary state, held through the RK stages, from continuous state carried with the rigid body. `app/sim/simulation.gd:403–490` obtains a pre-tick/sample result, then stage loads and optional coupled derivatives; `:491–522` reuses the first derivative under the documented pure-evaluation condition and dispatches RK4, events, substeps or a model-owned policy. `app/physics/integrator.gd:19–42` evaluates stages at their proper times and normalizes attitude after a step. Changing a force evaluator or its input context can invalidate first-stage reuse.

In the fixed-wing implementation, `FlightSession._pre_step` advances the sampled servos and other auxiliaries once per tick (`app/sim/flight_session.gd:5368–5455`). Its sampled tail-downwash update uses a first-order exact exponential for the **frozen target and speed during that sampled update**, then the result is held across stages (`:5437–5455`); it is not an exact solution of the fully coupled changing-flow problem. In contrast, `_wash_derivative` evaluates a declared continuous wash at stage airspeed/state while using sampled RPM/servos (`:4611–4627`), and other explicitly configured paths include coupled shaft, battery and fuel blocks. Therefore “all propulsion and wake are frozen” and “all aircraft components are fourth-order coupled” are both incorrect generalizations. Event location, accepted endpoint transitions, collision substeps and rollback also belong to the existing shared model contract.

## Numerical counterexample and literature

Consider only the scalar illustrative system `y′=u`, where a rate-limited actuator ramps linearly from `u=0` to `u=rh` during a tick of length `h`. Integrating the actual ramp gives `Δy=rh²/2`; advancing the actuator to its endpoint first and holding that endpoint over the whole step gives `Δy=rh²`. This shows how an otherwise fourth-order body integrator can inherit lower-order splitting error in a changing input. It does **not** quantify an aircraft force error, establish an implementation bug, or mean all inputs should be stage-interpolated: a sampled zero-order hold may be the intended discrete model.

[SciPy 1.17 `solve_ivp` (REV-S11)](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.17.0/reference/generated/scipy.integrate.solve_ivp.html) distinguishes explicit and stiff solvers and warns that event detection based on step sign changes can miss multiple crossings. An adaptive solver can provide an **offline** independent convergence reference with explicitly matched continuous equations and events; default tolerances or adaptive timing do not reproduce the pinned interactive law. An original [JSBSim maintainer discussion on propagation (REV-S12)](https://github.com/JSBSim-Team/jsbsim/discussions/1391) illustrates why execution order and state ownership matter, but its implementation and engineering discussion do not validate OpenRC or prescribe a replacement.

## Alternatives and acceptance

First compare the current policy with (a) smaller outer timesteps, (b) the existing declared substeps where applicable, and (c) an offline continuous-actuator reference under identical command, limits and initial state. Smaller **internal** RK pieces alone cannot correct a servo that is intentionally updated only once at the outer tick; refine actuator sampling as a separately versioned choice. A stage-coupled candidate must declare new continuous layout/version and checkpoint/replay identity, integrate rate limits and discontinuities deliberately, and keep stage evaluations pure. A closed-form servo path within the tick can be cheaper than another ODE state where the target and rate are truly constant, but mixed commands, jams, saturation and actuator events must be specified.

Test analytic ramp/step cases, a steady trim, simultaneous surface and throttle steps, reversed controls, stall-entry smoothness, electric stop/restart, wake and fuel boundaries, wheel contact/substeps and invalid-state rollback. Compare 120/240/480/960 Hz under fixed *physical* input timing in a fresh, correctly initialized session at each rate; the current kernel rejects a timestep change during an established run. Report observed convergence for each state and event rather than assuming order four across switches. Preserve float64, `VehicleModel` separation, the old exact-replay path and golden flights unless an intentional new law is approved. Measure CPU/frame impact and human/controller response on real supported hardware, first at 1080p. Existing replay/parity does not establish physical accuracy, and no performance saving has been measured.
