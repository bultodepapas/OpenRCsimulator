# R24 — Experiments that justify an optimization

**Decision: use existing checked capture and replay infrastructure; propose a matched, repeated measurement campaign before optimizing.** All opportunities below are **unmeasured** for this research round. A faster isolated calculation is not automatically a faster, equally correct, rendered flight.

## Existing assets and unqualified boundaries

`tools/godot/README.md` documents checked engine runs, resource streams, frame streams and Perfetto export. `tools/godtest/README.md:88–132` describes optional session instrumentation and explicitly says that fixed-input-tape rendered replay, repeated paired statistics and automatic promotion are still pending. Its sampled trajectory is at most 10 Hz; it cannot establish tick-by-tick equality, physical input latency or actual monitor presentation. [`docs/FLIGHT-LOGGING.md`](../../../../FLIGHT-LOGGING.md) defines complete exact replay separately from candidate/changed-solver and recovered-prefix outputs. Historical speed ratios and native/macOS measurements in `docs/APPLE-SILICON-TECHNICAL-BACKGROUND.md` belong to their recorded host and scenarios; they cannot be promoted to a new machine, a new solver, GPU performance or current FPS.

At baseline `08f0c08348395c0d6d9f7e224f66460828f59a7f`, candidate hotspots include input polling and the many stage/validation operations in `app/sim/simulation.gd:403–522`; these are hypotheses only. Some work is deliberate validation, transactional rollback or shared-kernel semantics. Removing it without a measured bottleneck and equivalent failure tests is not an optimization recommendation.

## External methods and their scope

[Godot 4.5 Profiler documentation (REV-S25)](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.5/tutorials/scripting/debug/the_profiler.html) describes profiling by function/engine category. It is useful for attribution in a diagnostic build, but profiler instrumentation and the 4.5 documentation do not establish production timing for pinned Godot 4.8 beta 2. [pyperf's system guidance (REV-S26)](https://pyperf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/system.html) describes CPU affinity and environmental variation; system tuning changes the environment, so record settings and compare normal supported configurations too. It does not measure rendering or flight input.

[Google Benchmark's random-interleaving guide (REV-S27)](https://github.com/google/benchmark/blob/main/docs/random_interleaving.md) and the [original maintainer issue (REV-S28)](https://github.com/google/benchmark/issues/1051) discuss interleaving to reduce run-to-run variation and note its tradeoff with within-run variation. Their microbenchmark examples are not a promise of improvement in OpenRC. Pair or balance baseline/candidate order, retain raw runs and record whether cache, temperature, load and instrumentation differ. Decide sample size, practical effect threshold and uncertainty method *before* observing the outcome; do not claim significance from one pair or discard unfavorable runs without a recorded reason.

## Concrete experiment ladder

1. **Correctness first.** Freeze the baseline, exact aircraft/configuration and representative input/transition tape; verify complete same-identity exact replay, finite-state/invariant checks, native/reference admission and relevant golden flights. For a changed-law candidate, retain the original and label it as candidate rather than exact replay. This isolates numerical behavior from timing.
2. **Component attribution.** On an isolated supported host, profile the suspected input, stage, observer or allocation path with the same model and workload. Collect enough repetitions to separate timer resolution, warmup, GC/allocation and observer cost. A cache idea must prove its key includes state, time, weather, inputs, backend and invalidation boundaries; otherwise it may return a fast but wrong answer. Microbenchmarks test a component, not a player's frame rate.
3. **Whole-flight test.** Use a real release-like or explicitly labeled debug build on named hardware. Match backend, aircraft, weather, scene, camera, resolution, graphics preset, warmup, duration, power/thermal state and input tape where the harness supports it. Record requested **and actual 1920×1080 viewport**, renderer/driver, presentation configuration and refresh rate; 1080p is the primary target, higher resolutions secondary, 720p a fallback. A headless run is not a GPU/display qualification. Existing adaptive-pilot paths are not a substitute for matched fixed-tape rendered replay.
4. **Analyze and report.** Retain all per-run observations and completion/coverage receipts; report paired per-run median and relevant tail frame/tick distributions with uncertainty, not a pooled mean alone. Separate startup, active flight, pauses and loading. CPU time, GPU time, OS samples and presented frame intervals may overlap and have different clocks; do not add them as if they were a serial critical path. Compare profiler/recorder on versus off to bound observation overhead and disclose missing sampling channels. On long runs inspect thermal drift, frequency, memory and storage; failure, partial and unsupported runs remain visible.

## Acceptance

Predeclare the practical performance goal and evidence needed for a change; there is no invented universal improvement percentage here. Require correctness/replay and fail-closed boundary checks for the actual patch, completed matched observations with source/engine/backend identities, balanced repeated trials on the named supported target, unchanged or explicitly reviewed visible quality and a retained no-observer control. Qualify each resolution/platform separately. Real radio/pilot feel and physical flight validation require additional independent measurements. Source review alone establishes neither speedup nor regression.
