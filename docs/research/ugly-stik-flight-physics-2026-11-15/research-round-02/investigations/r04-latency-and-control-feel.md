# R04 — Latency, control shaping and actuator response

**Decision: prototype/measure first.** Compare the same physical or normalized command at named boundaries before changing the simulator's tick, input curves or servo model. Measure end-to-end latency separately from simulator-internal timing and from airframe response.

## Current command chain

`FlightSession._physics_process` polls `RcInput`, obtains `sticks()`, shapes through `flown_commands`, adds trims and publishes `_inputs()` before the kernel tick (`app/sim/flight_session.gd:3059–3112,4133–4146,4204–4224,4262–4265`). `ControllerProfile.condition_axis` applies a rescaled deadzone and cubic expo to **gamepads**; calibrated radio values pass linearly through that stage. Radio profiles disable the aircraft rate profile because transmitter shaping is already upstream. A calibrated non-radio joystick can use aircraft rates/expo; a gamepad receives only aircraft travel scaling when that profile is active, with no second expo (`app/input/controller_profile.gd:579–660`; `app/input/commands.gd:58–82`). Some gamepad yaw paths also have a declared response time. Virtual rate throttle and absolute radio/trigger throttle have different semantics. Any control-feel proposal must preserve these distinctions.

The actual servo is advanced once per tick by the bounded `ControlActuators.rate_limit`, and its accepted output is held through force stages (`app/physics/control_actuators.gd:17–23`; `app/sim/flight_session.gd:5426–5441`). The Stik's 0.14 s neutral-to-full assumption is not measured servo behavior; a full negative-to-positive reversal is a longer travel under the same rate. `LatencyPatch._physics_process` reads the flight reader's raw axis after its poll and shows a threshold marker. It is explicitly a camera marker, not a millisecond measurement; `has_axis_sample` reflects retained event history for radios or a verified gamepad poll and does not by itself certify that the displayed poll is fresh (`app/input/latency_patch.gd:1,147–174`).

## External measurement guidance

Godot's [input-lag guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/rendering/jitter_stutter.html) suggests filming both input and display with a high-speed camera and recording refresh and device rates; it also notes tradeoffs of input accumulation and physics/render cadence (latest, unstable; IN-S15). [NVIDIA's LDAT description](https://developer.nvidia.com/nvidia-latency-display-analysis-tool) gives a luminance-sensor example for input-to-photon timing (IN-S16). Neither establishes a latency for this application, an RC axis, or every display. Microsoft's [Dynamic Latency Input documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/gdk/docs/features/common/input/advanced/input-synchronization?view=gdk-2604) explains report/read synchronization on supported Xbox hardware and explicitly says it is unavailable on PC (updated March 2026; IN-S17). It is a timing analogy, not an API to assume in Godot.

## Experiment and alternatives

Record repeated known stick steps, reversals and small center crossings at the same calibrated *post-shaping* target. Capture external stick threshold and on-screen marker with a high-speed camera in one frame, or synchronize a physical trigger and luminance sensor; document frame exposure/rolling shutter, scanout position, refresh, sampling resolution and confidence interval. Record the marker's precise axis and threshold, because its response includes raw input through rendering but **not** the slower servo or resulting aircraft motion. Separately log event receipt, poll, accepted command tick, actual servo, attitude/angular rate, render frame and estimated display presentation, retaining clock domain/offsets and event drops. A marker driven by raw axis is not interchangeable with surface motion; visual aircraft movement includes aerodynamics, camera and perspective.

Use baseline and one candidate on the same controller, firmware, USB/Bluetooth path, display, 1920×1080 scene, renderer and workload; randomize step timing to sample phase. Record distribution, sample count, p50/tail and measurement uncertainty, plus frame/tick hitches and dropped frames. Compare input-to-marker, command-to-servo and command-to-flight response separately. For numerical comparisons preserve an unchanged `.orfl`, verify exact replay and compare the same tick/input tape as required in `docs/FLIGHT-LOGGING.md`; exact replay does not measure hardware-to-photon latency.

Counterexamples matter: lowering a deadzone can look faster at small movement but amplify drift; increasing servo speed can hide display delay and incorrectly alter real actuator dynamics. A higher tick rate can change CPU load, replay identity and sampling phase. Transmitter expo and simulator expo can compound even if the latter is individually reasonable. Do not rewrite a curve, increase tick rate, add buffering or change estimated servo time until the relevant component has been measured and the effect on endpoint, reversals, overshoot and feel is checked. Pilot A/B tests should document or mask ordering and retain individual observations; preference is not a measured aerodynamic coefficient.

**Disposition: prototype/measure first.** The existing camera patch and replay tools save new infrastructure work; qualify the physical measurement chain and component boundaries before implementing latency or shaping changes.
