# R18 — System identification and independent validation

**Decision: prototype/measure first.** Separate command-chain, mass/CG, actuator and propulsion uncertainty before fitting aerodynamic parameters; reserve independent flights or conditions for validation. A close match to the same simulator-generated or fitted data is not physical validation.

## Available project instruments

`research/flight-test/flight_test.gd` runs scripted/closed-loop cases through the session, records trace metadata and normally exports every twelfth 240 Hz tick (20 Hz), with a full-trace option for the ordinary trace (`:1–12,38,224–311,375–395`). The separate aerodynamic diagnostic rows remain sampled every twelfth tick even with full traces (`:293–310`). `app/sim/flight_session.gd` publishes calibrated/rate-shaped inputs and sampled servo positions, while `app/sim/trace.gd` declares trace columns and timing. `docs/FLIGHT-LOGGING.md` describes `.orfl` exact replay, completion and source/solver identity checks; debrief distinguishes endpoint state samples from commands/servos/loads for the preceding interval. Replay is an excellent control for software changes, but matching a simulator's own outputs does not measure the real Jensen. Twenty-Hz export can miss short input/servo transients; select full-rate data and assess sensor bandwidth, filtering and aliasing for each experiment.

The existing [primary-source program](../../research-sources-and-validation.md) recommends measured installation, input chain and held-out maneuvers. The [prior controls findings](../../findings-register.md) identify estimated throw/servo timing and estimated mass/CG/inertia; the later [modal diagnosis](../../follow-up-01/mode-and-trim-diagnosis.md) also shows that a projected, frozen-control/fixed-RPM linearization can miss coupled behavior. Do not fit flight dynamics to the latter report's labels as if they were independent full-session observations.

## External work and applicability

Morelli's [F-18 optimal-input flight test](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040105626) compared designed and conventional excitation and discusses distortion by the feedback system (1997, IN-S23). The result supports measuring actual control-surface inputs and designing informative maneuvers, not copying F-18 input amplitudes. Morelli's [subscale flight study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110014634) used designed multisine control-surface excitation on a jet transport near reference conditions (2011, IN-S24). It is stronger than a simulator-only example, but its vehicle and test setup are not the Jensen and sophisticated combined inputs may be inappropriate for an initial RC campaign.

Grauer and Morelli's [NASA measurement-sensitivity study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/20140003885.pdf) perturbed sensor noise, resolution/bias, mass properties and geometry in identification analyses, with nonlinear F-16 simulation among its checks (2013, IN-S25). Its particular accuracy recommendations do not automatically transfer to a small RC aircraft. The independent lesson is to propagate stated uncertainty and reject non-identifiable parameter combinations, not to tune against an unmeasured CG or servo lag.

## An identifiable sequence

1. **Freeze the object and measurement chain.** Identify exact physical build, propulsion/pack, linkage, transmitter rates/trims, firmware and atmospheric/wind conditions; weigh and determine CG/inertia with repeated measurements and bounds. Record actual surface angle rather than inferring it solely from transmitter stick. Calibrate sensor time bases, axes, sign, sampling/filtering, missing data and uncertainties; document synchronization to an external event. Compare simulator and physical signals in the same units/frame.
2. **Measure components before fitting flight coefficients.** Capture endpoint/center/asymmetry and step/reversal behavior of the actual controller, loaded servo/linkage and propulsion. Estimate delays and saturation independently, with repeatability and constraints. Do not use the same motor chart used to fit a model as its independent validation. Fix, bound or jointly estimate poorly observed nuisance parameters with uncertainty; a single aircraft response cannot uniquely distinguish slow actuator from weak or strongly damped aerodynamics.
3. **Design informative, bounded maneuvers.** At multiple independently measured trim speeds and power levels, use small positive and negative pulse/doublet/reversal inputs for each axis where practical; record cross-axis response, surface movement, airspeed, body rates and attitude. Use sensitivity/correlation or rank/conditioning analysis on the measured candidate inputs to identify which parameter combinations can actually be estimated. If controls or frequencies are collinear, parameters remain unidentifiable even when an optimizer converges. Expand to jointly informative inputs only with appropriate range/pilot safeguards and adequate bandwidth; design separate slow/high-angle and ground experiments.
4. **Predeclare training and holdout.** Split by entire flight, maneuver block or condition, not adjacent samples of the same run. Fit one specified model/parameter family using only training data; freeze choices before evaluating independent runs, speeds, directions, power and, when appropriate, a configuration shift. Report signs, peak, lag, steady response, damping and complete time-history residuals, confidence/uncertainty and failed cases. Establish acceptance bands from instrument error and repeatability before tuning. Sensitivity to wind, airspeed, CG, actuator lag and timing must be explicit; avoid compensating one unknown with another.
5. **Keep gates separate.** Verify schema/units/signs, convergence and exact replay first; compare component predictions to held-out component measurements next; compare aircraft trajectories and coupled modes to held-out flight data last. A later blind/counterbalanced pilot evaluation assesses feel in documented conditions and does not replace measured physical validation.

A simpler parameter sweep may be more honest than point estimates when available sensors cannot resolve correlated effects. Repeat conditions and retain non-matching cases; report domain limits rather than generalizing from a single good trajectory.

**Disposition: prototype/measure first.** Existing replay, card and trace tools provide the software scaffold. Independent sensor, actuator, propulsion and mass-property evidence is needed before fitting, and withheld real flights are needed before claiming validation.
