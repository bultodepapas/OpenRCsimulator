# R09 — Glow installation and operating envelope

**Decision: prototype/measure first; defer coefficient and limit changes.** Manufacturer operating guidance and a simulator's numerical table support are distinct. Reviewed 2026-11-15 at baseline `08f0c08348395c0d6d9f7e224f66460828f59a7f`; no engine or flight tests were run.

## Repository findings

The basic Jensen route prescribes `2800 + 8349 × throttle` RPM with first-order lag in `propulsion.gd`, so it does not unload by shaft balance. Technical 61FX/FS-64V routes use `glow/source.gd`, `two_stroke.gd` or `four_stroke.gd`, fuel and annular propeller coupling. `glow/profile.gd` requires every RPM table to cover the declared `operation.max_rpm`, and `source.gd::evaluate()` rejects evaluations above it; `glow/flight.gd` exposes `above_recommended_rpm` using the lesser of recommended engine and propeller values. An out-of-domain error, an advisory threshold, a measured engine/propeller rating and a modeled physical failure are different events. The candidate 61FX profile's 32,000-rpm maximum is explicitly estimated numerical support, not a manufacturer rating or proof of safe/fitted performance there.

The 61FX technical installation's estimated annular blade chord is transferred from a different engine/prop combination. Its source predicts static RPM; neither that prediction nor the basic model's nominal prop thrust is a measured installed torque map. The FS-64V 14×6 installation's propeller maximum 11,785.7 rpm is a derived generic plastic-prop estimate, explicitly provisional for its composite K-series material, not verified for the exact SKU. The 13×6 APC variant uses a different propeller and rating assumption. These installations must not be mixed.

## Manufacturer evidence and limitations

The [O.S. 61FX manual](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/english/line_up/engine/air/aircraft/manual/50sx_40-91fx.pdf) [PHY-S12] lists practical 2,000–17,000 rpm and 1.9 PS at 16,000 rpm; its printed horsepower equivalent is inconsistent with 1.9 PS, so use the PS quantity and identify the source rather than averaging conflicting conversions. The [FS-64V catalog](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/english/line_up/engine/air/single/catalog/3AY00.html) [PHY-S13] lists practical 2,400–11,000 rpm, 1.14 PS at 11,000 rpm and suggested 12–14-inch combinations. [O.S. propeller guidance](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/english/line_up/propelle/prop.htm) [PHY-S14] says same nominal dimensions differ by manufacturer and selection depends on engine and use. [APC's RPM limit page](https://www.apcprop.com/technical-information/rpm-limits/) [PHY-S15] gives type-specific criteria; use it only for the actually identified APC type, not a different brand or material. None of these sources gives a full installed torque-vs-RPM/throttle/mixture curve or establishes an immediate hard stop at the practical range boundary.

## Integration proposal and acceptance

Preserve physical provenance for the exact engine, exhaust, fuel, propeller, temperature and needle settings. Measure static thrust, RPM and, where feasible, shaft torque at multiple stabilized settings; collect synchronized throttle/RPM and airspeed under controlled changes to identify unloading, repeatability and margins. Keep practical engine range, propeller certified/appropriate limit, calibration domain, numerical solver support and actual damage model as separate explicit fields and observations. Do not enforce a magic clamp or silently extrapolate an estimated 32,000-rpm map as validated. If a trajectory exits numerical support, surface the failure or a separately identified bounded continuation; a physical overspeed event requires a justified model.

Test idle/start, full throttle, acceleration/deceleration, increasing advance ratio, limit crossings and out-of-domain reporting with timestep refinement and energy-consistent shaft torque. Preserve same-input comparison and current identities for unchanged routes. Compare observed stationary and in-flight RPM plus fuel/temperature over held-out settings before changing authoring sources through the owning generators. No manufacturer figure above substitutes for target-engine measurements.
