# S2-02 — Acceptance contracts for signed propulsion and induced flow

**Disposition: prototype/measure first.** These are proposed synthetic, model-specific checks for a future opt-in shared-kernel prototype, not measured Stik targets. Source review uses runtime baseline `08f0c08348395c0d6d9f7e224f66460828f59a7f`, 2026-11-15. The small arithmetic calculation below was independently evaluated with Python fractions and floating point; no simulator, hardware, flight or performance run was made.

## Conventions and existing seams

Let `U` be air-relative axial velocity along the thrust axis, `Ω` rotor speed in rad/s relative to a **fixed** carrier, `T` aerodynamic axial force on the aircraft/propeller in the `+U` direction, and `Q_air` aerodynamic torque on the rotor in the positive spin direction. Existing `Propulsion.prop_torque()` and `GlowProp.evaluate()` instead report **absorbed** torque `Q_abs = −Q_air`; negative absorbed torque can drive a windmill. Convert `rpm` with `Ω = rpm·2π/60`, never confuse it with revolutions/s.

`propulsion.gd::prop_torque()/thrust_torque()/loads()` zero the non-glow propeller below 1 rpm; the current Stik electric Cp data cannot drive a stopped shaft. `glow/propeller.gd::evaluate()` is a different estimated annulus law and already reports `shaft_power_w`, `thrust_power_w` and `dissipation_w`. `electric_motor.gd::evaluate()` is one-quadrant and clamps its **isolated** rest loss. Crucially, `flight_session.gd::_coupled_response()` calculates a propeller load first, but its electric exact-rest friction branch recomputes the hold/breakaway decision from motor drive and body acceleration without a direct aerodynamic shaft torque; today's zero electric torque at rest masks that seam. `aircraft_ground_source.gd::_split()` separately exposes shaft friction capacity for its ground-contact route. A new map alone is not sufficient. `electric_stop.gd`/`event_step.gd` localize unilateral stops; negative trial RPMs are not accepted reverse operation.

`aircraft_data.gd::effective_angle_map()` computes `E=(I+a₀K)⁻¹` for its uniform reference condition. `aero.gd::_wing_flow()` normalizes source section-plane speeds by the largest **3D** station speed; `_local_loads()` applies the resulting weights to off-diagonal terms. This isolates the comparison below; it is not a full 3D aerodynamic law.

## A. Signed shaft, passivity and stop/restart

1. **Independent algebra fixture.** For a clamped carrier use the deliberately invented, passive law `T=−0.2U+0.01Ω`, `Q_air=0.01U−0.002Ω` in consistent SI units. Its dissipation matrix has determinant `0.0003>0`; consequently `T·U+Q_air·Ω≤0`. It is **not** a recommended Stik map. Evaluate:

   | U (m/s) | Ω (rad/s) | T (N) | Q_air (N·m) | T·U + Q_air·Ω (W) |
   | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
   | 10 | 0 | −2 | +0.10 | −20 |
   | 2 | 0 | −0.4 | +0.02 | −0.8 |
   | 10 | 100 | −1 | −0.10 | −20 |
   | 0 | 100 | +1 | −0.20 | −20 |
   | −10 | 100 | +3 | −0.30 | −60 |

   This fixture proves no particular propeller follows the law. Check all five rows only in a **test fixture implementing this law**; for a physical candidate use its own independently justified reference values. Reverse inflow may instead be explicitly rejected if outside the model contract. For a steady isolated passive propeller in uniform ambient flow, check `Q_abs·Ω − T·U ≥ 0`, not equality in general. The existing annular law has the stronger model-specific equality `Q_abs·Ω − T·U = dissipation_w ≥ 0` because of its element algebra; do not require equality of a different wake model.

2. **Hold versus breakaway.** For the first two rows, motor off, `I_rot=0.01 kg·m²`, fixed carrier, and static friction capacity `0.05 N·m`: `U=2` stays locked with balancing friction `−0.02 N·m`; `U=10` breaks forward with initial `Ω̇=(0.10−0.05)/0.01=5 rad/s²`. A stopped rotor may have nonzero drag and nonzero shaft torque while shaft power remains zero. Below threshold, verify the constraint does not invent reverse motion or dissipate energy at zero relative speed. Specify a real brake separately from passive friction; a different friction law can have a different breakaway threshold.

3. **Free-body coupling.** In a separate synthetic coaxial fixture at rest, use locked-body axial inertia `J=0.1`, rotor inertia `I_rot=0.01 kg·m²`, external aerodynamic rotor couple `+0.10 N·m`, zero motor torque and opposing moving friction `0.05 N·m`. With no other moment, the `rotor_coupling.gd` locked-inertia equations predict body acceleration `5/9`, relative rotor acceleration `40/9` rad/s² and `J·body_accel+I_rot·relative_accel=0.10 N·m`. An exact-rest static hold would require friction magnitude `0.09 N·m`, above the chosen limit. Verify the external propeller couple enters the body wrench once and the shaft equation once as the **same** external torque, with internal reactions cancelling in total angular momentum; the two appearances are different equations, not two external moments. Do not apply the clamped-carrier `½IΩ²` ledger alone to a freely rotating body: the relative-spin/body cross term matters.

4. **Limits and events.** Sample `Ω=0`, small positive values approaching zero, and the old 1-rpm seam at fixed `U`; require finite results and continuity **where the candidate declares a continuous phase-averaged law**. A locked blade's angle-specific drag can legitimately differ from a revolving phase average; label its azimuth or use a compatible averaging convention. Test zero flow with no retained wake, sign changes, stop→hold→restart, brake/coast, unsupported reverse spin, timestep refinement, and replay. Stateful wakes can release stored energy instantaneously: include their energy/flux before asserting an instantaneous passive inequality. At actual shutdown, event-localization and saved-state identities must agree with the stage law.

## B. Motor and finite-bus accounting

Use `Kv=1000 rpm/V`, equivalent `Rm=0.5 Ω`, no-load current `I₀=1 A`, `rpm=5000`, duty `0.5`, bus `20 V`. `electric_motor.gd` predicts back-EMF `5 V`, motor current `10 A`, battery-side motor current `5 A`, copper loss `50 W`, rotational loss `5 W`, and shaft power `45 W`: `100=50+5+45 W`. Disabled at the same speed: zero electrical current, `−5 W` shaft power and `+5 W` rotational loss. This checks the *existing ideal one-quadrant model*, not a particular ESC; regenerative or actively braked controllers need a different signed ledger.

For `electric_supply.gd` add constant `OCV=20 V`, `R_pack=0.1 Ω`, `I_aux=1 A`, capacity `1 Ah`, SOC `0.5` and keep the same motor inputs. Expected `I_motor=66/7 A`, `I_pack=40/7 A`, and `V_bus=136/7 V`. OCV-source power is `114.285714 W`, pack resistance loss `3.265306 W`, terminal power `111.020408 W`; terminal power splits into auxiliary `19.428571 W` plus motor-bus `91.591837 W`. Motor power splits into copper `44.448980 W`, rotation loss `5 W`, and shaft `42.142857 W`. Charge falls at `40/7 C/s` while connected. The OCV-resistance fixture is memoryless; a `pack_ecm` with polarization/thermal states needs their stored-energy and heat terms, not this same memoryless equality. At exact SOC zero this implementation deliberately disconnects all loads; it need not be continuous with its connected left limit. Check the ordered event, accepted charge, and no energy from discarded trials.

## C. Receiver-local flow and degenerate stations

1. **Crossflow normalization fixture.** With attached-flow/geometry frozen, no rates or controls, compare identical station flows `(10,0,1)` and `(10,4.686333966,1)` m/s. The section-plane speed and angle are identical; the latter has 25° sideslip relative to that plane. A frozen two-dimensional section/induction fixture must not change its induced input merely due to the added spanwise component. The current `_wing_flow()` source weight changes from `1` to `cos(25°)=0.906307787`, despite equal section-plane speeds. Restrict this check to the isolated closure: real yaw alters the three-dimensional wake, and full-aircraft side forces, sweep, Reynolds effects or a model explicitly resolving spanwise flow need not remain unchanged.

2. **Two-receiver reference.** Independently solve the attached linear system `C_i=a₀[α_i−Σ K_ij (V_j/V_i) C_j]` with `a₀=2 rad⁻¹`, `α=(0.1,0.1)` rad, `K=[[0.2,0.1],[0.1,0.2]]` and no zero-lift offset. For `V=(10,10)` m/s, `C=(1/8,1/8)`; for `V=(10,20)`, `C=(5/48,13/96)`. Under these exact frozen-matrix assumptions, `E_nonuniform=D⁻¹ED`, `D=diag(V_i)`, so off-diagonal weights are receiver-specific `V_j/V_i`; diagonal weights remain one. Applying the current max-speed source weighting to this same two-strip matrix instead gives `(1/8,13/96)`, exposing the first receiver’s mismatch. This is an analytic fixture, **not** a general prescription for separated or changing wake geometry. Mirroring station order reverses the result; scaling both speeds together leaves `C` unchanged and, with density and coefficients fixed, scales forces with speed squared.

3. **Stagnation and symmetry.** For stations `y=±0.5 m`, zero other rates, `v_x=0.5 m/s` and yaw rate `r=1 rad/s`, `_wing_flow()` gives local axial components `1` and `0 m/s`. Sweep the slow receiver toward zero and reverse it. An angle-based `V_j/V_i` expression becomes singular; either solve using finite vector velocities/forces with a documented continuation, or reject the case as outside the attached model. Never silently emit NaN or claim convergence from a clamp. For a symmetric non-propeller fixture, mirror y and controls/rates: force transforms as `(Fx,−Fy,Fz)` and moment, an axial vector, as `(−Mx,My,−Mz)`. A handed propeller, asymmetric aircraft, wake or control configuration invalidates this full-model symmetry assumption.

## Acceptance and evidence basis

For exact synthetic algebra, compare scalar values with `1e−10·max(1,|expected|)` and power/torque residuals against a similarly scaled tolerance; label tolerances numerical, not physical accuracy. Reuse and extend the **existing** focused tests `test_glow_propeller_momentum.gd`, `test_electric_motor.gd`, `test_electric_event_localization.gd`, and `test_strip_induced.gd`; run them only in the implementing owner’s authorized test session. Add old-path unchanged/replay checks, provenance and fixed-geometry station/time refinement. Compare a separated-flow candidate only within its documented domain and later against held-out matched measurements.
