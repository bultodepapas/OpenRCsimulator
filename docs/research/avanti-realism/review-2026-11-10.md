## 1. Executive assessment

The Avanti already has substantially more machinery than a simple game flight model: double-precision state, a shared RK4 force integration path, continuous turbine state, finite fuel with live mass properties, retracts and wheel rotation, flap actuators and aerodynamic effects, a progressive wing-polar approximation, input conditioning, and accepted-tick flight recordings. A native evaluator also exists. Recommendations should build on these systems rather than describe them as missing. The aircraft guide correctly labels the aircraft experimental and separates software implementation from physical validation. [C01][C02][C03][C04]

The main weakness is the gap between an internally elaborate model and independently identified Avanti behavior. Many decisive quantities remain estimates: physical wing/tail geometry, airfoil and twist, aerodynamic-center anchoring, inertia, fuel placement, installation thrust loss, intermediate engine response, flap drag, and tire behavior. Increasing model complexity without measuring these quantities can make the model harder to tune without making it more accurate. [C05][C06][C07]

### Recommended priority order

| Priority | Finding or need | Evidence strength | Why it comes first |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Establish a reproducible baseline for the current full session, backend, aircraft data, input profile, and telemetry | Existing infrastructure; current qualification incomplete | Prevents tuning against changing configurations or mistaking historical evidence for current results |
| P0 | Correct or explicitly reconcile the pitch-damping reference convention | High-confidence code/algebra finding | Generated CG-referenced damping is interpreted at another point before the force is transferred |
| P0 | Characterize and correct the active wheel/brake relaxation law | High-confidence analytical limitation; coupled rollout still required | Numerical regularization can dominate braking more than the nominal brake torque |
| P1 | Resolve lateral derivative consistency across global/local aerodynamic regimes | High-confidence code-path difference; flight effect unquantified | Sideslip, rudder response, roll coupling, and recovery can change with regime selection |
| P1 | Replace or qualify uniform half-wing direct aileron incidence | Confirmed simplification; physical error unmeasured | Aggregate roll agreement can hide incorrect spanwise loading near stall and with flaps |
| P1 | Define one owner for flap/elevator mixing and expose the actual control chain | Confirmed conditional configuration hazard | A pre-mixed radio can receive a second simulator mix; gamepad conditioning can resemble weak physics |
| P1 | Identify mass, CG, inertia, lift/drag, flap response, and installed engine behavior | Major evidence gap | These determine credible loops, roll acceleration, approach energy, and level/dive speed |
| P2 | Add engineering performance metrics to the debrief | Existing robust statistics; missing maneuver-specific interpretation | Enables objective comparison without conflating a dive peak with steady level performance |
| P2 | Profile the full experience and optimize demonstrated costs | Native implementation already exists; no new target-machine benchmark here | Protects 240 Hz physics while addressing frame pacing, recorder I/O, and history operations |
| Later | Full ECU/thermal/relight, detailed fuel transfer/slosh, advanced flexibility | Legitimate future fidelity work | These should follow, rather than distract from, unresolved core handling and ground calibration |

**What this review does not establish:** a measured Avanti top speed, verified real-world stall speed, calibrated stopping distance, pilot-approved feel, current native/reference parity, or release performance. The source findings below are actionable without pretending those larger claims have been proved.

## 2. Evidence policy and audit boundaries

### 2.1 Use five distinct kinds of evidence

1. **Observed in code:** a branch, parameter, equation, or data path exists in the reviewed source.
2. **Analytically derived:** a consequence follows from stated equations and assumptions. It may still require runtime confirmation.
3. **Measured in simulation:** a reproducible run produced a trace. It validates software behavior under that setup.
4. **Published or measured on hardware:** manufacturer data, bench results, metrology, or instrumented flight, with an identified installation.
5. **Pilot anecdote:** useful clues about behavior, but sensitive to build, engine, CG, radio settings, surface, weather, and pilot technique.

Every proposed fix should state which category supports it. Agreement between two implementations of the same equations does not identify the real aircraft. A historical test labeled “valid” does not necessarily mean “physically accurate.”

### 2.2 Scope

This audit examined the pinned repository, aircraft source/generation chain, runtime aerodynamic and ground code, control conditioning, turbine/fuel integration, statistics and recording paths, retained research/test records, the original SebArt manual, historical JetCat specifications, relevant engine/aerodynamic research, and owner discussions.

The reviewed sources include several already completed repair programs. AV-17-R1/R2/R3 implemented keyboard pitch progression, a progressive wing polar, gamepad pitch/yaw policies, and a yaw-rate reference correction. Those repairs should be retained and evaluated; they must not be proposed again as nonexistent work. The remaining pitch-reference finding in Section 4.1 is separate from the completed yaw repair. [C01][C08]

The retained FT-05 flight-card record is dated 19 October 2026, used Godot 4.7.2, and reports 39/40 valid scenarios, with the right inverted-spin recovery reaching the floor. That is historical simulation evidence. The current aircraft guide explicitly leaves current session, replay, ground, physical identification, and broader release qualification open. [C01][C09]

Audit execution and its limits are recorded in Appendix A. There was no instrumented real-aircraft flight or human pilot trial during this review.

## 3. Reference aircraft, parameter quality, and expected character

### 3.1 Lock the correct Avanti configuration

The reference is the original **SebArt A200 turbine airframe**, not a smaller Freewing/FMS EDF, an XS/Mini version, or a different manufacturer’s similarly named jet. Likewise, the historical P100-RX should not silently inherit a current P100-RX-BL controller, spool curve, mass, or performance map. The repository already makes this identity distinction. [C01][S01][S03]

A comparison dataset must identify the exact engine, ECU/firmware, tailpipe, intake installation, fuel load, gear/brake hardware, radio settings, gyro state, and whether thrust vectoring is installed. A P140/P180 owner’s vertical performance is informative about that installation; it is not a P100 target.

## 4. Concrete source findings and proposed corrections

### 4.1 Pitch damping uses inconsistent reference conventions

**Classification:** high-confidence code/algebra finding; handling consequence not yet measured.

The AV-06 generator derives and labels `Cmq` about the CG. The runtime global aerodynamic path treats the stored pitch moment as an ARP moment and then transfers the force to the live CG. Rate-dependent lift therefore contributes an additional pitch moment. Relevant locations:

- [Generator derivation, including CG-referenced damping](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/313bb24e662faa0ed54981de552bc1722dc968b0/research/avanti-s/av06/derive_physics.py#L572-L573).
- [Serialized derivative construction](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/313bb24e662faa0ed54981de552bc1722dc968b0/research/avanti-s/av06/derive_physics.py#L687).
- [Runtime ARP-to-CG force/moment transfer](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/313bb24e662faa0ed54981de552bc1722dc968b0/app/physics/aero.gd#L216-L246).

Using the repository’s aft-positive geometry coordinates, at zero angle of attack and sideslip:

$$
C_{m_q,CG}
=
C_{m_q,stored}
+
C_{L_q}\frac{x_{CG}-x_{ARP}}{c}.
$$

With the current values:

$$
-7.751139+
6.059633\frac{0.23036732695-0.206331}{0.351137}
=
-7.336339876.
$$

The effective damping magnitude is approximately **5.35% smaller** than the generator’s intended CG-referenced value at this configuration. This is **not** a prediction that the aircraft pitches 5.35% faster or that a loop is 5.35% larger.

An earlier internal neutral-point investigation already noticed this reference issue. The practical recommendation is to close that known issue with a narrow proof rather than add another undocumented damping multiplier. [C11]

**Implementation plan**

1. Declare the intended moment reference for every stored derivative.
2. Use a central-difference pitch-rate probe at fixed air state and a documented reference CG.
3. Check the identity above at several fuel/CG states, preserving the physical effect of moving the CG.
4. Correct the generator or shared contract so that force transfer occurs exactly once.
5. Regenerate through the owning pipeline and compare reference/native outputs.
6. Run pitch pulse/release and loop-entry comparisons with the same accepted command tape.

Do not recompute a “correction” at every live CG merely to keep CG damping artificially constant. The chosen reference convention and physical force locations must determine the result.

**Acceptance:** derivative identity matches its stated convention; local/global agreement is evaluated independently; the same candidate produces a documented pitch-response change without unexplained energy or lateral artifacts. The existing regime-consistency test’s broad relative band does not by itself prove the intended CG datum. [C12]

### 4.2 The active brake law can be dominated by numerical regularization

**Classification:** high-confidence analytical limitation under specified conditions; stopping-distance effect needs a coupled run.

The stock Avanti reaches the existing wheel evaluator through the ordinary ground path. The newer GP-12 capacity/impulse work is not enabled on stock aircraft. Reuse that track’s reasoning and ownership rather than turning on an unfinished ground model as an incidental Avanti change. [C13][C14]

In [ground_wheels.gd, lines 151–187](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/313bb24e662faa0ed54981de552bc1722dc968b0/app/physics/ground_wheels.gd#L151-L187), the tire slip slope and brake torque slope are bounded using:

$$
m_{eff}=\frac{I_w}{R^2}, \qquad
k_{tire}\leq\frac{m_{eff}}{\tau}.
$$

For the main-wheel profile:

- `R = 0.04338 m`;
- `I_w = 5.5552 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m²`;
- relaxation `τ = 0.03 s`;
- provisional maximum brake torque `Tmax = 1 N·m`.

Thus `m_eff ≈ 0.02952 kg` and the limiting tire slope is approximately `0.984 N/(m/s)`. At full brake, a constant axle speed of **3 m/s**, sufficient normal load, negligible lateral force, and the unsaturated limiter branches, the equations settle approximately at:

| Quantity | Analytical result |
|---|---:|
| Wheel rim speed | 1.5 m/s |
| Longitudinal braking tire force | 1.476 N per main wheel |
| Actual brake torque | 0.064 N·m per main wheel |
| Nominal torque/radius force capacity | 23.05 N per main wheel |

The last number is a capacity, not the force the current law realizes in that condition. Rolling resistance is separate. Aircraft acceleration, wheel transients, lift, tire saturation, and surface state can change a real rollout. This calculation should therefore be treated as a sharply defined test case, not a measured stopping distance.

**Why it matters:** increasing the nominal brake torque can fail to correct soft braking because the inertia/relaxation cap still sets the active slope.

**Implementation plan**

1. Measure a single-wheel loaded case over speed, load, brake demand, and surface.
2. Record commanded and actual brake torque, wheel angular speed, longitudinal/lateral slip, normal load, tire forces, and all limiter branches.
3. Run full-session coastdown and braking from identical groundspeeds, mass, air/wind state, and touchdown conditions.
4. Compare the existing law with the GP-owned candidate that separates torque capacity, traction capacity, and numerical stabilization.
5. Require nonpositive dissipation for passive wheel/brake components and stable low-speed rest.
6. Add wheel spin-up at touchdown, unloading, brake release/restart, and timestep refinement.

A useful diagnostic grid is brake demand `0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0` at `1 / 3 / 10 / 20 m/s`, with several normal loads. These are **proposed laboratory cases**, not Avanti operating limits.

### 4.3 The direct aileron input is spread over the whole half-wing

**Classification:** confirmed spatial simplification; real flight error unquantified.

The geometry surrogate places the aileron approximately **0.55–0.95 m** from the centerline and the flap **0.18–0.53 m**. The generator uses the finite aileron span to obtain aggregate roll authority. Runtime then applies the same effective aileron-incidence shift to every strip on that side. [C15]

The current effectiveness is `0.38063`. Full Normal roll gives a down-going aileron of 12.5°, corresponding to about **4.76°** of direct effective incidence across that entire half-wing. Induced aerodynamic influence can spread beyond a control surface; the problem to investigate is treating the *direct* camber/incidence effect as spatially uniform.

A calibrated aggregate derivative can still produce plausible ordinary roll rate. Therefore this finding does not establish that all rolls are wrong. It makes low-speed rolling, partial-span separation, flap interaction, and adverse yaw especially uncertain.

**Implementation plan**

- Store per-strip aileron coverage and local chord/hinge effectiveness.
- Apply direct control effects only where geometrically appropriate.
- Retain whole-wing induced coupling through the existing flow model.
- Preserve the baseline attached-flow aggregate roll derivative initially.
- Compare strip loads, left/right roll acceleration, settled rate, release damping, and stall onset with partial flaps and both aileron directions.
- Calibrate against measured surface travel and flight response before claiming improvement.

The existing roll test derives its expected response from the same `Clda` and `Clp` used by the simulation. That is a useful consistency test, but not independent evidence for the Avanti’s roll speed. [C16]

### 4.4 Lateral aerodynamic contributions differ across regimes

**Classification:** confirmed code-path difference; magnitude and desired physical correction remain open.

The global build-up includes an estimated body contribution of approximately `CYβ_body = −0.1949` within total `CYβ = −0.8554`, plus wing-body contributions to rolling response. The generated aircraft does not supply optional `body_cy_beta` and `wing_body_cl_beta` terms, so the local path omits those residuals. The selector blends toward local evaluation in response to angle, sideslip, control deflection, and body rates. [C17]

Separately, the generator estimates a destabilizing fuselage yaw contribution around `Cnβ_body = −0.04485` but retains a fin-only total around `+0.20234`, citing an older contract. The loader now supports an explicit body yaw term and a fin-plus-body consistency check. This supports a review, not automatic activation of uncertain estimates. [C18]

**Implementation plan**

1. Tabulate finite-difference `CYβ, Clβ, Cnβ, Clp, Cnr` at matched physical CG, speed, and angle of attack.
2. Sweep the actual global/local selector and inspect force and derivative continuity.
3. Separate residual body terms from local fin/wing effects; avoid double counting.
4. Test one provenance-labeled candidate at a time.
5. Compare rudder step/release, sideslip, knife-edge, crosswind approach, and low-speed recovery.
6. Preserve the already implemented yaw-rate reference repair.

**Acceptance:** smooth and explained transitions; no hidden disappearance or duplication of a modeled force; correct sign and symmetry; improved agreement with independent evidence where available. Smoothness alone is not physical validation.

### 4.5 Two different stall closures need a sensitivity check

**Classification:** confirmed use of separate approximations; not yet a demonstrated defect.

The induced-flow donor attenuation still follows the older smoothstep separation envelope, while the R2 tabulated polar separately governs strip lift. Near 14.77°, the former donor transition has finished while the tabulated attached-fraction proxy is about 0.4405. Neither quantity is a directly measured circulation field. [C19]

The model also retains estimated spanwise incidence, section limits, tail separation behavior, and a proxy polar rather than a measured Avanti wing. The existing table contains no identified aircraft-specific dynamic-stall hysteresis. [C10]

**Implementation plan:** compare the current closures against a consistent candidate over angle, control deflection, and pitch/roll rate. Measure integrated lift, drag, `Clp`, spanwise load, and transient recovery. Change the closure only after showing why the alternative is better. A more complex stall model with unmeasured parameters is not automatically an improvement.

Do not define stall as “trim solver failed,” “speed crossed a fixed threshold,” or “the largest lift value anywhere in a −8° to 75° sweep.” Identify the first relevant loss of controllable lift, the onset of separation/wing drop, and the recovery path for each configuration.

### 4.6 Flap/elevator mix can have two owners

**Classification:** conditional configuration hazard.

The session applies the simulator flap/elevator mix using actual flap travel. Calibrated physical-radio channels can already contain transmitter shaping and mixing. If a transmitter sends pre-mixed elevator along with flap position, the simulator can add the same compensation again. This is not evidence that every radio is affected. [C20]

**Recommendation:** define and record one mix owner:

- **Simulator-owned:** raw calibrated channels, with the documented simulator mix.
- **Transmitter-owned:** the radio provides the finished elevator mix; simulator compensation is disabled.
- **Explicit custom:** separately named configuration with a recorded law.

The default must be understandable, and existing profiles should migrate without silently changing behavior. Test elevator-only, flap-only, and pre-mixed-radio cases, including partial flap travel and interrupted actuator movement. Keep aerodynamic flap pitching moment and pilot compensation separate: the latter must not erase the former from the physical model.

### 4.7 Core physical parameters need identification before broad retuning

**Classification:** calibration gap, not a single software bug.

The current zero-lift drag, transition assumptions, inlet/excrescence estimates, wing/tail geometry, aerodynamic center, inertia, fuel arm, and thrust installation factor jointly influence the observed response. Compensating one error with another can match one maneuver and fail elsewhere. [C05][C06][C07]

Examples:

- Too much drag and too much thrust can give the right level speed but the wrong idle glide and powered loop.
- Wrong inertia and stronger control effectiveness can match settled roll rate but miss acceleration and roll stopping.
- A forward CG and excessive elevator effectiveness can mask each other at one speed while failing approach trim.
- A slow input throttle integrator can make a correct turbine law feel slow.
- Brake softness can be partly concealed by excessive rolling resistance, then corrupt takeoff and coasting.

**Recommended identification order:** geometry and mass properties → control/servo calibration → installed propulsion → attached-flow lift/drag/trim → dynamic derivatives → flap and separation behavior → coupled ground/flight transitions. Maintain parameter uncertainties and reserve some runs as held-out validation data.

### 4.8 Documentation and acceptance boundaries need tightening

The statistics code identifies rules version v3 while parts of the guide still describe v2. This is documentation drift, not proof of a classification defect. Landing-credit profiles deliberately require reviewed support geometry; changing contact geometry can invalidate their match. A physics improvement must update the relevant reviewed profile and tests rather than silently lose landing statistics. [C21][C22]

Historical speed, loop, and flight-card outputs should retain their source/configuration identities. Do not promote a permissive test range or a completed simulator scenario into a public aircraft specification.

## 5. Physics requirements for speed, dives, loops, rolls, and stability

### 5.1 Preserve force-based rigid-body dynamics

Use the local air-relative velocity at each aerodynamic or propulsion element:

$$
\mathbf{v}_{air,local}
=
\mathbf{v}_{CG}-\mathbf{v}_{wind}
+
\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}.
$$

Apply the established coordinate transforms and signs consistently. The repository distinguishes body-frame axes from geometry datum coordinates; an aft-positive geometry position is not a forward-positive body coordinate.

For a fixed-mass rigid body, rotational dynamics include:

$$
\mathbf{I}\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
+
\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\mathbf{I}\boldsymbol{\omega})
=
\mathbf{M}_{CG}.
$$

Keep live fuel mass, CG, and inertia synchronized at integration stages. If extending variable-mass dynamics, derive one consistent open-system formulation; do not add an extra “rocket” or inertia-change term on top of momentum effects already represented by the propulsion/fuel model.

Control input should change surface position and aerodynamic force/moment. Avoid injecting a desired angular velocity to make the jet feel powerful. Any deliberate stabilization or training assistance should be an explicit optional system with its own telemetry.

### 5.2 Airspeed, Reynolds number, and lift limits

Dynamic pressure and basic lift/drag relations are:

$$
\bar q=\frac12\rho V_{TAS}^2,\qquad
L=\bar q S C_L,\qquad D=\bar q S C_D.
$$

For attached flight, a useful diagnostic approximation is:

$$
C_D \approx C_{D0}+\frac{C_L^2}{\pi e AR}.
$$

This is not a complete high-angle or flap drag model. Reference area and coefficient conventions must stay consistent across components. [S04]

At standard sea-level density, viscosity `1.81×10⁻⁵ Pa·s`, and the model’s reference chord, speeds 18–70 m/s correspond to an illustrative Reynolds-number range of approximately **0.43–1.66 million**. That explains why transition, surface condition, and section data matter. It does not establish the real wing’s airfoil or polar.

For a nominal one-g level stall estimate:

$$
V_s=\sqrt{\frac{2mg}{\rho S C_{L,max}}}.
$$

Using the current half-main inventory mass, sea-level density, and nominal `CLmax=0.85` gives approximately **17.52 m/s / 63.1 km/h**. Varying that estimated coefficient from 0.75 to 0.95 gives roughly **67.1–59.6 km/h**. These are analytical estimates from model inputs, **not measured Avanti stall speeds and not a substitute for probing the current progressive polar**.

At positive load factor `n`, the familiar attached-flight approximation is:

$$
V_{s,n}\approx V_{s,1}\sqrt{n}.
$$

At 3 g, the same nominal estimate becomes about 30.34 m/s. This is why a jet can stall while moving fast in a tight pull. Angle of attack and load demand matter, not just speed. [S05]

### 5.3 Top speed: identify thrust and drag separately

A credible steady level maximum requires a trimmed solution satisfying approximately:

$$
L \approx W,\qquad
T_\parallel \approx D,\qquad
\dot V\approx0,\qquad
\dot h\approx0.
$$

Measure with gear/flaps up, known mass and density, and a sufficiently long settled window. Record TAS and groundspeed separately. Derive candidate tolerances from noise and convergence; do not silently accept a descending “level” pass.

The current installed-static model at the half-main mass gives an illustrative thrust/weight ratio of approximately **0.82**. This is based on the estimated installation factor. It provides no basis for unlimited sustained vertical flight from static thrust alone; a zoom climb can still use kinetic energy. It is not a substitute for a measured installed thrust curve.

Use at least three kinds of observations to identify top-speed behavior:

1. Static/low-speed installed thrust and RPM response.
2. Idle glides or coastdown-like flight segments over several speeds.
3. Powered trimmed acceleration and settled level runs.

Do not fit the model to one forum speed claim. If thrust and drag are allowed to compensate freely, the correct top-speed number can conceal incorrect energy retention everywhere else.

### 5.4 Dives: gravity contributes to acceleration

For a simplified straight flight-path balance with climb angle `γ` positive upward:

$$
m\dot V=T_\parallel-D-mg\sin\gamma.
$$

In a descent, gravity can increase speed beyond the steady level equilibrium. A steady descending balance includes the gravity component; it is not the same as `T=D`.

Test idle and powered dives from identical initial states, at several descent angles. Log airspeed, altitude, acceleration, angle of attack, thrust, drag, and load factor. The manual speed advisory can drive an indicated overspeed warning. Any structural failure/flutter model needs its own justified envelope; a velocity clamp would conceal energy and force errors.

In still air, for a simple point-mass interpretation:

$$
e=\frac{V^2}{2}+gh,\qquad
\dot e=\frac{(T_\parallel-D)V}{m}.
$$

Use correctly timed forces and the appropriate variable-mass formulation. In wind gradients or gusts, air-relative energy has additional terms; an apparent airspeed-energy residual is not automatically a solver defect. Start with calm-air tests before adding weather.

### 5.5 Loops: measure the trajectory, energy, and load

Body pitch-rate integration is not proof of a planar loop. The aircraft can rotate substantially while its flight path forms an ellipse, corkscrew, stalled tumble, or displaced arc.

For an ideal vertical circular path, with inward lift positive:

$$
n_{bottom}=1+\frac{V_{bottom}^2}{gR},
\qquad
n_{top}=\frac{V_{top}^2}{gR}-1.
$$

The signs describe the lift required by the chosen path; real loops have varying speed and radius. For illustration, 50 m/s at the bottom with 4 g corresponds to about an 85 m radius. That is a geometry example, not an Avanti maneuver requirement.

A useful loop evaluation records:

- entry, top, and exit speed and altitude;
- three-dimensional trajectory and plane deviation;
- actual elevator, pitch rate, angle of attack, and normal load;
- positive/negative lift behavior;
- engine RPM/thrust and energy change;
- exit heading, bank, and available control margin.

Repeat at several entry speeds and fuel states using fixed command tapes for model comparisons. Separately evaluate pilot-flown loops for controllability. Do not require a perfect circle from a constant elevator command or assume the top is stalled merely because its speed is low.

### 5.6 Rolls: separate acceleration, established rate, and stopping

For a small-angle, attached, approximately linear diagnostic model:

$$
C_\ell \approx C_{\ell_{\delta_a}}\delta_a
+
C_{\ell_p}\frac{pb}{2V},
$$

giving an approximate equilibrium rate:

$$
p_{ss}\approx
-\frac{C_{\ell_{\delta_a}}}{C_{\ell_p}}
\frac{2V}{b}\delta_a.
$$

Angles and rates must use compatible radian units, and the actual differential aileron configuration must be represented. This expression is a diagnostic, not a global post-stall law.

Test three separate properties:

- **Initial roll acceleration:** control effectiveness and roll inertia.
- **Established body roll rate:** authority versus aerodynamic damping.
- **Release/stop response:** damping, actuator return, coupling, and input shaping.

Then test a slow roll with coordinated elevator/rudder, four-point roll, inverted segment, and near-stall roll response. Record adverse yaw and heading/altitude departure. Use quaternion-based attitude/event logic; Euler wrapping and singularities can create false maneuver counts. Even integrated body roll rate is not an exact revolution count during arbitrary coupled motion.

There is no credible single “correct Avanti roll rate” in the available evidence. Set a measured target only after matching actual travel, mode, airspeed, CG, mass, and pilot input.

### 5.7 Rudder, elevator, and stability

For elevator, identify trim gradient with speed, pulse/release damping, high-load authority, inverted trim, flap compensation, and power-related pitch response. Distinguish aerodynamic response from CG, thrust line, flap zero, servo travel, and input conditioning.

For rudder, test positive/negative steps, release oscillation, sideslip decay, yaw-to-roll and yaw-to-pitch coupling, knife-edge support, and crosswind alignment. A fin-only model can be too stable in some conditions, but suppressing oscillation by arbitrary damping is not a substitute for the derivative audit in Section 4.4.

Use small-amplitude system identification in the attached regime first. Large rudder/elevator deflections near separation answer a different question and should not be used to estimate linear derivatives.

## 6. Flaps, gear, ground resistance, and braking

### 6.1 Flaps need a complete energy and trim description

The current flaps already have travel, forces, wake interaction, and elevator compensation. Remaining work is identification and coupled approach validation, not simply enabling flap animation. The prior AV-09 paired sensitivity recording was a high-altitude experiment, not an approach/landing validation. [C01][C23]

Evaluate 0°, 20°, and 50° in matched configurations:

| Property | What to measure | Failure to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Lift and first load loss | Trim curve, maximum usable lift, separation pattern | Large flap deflection treated as unlimited lift |
| Drag and energy | Sink rate, deceleration, required thrust | Full flap that barely slows the jet, or excessive drag masking weak brakes |
| Pitch and compensation | Transient moment, steady trim, actual mixed elevator | Compensation hiding wrong physical moment or being applied twice |
| Wake/tail interaction | Tail angle/force and response delay | Flap extension causing an unexplained abrupt elevator response |
| Aileron interaction | Roll authority, adverse yaw, wing-drop tendency | Inboard flap and outboard aileron effects spatially conflated |
| Go-around/retraction | RPM/thrust, sink, attitude, lift transient | Instant thrust or lift discontinuities during configuration change |

The current 1.5 s travel, effective-incidence bound, and profile-drag factor are estimated. Record actual flap angle rather than commanded detent when comparing traces. A loaded servo/hinge model should be added only if measured response supports it; do not invent blowback or an operational flap speed limit from intuition. [C05]

### 6.2 Gear state must affect contact and flight consistently

Keep extension state, visual wheel position, contact witnesses, drag, and steering availability synchronized. Compare both loaded and unloaded transitions. Contact must not be available merely because the commanded gear switch is down if the actuator has not reached a valid state.

A reference test should cover gear-up/down trim and drag, partial extension behavior, touchdown spin-up, nose-wheel contact after the mains, and a bounce with changing normal loads. The existing gear implementation deserves focused qualification rather than replacement based on appearance.

### 6.3 Separate resistance mechanisms

Ground deceleration contains distinct mechanisms:

$$
F_{long}\approx
F_{propulsion}+F_{aero}+F_{rolling}+F_{brake}+F_{slope},
$$

where forces are signed along the ground path and residual turbine thrust remains included. A basic rolling-resistance approximation is `F_rr=C_rr N`. Brake-limited traction is approximately bounded by:

$$
|F_{brake}|\leq
\min\left(\frac{|T_{brake}|}{R},\,\mu N\right)
$$

only under assumptions that make such a scalar comparison appropriate. A combined tire law should share traction between longitudinal and lateral demand, for example through a calibrated friction ellipse:

$$
\left(\frac{F_x}{\mu_xN}\right)^2+
\left(\frac{F_y}{\mu_yN}\right)^2\leq1.
$$

These are diagnostic models, not identified Avanti tire data. Suspension, tire deformation, wheel angular dynamics, contact constraints, and low-speed regularization must remain consistent with the chosen solver.

Normal load is not always simply aircraft weight divided by three. Aerodynamic lift, pitching moment, braking load transfer, vertical acceleration, and surface geometry affect each wheel. Ground forces must respond to wheel/contact motion relative to the surface, not TAS.

**Required behavior**

- Coasting and braking remain distinguishable.
- A lightly loaded wheel loses available traction.
- Main-wheel braking and nose steering retain separate roles.
- Steering effectiveness depends on contact and speed.
- Touchdown wheel spin-up consumes appropriate energy.
- Brake holding and rest do not create creep or reverse acceleration.
- Releasing brakes permits a smooth restart.
- Grass/asphalt differences are based on defined parameters and observations, not a blanket velocity multiplier.

The original and aftermarket brake installations differ. Choose and document one reference configuration before measuring hardware response; an electromagnetic aftermarket unit is not automatically the original pneumatic system. [S02][S11]

### 6.4 Ground test matrix

Use repeated matched runs and report both median and spread.

| Test | Controlled state | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Parked idle, brakes off/on | Level surface; stated fuel and wind | Creep, hold force, wheel speed, contact jitter |
| Slope hold/release | Small known grade, both directions | Rest stability, restart, energy |
| Coastdown | Fixed initial groundspeed, engine state, surface | Speed-distance curve, rolling/aero contributions |
| Brake ramp | Same start; several demands | Torque, force, slip, distance, peak deceleration |
| Wind discrimination | Same groundspeed; calm/headwind/tailwind | Correct separation of aerodynamic and tire effects |
| Mass and fuel | Same CG convention and speed | Load distribution and braking response |
| Touchdown | Matched sink rate, pitch, speed | Spin-up, normal impulse, bounce, nose drop |
| Steering | Both directions across taxi speeds | Turn radius, slip, yaw response, nose load |
| Surface transition | Defined hard/soft boundary | Continuity, traction limits, no artificial energy |
| Refinement | Same physical case at validated timesteps | Convergence of impulse, distance, and rest state |

Do not calibrate stopping distance until the active wheel law has passed the isolated checks in Section 4.2.

## 7. Improving the feeling without hiding its causes

### 7.1 Treat the entire input-to-perception chain as a measurable system

The chain is:

**device → calibration → deadzone/expo/rates/mixes → actuator → force model → integration → rendering/camera/audio → pilot perception.**

A complaint such as “the jet is slow,” “the elevator is too soft,” or “it does not stop rolling” is ambiguous until those stages are separated.

Current implementation details matter:

- Gamepad defaults include deadzone 0.08 and expo 0.3.
- Default stick-rate throttle changes command at 0.5 per second: a full sustained rate input takes approximately **two seconds** to move from zero to full command, before turbine response.
- Absolute trigger and physical-radio throttle paths behave differently.
- Normal/sport gamepad policies include additional pitch/yaw scaling and yaw smoothing.
- Physical-radio inputs can already be transmitter-shaped.
- Keyboard pitch uses a deliberate progression.
- Logged `raw_*` values are session-level samples after `radio.sticks()`, not raw USB hardware samples. [C20][C24]

Do not make the P100 artificially spool faster to compensate for throttle integration. Do not increase elevator effectiveness to compensate for a gamepad rate reduction. Expose the active profile and effective surface limits in the setup/debrief.

### 7.2 Recommended control-profile behavior

Maintain separate, clearly named configurations for:

- **Reference radio setup:** actual surface travel and explicit ownership of rates, expo, and mixes.
- **Gamepad setup:** deliberate usability shaping with its settings recorded.
- **Keyboard setup:** existing progression acknowledged as an input policy.
- **Optional assistance:** separately enabled and logged; never silently blended into the aircraft model.

At the current full elevator travel, 50% Normal rate corresponds to 15°. An additional 0.4 gamepad pitch scale corresponds to roughly 6° before trim/mix and the detailed policy. That can be an intentional usability choice, but it is materially different from a physical radio providing the full aircraft endpoint. [C20]

Measure the transfer function from device input to commanded surface and from commanded surface to actual surface. Then evaluate aircraft response to the actual surface. This prevents duplicate expo, rates, deadzones, or mixes.

### 7.3 Pilot evaluation should be structured

Recruit pilots with the relevant turbine Avanti configuration where possible. Keep build, fuel/CG, radio, display, camera, wind, and frame pacing fixed. Use randomized baseline/candidate order and short repeated tasks.

Ask about concrete sensations:

- trim predictability and center-stick precision;
- roll acceleration, tracking, and release;
- elevator buildup and tendency to over-pull;
- rudder coupling and settling;
- approach deceleration, sink response, and go-around delay;
- touchdown, brake modulation, and steering;
- clarity of speed and energy cues.

Pair each rating with a trace and a short explanation. Do not average unrelated pilot preferences into a universal coefficient. Distinguish “more enjoyable,” “easier,” and “closer to my aircraft.”

Camera FOV, zoom, tracking, horizon stability, shadows, and turbine sound can influence speed perception. Test them independently from physics. A change in camera or audio must not be reported as proof of improved aerodynamic fidelity.

### 7.4 Calibrate the P100 as an installed propulsion system

The historical JetCat technical table identifies the non-BL P100-RX at approximately **44,000–154,000 rpm**, **2–100 N** static thrust, **80–390 ml/min** fuel flow, and **1,080 g** engine mass under its stated reference conditions. Endpoint specifications do not identify the full throttle-to-RPM-to-thrust transient or the installed tailpipe losses. [S03]

The existing continuous model is the right place to improve identification. It already includes demand mapping, asymmetric spool schedules, stage RPM-dependent thrust/gyro, and local intake velocity. The current installation factor and intermediate maps remain estimates. [C01][C07]

Obtain, where practicable:

- steady installed thrust/RPM/fuel-flow points across command;
- small and large steps from several initial RPMs;
- acceleration and deceleration traces;
- density/temperature and ECU setting records;
- timestamps aligned across command, ECU telemetry, and force measurement;
- repeatability and sensor uncertainty.

Measure command-to-demand separately from demand-to-RPM and RPM-to-force. Report `t10/t50/t90`, settling, overshoot if present, and the initial/final operating points. A first-order time constant, a bandwidth, and an idle-to-full step time are different quantities.

The P100 system-identification paper by Many and colleagues is useful because it measures an actual P100-RX installation, but its excitation range, airframe, sampling, and fitting limitations matter. A roughly 0.4 Hz response feature should not become a universal full-range spool constant. L’Erario and colleagues provide relevant nonlinear turbine identification methods; the engine models in Momin and colleagues are different JetCat types and should remain methodological/surrogate evidence. [S06][S07][S12]

Do not use a weakly identified forward-speed thrust correction as if it were a measured P100 ram-drag law. Investigate it through coupled thrust/drag evidence and retain uncertainty. Full ECU startup, thermal history, cooling, relight, pump/supply dynamics, and hopper transfer are future systems, separate from the already implemented basic finite-fuel exhaustion and rundown.

## 8. Statistics and telemetry that make improvements measurable

### 8.1 Preserve the current robust statistics foundation

The current statistics already distinguish airspeed and horizontal groundspeed and use filtered/maturing maxima with discontinuity and plausibility rejection. They should not be replaced by a single maximum sample. The present speed plausibility cap is a statistics integrity rule, not a force or airspeed limiter. [C21][C25]

The important addition is **classification and context**. A transient powered dive peak, a steady level maximum, and an approach speed must not appear as equivalent evidence of aircraft performance.

### 8.2 Add an engineering debrief alongside player statistics

| Metric | Definition / required context | Engineering purpose |

| TAS / EAS / horizontal groundspeed | Density, wind, altitude, units, and source | Separate aerodynamic loading from map-relative motion |
| Steady level maximum | Settled speed with bounded vertical rate, acceleration, bank, and known configuration | Identify thrust/drag equilibrium |
| Peak dive speed | Peak plus sustained-window value, descent angle, height loss, power state | Evaluate energy and overspeed behavior |
| Stall event | AoA, first relevant load loss, roll/yaw departure, mass/CG, flap/gear and input | Avoid one universal stall-speed number |
| Roll response | Actual aileron; acceleration, established body rate, release transient, attitude path | Separate inertia, effectiveness, damping, and coupling |
| Loop quality | Entry/top/exit speed, plane deviation, radius history, g/AoA, energy | Distinguish rotation from trajectory and performance |
| Pitch/yaw release | Peak rate, opposite-rate excursion, decay/settling and actual surface | Quantify control feel and stability |
| Normal load factor | Body-normal specific force with an explicit sign convention, distinct from vertical acceleration divided by g | Compare pulls, inverted flight, and accelerated stall consistently |
| Flap transition | Commanded/actual flap, elevator mix, lift/drag/moment and sink | Identify transient and steady effects |
| Turbine response | Demand, RPM, thrust, fuel flow, t10/t50/t90, installed configuration | Separate controls from engine dynamics |
| Coast/brake rollout | Touchdown/start state, brake demand/torque, slip, normal forces, distance | Identify ground behavior |
| Approach/landing | TAS and groundspeed, sink, pitch/roll, contact sequence, bounce, rollout | Evaluate the complete landing task |
| Energy residual | Matched-time state and force/work terms, stated wind/mass assumptions | Detect unexplained numerical or modeling energy |
| Performance cost | CPU/GPU frame percentiles, physics work, catch-up, sim/wall ratio, I/O | Explain stutter and timing-related feel |

Implement these as derived analysis where possible. Keep the accepted physical state and recorder as the evidence source; do not introduce a second physics implementation in the UI.

### 8.3 Preserve timing semantics and replay validity

The flight recorder already provides accepted-tick states, control stages, surfaces, forces/moments, mass/CG information, and backend identity. Its force samples are RK4 **k1 at interval start**, while committed state and air data are at the endpoint. Energy/work analysis must use `loads_t_s` and appropriate integration rather than multiply a start force by an unrelated endpoint state. [C03]

For a physics change:

1. Capture and verify one baseline recording.
2. Replay it against the exact baseline.
3. Apply one candidate to the same accepted command tape.
4. Compare full traces and declared metrics.

That open-loop comparison does not re-trim, adapt the pilot, or replay a changed device/rates/expo pipeline. Input-policy changes require a fresh controlled input experiment, and pilot acceptance requires new pilot runs.

### 8.4 Data provenance and usability

Record the commit, generated aircraft hash, source inputs, backend and native binary identity, Godot version, controller profile, mix ownership, environment, initial fuel, CG/mass, and camera/performance settings where relevant.

Keep detailed engineering charts out of ordinary flight flows unless the user opens the debrief. The player needs understandable explanations such as “highest steady level speed” and “highest dive speed,” not solver internals.

After contact-geometry changes, rerun reviewed landing-profile matching and update its source identity. The current exact geometry contract is intentional. Do not relax it merely to make the landing statistic reappear. [C22]

## 9. Optimization plan: preserve the physical result

### 9.1 Profile the complete shipped path first

The app requests a native evaluator by default and has an explicit reference fallback. A recommendation to “rewrite physics in C++” would miss existing work. Verify the **actual** backend and supported configuration before measuring or claiming native performance. [C04]

Use the project’s 1080p target and a declared hardware/software configuration. Measure:

- CPU and GPU frame p50/p95/p99 and long-frame counts;
- total physics work per accepted tick and per rendered frame;
- native/reference ownership and call coverage;
- catch-up steps, dropped/limited catch-up, and simulation/wall-clock ratio;
- input-to-surface and input-to-visible-response latency;
- allocations, garbage/cleanup pressure where applicable;
- recorder off/on and statistics-history size;
- representative aircraft distance, scenery, shadows, smoke, and gear/flap states.

The physics runs at 240 Hz. The project’s maximum catch-up setting is 12 steps per frame, so a sustained render rate below roughly 20 fps cannot keep real time merely by catching up under that limit. This is a configuration consequence, not a benchmark result from this review. [C26]

Do not improve a displayed frame-rate number by lowering physical fidelity without an explicit, separately validated mode.

### 9.2 Investigate likely costs without declaring them proven bottlenecks

| Area | Current evidence | Candidate action only if profiling supports it |
|---|---|---|
| Flight recorder | Serialization/write/flush are synchronous on the simulation thread | Bounded asynchronous persistence with ordered records, explicit failure/incomplete handling, and durable close |
| Speed statistics | Two bounded speed windows are copied/sorted on eligible ticks | Reuse storage or a bounded order-statistic structure while preserving exact classification |
| Large statistics history | Automatic persistence already uses a worker; some management/query paths scale with history | Incremental indexes, bounded UI work, paging, and targeted operation benchmarks |
| Aerodynamic stage work | Multiple stages, strips, transforms, and coefficient lookups | Cache configuration-only quantities and reuse buffers while keeping stage-dependent state fresh |
| Native adapter/configuration guards | Already present and correctness-sensitive | Measure full production overhead; do not infer app speed from a kernel-only microbenchmark |
| Rendering/presentation | Not measured by this audit | Profile actual target GPU, camera, transparency/smoke, shadows, and scenery before changing physics |

Sources: recorder/statistics guides, detector, native evaluator guide. [C03][C04][C21][C25]

Never cache a quantity across RK stages if it depends on instantaneous state, fuel/CG, actuator position, wake history, or wind. Never let background work mutate active physics data. Preserve the stage transaction and rollback guarantees.

### 9.3 Optimization acceptance

Every optimization should publish:

- exact baseline/candidate identities;
- the same scenario and comparable hardware conditions;
- warmup and repeated samples;
- meaningful end-to-end timing, not only a selected function;
- source/backend equivalence checks;
- any numerical difference and its accepted bound;
- recorder/statistics integrity under the new scheduling behavior.

Stop optimizing when the measured target is met and no concrete bottleneck remains. Further architectural work should have a separate justification.

## 10. Validation program and evidence collection

### 10.1 Establish one reference configuration

Create a configuration sheet for the chosen A200/P100 installation:

- geometric datum, span/chords, wing/tail incidence, dihedral/sweep, hinge locations and gaps;
- actual aileron differential, elevator/rudder travel, flap neutral/detents;
- component masses, dry/filled mass, fuel density and fill volume;
- CG at empty, intermediate, and full usable fuel;
- inertia estimates with uncertainty, preferably supported by a suitable physical measurement;
- engine/ECU/tailpipe/intake and brake/retract hardware;
- radio firmware, rates, expo, mixes, gyro/assist state;
- test density, wind, runway/surface, and measurement uncertainty.

A geometric mesh match is useful but does not establish aerodynamic geometry, mass distribution, or hinge effectiveness. Keep physical and visual evidence connected without conflating them.

### 10.2 Maneuver cards

Use the existing flight-card and accepted-tape workflow. The following cases extend it with discriminating outputs rather than creating a second test framework.

Do not require identical trajectories for different controllers or fuel loads. Require the differences to be explained by the actual commands and physical configuration.

### 10.3 Numerical and software gates

**Smooth dynamics:** compare appropriate 120/240/480 Hz probes where the model permits them; expect meaningful convergence for smooth states. Do not demand ideal fourth-order convergence across contact, saturation, or discontinuous events.

**Events:** compare event times, impulses, fuel exhaustion, actuator completion, and contact transitions with appropriate tolerances. Include failed-stage rollback and checkpoint continuation.

**Invariants:** finite state, normalized attitude, no uncommanded energy creation by passive components, correct symmetries, and consistent force/moment reference transfer.

**Native/reference:** require actual native execution, not matching fallback output. Test changed branches with the native identity recorded.

**Regression:** use the smallest relevant existing suite. Broaden only when a shared-law change creates a concrete risk to other aircraft or a required gate demands it.

Suggested initial engineering tolerances should be agreed before evaluating candidates and labeled as provisional. A useful starting principle is that numerical differences should be substantially smaller than the smallest physical effect the experiment is meant to distinguish. Avoid arbitrary tolerances that are either too loose to detect the defect or tighter than the model’s uncertainty.

### 10.4 Physical identification and held-out validation

Collect at least repeated runs for each fitted condition and retain variation. Estimate a small identifiable parameter group at a time. Fit attached-flight data before trying to fit high-angle behavior.

Reserve independent speeds, fuel states, maneuver directions, and some pilot runs for validation. Report residuals against sensor and setup uncertainty, not only a single aggregate score.

Examples of useful held-out checks:

- fit roll response at two speeds, predict a third;
- fit clean drag, predict a different mass/trim condition;
- fit engine steps at moderate RPM, test a large idle-origin step separately;
- fit one brake-demand range, predict another;
- fit clean trim, then evaluate flap compensation without refitting elevator effectiveness.

If multiple parameter combinations fit equally well, report that non-identifiability and obtain another discriminating measurement.

### 10.5 Definition of improved feeling

A candidate improves realism only when the relevant physical observations support it. A candidate improves usability when controlled pilots complete the same tasks more predictably or prefer it for stated reasons. Both may matter, but they must be reported separately.

Require a short decision record: baseline complaint, hypothesized cause, changed parameter/law, objective effect, pilot feedback, tradeoffs, and remaining uncertainty.

- Checkpoints, rollback, fuel, actuators, and recordings remain consistent.

- Landing statistics remain correctly available after reviewed geometry changes.

### B. Numerical credibility

- Moment reference identities and units are documented and tested.
- Smooth-flow and event/refinement checks are appropriate to their regimes.
- Passive ground/brake components do not generate unexplained energy.
- Regime changes have explained force/derivative behavior.
- The fixed simulation cadence is independent of render timing within declared catch-up behavior.

### C. Physical identification

- The exact A200/P100 installation and mass/CG state are specified.
- Key parameters retain measured/derived/estimated provenance and uncertainty.
- Lift/drag/trim, control response, propulsion, and ground behavior are compared with independent evidence.
- Held-out maneuvers are reported.
- Unidentified behavior remains explicitly experimental.

### D. Pilot and presentation acceptance

- Relevant pilots evaluate matched configurations.
- Input policies and assistance are visible and recorded.
- Speed/energy cues and frame pacing are evaluated separately from aerodynamics.
- Reports distinguish realism, controllability, preference, and remaining disagreement.

### E. Performance and release

- Actual backend and binary/source identities are recorded.
- Target-machine 1080p frame pacing and simulation throughput meet an agreed budget.
- Recording and realistic statistics-history operations do not cause unacceptable stalls.
- The exported application route, controls, audio/visual cues, and replay are checked.
- Public performance claims match the evidence and configuration.

A green unit test is necessary evidence for its assertion. It is not a substitute for the other four streams.

## Appendix B. Evidence gaps that most constrain realism

| Unknown | Why it matters | Best next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Actual wing/tail sections, incidence, twist, and hinge geometry | Lift slope, separation, control authority and flap interaction | Survey a matched A200; retain dimensions and uncertainty |
| Loaded mass, CG versus fuel, and inertia | Trim, pitch sensitivity, roll acceleration, energy | Build inventory, weighing/balance, suitable inertia measurement |
| Neutral point and dynamic derivatives | Static stability and release behavior | Small controlled perturbations at several speeds/CGs |
| Installed P100 thrust and transient response | Acceleration, approach/go-around, vertical energy | Timestamped bench/installation traces with ECU configuration |
| Multi-speed drag and gear/flap increments | Level speed, idle glide, loop/dive energy | Matched flight trajectories and propulsion observations |
| Flap zero and compensation ownership | Trim and power/flap coupling | Rigging survey and actual channel/surface recording |
| Tire/contact/brake behavior and surface | Takeoff, rollout, steering and holding | Loaded wheel and matched runway measurements |
| Aircraft-specific stall and recovery | Wing drop, roll authority, flare and accelerated stall | Controlled configuration-matched observations, not a generic section polar |
| Pilot expectation under the selected controller | Perceived precision, latency and authority | Structured radio/gamepad comparisons with recorded settings |

XFOIL can help form attached-flow section priors once the geometry is known. Its isolated two-dimensional airfoil analysis and separation limits do not provide a complete three-dimensional post-stall Avanti model. The Sandia NACA 0015 report supports the provenance of the surrogate data, including its measured/synthesized context; it does not identify the Avanti wing. [S13][S14]
