# AV-17-R1 aerodynamic audit

2026-10-14 · Software investigation of the SebArt Avanti S A200. The AV-06 aerodynamic inputs are estimates; these results do not validate a matched aircraft.

## Finding

The reproduced keyboard over-pull was caused by the generic virtual-stick ramp: it reached full input in 0.25 s (4 command units/s) before applying the Avanti's normal dual rate. Normal mode halves the manual ±30° elevator throw to ±15°; 40% expo softens intermediate stick positions but leaves the held endpoint unchanged. In the baseline live keyboard sampler, a 0.35 s full-up press from a 45 m/s, −45° dive reached 18.85° angle of attack and full strip stall. At 60 m/s, a 0.20 s press reached 11.21 g with outer-strip stall weight 0.64. This is an accelerated-stall mechanism: dynamic pressure makes a given elevator input produce more moment, and pullout load raises the required lift. Command shaping is in [commands.gd](../../../../app/input/commands.gd) and its live keyboard path is in [flight_session.gd](../../../../app/sim/flight_session.gd).

AV-17-R1 adds an aircraft-scoped keyboard virtual-stick response of 1.0 s neutral-to-full and 0.25 s full-to-neutral, applied only to live keyboard input in normal mode before the existing rates, expo and actuator limits. This timing is a keyboard handling estimate, not a pilot-input measurement or servo time. A half-second pull at 45 m/s now peaks at 7.28° and 5.21 g with the strips attached; a sustained two-second pull still stalls. Repeated 0.45 s press / 0.15 s release pulses recover the −45° dive in 2.221, 1.787 and 1.546 s from 29, 45 and 60 m/s respectively, with all strips attached. The change is in input response: the aircraft mass, CG, aerodynamic coefficients and stall data are unchanged. The reproducible checks are in [test_avanti_keyboard.gd](../../../../app/tests/test_avanti_keyboard.gd) and its [pull-up scenario](../../../../app/tests/avanti_pullup_base.gd).

## Airframe and pitch authority

AV-06 uses 11.705 kg flight mass, 0.7023 m² wing area and estimated clean CLmax 0.85, giving a model 1-g stall speed of 17.72 m/s. On that basis the scalar clean lift limits are 2.68 g at 29 m/s, 6.45 g at 45 m/s and 11.46 g at 60 m/s. The measured 11.21 g at 60 m/s is about 98% of that estimated limit. These are consequences of AV-06 inputs, not manufacturer load limits; the A200 manual warns against exceeding 250 km/h for flutter or structural risk but does not publish a g limit. See the [AV-06 derivation](../../../../research/avanti-s/av06/derivation.md) and [manufacturer audit](manufacturer-audit.md).

The modeled CG is the manual's 240 mm beginner position. AV-06's estimated neutral-point anchor gives 8.517% MAC static margin there (5.759% at the manual's 250 mm recommendation and 3% at 260 mm). Within that calibration, the CG is forward and statically stable; it does not indicate an aft-CG pitch-instability defect. The derived Cma about this CG is −0.407/rad. Cmde is −0.9546/rad at the aerodynamic reference; the normal ±15° endpoint corresponds to roughly 0.25 coefficient moment there, before moment-reference transfer. Elevator authority is therefore still substantial in normal mode.

The tail downwash repair is present in the current loader: downwash gradient 0.5088 gives free tail slope 3.4039/rad and effective elevator tau 0.5511. Its modeled wake lag length is about 0.88 m, so the convective first-order time constant is about 0.020 s at 45 m/s and 0.015 s at 60 m/s. The lag advances exactly as `CL_now + (CL_lag − CL_now) exp(−V·dt/L)`. I found no release sign or initialization defect; this lag is much shorter than the 0.25 s keyboard recenter. Tail stall itself remains provisional: the model blends from 12° to 24° using a borrowed flat-plate curve, so high-alpha recovery can lose tail authority, but no matched tail polar or recovery measurement establishes that as a bug. The implementation is in [aircraft_data.gd](../../../../app/physics/aircraft_data.gd), [aero.gd](../../../../app/physics/aero.gd) and [flight_session.gd](../../../../app/sim/flight_session.gd).

## Spanwise stall audit

The [static strip probe](../../../../research/avanti-s/av17-r1/spanwise_probe.gd) evaluates the current six-strip model at 45 m/s with neutral controls and no ground effect. At body alpha 8°, a tip strip has geometric/effective alpha 9.146°/6.718° and CL 0.651; the root strip has 6.854°/5.401° and CL 0.523. At body alpha 10.34°, the tip has 11.486° geometric versus 8.511° induced/effective alpha, CL 0.709 and stall weight 0.198; the center remains unstalled and the root has stall weight zero. By 12°, tip, mid and root weights are 0.785, 0.372 and 0.045. At the 14.34° blend end, mean wing-strip CL has fallen to 0.338.

`Aero._local_loads` applies the induced map to attached lift, but evaluates stall weight and the flat-plate branch on each strip's geometric angle. This makes the estimated wash-in drive tip-first stall. In a zero-incidence copy with the induced map rederived, the tapered planform alone gives tip/root CL 0.597/0.572 at 8° (about a 4.4% difference); the accepted ±0.02 rad station incidence gives 0.651/0.523 (about 24.5%). That is close to the [input's estimated 0.9/1.0/1.12 root-to-tip target](../../../../research/avanti-s/av06/inputs.json). The map accounts for taper but is explicitly planar and unswept, so the twist may encode the omitted sweep effect; the evidence does not establish double-counting.

The geometric-angle stall criterion is a model-closure choice, not a proven defect: `a1`/`a2` are solved from the whole-aircraft CLmax and blend-width inputs. Switching stall evaluation to induced/effective section alpha without recalibrating local onset would delay stall and change the integrated envelope. AV-06 has no measured A200 section polar, twist, or stripwise stall progression. Its 4° wing blend and flat-plate post-stall loss are estimates, with no dynamic-stall hysteresis. Keep these uncertainties distinct from the demonstrated keyboard input issue; do not raise CLmax or alter stall progression from this pull-up symptom alone.
