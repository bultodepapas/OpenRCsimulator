# CARF F4U-1D crash damage evidence

Reviewed 2026-10-25. Scope: component inspection and disposition evidence for the CARF F4U-1D 1:4.5 with a MOKI S250 and 30×18 three-blade propeller. This is a conservative decision aid, not a calibrated failure model. It sets no impact-energy or severity cutoffs and assigns no damage probabilities.

## Configuration and evidence limits

The [CARF F4U-1D manual](https://carf-models.com/files/product/34/ed/06/corsair_manual.pdf) identifies a three-part wing with a folding mechanism and composite construction. It describes sandwich foam, fiberglass skins, wood structure and carbon parts in the wing/gear-door assembly. Its build guidance says to inspect exposed glue joints and describes using epoxy and small fiberglass patches during assembly; it is not a post-crash structural repair manual. The manual also emphasizes close gear fit, free cycling, aligned rails and freely moving gear doors. The simulator uses folding wings; priced service parts must still match the installed kit revision and paint.

The primary [MOKI 250 product page](https://www.mokimotor.de/produkte/motoren/moki-250/) identifies the 250 cc five-cylinder radial and recommends a three-blade SEP 30×18. The separately named [MOKI 250/7](https://www.mokimotor.de/produkte/motoren/moki-257/) is a seven-cylinder model; its page also recommends a three-blade SEP 30×18. Confirm the installed engine nameplate and manual before attaching a replacement price or inventory identity. MOKI links an English S250 manual on its product page, but the PDF download redirect was not accessible to the browser used for this review; the accessible product page establishes the recommended propeller but does not provide a post-strike procedure.

The 30×18 size alone does not identify material or manufacturer. A [SEP retailer listing](https://www.aeroscale.shop/products/sep-propellers-2-blade-beech-sipo-black-scale-blade) describes SEP propellers as laminated beech/Sipo wood. A separate [Biela listing](https://www.pp-rc.de/BIELA-PROPELLER/THREE-BLADE/SEMISCALE/3-blade-CFK-propeller-Biela-30-x-18-Semiscale.htm?ProdNr=A3069&a=article&p=257&shop=pp-rc_en) describes a 30×18 three-blade carbon-fiber propeller for MOKI 250. Record the installed propeller brand, SKU and material before applying a maker-specific repair rule.

## Disposition rules

“Inspect” means an unresolved condition that requires a simulated assessment. It is not itself proof of breakage, a repair action, or a priced purchase. Charge a repair or replacement only after the simulated inspection explicitly records that outcome and an applicable price is known. Use `unknown` when the part, material, damage extent or repair rule is unresolved.

| Contact or suspected damage | Initial result from contact alone | Evidence-based follow-up disposition | Cost treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Propeller strike, any blade material | Inspect propeller, hub, bolts and spinner. A strike event alone does not establish the visible defect, internal damage or exact prop model. | For a confirmed defect, follow that propeller’s instructions. BMFA model-safety guidance says damaged propellers must not be used. The [Mejzlik carbon fixed-pitch manual](https://www.mejzlik.eu/files/qw-b20003201en-propeller-maintenance-and-repair-manual-of-mejzlik-fixed-pitch-propellers.pdf) allows small surface scratches; it says cracks or damage beyond scratches are unacceptable, blades cannot be repaired, and the propeller must be replaced. Its rule applies only to Mejzlik propellers. A wood propeller may have different maker-approved limits; without its exact manual, keep the outcome unknown until inspected. | `inspect` has no assumed parts charge. `replace` only after the inspection confirms damage that the applicable maker does not permit to remain in service. Do not charge a full airframe or engine for a prop defect. |
| MOKI S250 engine and radial mount after prop strike or nose impact | Inspect engine mount, firewall, prop hub/fasteners and external engine hardware. Keep internal condition unresolved until evidence is supplied. | MOKI’s accessible S250 guidance covers routine maintenance, not a crash teardown/return-to-service rule. Its operating instructions call for periodic checks of external fasteners and valve clearance. These support checking those items; they do not support automatically replacing the engine or internal parts after every prop strike. If engine function, rotation, mount integrity or internal condition cannot be established, disposition remains `inspect`/`unknown`. | No engine replacement charge from contact or aircraft severity alone. Bill a confirmed, priced engine or service-part replacement only after an explicit assessment. No applicable MOKI post-strike labor or repair quote was found. |
| Fiberglass skin, foam sandwich, wood spar/joint, fuselage or tail | Inspect the local contact area and adjacent structure, including internal bonded joints where the hit can load them. Distinguish finish scratches from skin cracks, crushing, puncture, delamination, core damage and joint separation. | Repair only when a material-compatible repair is explicitly selected and supported for the exact construction. Replace a discrete part only when the damage is confirmed and that part is declared unrepairable. Otherwise retain `unknown`. The [FAA composite repair AC](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_43-214A.pdf) warns that composites can rebound and mask impact damage, and recommends additional inspection after teardown. This is full-scale aircraft guidance, not an RC repair authorization or a CARF-specific threshold. | Price the confirmed affected part/repair recipe once. A skin or joint inspection does not imply replacement of the complete aircraft. The CARF assembly instructions are not post-crash repair data. |
| Main/tail retract, strut, wheel, rails, doors, hinges or hydraulic actuation | Inspect the struck gear assembly, attachment rails, adjacent wing/fuselage, linkages, doors and lines; cycle the gear and check for binding, looseness, misalignment and leakage. | Repair or replace the specific failed component after evidence. A cracked or bent structural gear member, loose mount or failed actuator needs a diagnosed component-level result; otherwise remain `inspect`/`unknown`. CARF's manual supports free movement/alignment checks. The [FAA landing-gear inspection guidance](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/advisory_circular/ac_43.13-1b_w-chg1.pdf) lists cracks, fastener condition, loose joints, leaks and smooth operation for retracts. It is a checklist analogy, not an RC failure threshold. | Do not replace both retracts, the whole wing, or all doors based only on crash severity. Cost only the failed part and selected repair labor. |
| Wing-fold joints, pins, actuators and adjacent center/outer wing structure (if fitted) | Inspect the fold joint, locking/drive hardware, alignment, local skin/core and bonded structure. | `Repair` only for a defined compatible repair; `replace` only a confirmed damaged unit or part. If wing-fold fit or structural integrity cannot be established, retain `unknown` and do not represent the airplane as serviceable. CARF calls out the three-part folding wing, but supplies no impact limits. | No automatic replacement of all wing sections. Determine exact kit/ARF fold hardware and price coverage first. |
| Servos, mounts, horns, linkages, hinges or control surfaces | Inspect only units in the contact/load path: case and mounting tabs, output spline/backlash, gear noise or skipped motion, leads/connectors, linkage and hinge/horn security; function-check centering and full travel after freeing any jam. | Broken gears with an available compatible gear set can be `repair`; failed electronics, cracked cases or unserviceable components can be `replace` after confirmation. [Hitec’s manufacturer crash FAQ](https://hitecrcd.co.jp/support/faq-d-h-p-07/) says crash-damaged servo gears may be repaired by replacing gears or the servo. That describes Hitec service options, not the identity or repairability of the Corsair’s installed servos. | No all-servos-replace rule. Bind the affected servo to the actual installed SKU and use the matching gearset/service price if repair is confirmed. |
| Canopy, cowl, spinner, cowling tabs and other non-structural fittings | Inspect for cracks, detached tabs, sharp fragments and interference with controls, propeller or cooling airflow. | Cosmetic finish damage can be recorded separately. A cracked or interfering part is repaired or replaced only after diagnosis; unresolved condition stays `unknown`. | Use a discrete part cost. Do not count the complete airframe price for a replaceable cowl or canopy. |
| Receiver, ignition, batteries, wiring, fuel or hydraulic plumbing exposed by impact/water/fire | Inspect displaced mounts, connector damage, crushed cases, broken wires, leaks and reliable operation before reuse. | Confirmed failure can be repaired or replaced at the identified component level. Where safe operation or exact condition cannot be established, quarantine as `unknown`; the crash event alone does not prove every electronic item failed. | Do not deduct salvage value or claim reuse until an explicit inspection outcome supports it. |

For RC propeller policy, the [BMFA handbook](https://handbook.bmfa.org/13-general-model-safety) says damaged propellers must not be used and should be replaced if not in good condition. Vogelsang Aeroscale's [SEP/Fiala seller safety instructions](https://www.aeroscale.shop/pages/disclaimer) are stricter: inspect after every flight and discard a propeller with nicks, scratches or other visible defects; do not repair or modify it. Apply that latter rule only when the installed product and its accompanying instructions match. It is a seller instruction, not evidence that every 30×18 wooden or carbon propeller shares identical repair limits.

## Recommended simulation boundary

Maintain separate records for (1) contact and affected economic component, (2) required inspection, and (3) confirmed physical condition/disposition. Contact mapping may add an `inspect` notice for the locally affected fuselage, wing/gear, propeller, control or engine-mount group. It must not infer hidden damage, breakage, replacement quantity or a cost from the contact label or a visual crash-severity class. An inspection receipt can later set `repair`, `replace`, `reuse` or `unknown` with evidence and a compatible price basis.

The repository already follows most of this boundary. [`incident.gd`](../../../../app/economy/incident.gd) marks mapped non-propeller contacts for inspection and adds an unresolved structural inspection; it does not replace every item based on severity. [`calculator.gd`](../../../../app/economy/calculator.gd) sets `total_minor` to `null` when the assessment is incomplete, and [`accident_report.gd`](../../../../app/ui/accident_report.gd) labels partial bounds/subtotals as incomplete. No present rule here treats an incomplete known subtotal as the full repair bill. The canonical [CR-28…38 economics plan](../../../plans/crash-damage-economics.md) separates component condition from disposition and states that unknown essential prices prevent an exact total. The dated [CR-39 implementation audit](../../../research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-39/README.md) records the complete-dossier ceiling and explicit incomplete-dossier handling.

Pre-change defect, corrected by F4U-11-R2 in both observation and checkpoint reconstruction: [`incident.gd`](../../../../app/economy/incident.gd) sets the propeller component to `replace` in `_mark_propeller()`, then applies `inspect` to every ID in `propeller_inspection_ids`. If that list includes the propeller itself, the later write overwrites the replacement. The generator now keeps these IDs separate and runtime guards against a self-reference. The new regression deliberately adds a legacy self-reference to verify that it cannot downgrade replacement. In addition, the current prop-strike policy assumes a confirmed strike means the propeller is already damaged. Sources support replacing a *damaged* propeller, but do not establish that every registered contact has damage; F4U-11-R2 preserves that existing conservative simulation rule; it does not establish an empirically calibrated contact-to-fracture threshold.
