# Five-metre RC blimp: physics research

**Date:** 2026-11-08. **Scope:** a generic, helium-filled, free-flying RC blimp specified as **5 m long**; envelope versus overall length is still to be resolved at intake. The user will provide its visual model later. Diameter, gas volume, mass, center of gravity (CG), propulsion, control layout and operating conditions are unknown. The [integration plan](../../plans/rc-airship.md) owns proposed implementation steps. This is research, not an implemented model, a measured replica or physical validation. No coefficients, motor layout or neutral buoyancy condition follow from length alone.

Existing repository research contained no airship-specific report when searched. The [physics lessons](../../learnings/physics.md) are relevant: retain rigid and apparent inertia separately; calculate work using local relative flow; identify geometry and coefficients for the actual vehicle; and check coupled states at the same integration stages. The [wind reference](../../WIND-ATMOSPHERE-REFERENCE.md) describes existing environmental work, not airship validation.

## Hydrostatics and mass properties

Use an inertial/world frame `W`, a body frame `B`, and `R` that maps body vectors to world vectors. Take world gravitational acceleration `g_W` as a vector pointing down. For ambient density `rho_a`, displaced air volume `V_disp` and total *actual* mass `m` including helium, the hydrostatic loads are

```text
F_b,W = -rho_a V_disp g_W          F_g,W = m g_W
tau_CB,B = (r_CG - r_CB) x (R^T F_g,W)
             + other forces' moments about CB.
```

Here the hydrostatic buoyancy resultant acts at the center of buoyancy `CB`; the displayed torque takes moments about `CB` and assumes approximately uniform density over the craft. Its rigid mass/inertia includes envelope, hardware, payload, ballast and lifting gas. Equivalently, sum moments about CG and use `(r_CB-r_CG) x R^T F_b,W`. Do not subtract helium from buoyancy **and** include helium in total weight: that counts helium twice. Count the volume displaced by the inflated envelope and, if relevant, by other immersed components without double counting their volumes.

At a specified fill state, `m_He = rho_He V_He` and an ideal pure-gas approximation is `rho_He = p_He M_He / (R_universal T_He)`, using absolute pressure and temperature. Ambient density needs the local atmospheric state and, when relevant, humidity; gas purity and balloonets change the contents. A 5 m length alone does not determine volume or lift. A fixed mass/volume, constant-temperature approximation may be suitable for short tests but must be declared. A compliant envelope, gas leakage, venting, pressure, heating and volume change require their own measured model; blindly recomputing `rho_He V_He` while holding an inconsistent combination of pressure, temperature and volume fixed can create gas mass.

Neutral buoyancy means `rho_a V_disp = m`, not zero mass or zero inertia. Even if the net force vanishes, a CB above CG produces a restoring pitch/roll couple when tilted; an offset along the hull creates static trim. For a small angular displacement `theta` and a vertical CG-to-CB separation `d`, the restoring moment magnitude is approximately `B d theta`, with `B = rho_a V_disp |g_W|`, for the corresponding tilt. Free translation couples into the oscillation: a simple `sqrt(B d / I)` frequency only applies to the relevant constrained/decoupled effective inertia, not automatically to an unconstrained 6-DOF vehicle. The net vertical force can be a small difference of large terms, so report fill, ballast and density uncertainties rather than forcing an exactly neutral state.

## Six degrees of freedom and ambient wind

The full formulation should use a rigid-body mass/inertia matrix plus a **separate** added-air matrix and the associated velocity-coupling terms. Added mass is not extra physical weight and must not be applied twice as both force and inertia. A symmetric positive semidefinite added-mass matrix and a positive-definite total generalized mass matrix are useful admission checks; units differ across translational, coupling and rotational blocks. Shape, gas envelope condition and the reference point matter. An ellipsoid estimate is a documented preliminary assumption, not a measured property; record coefficient provenance and uncertainty.

The following bounded equations transcribe the form in Peddiraju et al., Eqs. 11 and 23–25, with notation adapted here. They are a useful **oracle for a fixed, symmetric envelope**, not a general formula for asymmetric added-mass cross-couplings, deforming hulls, strongly sheared flow or spatially rotating ambient air. At CB, let `v` and `omega` be ground-relative body velocity and angular velocity; `c` be the CB-to-CG vector; `C a = c x a`; `Omega a = omega x a`; `w = R^T w_W(t)` be spatially uniform wind; and `u = v - w`. Let `A` and `J_A` be the constant translational and rotational added-air matrices about CB, `J` the actual rigid-body inertia about CB, `M = m I + A`, `J_e = J + J_A`, and `D = rho_a V_disp I + A`. Let `a_w = R^T d(w_W)/dt` be the world/inertial time derivative of this uniform wind **expressed in body axes**. Then

```text
f_rhs   = -Omega M v + Omega m C omega
          + Omega D w - D Omega w + D a_w + F
tau_rhs = -m C Omega v - Omega J_e omega - u x (A u) + tau

[ M    -m C ] [ v_dot     ]   [ f_rhs   ]
[ m C   J_e ] [ omega_dot ] = [ tau_rhs ]

x_CB_dot,W = R v                   R_dot = R Omega
```

`F` contains non-added-mass aerodynamic and thrust loads plus `(m - rho_a V_disp) R^T g_W`; `tau` contains their moments about CB plus `c x (m R^T g_W)`. The `-u x (A u)` term is the Munk moment in this formulation: do not add a second copy in an aerodynamic hull model. `A`, `J_A`, the rigid-body parallel-axis shift, signs and cross terms must all be expressed at the same reference point. If geometry creates translational/rotational added-mass cross terms, derive the general 6×6 momentum formulation instead of applying this restricted example unchanged.

The body-frame wind derivative is not merely `R^T d(w_W)/dt`: `w_dot = -omega x w + R^T d(w_W)/dt`. Even a uniform steady world wind changes its *body components* as the body rotates. The displaced-air contribution `rho_a V_disp I` in `D` also makes simply replacing ground speed by airspeed in a no-wind added-mass equation an incomplete accelerating-wind model. For spatially varying wind, the derivative sampled **along the craft** includes `(v_W · grad)w_W`, while ambient-fluid material acceleration includes `(w_W · grad)w_W`; they are not interchangeable. The uniform-wind equations do not establish the pressure-gradient and rotational-flow loads in a sheared gust. Local loads may sample the available wind field at their own locations; a complete added-mass treatment in gradients needs a separately derived and validated model.

These constant-property equations also assume constant ambient density. The initial plan admits uniform atmosphere. Scaling added mass with altitude-dependent density requires a consistent variable-property momentum law or a quantified approximation; the constant matrices above cannot simply be replaced with stage-varying matrices without examining the additional terms.

## Hull, fins, thrusters and controls

At a body-fixed station `r_i` from CB, use local vehicle velocity relative to the air, including body rotation and any modeled induced flow:

```text
u_i,B = v + omega x r_i - R^T wind_W(x_CB + R r_i, t) - induced_i,B
```

For a dissipative component, a provisional drag law such as `F_D = -0.5 rho_a C_D A_ref |u_i| u_i` has non-positive work against `u_i`; component-axis drag may be used when its axes, fitted coefficients and limits are explicit. The wind can do positive work in the world frame. Hull axial/cross-flow drag, moments and rotational damping depend on shape, angle, Reynolds number, support/wake interference and, potentially, rate. `Re = rho_a |u| L_ref / mu` uses the appropriate hull length or local fin chord; propeller blade Reynolds number uses its own characteristic chord and rotational/inflow speed. A single full-scale drag constant, a fixed-wing polar or a fin coefficient is not evidence for this 5 m hull.

Cover zero velocity, sideslip, high angle of attack and reverse motion explicitly. A table or smooth bounded force law must retain sensible signs and continuity at zero and across its declared 360° domain; do not evaluate undefined normalized direction or angles at zero speed. A low-speed linear damping term is a hypothesis unless measured, and simple independent axial/crossflow fits do not prove separated-flow accuracy. The Peddiraju 360° hull method is an approximation for its own shape. If a hull load already includes the inviscid Munk term, remove it from one location before combining models.

Fin force and moment use local air-relative dynamic pressure, the actual surface orientation, area, deflection and lever arm. Their conventional free-stream authority tends to zero with local airspeed; wind, rotation or an overlapping propeller slipstream can still provide flow at low ground speed. The slipstream and hull wake must be accounted for once and their coverage is configuration-dependent. Sky Probe-J's ineffective fins are a warning about wake and scale, not proof all 5 m blimp fins are ineffective. Reverse flow and stalled controls cannot reuse a forward small-angle slope without evidence.

For each installed thruster, define its position `r_i` relative to CB, signed axis `e_i`, and measured thrust `T_i`:

```text
F_prop = sum(T_i e_i)       tau_prop = sum(r_i x (T_i e_i) + Q_i)
```

`Q_i` is motor/propeller reaction torque if represented. A servo changes `e_i` subject to travel, rate and any measured lag. Independent motor commands yield common and differential thrust through their **actual** locations and signs. A tail thruster, vertical thruster, vectoring pair or aerodynamic rudder are alternatives to establish from the model and controller; none is implied by length. Finless thrust-controlled vehicles can swing because thrust, CG and CB are separated; they need not exhibit airplane-like response. Ground-relative motion is not a substitute for thruster inflow.

For a known propeller, `T = rho_a n² D_p⁴ C_T(J, Re, ...)` with `n` in revolutions/s and `J = V_axial/(n D_p)` is useful away from zero RPM within its measured operating domain. Near zero RPM, reverse flow, reverse rotation and windmilling need a separately defined bounded branch or measured map: the advance-ratio expression is singular and reverse thrust is not necessarily the negative of forward thrust. Installed interference, different propeller camber, ESC deadband and saturation can make forward/reverse behavior asymmetric.

Keep sampled commands distinct from actuator state. Use measured servo and motor response, or an explicitly provisional bounded lag, rather than instantaneous thrust. Battery voltage, load, state of charge, propulsion current and protection/cutoff can affect available thrust; couple existing model components only when their supported assumptions match the identified hardware. An ESC may be forward-only, directly reversible, or require a brake/neutral sequence; no mode or delay should be assumed solely from a generic blimp label. Any coupled continuous actuator states must be evaluated at each solver stage and included in reset, checkpoint and replay. The microblimp's observed one-second response is a reason to measure this, not a transferable parameter.

## Recommended staged fidelity and evidence

1. **Specify the instance.** From the forthcoming model and any physical information, establish envelope shape and external displacement, fin/actuator positions and axes, CG/CB, ballast, battery, expected transmitter mapping and intended indoor/outdoor conditions. Record inferred geometry separately from measured hardware. Identify whether the object is neutrally buoyant, intentionally heavy, or another configuration. Use a named generic estimated configuration only if unknown values are explicitly marked as estimates, with ranges and provenance.
2. **Build and verify an isolated baseline.** Add hydrostatics, full 6-DOF rigid motion and a separate estimated added-air matrix in a vehicle-owned model under the shared kernel contracts. Use fixed gas mass/fill and still air first. A bounded hull force model and only the actual declared actuators are sufficient for an initial controllable experiment. This is a software-verified hypothesis, not a realism claim.
3. **Identify dominant behavior.** Measure static lift/net ballast and CG location; use repeatable pitch/roll displacement and release; restrained motor thrust/torque, step, reverse and vector-servo sweeps; and powered/unpowered translation and yaw releases with measured applied inputs. Compare independent repeated fit and held-out runs. The Tao work shows how known added weights and repeated motion tracking can separate some effective mass and damping; observability is setup-dependent. Oscillation periods and decay alone can identify combinations of restoring moment, inertia and damping rather than all parameters independently. Measure actual mass/inertia separately and do not silently treat an in-air pendulum inertia as vacuum rigid inertia.
4. **Expand the operating envelope.** Fit forward, crossflow and reverse loads, local fin/slipstream behavior, thrust-vs-inflow, and where evidence supports it added-air rotational/coupling terms. Add uniform wind and its acceleration first with a declared model; treat gradient, turbulence, ground effect, deformation, fill/temperature dynamics and outdoor contact as distinct later studies. Indoor measurements reduce environmental uncertainty but do not validate outdoor gusts; outdoor validation needs simultaneous wind and location measurements.

### Discriminating verification before any fidelity claim

- **Statics and frames:** verify mass including gas, lift and trim from independent scalar force/moment balances; vary CG/CB orientation and check that both choices of moment origin give equivalent wrenches. A neutrally buoyant but bottom-heavy case must still right itself when tilted. Test slightly heavy and slightly light cases without contact.
- **Momentum and invariance:** with added mass off, recover the existing free rigid-body solution. For a symmetric fixed envelope, compare the restricted 6-DOF equations above against an independent transcription and timestep refinement; test the cross-coupled Munk sign and positive-definite mass matrix. In a steady uniform wind, add the same constant world velocity to craft and air and compare relative behavior. In time-varying uniform wind, use an independent analytic force/acceleration case that exercises both added and displaced air, including body rotation; do not freeze the wind derivative during intermediate integrator stages. A sheared-wind test does not validate a uniform-wind theory.
- **Dissipation and controls:** check aerodynamic work at each local air-relative velocity, finite behavior at zero speed, reversed-flow sign, force and moment from each motor, forward/reverse bounds, servo stops and no fictitious fin authority in stagnant air. Verify motor lag, battery coupling, checkpoint/reset and exact replay for the states actually implemented.
- **Measured acceptance:** compare timed, recorded six-axis motion, actual commands and environmental conditions with held-out physical runs, including pitch/roll swing, coast-down, yaw turn, vertical drift and reverse. Preserve mass, fill, temperature, battery, firmware, rates, craft geometry and uncertainty alongside recordings. After implementing and qualifying the airship recorder/replay adapter, compare one candidate with the same input tape using the discipline of the repository [flight-logging workflow](../../FLIGHT-LOGGING.md); the current fixed-wing CLI is not an airship replay implementation. Exact replay verifies software behavior only. No flight logs or measurements for the requested blimp were available for this report.
