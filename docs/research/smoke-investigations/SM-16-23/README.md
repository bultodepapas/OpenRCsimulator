### Live outlet attachment and draw-order decision

A same-draw 40 m/s, 30 FPS fixture follows an opaque nozzle marker with an orthographic camera. Each interval submits one particle update, awaits one frame/draw, then immediately reads the image, with no settling frames. V2 lifetime ordering produced **zero projected gap and 216 red pixels within 0.3 m of the outlet**. Overriding the same disposable fixture to the former view-depth policy produced **3.9167 m / 235 pixels of gap and no near-outlet red pixels**. See [fresh output](renderer/live-outlet-lifetime-fresh.png), [historical ordering comparison](renderer/live-outlet-view-depth-history.png) and the exact methodology in [native.json](renderer/native.json).

The [pinned GLES3 implementation](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/drivers/gles3/storage/particles_storage.cpp#L734-L778) explains the historical-buffer behavior. V2 now uses current-buffer lifetime ordering; v1 retains its historical path. This supersedes the initial plan's view-depth choice. It adds no draw pass or runtime wait. Four controlled red/blue/yellow crossing images compare both orders from opposing views under symmetric lighting: [lifetime front](renderer/ordering-lifetime-front.png), [lifetime back](renderer/ordering-lifetime-back.png), [depth front](renderer/ordering-view-depth-front.png), [depth back](renderer/ordering-view-depth-back.png).
