# D8b-R2 S01–S06: simulation verification and validation research

2026-10-11 · **Status: literature review complete; aircraft-specific validation remains open.**

This note develops the numerical and validation work proposed in the [Ugly Stik pull-up plan](../../../UGLY-STIK-PULLUP-PLAN.md), using the reproduced mechanism documented in [D8b-R2 evidence](README.md). Sources were reviewed on 2026-10-11. The literature supports methods and test design; it does not provide calibrated Ugly Stik coefficients. No new flight or application checks are claimed here. The separate [executed static experiments](research-experiments.md) supply the numerical evidence cited in S04 and S05.

## S01 — Can mass balance establish the full inertia tensor?

**Question.** Does a centered, statically balanced airplane justify the modeled product of inertia, especially when an off-center item or virtual counterweight dominates it?

**Finding.** Static balance locates the center of mass by checking first moments. Products of inertia depend on the distribution of mass around that center, so a balanced airframe can retain nonzero cross terms. NASA's small-UAV method recommends experimentally determining mass properties and estimates a principal-axis inclination by fitting measured moments about several axes; its demonstration used a UAV at Armstrong. That method is relevant as a measurement pattern, not as a Stik value or direct measurement of the same tensor plane. [NASA, *Determining the Products of Inertia for Small Scale UAVs*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20170000461)

The current diagnosis attributes roughly 69% of the modeled `Jxy` to virtual reference-build ballast. This does not show that the tensor algebra is wrong. It does show that the inventory represents an assumed build, and that matching total mass and CG alone cannot validate its `Jxy`.

**Decision.** Keep the full tensor in the equations. First identify the physical reference build and measure component masses and positions relative to a measured CG; recompute the tensor with the project's explicit axis and sign convention. Separately measure principal moments/products on the assembled airframe using a repeatable multi-axis pendulum or equivalent fixture, and compare the measured result with the inventory-derived tensor and its uncertainty. Replace virtual ballast only with a defensible installed mass configuration. Add checks for tensor symmetry, positive eigenvalues, inverse consistency, and analytical point-mass cases; include a reflected symmetric layout whose cross terms should cancel. A pure-axis moment test should confirm that the full inverse tensor produces the expected coupled angular acceleration.

**Limits.** NASA's paper demonstrates a principal-axis fit in the UAV's x-z plane; the present concern is `Jxy`. Its method must be adapted to the Stik's axes, fixture, and uncertainty. Neither the paper nor static balance establishes this aircraft's installed masses or products.

## S02 — Which propeller effects seed the lateral departure?

**Question.** Are rotor gyroscopic coupling, shaft reaction torque, P-factor, and propwash being treated as separate causes?

**Finding.** JSBSim's propeller reference describes reaction torque and gyroscopic moment as distinct moments. Its implementation models P-factor by shifting the thrust acting point with propeller incidence, which can create a separate yawing moment. NASA's high-incidence propeller wind-tunnel study measured off-axis forces and moments and found that oblique inflow can change more than axial thrust and shaft torque. Those are distinct physical mechanisms and should not be collapsed into a generic “prop effect.” [JSBSim FGPropeller reference](https://jsbsim-team.github.io/jsbsim/classJSBSim_1_1FGPropeller.html) · [NASA, *Investigation of High Incidence Angle Propeller Aerodynamics for Subscale eVTOL Aircraft*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210014010)

For this event, the plan's same-state no-gyro ablation leaves a large departure, so gyroscopic momentum is not required for that synthetic case. The separately trimmed glide also departs, but it changes RPM, power, and trim together; it cannot isolate any one powered-flight term. The Stik data currently enables no slipstream block, and the report identifies no P-factor model for it.

**Decision.** Build a moment ledger by source, then use paired, one-term-at-a-time ablations from the same state, trim, command history, engine state, and RPM: rotor momentum only; shaft reaction torque only; incidence-dependent thrust offset/P-factor only if implemented; then wake/downwash only if active. Recompute the full coupled response after each ablation and preserve the force and moment time histories. Test sign and limiting cases for each term. Retain any effect whose magnitude is supported by this airframe's propeller data; do not remove it as a symptom fix.

**Limits.** JSBSim is an implementation comparison, not Stik flight evidence. NASA's propeller study concerns a subscale eVTOL propeller and supplies no coefficients for this model. Its relevance is the separation of loads and the need for incidence-matched measurements.

## S03 — Do tick-rate traces prove temporal convergence of the sampled flight session?

**Question.** Do similar 240/480/960 Hz roll peaks establish RK4 accuracy for the keyboard, servo, aerodynamic, and downwash path?

**Finding.** Godot calls `_physics_process()` at a fixed tick rate, independently of rendered frames. NASA's temporal-convergence guidance says to examine sensitivity to time-step size. JSBSim's state-space overview makes a related assumption explicit: the input must be adequately approximated as constant during each integration interval. The current D8b-R2 result is useful end-to-end evidence that the departure persists as the session tick rate changes; it does not, by itself, establish fourth-order convergence or separate changes in command sampling from integration error. [Godot 4.7 physics processing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/scripting/idle_and_physics_processing.html) · [NASA temporal-convergence guide](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/tempconv.html) · [JSBSim state-space overview](https://jsbsim-team.github.io/jsbsim-reference-manual/formulation/overview/)

The session has sampled inputs, servos, RPM and tail-downwash auxiliary updates alongside continuously evaluated flight derivatives. The current sampled tail-downwash value is held during the body RK step; it must not be silently replaced with a continuous state in a numerical reference. Optional continuous transport states, where active, require their own stage-state evaluation. A tick-rate sweep can alter sample schedules as well as RK4's step.

**Decision.** Preserve one timestamped command history, including keyboard shaping and throttle. For an integrator-only check, keep the sampled servo/RPM/downwash update maps at fixed boundary times and refine only integration between those boundaries. Evaluate the body and any already-continuous states with the same RHS and held auxiliaries at each RK stage. Compare this hybrid system with an independent high-accuracy integration, such as SciPy `solve_ivp` with DOP853 and explicit per-state tolerances; split the reference at every discrete update or command discontinuity and apply the same boundary map. SciPy documents DOP853 as an eighth-order explicit Runge–Kutta solver and supports dense output for common-time comparisons. Converting a sampled state to a continuous lag is a separate model change, not this convergence check. [SciPy `solve_ivp`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.integrate.solve_ivp.html)

Report state/load errors and maneuver metrics at common times, plus observed refinement ratios over smooth intervals. Then run a separate end-to-end session sweep to measure effects from resampling input and servo policies. Use analytic component cases to check the independent RHS and integrator before comparing full trajectories. Do not require fourth-order error decay across an input step or nonsmooth saturation boundary.

**Limits.** DOP853 is independent as an integration algorithm, not automatically as a model; sharing the same RHS leaves model/code errors common to both. NASA's temporal page is written for CFD, so its refinement principle transfers while its quantitative practices need adapting to this sampled ODE and its events.

## S04 — Is the post-stall result stable to strip resolution and model uncertainty?

**Question.** Is the positive roll feedback a property of the modeled post-stall physics, or is its sign/severity materially affected by strip discretization or the assumed stall law?

**Finding.** NASA's verification guidance separates discretization error from model uncertainty; its spatial-refinement method estimates numerical uncertainty only when a sequence is in an appropriate convergence regime. Its validation guidance calls for comparing verified calculations with increasingly complex experiments and examining uncertain physical models. NASA TP-1589 shows why this matters near stall: measured roll damping and autorotation varied with wing-leading-edge configuration and spanwise separation, and forced-oscillation results were used to assess dynamic roll damping. These sources support refinement and physical sensitivity checks; they do not establish a strip count or coefficient set for the Stik. [NASA spatial-convergence guide](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/spatconv.html) · [NASA validation assessment](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/WWW/wind/valid/tutorial/valassess.html) · [NASA TP-1589](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19800004771)

The [E02 regenerated-map sweep](research-experiments.md#e02--can-strip-refinement-remove-the-departure-mechanism) spans alpha 0–25° at 25 m/s, neutral surfaces and zero base rates: 3, 6, 12, and 24 strips per side gave peak `dMx/dp` of 11.121, 10.357, 9.794, and 9.471 N·m per (rad/s), respectively; regeneration error against baseline was `1.45e-10`. The feedback remained positive while its magnitude moved about 15%. Each mesh re-solves section slope to retain the attached calibration, so this is not strict fixed-constitutive-input spatial convergence.

**Decision.** Compare 3/6/12/24 strips with conserved planform/area, consistent quadrature and regenerated maps at identical states spanning attached flow, stall onset, and post-stall. Distinguish a fixed-section-polar refinement from any re-fitted calibration. Track integrated lift/side force and rolling moment, `dMx/dp`, local stall weights, and complete pull/recovery metrics. Set an error band before tuning; use Richardson/GCI-style estimates only with fixed physical inputs and a defensible asymptotic trend. Separately sweep post-stall lift slope, transition width, spanwise onset, and any separation-lag candidate across documented evidence bounds. Report discretization and model-form uncertainty separately. A stable refined sign is evidence about the current equations, not validation of their severity.

**Limits.** NASA's grid method was developed for CFD meshes, not finite-wing strip quadrature. NASA's stall study used another aircraft and does not furnish Stik stall data. If the finest strip sequence has not reached an asymptotic regime, report the observed spread rather than a false extrapolated precision.

## S05 — What does the scalar `dMx/dp / Jxx` say about nonlinear stability?

**Question.** Can a frozen-state scalar roll derivative establish the stability or finite-time growth rate of the coupled pull-up?

**Finding.** No. `dMx/dp / Jxx` isolates one moment-rate path while holding other state components fixed. The simulator applies the inverse full inertia tensor, so angular accelerations couple across axes when products of inertia are nonzero. Aerodynamic loads also depend on local flow, angle of attack, sideslip, other body rates, control surfaces, and propulsion. NASA flight-dynamics analyses obtain aircraft modes from eigenvalues of the linearized equations of motion; NASA's stall study separately measured unstable roll damping near stall. Those methods support a full coupled linearization and dynamic perturbation tests. Neither paper calibrates the Stik. [NASA dynamic flight-stability analysis](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230000002) · [NASA TP-1589](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19800004771)

For this particular scalar, [E03](research-experiments.md#e03--is-the-destabilizing-derivative-a-numerical-difference-artifact) changes the finite-difference perturbation from `0.01` through `0.000001` rad/s and obtains `dMx/dp` from 11.12062283 to 11.120703100. This suggests the reported scalar is locally insensitive to that step-size range. It does not convert the scalar into an eigenvalue or a finite-time growth rate.

**Decision.** At symmetric attached and near-stall reference states, construct the continuous Jacobian `A = ∂f/∂x` with scaled central differences over body state and any active continuous propulsion/wash states. Express attitude perturbations in a local three-axis rotation coordinate and renormalize the mapped quaternion; raw independent quaternion-component steps leave the unit-quaternion manifold. Also differentiate the sampled servo/RPM/downwash boundary maps to describe the complete hybrid system. Sweep step sizes and check analytic or independently implemented components. Compute modes at true equilibria using the applicable continuous or sampled transition operator. Along the pull, propagate the tangent system between boundaries and through their maps, or compare paired nonlinear runs from mirrored perturbations. Define state scaling and norm units explicitly; report growth against time, not only the scalar derivative or final attitude.

Test reflection symmetry first at the instantaneous load/derivative level for exactly mirrored state and geometry. Then test paired `+ε/-ε` seeds over finite horizons, retaining precision, seed, and configuration. If the symmetric mode is unstable, round-off or small physical asymmetry can eventually grow; require vector-field symmetry and report trajectory sensitivity, not indefinite bitwise symmetry through a departure.

**Limits.** A Jacobian eigenanalysis is local to its reference state; finite-time growth on a nonlinear, time-varying pull can differ from its frozen eigenvalues. Stall transitions and clamps can make derivatives step-size-dependent, so report step sweeps. The lead's scalar sweep addresses only one finite difference.

## S06 — What evidence can identify and validate a Stik correction?

**Question.** How should model fitting distinguish parameters that are observable in one pull from predictions that generalize to other maneuvers?

**Finding.** NASA-hosted research on low-cost fixed-wing flight tests describes designed and piloted input excitations, servo/instrumentation challenges, and nonlinear-model identification for small remotely piloted aircraft. NASA Langley's empirical-model framework describes validating with an independent flight-test maneuver, carrying parameter uncertainty, and using additional maneuvers to tighten the validation domain. NASA's UAS handling-qualities report connects task definitions to demonstration maneuvers and compares modeled responses with flight data. Together these favor deliberate excitation and held-out tasks rather than judging a model only on the trace used to tune it. [Simmons, *Advances in Aero-Propulsive Modeling for Fixed-Wing and eVTOL Aircraft Using Experimental Data*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230004001) · [Crespo et al., *A Formal Approach to Empirical Dynamic Model Optimization and Validation*](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140011905) · [NASA CR-2020-220564, UAS handling qualities](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20200002019)

**Decision.** Separate evidence by complete maneuver and configuration. Use static measurements and safe, bounded attached-flow tests to identify mass properties, trim, servo throw/rate, and attached derivatives. Use designed elevator/aileron/rudder excitations with enough independent variation to separate effects. Freeze those estimates before scoring held-out full pull-ups, releases/recoveries, and loops at different speeds and powers, including both turn directions. Preserve the exact command/servo history, engine state, airframe configuration, wind/density, sensor calibration, and build identity. Predefine time-history and outcome metrics such as `alpha`, `beta`, `p/q/r`, path deviation, peak load, altitude loss, and recovery time. Compare predicted and measured traces with measurement and parameter uncertainty; if several parameter sets fit the identification maneuvers but predict materially different held-out outcomes, retain that uncertainty or improve the model/data rather than selecting one fit as calibrated.

**Limits.** The small-aircraft method is relevant to an RC workflow but the cited research aircraft, sensors, pilots, and aerodynamics differ from the owner's Stik. The NASA validation framework's example is an F-16, and the UAS handling-quality report concerns broader mission categories. None replaces measured flight data for this installed aircraft. The current reproduction remains a synthetic simulator diagnosis; the original throttle, entry speed, and trace are unavailable.

## Synthesis for D8b-R2

Keep four claims separate in the follow-up: measured mass properties establish the modeled configuration; temporal and strip refinement establish numerical resolution; the full coupled Jacobian and paired perturbations describe local and finite-time sensitivity; held-out physical maneuvers assess agreement with the aircraft. The present positive post-stall roll feedback may be physically possible, while its Stik-specific magnitude and recovery remain unsupported. A candidate correction should retain uncertainty bounds and be accepted only against evidence that was not used to tune it.
