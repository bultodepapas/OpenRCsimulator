# EX-06: Primary-source audit for the GPMA0236 Extra 300S .60

Status: source audit complete. Numbers described as the baseline precede EX-06 implementation; see the [realism plan](../../../EXTRA-300-REALISM-PLAN.md) for execution status.

## Scope and sources

This note concerns the 64 in Great Planes GPMA0236, a 60-class, semi-scale, built-up balsa/lite-ply R/C sport model. It does not describe the full-size Extra 300S or a modern foam 3D model. The [Great Planes manual](https://www.greathobbies.com/manuals/g/gpm/gpma0236.pdf) identifies the kit and supplies the setup/handling guidance below. Printed page references point to the manual; the page links are a searchable transcription mirror. The original plan provenance and 64 in span are also indexed by [Outerzone](https://outerzone.co.uk/plan_details.asp?ID=10977).

Internal evidence reviewed: [EX-05 derivation](../../../../research/extra-300/ex05/derivation.md), [model-v1 geometry audit](../../extra-300-model-v1.md), and [resource inventory](../../extra-300-resources.md). EX-05 labels its aerodynamic coefficients as estimates or derivations, not flight measurements.

The pre-EX-06 baseline's configured 3.364 kg mass includes 87 g of derived virtual balancing mass. The manual's ready-to-fly, empty-tank CG condition is the setup datum; the loaded flight CG must be derived after adding fuel. Replace the estimated inventory with the measured mass/CG of the target build when available.

## Powerplant and propeller evidence

O.S.'s official [MAX-61FX specification page](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/english/line_up/engine/air/aircraft/catalog/17750.htm) gives 9.95 cc, 1.9 PS at 16,000 rpm, a practical range of 2,000–17,000 rpm, and 550 g. Its [suggested-propeller table](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/english/line_up/propelle/prop.htm) lists 12×6–8 and 13×6–7 for 61FX sport use and cautions that the ideal prop varies with the specific engine, model, and use. These specifications bound the engine, but they are not a full throttle-to-thrust map.

The current EX-05 propulsion approximation uses APC 12×6 with UIUC APC 11×6 measurements. UIUC [Volume 1](https://m-selig.web.engr.illinois.edu/props/volume-1/propDB-volume-1.html) has measured APC 11×6 propeller data; [Volume 4](https://m-selig.web.engr.illinois.edu/props/volume-4/propDB-volume-4.html) lists a 12×6 **Thin Electric** propeller. APC's [FAQ](https://www.apcprop.com/faq/) says a plain size denotes its Sport prop (intended for internal-combustion use), while “E” is electric-only, and says to take size guidance from the engine manufacturer. Thus the UIUC 11×6 is a proxy, and the UIUC 12×6E must not be substituted for a glow-engine APC 12×6 Sport prop.

The exact propeller fitted to Great Planes' .61FX prototype is not identified in the kit manual. A defensible next propulsion dataset needs the exact prop make/type and measured RPM (preferably thrust/torque across throttle and forward speed); the O.S. peak power rating or an 11×6 proxy cannot determine installed thrust. Until then, keep thrust and power-on yaw response explicitly provisional.

## Wing and thrust-axis evidence

The archived plan-derived [model-v1 audit](../../extra-300-model-v1.md) identifies a symmetric root section of about 13.1% thickness, zero dihedral, and zero wing incidence from the drawing. It finds no named airfoil and no section polar. These are geometry readings, not written manufacturer airfoil specifications. The manual's p.12 rib-angle gauge is a construction aid, and p.30 says to align the stabilizer parallel to the wing; neither supplies a measured aerodynamic polar. ([p.12](https://great-planes.manymanuals.com/air-equipment/extra-300s-60-kit-gpma0236/user-manual-44588/12), [p.30](https://great-planes.manymanuals.com/air-equipment/extra-300s-60-kit-gpma0236/user-manual-44588/30))

The original fuselage plan specifies 2° right thrust and ½° down thrust; the [resource audit](../../extra-300-resources.md) records this drawing evidence and notes the datum still needs a physical-frame definition. The pre-EX-06 baseline omits this axis offset. Applying the specified thrust vector at the propulsor, with its moment arm, is a high-confidence correction because it is explicit plan geometry. Keep reaction torque, gyroscopic precession, asymmetric blade loading, and propwash distinguishable: the manual's observed takeoff yaw does not quantify their individual contributions.

## High-confidence next realism targets

1. Preserve the plan's 2° right / ½° down thrust axis in physics as well as rendering; document the axis datum and verify thrust force, engine reaction torque, and rotor momentum use the same frame.
2. Encode the manual's two measured rate sets and empty-tank first-flight CG. Treat aft CG as the more agile/snappy setting and forward CG as the more stable, faster approach/takeoff setting.
3. Use the manual's low-rate takeoff/normal-flight and high-elevator snap/spin guidance as qualitative behaviour checks. Validate tail-up ground handling, powered rudder correction, shallow rotation, and approach with residual power in a complete flight scenario.
4. Keep EX-05 CLmax 1.05, 10.3 m/s 1-g stall, and 17 m/s trimmed in-air start marked provisional. EX-05 derives 1.05 from an assumed 13%-thick symmetric section at Re≈3×10⁵ with a 0.9 wing correction and no identified polar; 17 m/s is a rounded 1.6× estimated stall, not a manual recommendation. A model-specific low-Re polar or measured flight data is still needed before tightening stall/post-stall behavior.

## Unresolved / stale assumptions

- The manual does not name the wing section or provide CLmax, stall speed, control derivatives, takeoff speed, structural G/V limits, or a snap/spin entry envelope.
- Beyond the stall estimate, EX-05 still predicts rather than validates the strip/post-stall distribution, roll damping, and control/cross derivatives; it freezes several CL-dependent terms at the trimmed reference condition. Its CD0, Oswald efficiency, and tail/ground-contact parameters are estimates. See the derivation's “Known simplifications” before treating a handling trace as independent validation.
- Plan-derived symmetry, zero incidence/dihedral, and thrust angles are stronger than a generic airfoil guess, but section thickness, twist, washout, and the thrust datum should be checked against the original plan scan before changing physics values.
- EX-05's common 12×6 APC proxy plus UIUC 11×6 curve is not exact installed-prop evidence. O.S.'s 12×6–8 recommendation is a size range, not proof of prototype installation.
- Do not substitute full-size Extra 300S handling/performance limits or foam 3D model assumptions for this balsa/lite-ply kit. Reynolds number, inertia, control geometry, power loading, and construction differ; that comparison is a scaling inference, not a new numerical source.
- A retailer listing says 48 in fuselage length, while the plan/manual metrology recorded locally gives about 54¼ in. Treat that retailer field as a data-entry conflict; the model-v1 resource audit is based on the original plan/manual evidence.
