# OpenRC afterburner: shader and geometry investigations FX07–FX12

**Scope and provenance.** Read-only investigation of OpenRC commit `ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f`, the supplied standalone prototype [P1–P2], and the previous graphics investigation. The project's downloader names `4.8.beta2.official.cafc17a19`; Godot's release announcement confirms the commit [G1]. The exact beta2 engine source URLs could not be retrieved in this investigation. The Godot 4.7 manual and the explicitly identified 4.7.2 project research below are **adjacent-version references, not beta2 proof**. No Godot, renderer, target GPU or physical exhaust was tested. Proposed thresholds are starting points, not achieved acceptance results.

## FX07 — Choose a geometric representation that survives an orbit

**Question and method.** What is the lowest-complexity plume candidate that still reads from the rear, side and inside? Compare a tapered shell with an integrated brighter facing/core region; the existing separate outer/inner shells; two or three axial crossed cards; camera-facing axial ribbons or stacked slices; and a bounded analytic ray/volume proxy. Hold the nozzle, length, intensity, timeline and projected size constant. Inspect [P1–P2], Godot's cone/cap API [G2], and its culling and transparency descriptions [G3, G4].

**New finding.** The existing candidate is two separate, double-sided tapered `CylinderMesh` objects: the outer is open and the inner has a root cap. A side view can shade both sides of both cones, while a rear view exposes a different combination; a tip whose radius reaches zero and a small root cap do not by themselves guarantee a filled, readable rear silhouette. Two crossed axial cards are inexpensive geometry but can appear as spokes looking along the nozzle and as alternating sheets during an orbit. A camera-facing axial ribbon becomes ill-conditioned when view and axis align; view-aligned slice stacks avoid the pure edge-on case but add layers, sorting and camera-specific work. A ray-cone proxy could give continuous interior coverage, but evaluating an intersection and an integral or fixed-step march per covered pixel, including scene-depth clipping, is a materially different workload. None is universally cheapest without a target measurement.

**Proposed implementation.** Start with **one** capped-as-needed, double-sided tapered shell, using a fragment-facing mask to suggest a central hot region and a soft root/tip. Remove a cap only after rear/inside checks; add a separately masked, slightly inset rear disc or second core only if the one-shell view fails. Do not put a hard opaque disk across the tailpipe. Keep the optional ring/card and analytic proxy as comparison candidates, not required dependencies. As the supplied vertex mapping reverses winding, fix the mesh orientation before considering culling; FX09 covers this. A no-deformation comparison can reduce axial subdivisions, but preserve enough geometry if silhouette motion stays in the vertex stage.

**Acceptance and limits.** At 1080p, capture a continuous 360° orbit, rear-on, broadside, oblique, root-close and camera-inside positions against sky and aircraft, including a seam-facing angle. Require no missing center, hard root edge, abrupt card flip, protruding cap or unacceptable self-overlap; compare the same active footprint and scene while measuring actual draws, shaded coverage and native GPU time. A single shell is the **first candidate**, not an established performance winner or evidence of physical flame volume.

## FX08 — Compositing, view-angle brightness and layer count

**Question and method.** Compare additive, straight alpha and premultiplied alpha with controlled background colors, viewing angle and number of intersected surfaces. Inspect the supplied `unshaded, blend_add, depth_draw_never, cull_disabled` shader [P1] and the Godot blend, transparency and material references [G3–G5]. Use [E1] as an idealized one-pixel arithmetic counterexample, not as an engine test.

**New finding.** For ideal linear arithmetic with background `B=0.2`, source `C=0.8`, and 0.2 contribution per layer, additive `B + n*C*0.2` is **0.36, 0.52, 0.84, 1.48** for 1, 2, 4, 8 layers before clipping/tonemapping. Straight mix gives 0.32, 0.416, 0.55424, 0.69934 instead. Thus changing shell count or seeing both sides changes apparent brightness even if every material value is unchanged. Alpha mix and premultiplied composition are mathematically equivalent when the premultiplied color is `P=alpha*C`, but a hybrid premultiplied contribution can also add light without masking the background. Differently colored translucent layers remain order-dependent: E1's orange/blue layers change from `(0.338, 0.281, 0.4085)` to `(0.419, 0.272, 0.323)` when reversed. An additive source cannot obscure a bright background, so blue flame can wash out against the sky.

**Proposed implementation.** Use `blend_add` first for the small, emission-like baseline; compare `blend_mix` and `blend_premul_alpha` when background contrast requires it. Actual Godot outputs are `ALBEDO`, `ALPHA`, and optionally `EMISSION`; for premultiplied unshaded output supply already-associated color, and use `PREMUL_ALPHA_FACTOR` only as documented for shaded lighting. Confirm factors on beta2 rather than assuming a conventional framebuffer equation. For a homogeneous conceptual volume with total optical depth `tau`, split across `n` equal samples using `alpha_n = 1 - exp(-tau/n)` and `P_n = C*alpha_n`; same-color composite then has total alpha `1-exp(-tau)`. For pure additive tests divide intended total contribution by `n`. These are controlled approximations: a hollow shell's front/back hits, variable path lengths and two different colors do not automatically meet those assumptions, and inverse-cosine compensation must be capped at grazing angles.

**Acceptance and limits.** Compare one/two/four layers, black/gray/sky backgrounds, front/side views and opaque-airframe overlaps; record pixel values before and after output transforms where instrumentation permits. Require comparable intended color and no abrupt brightness jump when a side, cap or LOD appears. Sorting, nonlinear output, background clipping and unequal path lengths prevent a universal layer-normalization guarantee.

## FX09 — Nozzle-local coordinates, seams, normals and transforms

**Question and method.** How can the plume's coordinates and normals remain stable across imported mounts and authored deformation? Trace the prototype's vertex/UV equations [P1], OpenRC's existing smoke endpoint admission [O1–O2], and Godot's coordinate and normal contracts [G3, G6]. Analytically compare a smooth cone with the prototype-style normal using [E1].

**New finding.** The prototype maps native `(x,y,z)` to `(r*x,r*z,L*(y+0.5))`; for positive `r,L` its linear part has determinant `-r²L`, reversing triangle winding. Double-sided rendering masks this, but simply switching to `cull_back` can remove the intended side. Its `NORMAL` rescales an undeformed cone normal while its radius also varies with axial and angular wave; that omits wave derivatives. In E1's idealized continuous cone using the prototype's frequencies and amplitudes, the difference is **3.95°** at `t=0.5` and reaches **10.15°** on the sampled grid near the tip. This is not a measurement of Godot's discrete mesh. The integer-frequency `sin(TAU*k*UV.x)` waves are periodic at 0/1, but arbitrary nonperiodic texture noise would introduce a seam. A cap's UV layout should be inspected, not assumed to use the side's polar parameterization.

**Proposed implementation.** Preserve the established axle convention adapter: existing smoke reads rearward `-basis.z`, whereas this prototype uses `+Z` [O1–O2, P2]. For example, postmultiply the smoke basis by a 180° local-Y rotation so its +Z maps onto the smoke rearward axis; the rotation preserves handedness. Parameterize normalized axial `t` and angle `theta` before deformation; make noise periodic by sampling a continuous `(cos(theta), sin(theta), t)` field or a texture explicitly tiled in angle. For a smooth surface `P=(R*cos(theta), R*sin(theta), L*t)`, use `normalize(cross(dP/dtheta,dP/dt))` or an equivalent Jacobian-derived normal, with a stable fading/normal fallback where `R` approaches zero. Treat the root disk with its own axial normal and radial mask. Compute derivatives before divergent branches. Inspect beta2 `get_mesh_arrays()` for side seam duplicates, cap UVs, normals, and winding.

Existing smoke binding permits finite rigid **uniform positive** scale and rejects reflected, sheared and nonuniform transforms. Adopt an equally explicit afterburner admission policy instead of silently normalizing invalid imports. Apply the admitted scale **once**: either inherit it and keep dimensions in local units, or use a rigid basis and scale the authored dimensions, not both. Verify axes, scale 0.5/1/2, rejected reflection/shear/nonuniform cases, seam-aligned view and finite tip normals. No imported mesh or beta2 renderer was run.

## FX10 — Filter narrow bands and animated detail in screen and time

**Question and method.** How can shock-like art bands and breakup survive changing projected size without flashing, crawling or disappearing? Examine the prototype's high-frequency sinusoidal pattern [P1], the existing OpenRC RGB strip's derivative-aware pattern path [O3], and Godot's shader functions [G7]. Extend the earlier pixel-size arithmetic with an explicit filtering experiment.

**New finding.** `fwidth(x)` estimates `abs(dFdx(x))+abs(dFdy(x))` in fragment space. Godot 4.7 documents ordinary derivatives in fragment shaders and restricts the coarse/fine variants on Compatibility [G7]; those details still require beta2/backend compilation. The prototype's `pow(0.5+0.5*cos(TAU*5.2*along),10)` creates peaks much narrower than one full cycle, so knowing only that a plume spans several pixels does not establish that each peak is sampled. Derivatives can become unreliable across discontinuous `fract`, seam branches, discarded neighbors or highly foreshortened triangles; they cannot restore energy that has already alias-folded.

**Proposed implementation.** As pseudocode, start with continuous `t=along`, `cycles=N*t`, and calculate `q=fwidth(cycles)` **before** `fract`, `discard` or divergent control. For an individual band use `v=cos(TAU*cycles)-cos(PI*duty)` and `w=max(0.5*fwidth(v),epsilon)`; a softened candidate is `band=smoothstep(-w,w,v)`. Blend that towards its duty-cycle mean as cycles per pixel approach the sampling limit: `detail_weight=1-smoothstep(0.25,0.5,q)` and `filtered=mix(duty,band,detail_weight)`. The thresholds and implicit-edge width are art heuristics, not an exact integral; use a mipmapped periodic texture or analytic box integration if the sweep still fails. Limit the amplitude and frequency of procedural noise. Derive continuous bounded phases from the accepted presentation clock; fade fast modulation towards its mean as its temporal frequency approaches half the measured display sampling rate. Include apparent phase speed from camera and aircraft motion, not just oscillator speed; a wrapped phase delta can undercount complete turns. Never choose independent random values every frame. The temporal filter needs a conservative, stable FPS estimate and reset handling; it is not a replay authority.

**Acceptance and limits.** Capture a slow zoom and subpixel translation in both directions, seam crossing, rotation, and moving background at actual 1080p internal resolution plus reconstruction modes. Quantify normalized temporal luminance variance and band duty/mean in a fixed plume mask against a higher-resolution reference, and review 20/30/60 FPS, pause and seek. Predeclare tolerance for camera exposure and coverage changes. Verify material compilation and visual stability on each beta2 renderer; no such shader was rendered here.

## FX11 — Soft intersections and camera-inside behavior

**Question and method.** Can the flame meet the nozzle and foreground without a hard intersection or camera-inside flash? Compare a direct depth-tested shell, an optional depth fade and an explicit near-camera fallback using [P1], the 4.7 inverse-projection documentation [G8], and Godot's material proximity-fade contract [G5]. This is **flame intersection handling**, separate from the earlier optional screen-refraction haze investigation.

**New finding.** `depth_draw_never` prevents the plume from writing depth; it does **not** disable depth testing [G3]. Keeping that test is essential for an opaque wing or tail in front of the flame. A depth fade only softens intersections against information present in the depth texture; it cannot make transparent smoke/glass sort correctly, and two visible shell sides can still blend twice. A double-sided shell helps when a camera enters it but does not prevent the near plane from cutting it into a sudden screen-filling shape. Existing `BaseMaterial3D` proximity fade shows the feature's intent [G5], not proof that the supplied custom shader receives it automatically.

**Proposed implementation.** Only if the visible baseline needs it, request `uniform sampler2D scene_depth : hint_depth_texture, repeat_disable, filter_nearest`. Reconstruct the sampled surface with `INV_PROJECTION_MATRIX`: under the 4.7 docs, NDC is `(2*uv-1, 2*d-1)` for Compatibility and `(2*uv-1, d)` for Mobile/Forward+ [G8]. After homogeneous division set `scene_z=-view.z` and `fragment_z=-VERTEX.z` in default fragment view space; apply `smoothstep(0, fade_width, scene_z-fragment_z)` to the visible contribution when the depth is valid. This is a **view-axis** gap, not a fixed ray distance. For a fixed along-ray width use a projection-appropriate normalized ray, divide the gap by `max(-ray.z,epsilon)`, and cap grazing values; perspective rays vary with pixel while orthographic rays are parallel. Handle clear sky, tiny `w`, missing depth and viewport edges explicitly, preserving ordinary depth testing.

For camera entry, fade against the nearest view-depth of a conservative bounding sphere: if its view-space center is `c`, radius is `R` and near distance is `near`, use `smoothstep(near, near+margin, -c.z-R)`. The fade reaches zero before the sphere intersects the near plane, including when the camera is inside it; restore only after exiting the margin. This can fade early because the sphere is conservative. Keep face behavior, cap and AABB tests coupled to the chosen representation.

**Acceptance and limits.** On every supported beta2 backend first validate near/far/clear depths and both camera projections with known surfaces; the older depth-direction documentation conflict remains open. Test nozzle contact, wing occlusion, sky, ground, near-clip approach and inside/outside transitions. Require finite values, no bright foreground leakage or hard screen-filling flash. Depth-based cost and actual renderer behavior remain unmeasured.

## FX12 — Color, fog, exposure, glow and local light

**Question and method.** How should the luminous core remain readable across OpenRC's day/night scene, fog and renderer paths without changing global atmosphere? Inspect the scaffold [P1], `Atmosphere.environment()` [O4], OpenRC's emitter and exposure contracts [O5–O7], and Godot's color, environment and material documentation [G3, G9–G10].

**New finding.** The supplied shader uses `source_color` uniforms, unshaded additive `ALBEDO`, and `fog_disabled`. The current world creates fog enabled with **glow disabled**, then applies profile settings [O4]; therefore the plume must look finished without bloom, and a fog-disabled emitter may look implausibly crisp through haze. Simply enabling engine fog on additive materials also needs testing because fog adds a color contribution, not merely attenuation. OpenRC's established night-light path uses bounded exposure-aware radiance and an `encode_for_engine()` helper [O5–O7]. Its FW-84 research specifically reports that **4.7.2** GLES3's unshaded branch used `ALBEDO`, ignored `EMISSION`, and still applied exposure to transparent additive material. Do not extrapolate that as verified 4.8-beta2 behavior. Godot 4.7 describes distinct Compatibility output/color/glow behavior, so one HDR-tuned image cannot qualify all renderers [G3, G9–G10].

**Proposed implementation.** Choose a saturated, readable core and falloff before any optional halo; retain `source_color` for authored sRGB colors, treat masks/noise as linear data, and perform color conversion exactly once along the project's validated renderer path. Review [O6]'s helper from the pinned git object: the sparse checkout does not materialize its `.gdshaderinc` file. Trial scene-linear gain bounded by an exposure-aware display cap, including accepted exposure rather than changing the world; verify that oscillations are applied after any chosen cap so they remain visible. Compare native fog with `fog_disabled` plus `T=exp(-beta*d)` only after matching the atmosphere owner’s coefficient and distance convention; avoid double fog and colored haze sheets. Do not add a new `WorldEnvironment` or turn bloom on globally. Optional shadowless local light is a separate opt-in experiment with small radius and bounded energy; emission alone does not guarantee nearby illumination and shadowless light can leak through aircraft parts.

**Acceptance and limits.** Compare fixed day, dusk, moonlit and moonless exposures with fog low/high, bloom Off/on where already available, and flame Off/on over sky, fuselage, runway and a foreground occluder. Include neutral and saturated swatches; record actual renderer, exposure and tone mapper. Require recognizable color/core and opaque foreground silhouette without white clipping, fog glow slabs or doubled light; measure incremental material, optional light and existing glow costs separately on native 1080p. This is a comparison plan, not visual calibration or photometric validation.
