# Round 2 graphics investigations I08–I14

**Scope and evidence.** This is a research supplement to the 14 November afterburner plan, not an implementation or a graphics qualification. It examines OpenRC commit `ad21070798bfb9658785576a9c0b8bfc9a71fc7f`, the existing standalone plume, the current rendering and smoke sources, and the primary sources registered below. The current project downloader still pins Godot `4.8.beta2.official.cafc17a19`; Godot identifies that build as a prerelease [G1]. The linked Godot 4.7 manuals establish documented behavior close to the pin, but they are not a substitute for checking that beta's actual GPU behavior. Exact engine renderer source at `cafc17a19` was not retrieved in this investigation. No render, shader-on-GPU, visual acceptance, or target-hardware performance test was run. The only new execution was the preserved arithmetic in `graphics_evidence/projected_size_phase.json`.

## I08 — Renderer, alpha, depth, shadow and color contract

**Question and method.** Which apparently small material choices could make the proposed plume disappear, cover the aircraft, or change color across the three actual OpenRC renderer paths? I compared the standalone shader and current `app/project.godot` and `app/get-godot.sh` with Godot's spatial shader, color-hint, and material descriptions [G1–G4]. The project defaults to Compatibility, selects Mobile for native Apple Silicon, and has a separate High/Forward+ path [O1, O2].

**Evidence and finding.** The prototype writes `ALPHA`, uses `blend_add`, `depth_draw_never`, `cull_disabled`, `unshaded`, `fog_disabled`, and explicitly disables instance shadow casting [O3]. Godot documents that even *reading* or writing `ALPHA` selects the transparent pipeline; `depth_draw_never` controls writing, not the normal occlusion depth test. `shadows_disabled` alone does not disable casting. Forward+ is documented to reject fragments at exactly equal depth in its default depth test, while the other renderers differ [G2]. Coplanar nozzle and plume geometry is consequently a concrete cross-renderer seam risk. Alpha blending also lacks ordinary opaque reflection/shadow behavior, and adding a depth prepass is a different material policy, not a free sorting fix [G3].

The 4.7 reference reports `OUTPUT_IS_SRGB` true for Compatibility and false for Mobile/Forward+; authored sRGB color textures need `source_color` conversion hints where required [G2, G4]. Compatibility glow uses a different implementation, so matching numerical colors or >1 intensity does not guarantee matching display colors [G5]. The starter already tags color uniforms `source_color`; blindly adding a second sRGB-to-linear conversion could be wrong.

**Change to plan.** Keep the starter's depth-tested, no-depth-write, unshaded baseline and explicit cast-shadow Off. Avoid exactly coplanar nozzle contacts; choose a small, model-scale local offset and check the root from each direction. Give each renderer a calibrated profile only if matched captures show that it needs one. Do not make visibility depend on glow or on HDR `EMISSION`.

**Acceptance and limits.** On the pinned beta, compile and capture the real aircraft at 1080p in each target renderer: foreground fuselage/terrain must occlude every plume pixel; no root seam, cast shadow, or vanished core; daylight and night colors remain recognizable with glow both on and off. Record engine, backend, output/internal dimensions, exposure and color settings. These are proposed checks, not passed results; confidence in the documented 4.7 distinctions is high, exact beta2 behavior remains unverified.

## I09 — Projected plume, cells and color at RC scale

**Question and method.** Are the prototype's narrow shock accents and breakup resolvable during flight-line use, rather than only in a close-up? I inspected its axial functions [O3], OpenRC's authored Vantage tailpipe radius [O4], the repository's reconstruction scales [O5], and NASA's X-59 ground-test photograph [G6]. I calculated projected sizes using a pinhole camera; the full assumptions and results are preserved in the JSON companion.

**Evidence and finding.** For illustrative side-on geometry, use the authored **0.025 m visual radius**, the starter's **0.42 m maximum plume length**, a **60° vertical FOV**, 1080 output pixels and full-resolution 3D rendering. These are not calibrated real exhaust dimensions or a claim about OpenRC's default camera. Projected size is `1080 × size_m / (2 × distance_m × tan(30°))`:

| Distance | Outlet diameter | Plume length | 5.2-cell period | Starter cell's half-maximum width |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 m | 4.68 px | 39.28 px | 7.55 px | 1.26 px |
| 20 m | 2.34 px | 19.64 px | 3.78 px | 0.63 px |
| 40 m | 1.17 px | 9.82 px | 1.89 px | 0.31 px |

The `pow(..., 10.0)` cell profile has a half-maximum width only 16.7% of its period before downstream attenuation. At 20 m the starter's separate 61-radian axial breakup has a 2.02 px period; at 40 m it is 1.01 px. OpenRC's optional two-thirds-per-axis reconstruction means the 20 m outlet is nominally 1.56 **input** pixels wide, not 2.34 [O5]. Foreshortening, movement, exposure, antialiasing and reconstruction change the actual result. NASA's image supports a possible stationary-aircraft shock pattern, not a universal RC-cell spacing or color.

**Change to plan.** Start with a coherent nozzle core and a low-frequency silhouette. Filter or smoothly attenuate the sharp cell and fine breakup terms by projected *internal* pixel footprint, with hysteresis; trial a wider/lower-contrast band before deleting it. As initial design thresholds, fade sharp cells when their half-height width drops below one input pixel or their spacing falls below four; tune from captures. Do not enlarge the plume or imply a measured jet model solely to make cells visible.

**Acceptance and limits.** Capture a measured camera sweep at 10/20/40 m, rear and side views, native 1080p and each supported reconstruction scale, day and dusk. Review moving video frame-by-frame for shimmer and verify a recognizable AB state without cell detail while preserving aircraft attitude. This is a sampling calculation, not proof of visibility or realism; visual and physical-reference confidence is limited.

## I10 — Pose-aligned time, wrapped phases and temporal sampling

**Question and method.** Can a smooth and repeatable flame remain synchronized with the displayed jet over pause, interpolation, long runs and a 1024-second wrap? I compared the prototype's four integer oscillators [O3] with the project shader clock and current flight presentation code [O6, O7], then evaluated a wrap counterexample and floating-point spacing [E1].

**Evidence and finding.** `ShaderClock` wraps the accepted world clock in CPU float64 at 1024 s. In normal live flight, current `main.gd` derives the smoke/aircraft-light clock from the interpolated pose: `sim.time() - sim.dt() + interpolation_fraction * sim.dt()`; its world update uses the accepted time [O6, O7]. Thus the global world clock and displayed-aircraft clock are not automatically interchangeable. The starter correctly computes bounded phases from time before sending its `Vector4` to the shader, whose scalar floats are 32-bit [G4]. Its frequencies are 2119, 1141, 3097 and 5052 cycles per 1024 s: approximately 2.069, 1.114, 3.024 and 4.934 Hz. The last has only 6.08 samples/cycle at 30 FPS and 4.05 at 20 FPS, before spatial motion or interacting terms.

A concrete interpolation trap survives a seamless periodic function: for the 5052-cycle oscillator at 1023.999 and 1024.001 s, the wrapped phases are approximately 6.252 and 0.031 rad. Their naive midpoint is π, giving cosine −1, while the phase computed from the actual midpoint time is 0, giving cosine +1 [E1]. Sending unbounded float32 seconds is also unsafe for long sessions: adjacent representable times are 7.8125 ms apart at one day and 62.5 ms at seven days.

**Change to plan.** Sample `ab_actual`, nozzle pose, and a single unwrapped float64 presentation timestamp at the same accepted/interpolated presentation boundary. Recompute each bounded periodic phase from that timestamp; never linearly interpolate the wrapped radians. Keep the high-frequency terms optional and attenuate them at distant projected sizes or low frame rates. Trial a dominant envelope no faster than about 2 Hz; that is an art starting point, not a physical exhaust-frequency claim.

**Acceptance and limits.** Check the two wrap endpoints and interpolated midpoint against the CPU oracle, plus pause/resume, rollback, replay seek and 24-hour-equivalent timestamps. On target hardware inspect matched 20/30/60 FPS motion captures for phase reversal or objectionable strobing. Numerical continuity does not establish matching images across GPUs; temporal behavior has not been rendered.

## I11 — Mesh layers, sorting, bounds, LOD and Off cost

**Question and method.** What concrete workload and failure modes follow from the proposed two-layer cone, and how should Off differ from a dark but resident effect? I inspected the starter's geometry/material setup and bounds [O3], the actual project resource policy [O8], and Godot's transparency and particle allocation descriptions [G3, G7, G8].

**Evidence and finding.** The scaffold creates **two** separate 24-radial-segment, 12-ring `CylinderMesh` instances per outlet, each with a separate mutable material; the inner layer has a root cap. Both are double-sided, additive and transparent, with a custom local AABB. This is a practical viewing baseline, but front/back sides and overlapping inner/outer layers can shade the same pixels repeatedly. A dense full-screen close-up may cost more in fragment work than the triangle count suggests. Godot sorts transparent *objects* from node positions, not their individual triangles, so material priority can fix a known whole-object order but cannot correctly sort intersecting surfaces at every camera angle [G7]. It also documents that alpha-blended objects do not benefit from its automatic instancing path [G8].

The AABB must reflect the maximum shader-deformed length and radius, not just the undeformed unit cylinder. If authorable dimensions change after initial construction, update both shader parameters and the AABB together. `visible=false` can avoid drawing yet still leave allocated geometry, materials or particle pools resident. Godot explicitly says `amount_ratio` does not reduce the full allocated/processed particle amount [G9].

**Change to plan.** Profile two layers against a one-layer/core-disc alternative on identical viewpoints; do not assume either is faster or visually adequate. Keep double-sidedness only where aft/side captures need it. Drive detail LOD from the internal projected dimensions in I09, and use entry/exit hysteresis to stop oscillating at a threshold. For the global Effects Off choice, construct no effect-specific particle, haze, light or plume resources; on switching Off, release/suspend owned resources and their updates through the existing lifecycle. For a momentarily unlit AB with effects enabled, a bounded warm cache may be reasonable if measured.

**Acceptance and limits.** Capture a full orbit and partly off-screen pass with minimum/maximum authored dimensions, while checking no clipping, hollow cap, opaque-looking overlap or flickering order. Compare effect-owned node/resource counts and render work at warm baseline, lit, cutoff and Effects Off over repeated toggles; Off must return to its agreed baseline after deferred cleanup, allowing separately identified shared engine caches. Verify both visual independence between engines and unchanged physical traces. No GPU cost or memory release has yet been measured.

## I12 — Particle motion in translation, rotation and discontinuities

**Question and method.** When should a particle belong to the jet, and how can a world-space detail trail avoid gaps, duplicated carrier velocity and stale history? I inspected the current `profile_smoke.gd` and `profile_process.gdshader` [O9], the existing smoke tools [O10], and Godot's particle contracts [G9, G10].

**Evidence and finding.** Godot says `local_coords=true` makes existing particles move and rotate with the emitter; `false` leaves already emitted particles in world space. The built-in `ParticleProcessMaterial.inherit_velocity_ratio` is applied at spawn [G9, G10]. At an illustrative 40 m/s and 30 displayed frames/s, an emitter travels 1.33 m between frame endpoints, over **26 nozzle diameters** of a 0.05 m visual outlet and more than three times the starter's 0.42 m illustrative length. Emitting only from the final pose can therefore leave a large spatial gap, even with an otherwise correct per-particle velocity.

The existing profile smoke path is a more precise project reference than assuming ordinary `ParticleProcessMaterial` behavior: it passes previous/current outlet positions and directions, computes carrier velocity from their difference over presented time, schedules fractional births, and uses its own `ShaderMaterial` to add carrier velocity once. Its direction interpolation has an explicit antipodal case; its reset recreates pools to address a retained-frame issue in Compatibility [O9]. This is a source observation, not a new claim that the smoke path has passed AB visual qualification. A rotational contribution matters as well: in a simple example a nozzle 1 m from the rotation center at 3 rad/s has 3 m/s tangential speed, or 0.6 m of path length over 0.2 s at that speed.

**Change to plan.** Keep the attached short core as geometry. If world-space wisps justify their cost, adapt the existing swept-birth/presented-time pattern to a distinct AB profile: interpolate the nozzle pose at each birth and form `v_birth = v_nozzle + v_relative_exhaust`, with one owner for the carrier term. Do not also enable a separate inheritance path for the same term. Limit interval gaps and reset/prime on teleport, restart, seek or invalid transforms; do not bridge discontinuities.

**Acceptance and limits.** Use the existing smoke harness as an integration starting point for a fixed 40 m/s, 30 FPS pass and a hard yaw/pitch turn; inspect birthplace spacing, rearward direction and rotation. Include 180° direction changes, pause, cutoff and seek. Confirm the surviving particles expire and no pre-reset frame leaks through. Native backend, GPU scheduling, interpolation fidelity and visual correctness remain to be tested.

## I13 — Optional heat haze: depth reconstruction and occlusion

**Question and method.** Can a bounded distortion mesh safely refract the scene without pulling foreground silhouettes across the aircraft? I compared Godot's 4.7 depth reconstruction, renderer table and screen-copy descriptions [G11, G12, G15] with the project's Compatibility/Mobile/Forward+ configurations [O1].

**Evidence and finding.** Godot 4.7's post-processing tutorial describes nonlinear reverse-Z depth with the near plane at 1 and far plane at 0, but its renderer overview says Compatibility uses 24-bit depth without reverse-Z [G11, G15]. These official descriptions conflict; they do not establish Compatibility's actual beta2 depth direction. The tutorial uses `INV_PROJECTION_MATRIX` and documents different NDC conversions: Compatibility maps sampled depth to `2d − 1`, whereas Mobile/Forward+ use the sampled 0–1 value [G11]. Test that reconstruction on the pinned engine instead of assuming either description resolves the conflict. Linearization and comparisons must use one view-space convention. Separately, the 3D screen copy is made after opaque geometry and before transparent geometry [G12]; a single-pass haze cannot accurately refract the transparent flame, smoke, glass or another haze layer. Even if the unshifted pixel is behind the haze, the *displaced sample* can cross a foreground tailplane edge and fetch a nearer object, creating a halo.

**Change to plan.** Retain haze as an optional High experiment after the flame baseline. At each fragment, reconstruct the positive view-space depths of both the original and displaced samples using the active renderer's documented NDC conversion and each sample's own UV. Compare the displaced depth to the haze surface depth in that same space: reject or fade displacement that samples a foreground surface, with a small camera-scale-aware transition. Handle clear-depth/sky and near-zero homogeneous `w` without infinities, clamp valid UVs or fade before the edge, and never treat raw depth as linear distance or assume its direction. These guards cannot restore missing transparent objects. The mesh can be small, but requesting a screen copy can still impose viewport-level work; measure it in the real scene.

**Acceptance and limits.** Before enabling it, on each pinned-beta2 renderer/backend capture sampled depth for near and far planes and known-distance opaque surfaces, including clear sky; verify the depth direction, inverse-projected distances and foreground ordering against those known positions. Then perform 1080p camera sweeps past the wing/tail, runway, sky, lake, screen boundary and two intersecting plumes. Require no foreground color pulled outside silhouettes, no NaN/edge streaks, and no disruption when the source depth is unavailable. Compare explicitly with haze Off and record the visual limitation with transparent scenery; if the failure is objectionable, ship it Off. The 4.7 documentation conflict remains unresolved here; beta2 GPU and Metal verification is required. No haze shader was run here.

## I14 — Reproducible visual and native performance qualification

**Question and method.** What evidence would establish that the plume works on the project's main 1080p target and fits its proposed +0.5 ms frame-p95 allocation? I read the current project graphics index, frame-diagnosis and smoke-harness guidance [O10–O12], plus Godot's Visual Profiler and GPU timing API descriptions [G13, G14].

**Evidence and finding.** OpenRC explicitly prioritizes 1920×1080, and its graphics guide says headless draws nothing while Xvfb/llvmpipe verifies image contracts rather than target-GPU performance [O8, O11]. `project.godot`'s 1280×720 viewport default and the old smoke README's beta1 command examples do not override the current beta2 pin or the product priority [O1, O2, O10]. The High profile can render internally below output resolution [O5]. Godot's Visual Profiler separates rendering CPU/GPU categories but does not include all script/physics CPU cost; documented viewport GPU timing requires measurement to be enabled and multiple rendered viewports must be accounted for [G13, G14]. VSync/caps and readbacks can hide a small effect or perturb timing. Godot documents that its Visual Profiler is unavailable on macOS Compatibility; use the project's applicable native diagnostics there instead of assuming profiler coverage [G13].

**Change to plan.** Reuse OpenRC's debug bridge/capture workflow for visual evidence and its `--frametimes=<fresh.json> --frame-diagnostics` plus `research/performance/frame_diagnose.py` path for timing. On each named native GPU/backend, compare the **same AB-equipped physical session and accepted input tape** with presentation Off versus the selected visual tier, then add optional particles/light/haze individually and combined. This avoids attributing physical or scene changes to the renderer. Use actual 1080p output at native internal scale first; separately record upscalers, 1440p/4K and each qualified renderer. Retain the engine, commit, GPU/driver, display mode, VSync/cap, viewport sizes, quality, time of day and emitter activity.

**Acceptance and limits.** After a 10 s warmup, collect seven alternating Off/On pairs of at least 60 s of uncaptured wall time per representative flight/close-up stress workload. Require completed, nonpaused runs, 0.99–1.01 simulated/wall time and at least 95% intended On activity; predeclare exceptions and reject incomplete pairs. Report each pair's `p95(On) − p95(Off)`, median/range and GPU/CPU context. The proposed combined visual budget passes only if the paired evidence supports **≤ +0.5 ms frame p95** on the named target without violating existing gates; if timing noise or caps are comparable to the delta, call it inconclusive. Test cold first use and repeated enable/disable separately; do not mix screenshots/readbacks with the timing sample. This is a proposed protocol, not a measured result or a universal hardware guarantee.
