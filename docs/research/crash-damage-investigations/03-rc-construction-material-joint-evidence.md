# CR-07 research: RC construction materials, joints, and post-impact disposition

2026-10-16 · **Evidence review; CR-07 remains open.** Sources reviewed for the active [crash-damage economics plan](../../plans/crash-damage-economics.md) and [CR-33a measurement contract](CR-33a/README.md). The studies below help specify material and joint tests; none supplies a qualified failure limit for the Jensen Das Ugly Stik 60 installation or its actual crash load paths. Do not use a clear-wood, panel, foam, film, or unrelated joint strength as an aircraft-component threshold.

## Quantitative material and joint evidence

| [Kotlarewski et al., Papua New Guinea balsa](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00107-015-0983-0), *Ochroma pyramidale*, plantation-grown PNG stock. Kiln-dried, nominal 12% moisture, small clear specimens tested to ASTM D143-09; density classes are 80–120, 120–180, and 180–220 kg/m³. Values below are mean ± SD; `n` is the number of coupons for that test. | **Light, 80–120 kg/m³:** bending MOE 1,222 ± 247 MPa (`n=6`), MOR 9.83 ± 1.72 MPa (`n=6`); compression perpendicular to grain 0.64 ± 0.19 MPa (`n=9`); shear 1.56 ± 0.09 MPa (`n=4`). **Medium, 120–180 kg/m³:** MOE 2,037 ± 375 MPa (`n=20`), MOR 16.63 ± 2.74 MPa (`n=20`); compression parallel 9.24 ± 0.61 MPa (`n=11`), perpendicular 1.14 ± 0.39 MPa (`n=12`); shear 1.90 ± 0.25 MPa (`n=29`). **Heavy, 180–220 kg/m³:** compression parallel 14.88 ± 0.42 MPa (`n=8`). Other heavy-class cells were not tested. | Direction, density class, small sample counts, and actual moisture matter. Test moisture averages differed by property (about 10–14% in the reported groups); around 150 kg/m³ specimens sometimes exceeded both lighter and denser specimens in perpendicular compression and shear. These are clear-wood coupon averages, not allowables for a balsa spar, rib, glue line, or airframe. The paper itself warns density alone does not predict individual strength. |
| [Borrega & Gibson, balsa mechanics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mechmat.2015.01.014), balsa over approximately 60–380 kg/m³ at about 6% moisture, tested as a density-dependent cellular material. | Axial compression, bending, and torsion modulus/strength rose approximately linearly with density; radial compression varied nonlinearly. | Independent evidence that “balsa strength” is not one value. This material-mechanics study does not characterize model stock selection, joints, or impact fracture in a built-up aircraft. |
| [Wang et al., birch plywood](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2022.127852), also documented in the [KTH thesis](https://kth.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1711342/FULLTEXT01.pdf). Commercial 21 mm, 15-veneer Koskisen panel, density about 693 kg/m³ and moisture about 12%; in-plane tests at 0°, 22.5°, 45°, 67.5°, and 90° to the face grain, with 12 coupons per mode/angle. | Mean strength in MPa at the five angles: **tension:** 62.5, 30.6, 21.5, 30.0, 56.7; **compression:** 31.3, 26.5, 19.4, 23.0, 23.9; **in-plane shear:** 11.9, 24.4, 32.0, 20.4, 13.2. The thesis's companion edgewise-bending study reports size and moisture effects. | This is thick construction plywood, not thin model aeroply, birch veneer gussets, or model plywood joints. Its principal use here is to require face-grain orientation and ply layup in any test record; it cannot set a model joint or mount limit. |
| [Wang et al., spruce-glulam to birch-plywood adhesive joints](https://doi.org/10.1080/17480272.2023.2201577). Longitudinal single-lap tensile-shear specimens with 40 × 40 mm (1,600 mm²) bonded area; 21 mm birch plywood bonded to spruce glulam. Three adhesives (MUF, PRF, 2C PUR), three pressing methods, and dry/moist conditions were studied. | Mean tensile-shear strength exceeded 5 MPa with 2C PUR; MUF and PRF groups were about 4–5 MPa. Failure included substrate wood as well as adhesive/interface failure. | Demonstrates that substrate, loading direction, bond geometry, adhesive, moisture, and application process belong in a joint test. The spruce/structural-plywood results do not transfer to RC balsa, thin plywood, CA, or epoxy joints. No generic adhesive strength can stand in for the installed joint. |
| [Rumianek et al., molded automotive EPP](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma14020249), bead-molded polypropylene foam from Izoblok, Poland. Authors tested compression at 0.2–25 mm/s using 20 × 20 × 30 mm and 80 × 80 × 40 mm specimens over a range of densities. Temperature tests at −30, 23, and 80 °C used 20 kg/m³, 20 × 20 × 30 mm coupons. | The paper’s Table 1 labels its density/strength/strain entries as manufacturer-delivered properties, not the authors’ coupon measurements. For 20/30/80/120/200 kg/m³, reported manufacturer “strength” is 0.235/0.421/0.954/0.980/1.10 MPa (converted from the table’s 10⁻² MPa units); failure strain is 12/17/16/30/35%. The authors’ tests show dependence on foam density, strain rate, temperature, specimen size, and cell morphology. | Do not treat the manufacturer table as an independently measured failure limit. Automotive molded EPP is not automatically a model’s EPP/EPO/EPS or branded foam. A tested foam needs the exact material, density, bead/molding direction, geometry, temperature, and loading rate. |
| [ORACOVER technical data](https://www.oracover.de/sites2/7/technische-datenblaetter), manufacturer specifications for ORACOVER/ORASTICK/ORATRIM/ORALINE film product family. | Nominal film thickness 23 μm; minimum longitudinal/transverse tensile strength 250/250 N/mm²; elongation at break 90/90%; product weight 64–109 g/m² depending on color; stated biaxial shrink 5% at 150 °C. | Manufacturer free-film values; the sheet does not provide a test method here and does not test film bonded over ribs or a damaged wing. The [NASA/USRA model design report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19940020535/downloads/19940020535.pdf) separately describes Monokote as tension- and shear-capable and notes its contribution to structural strength. Treat a covering tear as a reason to inspect the underlying structure; neither charge structural damage from a film-only defect nor assume the installed film contributes zero structure without configuration-specific evidence. |

The [USDA Wood Handbook](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62244) and [ASTM D143](https://store.astm.org/d0143-25.html) provide broader wood variability and small-clear-specimen context. They reinforce the boundary above: species-level or coupon properties are inputs to a test plan, not a failure envelope for a finished RC assembly.

## RC construction, joints, and landing-gear load paths

An earlier [NASA/USRA student aircraft design report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19940020535/downloads/19940020535.pdf) describes a roughly five-pound balsa/spruce model-aircraft structure with separate spar, carry-through, firewall, longeron, and gear-mount load paths. Its main gear mount is a 3/16 × 1.5 in spruce crosspiece glued to spruce longerons, carrying a nylon or plastic strut. The report gives dimensions and a design rationale, but no measured crash capacity. It shows why a gear event must resolve the leg, attachment, mount, longerons, and adjacent structure as separate possible outcomes; “gear compressed past travel” alone cannot identify a failed part.

The [2022 AIAA Design/Build/Fly report](https://aiaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2022-aiaa-dbf-top-3-reportsd97c1337115848488c92228e188607c7.pdf) documents a different aircraft built from plywood/balsa structure, foam, fiberglass, carbon reinforcement, cyanoacrylate/epoxy joints, and heat-shrink covering. Its team reports that fiberglassing raised its basswood strength-to-weight ratio from 101.9 to 225.2 and birch-plywood ratio from 164.5 to 311.5, then selected fiberglass-reinforced birch plywood for landing-gear mounts. The report’s project-specific figures show that reinforcement changes the tested material system; they are not crash capacities or transferable strength limits for the Stik.

For CR-07, a joint record must identify both adherends, species/ply layup, density and moisture, grain angle, bond area/edge geometry, adhesive and cure, surface preparation, clamp/pressure and temperature, and loading direction/rate. Record force/displacement/time and fracture location (wood, adhesive, or interface), not only the peak value. Record each physical leg-to-mount-to-frame path and the associated measured gear loads. Preserve independent samples and held-out aircraft cases as required by [CR-33a](CR-33a/README.md).

These manufacturer instructions can constrain safe in-simulation disposition after an explicit finding. They do not provide a probability that a component failed at a particular impact energy, nor do they define physical inspection acceptance criteria for all RC products.

## Encodable constraints and remaining CR-07 evidence

Current evidence supports narrow disposition rules: replace an explicitly damaged propeller; quarantine/replace a positively damaged LiPo; leave impacted servos, receivers, engines, mounts, and unclassified structure for inspection unless a product-specific test or mapped failure is available. Manufacturer-published service and gear-set availability can inform repair choices only after exact product identity and functionality are established. Treat these as safety/economic policies, not verified crash mechanics.

CR-07 is still open. The repository lacks a source-pinned Stik specimen record for each structural material, joint, and gear mount; representative installed construction/cure/repair variation; measured gear and impact histories; and replicated failure plus post-impact functionality outcomes tied to physical and economic component IDs. Those links are needed before any material/joint threshold, impact-to-failure rule, automatic structural surcharge, or salvage credit can be accepted. Generic strength values above may guide specimen selection and test instrumentation, but cannot close CR-07 or certify physical realism.
