# 02 — Godot techniques for destruction, debris and effects

**Status:** research, 2026-10-06. **Serves:** CRASH-DAMAGE-PLAN (CR- steps).

Scope: Godot 4.7.2-stable, Compatibility renderer, our own float64 sim at 240 Hz; Godot physics only for visual debris. Builds on [smoke investigations 01](../smoke-investigations/01-godot-backends.md) (GPU particles in Compatibility), [05](../smoke-investigations/05-materials-transparency.md) (transparency, proximity fade) and [11](../smoke-investigations/11-profiling-budgets.md) (how to measure); those findings are not repeated.

Measurements below were run on the dev VM (Intel i5-10500, 12 threads, no GPU, load average 4–10 from other jobs), with scratch projects outside `app/`. They are CPU-side and noisy (±50 %); treat them as orders of magnitude.

## Summary

1. **This project runs GodotPhysics3D, not Jolt.** `physics/3d/physics_engine` is unset (`DEFAULT`); Jolt is the default only for projects *created* in 4.6+. Verified: a body set to 1000 m/s keeps it (Jolt clamps to 500 m/s).
2. **Godot physics ticks at our 240 Hz**, the one global rate. Debris costs about 4× what it would in a 60 Hz game. Measured on the VM: ~3–5 µs per active body per tick, i.e. **~1–2 ms/frame for 50–100 colliding bodies** and 8–19 ms/frame for 500. Sleeping bodies cost almost nothing.
3. **Small fast debris tunnels without CCD, even at 240 Hz.** 2 cm cubes at 30–80 m/s went through a HeightMapShape3D or a thin box 72–98 % of the time; with `continuous_cd = true`, 0 %, at no measurable extra cost. Both engines behaved the same way.
4. **Break procedural models along their existing sub-meshes**, which are the natural pre-fractured sections. Do not use runtime Voronoi or CSG fracture. Duplicate the model at flight start (7.5–8 ms) and hide the copy. On impact, reparent sections into rigid bodies (<1 ms for 10). Building a model at crash time takes 24–224 ms, which is a visible hitch.
5. **Collision shapes:** use a box from the section's AABB (0.02 ms) or a convex hull precomputed with `simplify = false` (0.4–4.7 ms). Never call `create_convex_shape(true, true)` at runtime: it took 100–140 ms.
6. **Use a tiered debris design:** ≤ ~24 large sections as `RigidBody3D`, plus hundreds of splinters and crumbs as a `MultiMesh` moved by our own ballistic integrator. A GDScript test with 200 pieces took 0.1 ms/frame, and 1000 pieces 0.54 ms/frame. Particles cover dust, smoke and sparks.
7. **Compatibility particles:** GPUParticles3D work, including box, sphere and **heightfield** collision and sphere/box attractors. Not available: trails, sub-emitters, `emit_particle()`, SDF collision and vector-field attractors. CPUParticles3D measured ~0.35 µs per particle per frame (2000 particles = 0.69 ms).
8. **The `Decal` node exists but is not rendered in Compatibility.** For scorch marks and gouges, write a small uniform array of marks into `ground.gdshader`. For a flat field, a textured quad just above the ground also works.
9. **Compatibility has no ubershaders or pipeline precompilation.** The first time a material variant is drawn, the frame stalls. Measured with llvmpipe: 0.8–1.15 s cold and 65–122 ms with a warm `user://shader_cache`; the next instance of the same variant had no hitch. All crash materials must be drawn once (pre-warmed) before the first crash.
10. **`Engine.time_scale` scales Godot physics, `_process`/`_physics_process` delta, particles and shader `TIME`, but not the number of ticks per second, audio, or our sim**, because the sim ignores delta (verified in `main.cpp` 4.7.2). Slow motion for effects is cheap. A slow-motion replay of the flight must replay recorded sim states.
11. **Pausing:** menus pause the sim through `sim.set_paused()`, not `SceneTree.paused`. Godot physics and particles therefore keep running under a menu unless the effects root is paused explicitly, for example with `PhysicsServer3D.set_active(false)` and particle `speed_scale = 0`.
12. **Camera shake** should use Eiserloh's trauma model: trauma in [0,1] that decays linearly, shake = trauma², with smooth noise rather than random jumps, and small rotations before translations. It belongs in `pilot_camera.gd` as a render-only offset.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Section swap: group existing sub-meshes into break groups (a data table per aircraft) and reparent them into bodies on impact | Zero authoring; keeps materials and UVs; cheap (detach 10 sections in 0.5–0.7 ms); matches real RC failures (wing panel, tail group, gear leg, prop) | Breaks are clean cuts at panel seams; needs one-time splits where a node spans both sides (P-51 `wing`) | **Adopt** |

| Runtime plane slicing in GDScript (array walk + SurfaceTool) | Jagged break at the actual impact station | Measured 0.93 ms for 288 triangles and 12.5 ms for 4,224, plus the cap polygon and a new hull | Only for one cut per crash on low-polygon parts, prepared in a frame with spare time |
| CSG-based slicers (Godot-4-Concave-Mesh-Slicer, MIT, active 2026-07), Destronoi (MIT, Godot 4.2, last push 2025-01), mesh-slicing-gdextension (MIT, last push 2024-02) | Handle concave meshes | CSG at runtime is costly; a third-party dependency not proven on 4.7.2 | Reject for now |
| Jummit `godot-destruction-plugin` (MIT, 4.2 Compatibility, last push 2024-06) | Simple swap-to-shards pattern; caches convex shapes | No inherited velocity (random impulse); fades by switching materials to transparent; fixed 2 s lifetime | Pattern only; do not import |

**Recommended for this repo:**
- A per-aircraft **break table** listing group names, member node paths, estimated mass and the break location. It is stored as data, not code, and carries `{value, unit, kind, source}` like the physics files. Agree it with the model team.
- Build a **hidden debris twin** with `root.duplicate()` (it shares meshes and materials) when the flight starts.
- On a crash, hide the original `airplane`, show the twin and reparent each group subtree into a pooled `RigidBody3D` with `Node.reparent(body, true)`. The original keeps its node names and `*_hinge` interface untouched, and a restart simply hides the twin.
- Ask the model team to split nodes that span the centre line, such as the P-51 `wing`, or do one offline plane split per side in the generator.

## 2. Debris physics

**Engine facts (verified on 4.7.2 unless noted):**
- Jolt is built in since 4.4 and the default for new projects since 4.6; existing projects keep their setting. Our `DEFAULT` resolves to GodotPhysics3D (velocity-clamp probe).
- Jolt defaults that matter here: `max_linear_velocity` 500 m/s and **`max_angular_velocity` 47.1 rad/s (2700°/s)**. A propeller fragment at 9,000 rpm spins at 942 rad/s, so Jolt would clamp it unless the limit is raised.
- Jolt is deterministic per binary, but the Godot integration makes no determinism guarantee (search result only). GodotPhysics3D is not deterministic across platforms either. Debris does not need to be deterministic.
- `physics/common/physics_interpolation` is `false` here. 3D physics interpolation exists since 4.4. It is global and expects transforms to be set only during physics ticks, while `main.gd` poses the airplane in `_process` using `Engine.get_physics_interpolation_fraction()`. At 240 Hz, an uninterpolated body lags at most 4.2 ms. That is acceptable for debris (estimated), so leave interpolation off.
- `RigidBody3D.inertia = Vector3.ZERO` derives the inertia from the shapes, assuming uniform density. Set `mass`, `center_of_mass_mode = CUSTOM` and `inertia` from the break table instead: real tumbling depends on the inertia ratios. For contacts with static ground, mass alone barely changes the motion.

**Benchmark** (bodies dropped into a 6×6 m pile with 25 m/s forward speed and up to ±20 rad/s spin, `--fixed-fps 60`, 4 ticks per frame; ms/frame above an empty scene):

| Bodies | GodotPhysics3D, impact 0–2 s | Jolt, impact 0–2 s | After 6 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 (box / hull) | +0.7 / +1.6 | ~0 / +0.7 | everything asleep, ≈ 0 |
| 100–200 box | +1.8–2.8 | +2.4–3.3 | ≈ 0 |
| 500 box | +10–19 | +6.5–12 | ≈ 0–1.5 |

No engine was a clear winner at the 50–200 bodies a model aircraft needs, and Jolt showed a higher idle baseline (~+1 ms/frame) on this VM. **Keep GodotPhysics3D.** Switching engines is a one-line project setting that can be revisited with numbers from real hardware.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RigidBody3D` nodes, pooled | Simple; contacts, friction and sleeping come for free | 240 Hz cost; node overhead | **Adopt** for ≤ ~24 large sections |
| `PhysicsServer3D` bodies through RIDs | No node overhead; docs recommend it for "tens of thousands" of objects | Manual lifetime and RID references; queries stall the server | Only if node overhead is measured to dominate |
| Own ballistic integrator + `MultiMesh` (gravity, quadratic drag, bounce and friction against a ground-height function) | 0.48 µs/piece/frame measured; deterministic; runs at frame rate rather than 240 Hz; ignores the pause problem | No piece–piece contacts; ground only | **Adopt** for splinters, foam crumbs, clods |
| Jolt | Faster at 500+ bodies in our run | Angular clamp; changes a global setting; no gain at our counts | Not now |

**Recommended for this repo:**
- **Isolation.** Put debris under its own `crash_effects` node, on its own collision layer, so it never touches the airplane. The sim never reads debris. Effects take the sim's crash state as input and nothing flows back. Proof: `--trace` hash and golden flights are identical with effects on and off, the same kind of check D5.9 used.
- **Initial state per section.** `v_i = v_cg + ω × (R · r_i)` and `ω_i = ω`, where `r_i` is the section's CG minus the aircraft CG in the body frame and `R` is body-to-world. Compute it in float64 through the existing frame helpers (`render/frames.gd`), then cast. Add a separation impulse scaled by impact energy, labelled estimated.
- **Air drag on light parts.** A foam or balsa panel falls slowly. Godot's linear damping gives terminal speed `v_t = g / linear_damp`, so `linear_damp ≈ 9.81 / v_t` (e.g. 1.6 s⁻¹ for an estimated 6 m/s panel). This avoids per-tick GDScript forces.
- **Collision and sleep settings.** Set `continuous_cd = true` on every debris body and leave `can_sleep` on. Use `WorldBoundaryShape3D` for the current flat field (an infinite half-space, so nothing tunnels through it). Use `HeightMapShape3D` later: faster than a concave mesh, slower than primitives, with 1 m grid spacing scaled through the node and uniform scale only in GodotPhysics3D.
- **Lifetime.** Freeze bodies that have slept for a few seconds (`freeze = true`), and free or pool everything on restart. No alpha fade (see §7). Shrink or sink pieces into the ground instead, or use dithered fade on a pre-warmed material.

## 3. Particles in Compatibility

GLES3 particle storage in 4.7.2 handles sphere, box and heightfield colliders and sphere and box attractors. It warns that trails, sub-emitters, manual emission, SDF colliders and vector-field attractors are unavailable. `use_fixed_seed` and `seed` exist on both particle classes, which helps with repeatable captures.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GPUParticles3D one-shot (`one_shot`, `explosiveness` ≈ 1, `restart()`) | GPU simulation; heightfield collision for dirt and clods; `amount_ratio` changes counts without a restart | No sub-emitters, so "clod leaves dust trail" needs two emitters; `finished` signal timing ignores `speed_scale` (4.7 issue, search result only) | **Adopt** for dust burst, sparks, dirt |
| CPUParticles3D | Works anywhere; CPU cost is predictable (~0.35 µs per particle per frame) | Fewer features; scales on the CPU | Fallback, per smoke 01 |
| MultiMesh + own integrator | Full control; the same pool as splinters | Write it ourselves | **Adopt** for solid fragments (§2) |
| GPUParticlesCollisionHeightField3D | Ground-following particles in Compatibility | Renders a height map; resolution and update mode cost GPU time | Use when terrain stops being flat; a box collider suffices on the flat field |

**Recommended for this repo:** a pool of pre-built one-shot emitters per effect: impact dust (grass or dirt colour from the surface table), dirt clods, balsa splinters, sparks for the metal parts of the turbine jet, and a smoke column. Each emitter is placed at the impact point and re-armed with `restart(true)`. Use `preprocess = 0`, because crash effects start at the impact. Use world coordinates (`local_coords = false`) and set `visibility_aabb` to the expected extent, or the burst vanishes when its origin leaves the screen.

## 5. Fire, smoke and heat haze (turbine-jet crash)

- **Volumetric fog and FogVolume are unsupported** in Compatibility. Depth fog is supported.
- **Smoke column:** billboarded GPU particles with a flipbook or procedural mask, following the smoke plan (proximity fade, no custom shader until it is measured).
- **Fire:** additive, emissive billboard particles with a short lifetime. A small flipbook or a noise-scrolled shader on a few quads is enough at RC viewing distances (estimated). Glow is supported in Compatibility, so emission above 1.0 reads as hot.
- **Light flicker:** at most one `OmniLight3D` without shadows, with noise-driven energy. Compatibility renders up to 8 omni + 8 spot lights per mesh (`rendering/limits/opengl/max_lights_per_object = 8`, `max_renderable_lights = 32`) and uses multi-pass rendering for light shadows. A shadowed omni light is expensive, so avoid it. The ground is one big mesh, so a light near the crash affects the whole ground draw.
- **Heat haze:** a screen-reading refraction shader on a quad above the exhaust or fire. Compatibility supports `hint_screen_texture` (smoke 05). It is optional and must be measured, since screen reads copy the framebuffer.

## 6. Camera feedback and time

- **Trauma shake (Eiserloh, GDC 2016):** keep `trauma ∈ [0,1]`. An impact adds trauma in proportion to impact energy, and trauma decays linearly (e.g. 0.5–1 s⁻¹). The shake is `trauma²` (or `³`) times maximum yaw, pitch and roll (a few degrees) and a small offset, driven by smooth noise (`FastNoiseLite`) sampled with time. The Bevy example uses a 10° maximum angle, exponent 2 and decay 0.5 s⁻¹ (borrowed starting values). For the pilot-on-the-ground view, rotation shake must stay small because the pilot is far from the impact. Scale trauma by distance from the impact (estimated).
- **Hit-stop and slow motion:** `Engine.time_scale` multiplies the delta of `_process`, `_physics_process`, `PhysicsServer3D.step`, particle processing and shader `TIME`. The tick count per real second stays 240 and audio is unaffected (use `AudioServer.playback_speed_scale` for that). Our sim steps a fixed dt and ignores delta, and the crash freeze counts ticks, so `time_scale` slows only the effects. That is good for a slow-motion impact, but anything timed by ticks will not stretch. Known issue: shaders using `TIME` stutter at `time_scale = 0` (search result only). Use a small value, not 0.
- **Replay:** slow-motion replay of the crash means replaying recorded sim states (`sim/recorder.gd`) at a reduced playback rate with our own interpolation. Debris is not deterministic, so either record debris transforms (cheap: ≤ 24 bodies) or re-simulate it non-deterministically and accept differences.

## 7. Performance strategies

- **Budgets (proposed, estimated):** at the impact peak, CPU for effects ≤ 1.5 ms/frame on a mid-range PC. That allows ≤ 24 active rigid bodies, ≤ 300 MultiMesh fragments, ≤ 2,000 particles in total, 1 unshadowed light and ≤ 16 ground marks. On the VM, 24 bodies measured ~0.5–1 ms/frame. Validate with release builds on real hardware (smoke 11 method).
- **Pre-warm** every crash material, emitter and the debris twin while the scene loads. Draw them once inside the view behind the Home screen or the first flight frame. The Compatibility docs require this. Enable `rendering/shader_compiler/shader_cache` (on by default) to cut later sessions' hitches roughly tenfold, as measured.
- **Avoid variant switches mid-crash.** Turning `transparency` on to fade a piece creates a new shader variant and stalls the frame. Decide every material's final flags at load time.
- **Pooling:** create bodies, emitters and the MultiMesh once per flight. Hide unused MultiMesh instances with `visible_instance_count` (a MultiMesh is culled as a whole, so give it a custom AABB). Never `queue_free` and recreate debris on every crash.
- **Visibility ranges** work on MultiMeshInstance3D and particle nodes. Use `fade_mode = DISABLED`, because "Self" fade switches to alpha blending.

## Godot 4.7 pitfalls

- `Decal` exists in ClassDB but is not drawn in Compatibility. Its absence raises no error.
- Calling `create_convex_shape(clean=true, simplify=true)` at runtime took 100–140 ms. Precompute shapes, or use AABB boxes.
- Small pieces tunnel through heightmaps and thin boxes at 240 Hz without CCD.
- Jolt clamps angular velocity at 47 rad/s by default, which would visibly slow a spinning prop fragment.
- `RigidBody3D.inertia = ZERO` assumes uniform density from the shapes, which is wrong for a hollow fuselage.
- GPUParticles3D `emit_particle()`, trails and sub-emitters silently do nothing in Compatibility (warnings only).
- `Engine.time_scale` does not slow audio or reduce the tick count, and `0` stalls `TIME` shaders.
- The sim pause is not `SceneTree.paused`: Godot physics and particles run on under a menu.
- Rebuilding an aircraft model at crash time costs 24–224 ms. Duplicate it ahead of time.
- Compatibility has no ubershaders. A first-use stall happens on every new material variant.

## Limits of this research

- All timings come from one shared, loaded VM without a GPU, so they are CPU-side only. The GPU cost of particles, overdraw, lights and the ground-mark loop is **not measured**. Spread across repeated runs was up to 2×.
- The physics benchmark used a dense 6 m pile, a worst case for pair counts. A real crash scatters pieces over tens of metres.
- Shader-stall numbers come from llvmpipe. Real drivers compile faster but still stall, and macOS and Windows drivers were not tested.
- Jolt was compared only at default settings. Its higher idle cost here may be an artefact of the VM's thread contention.
- Nothing was integrated into `app/`. Break tables, the drag-by-damping approximation and the shake constants still need owner and model-team agreement, and their own CR- proofs.
