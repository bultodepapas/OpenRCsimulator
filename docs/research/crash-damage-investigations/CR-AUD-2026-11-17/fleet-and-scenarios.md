# Collision and damage: fleet and scenario coverage

**Audited baseline:** `a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61`. Source links below are pinned to that revision; relative links point to the repository's research and verification records. This document inventories the stock routes and defines acceptance cases. A case listed as a target is not a claim that the baseline satisfies it. See [verification.md](verification.md) for the executed audit checks, their inputs, results, and limitations.

## Fleet in scope

Home offers **13 vehicles**: eight selectable fixed-wing aircraft, one helicopter, one multirotor, one airship, and two ground vehicles. The aircraft registry also retains three hidden technical fixed-wing configurations. That distinction follows the [aircraft catalog and its player-selection filter](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/app_state/aircraft_catalog.gd#L19-L162) and the [additional activity roster](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/app_state/vehicle_roster.gd#L20-L54). Eleven fixed-wing **registry routes** therefore means eight selectable plus three technical; it does not mean eleven selectable aircraft. Alternative battery files and synthetic fixtures are variants or test inputs, not additional Home entries.

## What the fixed-wing runtime actually does

Normal interactive flights select soft hull contact unless disabled. Scripted, capture, and legacy trace routes normally retain the older any-hull-contact terminal policy; tests must name their route and configuration. The interactive hull has spring, damping and friction loads. A newly touching point can cause a terminal decision based on its **previous-boundary normal velocity** and effective mass; an already touching point is not regraded as a new impact, but an excessive depth can still terminate. Default cutoffs are 2.5 J/kg and 0.08 m, with the authored tolerant-hull override where present. These are implemented classification rules, not measured failure capacities. See [interactive selection](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/main.gd#L1249-L1254), the [session guard](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L3141-L3173), and the [hull terminal rule](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/physics/hull_contact.gd#L421-L467).

Gear down uses the installed support law; overtravel is a terminal `gear_limit` decision. A retractable wheel that is not fully downlocked retains its declared contact path but has zero support stiffness, damping and permitted compression. A belly hull contact with the gear up can therefore continue in soft mode if the posed wheel witnesses and other terminal rules permit it. This is not a simulated physical collapse or proof that every retract installation behaves alike. See [retractable-gear pose](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/physics/retractable_gear.gd#L44-L64) and [GP-14's Avanti gear-up observation](../../ground-physics/GP-14/README.md).

A powered ground prop-disc strike at or above 300 rpm is recorded once, cuts the source and ordinarily leaves the flight running. The audit's source-off spinning case did not register that strike even though the source-enabled control did; see [verification.md](verification.md). The current response does not apply a blade-impact impulse, remove propeller mass or replace the coupled intact-propeller aerodynamic and inertia model. A stopped-blade phase test and scenery prop strikes remain outside the stated scope. The terminal path can retain a simultaneous powered disc observation without changing the frozen aircraft state. See the [strike guard](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L3320-L3333), [coupled rotor and loads](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L4945-L4987), and [current crash and economics scope](../../../CRASH-DAMAGE-PLAN.md).

`AircraftGroundDamage` is a separate experimental foundation, not a stock FlightSession failure model. A known, authored normal capacity may select a weaker **attached fixed-hub** response, with an energy ledger and atomic transition; unknown strength does not fail by a fabricated default. It does not yet supply calibrated stock strengths, moving/collapsed legs, detached mass or persistent damaged-flight aerodynamics. Its boundary-only load sample is not an interval peak. The already documented accepted-path overload counterexample and stock-integration prerequisites are in [GP-16/17](../../ground-physics/GP-16/README.md). The active [crash guide](../../../CRASH-DAMAGE-PLAN.md) keeps calibrated physical failure and damaged flight open.

## Speed, angle and damage are different questions

300 km/h is approximately **83.33 m/s**. Total speed alone does not determine a contact outcome. For a stationary flat surface, angle here is measured from the surface tangent: 0° is exactly tangent and 90° is normal. With no rotational contribution, the point's normal speed is `v × sin(angle)` and its normal specific-energy term is `0.5 × (m_eff / m) × v_n²`. For illustration only, taking `m_eff = m`:

| Contact angle at 300 km/h | Normal speed | Normal term per whole-aircraft kilogram |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 90° | 83.33 m/s | 3,472.22 J/kg |
| 5° | 7.26 m/s | 26.38 J/kg |
| 2° | 2.91 m/s | 4.23 J/kg |
| 1° | 1.45 m/s | 1.06 J/kg |
| 0° | 0 m/s | 0 J/kg |

This arithmetic does **not** establish a 300 km/h simulated flight, a reproducible collision at that speed, or a physical damage threshold. Actual contact-point velocity includes angular motion; effective mass can be far below total mass, terrain can move or slope, and the selected probe and previous-boundary approximation matter. The stock terminal rule also has a depth condition and an aircraft-specific tolerance option. Existing scenery classes have their own response rules, so they cannot be inferred solely from the flat-hull cutoff.

A shallow scrape can still remove energy through tangential friction, abrasion or a catch on geometry even when normal energy is small; exactly zero normal energy is not proof of zero real-world damage. Conversely, high total speed does not justify applying the full translational kinetic energy as normal impact energy. The [presentation severity model](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/render/crash/severity.gd#L4-L15) caps its abrasion class and labels its cuts provisional. Those labels do not certify material strength, repairability, or a physically complete glancing collision.

It does not establish persistent structural damage. | Run both sides and several aircraft/probe layouts with actual contact IDs. Verify passive friction, bounded pose/rates, continued control authority as currently modeled, and one coherent contact lifecycle. ## Evidence and open gates

Existing focused tests include `test_soft_contacts.gd` for the Stik brush, nose-in and Avanti slide; `test_prop_strike.gd` and `test_aircraft_contact_session.gd` for powered strikes and history; `test_retractable_gear_ground.gd` for support and overtravel; `test_landscape_collision.gd` for swept proxy and grazing examples; and `test_crash_cost_fleet.gd` for constructed cost observations across the eleven fixed-wing registry configurations. The last is a serializer/economics fixture, not eleven full physical crash flights. Results for this audit's new scenarios belong in [verification.md](verification.md), not inferences from test names.

Before enabling a physical gear-damage response in stock flight, require provenance-bearing component capacity and failed-law data; a qualified accepted-path failure time or bounded error; and an atomic transition at the same physical state. Rejected integration attempts, abandoned stages and body-filtered paths must not publish damage. The [kernel receipt contract](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/vehicle_model.gd#L68-L77) transports accepted-path evidence but does not itself prove its physical meaning. Tests must cover checkpoint and reset, energy accounting, changed force response and rejected-tick rollback. Detached mass, changed CG/inertia, asymmetrical aerodynamic/control response and continued belly contact each require separate qualified models.

Uncertainties remain in probe coverage, obstruction and body/prop simultaneous-contact ordering, real material and joint strengths, moving gear work, stopped blade phase, contact-time kinematics, and any extrapolation to 300 km/h. Whole-hull microrelief remains an explicitly open migration while wheel contact already samples terrain; see [GP-10](../../ground-physics/GP-10/README.md). Software verification, an estimated repair bill and a plausible animation are not independent physical validation.
