**Reviewed application:** [`a866d880d8edbe84d2c59780624c35c8c7f107fc`](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/commit/a866d880d8edbe84d2c59780624c35c8c7f107fc).

**Deliverable:** twenty distinct research investigations, 43 primary-source reference records, current-code comparison and integration into existing plans. **Research and plan changes only; no new runtime behavior, calibrated failure law, visual capture or performance improvement is claimed.**

## Research outcome

The most useful direction is a consistent chain from admitted contact to component response and visible aftermath. A light brush needs accurate local contact, finite rubbing and a clear release. A major collision needs credible attachment failure, mass-aware separation, support-aware settling and finite source-specific effects. More particles alone cannot repair timing, geometry or ownership errors.

The implementation already contains soft fixed-wing hull contacts, nonterminal powered prop strikes, directional surface effects, staged real-part breakup, Stik fracture interiors, per-surface wreck response, fuel-kind effects, delayed battery venting and bounded pools. Experimental GP-16/17 already contains failed-attached gear response. SM-19/20 already implements coherent aircraft-smoke transport and lighting. The investigations below identify additions to those foundations, not twenty absent features.

The [active execution integration](../../../plans/crash-realism.md#10-research-integration-twenty-investigations--19-november-2026) owns scheduling and dependencies. The [nonfatal contract](../../../plans/crash-realism.md#9-recoverable-contact-and-near-misses--19-november-2026) remains authoritative for near misses, brushing, accepted work, water/prop branches and recovery. Numbers 01–20 identify research questions, **not new implementation step IDs**. The [source index](crash-inspiration-sources-2026-11-19.json) records authors, dates, supported claims and transfer limits; the [provenance](crash-inspiration-provenance-2026-11-19.json) pins the inspected source paths and verification scope.

### How to interpret the evidence

Primary experiments inform mechanisms and the measurements we still need. Developer papers and engine documentation inform algorithms and cost tradeoffs. Manufacturer manuals establish particular constructions. None automatically supplies strength, friction, nick depth, ignition probability or recovery limits for this fleet. Publisher/author abstracts are identified as such; an opened abstract is not a full experimental review. Online documentation versions are explicit and are not evidence of behavior on the pinned Godot 4.8 beta.

This pass checked the current source against the prior audit and deliberately kept historical probe results at their original revisions. The relevant physics/simulation/crash-render paths and three canonical plan bases did not change between the prior nonfatal pin `d736ee8a` and reviewed `a866d880`. That source comparison does not rerun or expand previous tests. Earlier CR-AUD-01–34 records remain at their stated statuses, with unresolved prerequisites referenced where relevant; they are not newly discovered findings in this research pass.

### Proposed integration boundaries

The float64 flight model owns accepted contact, physical damage and any continuation. Godot wrecks, cosmetic chips, audio and smoke consume those facts or explicitly labeled presentation estimates. A body that remains connected is not necessarily rigid, load-bearing or flyable. A nonterminal result is not a recovery demonstration. Decorative release timing never chooses physical detachment time.

Investigations 16–18 propose **one aftermath source/lifecycle owner**: investigation 16 supplies bounded fuel-source estimates; 17 supplies explicitly authored battery sequences; 18 owns source identity, attachment, timing, extinction and residual aging; 15 adapts plume transport/rendering; 19 consumes sound events; 20 budgets all presentation consumers. No such complete shared receipt exists yet. The owner must distinguish terminal flight freeze from user pause, anchor elapsed presentation time to the accepted incident, avoid shader `TIME`, and qualify bounded low-FPS catch-up without silently lengthening a fire or restarting an expired vent. Existing `effects.gd::update()` clamps each delta to 0.05 s, while live profile smoke expects advancing accepted presentation time; direct reuse without this boundary would be incorrect.

Investigation 20 owns the proposed combined presentation budget. Investigation 14 supplies its renderer/capability adapter, not a second global allocator. Existing local caps—24 wreck bodies, 700 crash particles, 160 chips and 768 aircraft-profile particles—are per relevant configured instance, not a proven concurrent-fleet ceiling. Graphics quality cannot change contact, damage, recovery, incident counting or repair valuation.

## 01 — How can fast grazing contact remain continuous without inventing a collision?

**Source records:** S01.

**Question and evidence.** Can a fast wingtip cross a whole contact episode between ticks while a genuinely clear wing remains untouched? Erin Catto's [*Continuous Collision*, GDC 2013](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_ContinuousCollision_GDC2013.pdf) compares speculative contacts, time of impact and conservative advancement. It explicitly illustrates false speculative contacts, lost motion when a body stops at impact time, and the extra cost of continuing the remaining interval. The talk supplies algorithmic tradeoffs, primarily in a game/2D setting; it does not prove a particular 3D aircraft sweep or a millimetre accuracy envelope.

**Existing baseline.** `app/sim/scenery_collision.gd::first_hit()` already sweeps candidates with rotational chord padding. `flight_session.gd` applies the selected response. The nonfatal audit's CR-AUD-31/32 witnesses show why conservative candidate bounds and post-contact time accounting need separate qualification; they are dated component/accepted-path evidence, not new runs here. §9 already requires physical admission, correct local velocity, existing subsystem/session ordering and bounded continuation. At 300 km/h a reference point moves approximately 0.3472 m in one 240 Hz tick; the displacement alone says nothing about normal impact severity.

**New delta and integration — E3d3, GP-10/14, CR-01.** Investigate a two-stage prepared-feature query: cheap conservative candidate discovery, then bounded local time bracketing/refinement against the physical feature before accepting contact. Use conservative interval bounds/subdivision or another qualified interior-minimum method: endpoint sign changes alone miss entry/exit between two clear endpoints and may miss tangency. Carry a stable feature identity, time interval and stated geometric error bound through selection. Keep candidate padding separate from the physical/compliant surface. After a recoverable hit, continue from the newly committed state, reevaluate loads and requery the remaining time; do not replay the first impulse. Preserve separate scenery/lake earliest-tie contracts and session precedence/refusal. On exhaustion, use an explicitly qualified versioned fallback or the existing fault/unsupported path; unresolved never means clear. Use local refinement only near candidates, retain prepared spatial bins, and measure near-clear rotational worst cases. This is an implementation candidate for §9, not a replacement global solver or a new contact policy.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Do not increase the whole simulator tick rate, delete conservative bounds, or turn tolerance overlap into damage. Require positive-gap, clear-clear interior entry/exit, true tangency and actual-entry controls, high tangent/low normal motion, rotating tips, thin edges, two contacts in one tick, a no-contact state-parity case and an explicit exhaustion case. Recovery includes §9's prescribed follow-on maneuver and supported final state. Record query counts, worst refinement depth and target-host tick cost; 1 mm fixtures qualify only their declared geometry/domain.

## 02 — Can a scrape act over a small patch instead of jumping between probes?

**Source records:** S02, S03.

**Question and evidence.** How can a belly or wingtip retain a stable contact location and resist twisting while sliding? Drake's [compliant-contact explanation](https://drake.mit.edu/doxygen_cxx/group__compliant__contact.html) describes discontinuous point witnesses and missing patch effects; its [hydroelastic guide](https://drake.mit.edu/doxygen_cxx/group__hydroelastic__user__guide.html) distributes pressure over a contact surface. These are useful design comparisons, not RC material measurements. A pressure-field modulus is not interchangeable with a material's Young's modulus, and mesh complexity has a cost.

**Existing baseline.** `app/physics/hull_contact.gd` supplies spring/damper forces and regularized friction per generated hull point. GP-07c already bounds the numerical friction regularization rate when several points touch; it does not establish total-force/work or strength invariance under probe refinement. GP-10 supplies typed support geometry, while GP-12's experimental coupled contact work has separate numerical and activation gates. GP-19 observes accepted endpoint friction power, not integrated patch work. §9 already prevents probe-count multiplication of physical work or damage.

**New delta and integration — GP-10/12/16, E3d3, GP-14/19.** Prototype a bounded persistent contact manifold keyed by physical component and counterpart surface. Reduce redundant witnesses deterministically while retaining distinct edges and separate surfaces; do not merge contacts merely because they are nearby. Where an authored, qualified patch extent exists, compare a small pressure-weighted support model with the existing point law. The opt-in patch law replaces the equivalent point-law contribution rather than stacking on it. Derive resultant force and moment from the same bounded distribution, including any admitted resistance to twist/rolling, and account for dissipation once. If area or compliance is unknown, retain the qualified point response and expose that limit; do not invent contact pressure from render-mesh density. Stable patch observations can then anchor scrape marks and sound without dictating physics. GP-21 remains the optional owner for deformable tire/soil patches; that work is not a prerequisite for exact hull-contact admission.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Do not port Drake wholesale, add arbitrary yaw damping, or multiply identical friction forces across extra visual samples. Compare sparse versus refined equivalent geometry, belly/wingtip flat/edge contact, translation versus pure twist, two materials at a seam, release/recontact and reordered input. Check passive force/work, actual point velocities and moments, endpoint versus integrated evidence, reset/replay and effects-off identity. Bound manifold capacity and preparation/update cost before opt-in activation; physical calibration remains separate.

## 03 — What should make a part bounce, stop, or keep sliding?

**Source records:** S02.

**Question and evidence.** Can elastic rebound be distinguished from penetration correction and artificial damping? Drake's [Hunt–Crossley dissipation description](https://drake.mit.edu/doxygen_cxx/group__compliant__contact.html) provides an example in which the dissipative contact force depends on deformation and normal velocity. It motivates testing load/unload behavior and energy loss rather than assigning a universal bounce constant. It is a numerical contact model, not a measured law for balsa on grass or foam on pavement.

**Existing baseline.** E3d1 hull stiffness/damping are explicitly numerical estimates (`OMEGA_RAD_S`, `ZETA`, onset regularization). GP-11/12 already contain experimental compliant support, stored potential, unilateral damping and coupled traction work. The visual wreck has CR-09d2 per-surface friction/restitution and `_limit_ground_rebound()` stabilization. Its strong in-air damping is deliberately retained because lowering it with the current boxes caused tunneling and settling problems. Adding a second rebound impulse on top of a releasing spring could count the same energy twice.

**New delta and integration — GP-11/12, E3d3, CR-09d3.** Establish response cards per installed component, contact geometry, material pair, loading rate and history that separately record a measured or explicitly estimated force–deflection curve, unloading/rebound, contact duration, friction and uncertainty. First compare the qualified existing law with one bounded compliant candidate on a single supported fixture; keep the stock law until the candidate passes. Coordinate hull work with E3d1 rather than applying a wheel law automatically. Reuse GP-11 potential and actual clipped-damper work accounting; forbid attractive contact force and preserve numerical admission. Hunt–Crossley dissipation in s/m is not the existing dimensionless ZETA or a linear damper in N·s/m. Inspect the whole energy balance: approach kinetic energy, stored elastic energy, dissipation, external work and stabilization changes. For post-terminal wrecks, investigate the bounce curve only after investigation 12 qualifies support shapes; report any clamp's intervention rather than claiming it is a material response. Physical flight contact and Godot wreck response remain separate implementations with separately stated evidence.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Reject increasing restitution to make crashes dramatic, removing air damping globally, or tuning every surface to the same stopping time. Use matched drop/oblique-slide cases over several normal speeds, repeated small bounces, supported rest, slopes and moving supports. Measure normal rebound ratio, penetration, contact duration, slide distance, angular response and energy terms under time-step refinement; a static no-drive case must not acquire unexplained energy. Performance gates include sustained/resting contact and contact bursts, not only a single drop. Coefficients remain estimates until actual component/material tests support them.

## 04 — Can a wingtip touch water, throw a brief sheet, and leave it again?

**Source records:** S04, S05.

**Question and evidence.** Which geometry and timing distinguish a skim from a terminal water entry? Scolan and Korobkin's [*Water entry of a body which moves in more than six degrees of freedom* (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4985040/) relates early loads to the evolving wetted region and body motion for a smooth body entering initially calm water. [Schnitzer and Hathaway, NACA-TN-2889 (1953)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930083799) studies approximate oblique-entry loads for elliptical cylinders, with limited comparison data. Neither source qualifies RC wing edges, propeller impacts, waves, cavitation or prolonged planing across arbitrary conditions.

**Existing baseline.** `app/sim/lake_collision.gd` identifies lake events; current selected fixed-wing water events are terminal. CR-AUD-33/34 document that policy and a moving-interface diagnostic limitation at their stated source. `water_effects.gd` already supplies terminal splash/rings/droplets. §9 requires a separately versioned transient-water capability, gap-rate versus fluid-velocity distinction and full event/continuation integration. Floats and boats require their own support capability.

**New delta and integration — E3d3, GP-10/14/16/17, lake owner.** Investigate an authored wet-feature proxy and bounded early-entry response for one specified component/configuration. Prepare simple geometry or an offline reference table; compare penetration, evolving wetted area and normal/tangent impulse against a clearly declared reference domain before running a reduced model in the shared kernel. Include release and re-entry, signed moving-interface gap rate and any boundary work. Interface height-rate is not fluid-particle velocity: without qualified current/wave particle kinematics or a separately qualified moving-water law, that branch remains unsupported. Gap motion alone cannot establish drag or hydrodynamic work. A near miss emits nothing; an admitted brief wet contact can feed a short localized spray/ripple observation without calling terminal sink presentation. The visual adapter consumes the accepted wet event and never authorizes survival. This is a staged capability proposal; it does not flip the current terminal flag.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Do not model water as low-friction concrete or scale splash/terminality solely with aircraft speed or depth. Test clear/tangent/entry and short entry/exit within the selected component domain, plus explicit refusal for unsupported nose/tip/wheel and moving-wave cases. Rising/receding-water qualification is additional research, not established by the cited calm-water studies. Retain a terminal positive control. Require hydrodynamic-reference error bounds, explicit unsupported domains, energy/impulse balance, checkpoint/replay/incident consumers and §9 recovery qualification. No full fluid grid is needed for the first candidate; bound feature count, lookup/refinement cost and presentation particles separately.

## 05 — How can a propeller nick or rotor strike respect spin, phase, and the released piece?

**Source records:** S06, S07.

**Question and evidence.** Why can a small geometric overlap have very different outcomes for a stopped blade and a spinning one? [APC's engineering account](https://www.apcprop.com/technical-information/engineering/) separates aerodynamic, centrifugal, bending and torsional loads and stresses the limits of structural estimates. The FAA's [AC 25.905-1 (2000), §6](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_25_905-1.pdf) explains tangential centroid motion, tumbling and release-phase dependence for detached propeller blades. Its transport-aircraft impact zones and numerical angles are not RC fragment cones or nick thresholds.

**Existing baseline.** E3d2's `flight_session.gd::_check_prop_strike()` already stops a running engine after sampled ground-disc penetration above an estimated rpm gate, and `prop_break.gd`/crash effects display fragments. CR-AUD-03/24 identify source-off spinning coverage and variable-terrain rim issues. GP/SF shaft work, typed events and §9 are prerequisites. Current body-reference velocity in the strike payload is not the local blade velocity, and a disc envelope does not establish actual stopped-blade phase.

**New delta and integration — E3d2/3, GP-16/17/18, CR-20, propulsion/rotor owners.** Research one component-local strike record containing translation, body rotation, shaft-relative rotation, strike radius, actual blade shape/phase when owned, target motion and impulse/work provenance. Keep unknown phase explicitly unknown. The helicopter already has a scoped phase-aware low-RPM scenery detector and a high-RPM disc envelope; that does not qualify fixed-wing phase or interval-exact blade coverage. RPM-lag modes also do not establish coupled shaft energy. Distinguish a supported cosmetic mark, admitted local blade damage, shaft/mount consequence and full release only when evidence supports each state. In one frame, use `v_fragment_COM = v_datum + omega_body × r_fragment_from_datum + Omega_relative × rho_fragment_from_hub`, plus separately owned mechanism motion; initial absolute fragment angular velocity is `omega_body + Omega_relative`. The signed shaft-relative rate is not a second absolute body rate. Coordinate admitted shaft energy, retained mass and balanced reaction with investigation 09; do not double-count rotor energy already in locked inertia/cross terms. Without supported spin/mass/energy ownership, physical release remains unsupported or explicitly visual. Decorative chips must not silently remove physical mass. A later asymmetric-blade response requires its own thrust/imbalance/mount qualification; rotor type is not interchangeable with an airplane propeller.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Reject a universal safe nick depth, engine-off equals stopped, or a radial particle burst standing in for blade kinematics. Test powered/coasting/windmilling/stopped cases, several phase/radius/target controls, moving terrain or objects, repeat suppression, finite source energy and reset/replay. Separate accepted physical continuation from a visually plausible chip. Bound blade/feature refinement and delayed-fragment work; do not animate physically authoritative phase from render time.

## 06 — Can a damaged airframe retain more than one load path?

**Source records:** S08, S09.

**Question and evidence.** How can separate attachments stay connected through an alternate route after one attachment fails? NVIDIA's [Blast SDK 5.0.6 Introduction](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/introduction.html), corporate author NVIDIA, date not stated, groups prefractured chunks using authored bonds, then detects islands of still-connected chunks after bonds break. Its separate [ExtStressSolver documentation](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/_build/docs/blast-sdk/latest/class_nv_1_1_blast_1_1_ext_stress_solver.html), NVIDIA, date not stated, calculates stresses only through an explicit solver with node and force inputs. A connectivity graph alone does not calculate load sharing, failure thresholds, fracture energy or an RC aircraft's strength. No Blast integration is proposed.

**Existing baseline.** The older `docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/05-architecture-impact-model.md` already sketches a single-parent section chain and an impact resolver. `app/render/crash/wreck.gd` `_release_kinds_for`, `_connected_members` and `_carry_attached` already group and release visual sections, but use presentation mappings and box proximity rather than an authored structural bond graph. GP-16/17 already have a bounded experimental *failed-attached* gear compliance model: `app/physics/gear_damage.gd` and `aircraft_ground_damage.gd`; this does not model general wing bonds. None constitutes a validated load-path solver.

**New delta and integration.** Under CR-06/07 and GP-16, first prototype a small, source-authored **topology-only** manifest: stable physical component and bond IDs, endpoints, declared connection kind/frame and evidence or `unknown`, separate from render section tags. Generator/loader validation should reject unknown endpoints, duplicate ownership and invalid connected sets. After an independently admitted bond transition, recompute components deterministically; a triangular fixture keeps its third part attached while one route remains, then forms two islands after the second route fails. Map accepted component identities to existing visual sections without treating every bond as a separate body. Connectivity establishes neither capacity nor one rigid body: a surviving edge may be rigid, hinged or tethered. A failed-but-attached edge retains its appropriate ownership until an independently admitted separation; topology alone cannot certify continued flight. A later evidence-backed force/work sharing law would be a separate step, coordinated with GP-17/18 and §9; neither scene proximity nor a graph edge invents capacity.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Reject extending the old parent-chain rule by always forwarding leftover impact energy to one parent, or calling connectivity a stress model. Test reordered input, cycles, bridges, isolated islands, repeated transition, missing data and reset/replay. Measure allocation and graph-update cost only at transitions and compare no-contact ticks on target hardware; declare capacity/exhaustion before activation. Effects settings cannot alter accepted topology.

## 07 — What does material-specific fracture actually justify?

**Source records:** S10, S11.

**Question and evidence.** Can balsa, foam and laminate failures be differentiated without using their appearance as an aircraft failure law? In [“Characterization load-induced cracks in balsa wood”](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/characterization-load-induced-cracks-in-balsa-wood/), A. Bentur and S. Mindess, February 1986, compact-tension specimens cracked generally along grain but showed cell bridging and irregular crack walls. [“Residual bending behaviour of sandwich composites after impact”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1099636218757164), Ismet Baran and Wouter Weijermars, first online 20 February 2018, studied glass/epoxy sandwich coupons with specific end-grain balsa, SAN and PET cores. Their PET core deformed plastically, and the predamaged foam specimens showed progressive core cracks in subsequent bending, whereas tested balsa panels showed a single core shear crack followed by skin-core delamination. These specimen-specific outcomes are not rules for every bare RC foam, balsa spar or composite wing.

**Existing baseline.** CR-07's `03-rc-construction-material-joint-evidence.md` already records grain/density/moisture, foam rate dependence, joints and source limitations; CR-03b already supplies surface-directed dust/ejecta/spark behavior and airframe-colored chips made from one box mesh. CR-09e/f and `ugly_stik_fracture.gd` already provide seeded balsa stubs and torn film. No new physical capacity follows from those meshes or names.

**New delta and integration.** Extend CR-07 physical test authoring, coordinating with the separate CR-33a economics measurement contract, with a configuration-specific mode record: wood grain frame and split direction; bare foam versus core chemistry/density; face layup and core-to-skin interface when present; bond and specimen provenance; loading mode/rate; measured deformation and visible/hidden damage with uncertainty. A bounded synthetic example can compare grain-parallel versus cross-grain evidence and skin/core separation versus bulk crush, while `unknown` means no inferred structural effect. Later accepted outcome tags could choose optional directional splinters, foam indentation or a partial skin flap, using existing CR-03/09 reveal and authoring pipelines; the art must not determine the outcome. Preserve E3d3 §9's clear distinction between contact, cosmetic mark and substantiated material damage.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Reject a universal “balsa breaks, foam dents, composite shatters” switch or applying coupon strengths to installed joints. Review evidence with actual aircraft construction and held-out component tests before physical calibration. Test missing grain/layup and cosmetic-only controls, reset and effects-off equivalence; measure generated geometry, allocations and rendered 1080p cost before expanding beyond one authored demonstration.

## 08 — Can a part change gradually while still attached?

**Source records:** S12, S13.

**Question and evidence.** What must be distinguished before a visual dent becomes a physical loss of stiffness or detachment? [“A Numerical and Experimental Study of Damage Growth in a CFRP Composite Honeycomb Sandwich”](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200006873/downloads/20200006873.pdf), Mark McElroy, James Ratcliffe, Michael Czabaj, John Wang and Fuh-Gwo Yuan, 8 September 2014, follows a supported aluminum-honeycomb/cross-ply specimen under quasi-static indentation: a matrix crack and delamination grow while the specimen remains present. Its compared continuum model did not correlate well with delamination growth, underlining that even detailed models need validation. [“Compression After Impact on Honeycomb Core Sandwich Panels With Thin Facesheets: Experiments – Part 1”](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120007657/downloads/20120007657.pdf), Thomas D. McQuigg, Rakesh K. Kapania, Stephen J. Scotti and Sandra P. Walker, 23 April 2012, reports different dent-growth and crack-propagation responses for its two Nomex-core constructions during subsequent compression. Neither study calibrates a model aircraft.

**Existing baseline.** `app/physics/gear_damage.gd` already has `HEALTHY` and `FAILED_ATTACHED`, with provenance, accepted-tick transition and a potential-energy loss receipt. `aircraft_ground_damage.gd` switches to authored weaker fixed-hub compliance; it explicitly does not represent moving/collapsed geometry. This is an experimental fixed-layout/fixed-hub accepted-boundary path; stock/session activation, peak-load localization and general dynamic continuation remain open. `ground_damage_view.gd` supplies render-only car dents. §9 already requires accepted episode/work accounting and separates cosmetic scuffs, local deformation, structural weakening and detachment.

**New delta and integration.** In GP-16/17 plus CR-07/11–15, research a minimal, *opt-in* constitutive trial between healthy and failed-attached: separately represent reversible deflection, evidence-supported irreversible set, and an optional damaged response, each with local frame, source/uncertainty and an explicit energy ledger. Evaluate constitutive forces at solver trial stages without persistent mutation; commit persistent damage/work once from accepted solver pieces with qualified event localization. Never use render frame rate or endpoint power misnamed as integrated work. Start with a synthetic single supported gear/mount fixture. Unknown evidence preserves the previously qualified law and discloses the unsupported extension; it does not imply no load or no damage. A later presentation adapter can show a bounded dent/tilt from accepted state, but a cosmetic dent alone changes no stiffness, collision geometry, handling or repair finding.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Reject accumulating one generic impact-severity fraction until a mesh pops off. Verify unloading/reloading, a repeated strike, zero-work and near-miss controls, nonnegative dissipation with stored/released energy accounted for, no probe-count duplication, rejected-tick atomicity, replay/reset and effects-on/off parity. Compare undamaged, first-hit and sustained-contact tick cost and target-hardware 1080p presentation; installed-aircraft physical use waits for measured force–displacement histories and validation.

## 09 — How should a detached piece inherit momentum?

**Source records:** S14, S15.

**Question and evidence.** Can an asymmetrically loaded, rotating assembly split without a jump in mass or momentum? [“Rigid Body Collision Response”](https://www.old.cescg.org/CESCG-2003/LKavan/), Ladislav Kavan, CESCG 2003, derives point velocity from center-of-mass velocity plus angular cross-offset, rotates the full inertia tensor to the world frame, and describes equal-and-opposite collision impulses. It is a real-time mechanics exposition, not an RC crash-validation experiment. The Godot Engine contributors' [RigidBody3D 4.6 documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_rigidbody3d.html), date not stated, explicitly allows a custom center offset from body origin and warns against repeatedly setting transforms or velocities. This is documentation for 4.6; verify behavior on the project's pinned engine/backend.

**Existing baseline.** `AircraftData.inertia_about` in `app/physics/aircraft_data.gd` derives approximate inventory-box inertia; GP-18 already requires mass, full inertia, momentum, energy and resource partition. In `app/render/crash/wreck.gd`, `_inventory_masses` assigns estimated mass/moments, `_set_center_of_mass` sets custom COM, and `_activate_group`/`_apply_launch` inherit motion and add bounded scatter. Audit CR-AUD-06/07/08 identified origin-versus-COM, split first-moment/linear-momentum and pending-part ownership issues at its stated source; those probes are historical, and no new rerun is claimed here.

**New delta and integration.** For the visual wreck, make each scheduled release an atomic accounting unit: a pending piece remains in its visual parent's mass, moment, inertia and collision ownership until visual birth, then partitions once from a common pose and COM-based velocity field. This visual delay cannot choose physical detachment time. In physical continuation, GP-18 and an agreed shared SF topology contract partition at the independently qualified committed detachment, even if the visible reveal is later. Check `Σm`, `Σm x`, full inertia via the parallel-axis theorem, linear momentum and angular momentum about one declared common point, with the actual externally applied impulses and relative rotor/fuel ownership accounted for. If optional scatter adds separation energy, apply balanced internal impulses and recoil, including any required torque, with an explicit energy budget; do not claim conservation when speed/energy clamps or shape-derived inertia invalidate it. The physical transaction must not extend failed support to match an animation; a Godot wreck remains a one-way visual handoff. Existing closed SF-00–09 IDs are not repurposed by this proposal.

**Rejected alternative and gate.** Reject merely adding every fragment's mass at impact, or giving all fragments center-origin velocity and independent kicks. Test asymmetric/custom-COM rotating assemblies, delayed birth before/after contact, zero-scatter split, repeated/reset/pause and over-capacity refusal. Measure transition, active-body and pool costs against existing budgets; preserve no-contact physics and do not turn decorative debris count into physical mass loss.

## 10 — What if a part is still hinged or tethered?

**Source records:** S16, S17, S18.

**Question and evidence.** How can an aileron, wheel or loose cover remain connected after a partial attachment failure, and expose the correct inside only when a seam opens? Hangar 9 / Horizon Hobby's [*P-51 Mustang 1.50 ARF Assembly Manual*](https://www.horizonhobby.com/on/demandware.static/Sites-horizon-us-Site/Sites-horizon-master/default/Manuals/han2400-manual.pdf), date not stated, describes multiple CA hinges joining an aileron to the wing and a separate control horn/linkage. It demonstrates this specific assembly, not crash failure strength or a dangling probability. Godot contributors' [HingeJoint3D 4.6 documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_hingejoint3d.html), date not stated, describes a rotational joint and limits; the [4.6 Jolt notes](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html) list hinge properties unsupported there. Backend and version behavior must be rechecked on the project engine.

**Existing baseline.** `wreck.gd` `CARRIERS`/`_carry_attached` make a surface ride rigidly with its parent; `_reveal` exposes hidden `crash_reveal` nodes. `ugly_stik_fracture.gd` already creates seeded splinters, torn film, spar stubs and torn CA hinges. `docs/BLENDER-AIRCRAFT-HANDOFF.md` already requests simple closure faces. These are current presentation, not a partially failed articulated connection.

**New delta and integration.** As an extension under CR-06/09 to the already delivered CR-09e/f work, prototype an authored seam record with two stable part endpoints, local anchors/axis, interior-face IDs, connector kind and unknown/evidence status. On an accepted physical outcome or explicitly estimated visual outcome, distinguish rigidly carried, rotationally connected, tethered and fully separate: reveal the matching faces on both owners only when that seam opens, retaining existing geometry and avoiding duplicate surfaces. ## 11 — How can different fragments drift, flutter, tumble, and fall without affecting flight?

**Source records:** S19.

**Findings and limits.** Andersen, Pesavento and Wang, *Unsteady aerodynamics of fluttering and tumbling plates*, Journal of Fluid Mechanics (2005), studied rigid plates in quasi-2D flow near Reynolds number 10³; rotation interacts with translation and the outcome changes with inertia and thickness/width. Flutter, tumble and transient motion are different regimes, not simply high random spin. Their equations and coefficients describe the tested plates, not arbitrary foam, film, or a motor. [Original paper](https://dragonfly.tam.cornell.edu/publications/2005_JFM_Andersen_Pesavento_Wang_a.pdf).

**Already present.** `wreck.gd::_apply_launch()` inherits aircraft point velocity, angular velocity, and energy-limited, momentum-led scatter; `_inventory_masses()` assigns section masses. `effects.gd::start()` already gives bounded, seeded MultiMesh chips forward-biased velocity and random spin; `update()` applies one global ballistic drag, a plane bounce and a rest latch. Colors already derive from the airframe and `EJECTA_FALL` distinguishes turf/soil/leaves. Wreck uses high global air damping deliberately because reducing it with box colliders caused tunneling and sleep failures; this is not an aerodynamic material model.

**New delta under CR-09d3 / CR-03b / CR-22c.** After investigation 12 proves stable shapes, add a few prepared visual archetypes selected by existing section/material metadata with an explicit generic fallback: thin covering film/flat chips get bounded orientation-dependent drag plus a restrained coupled rocking/tumbling tendency; short wood/fiberglass chips and foam crumbs get lower orientation response and material-specific area-to-mass *estimates*; the engine remains a massive rigid section with comparatively little air response. Precompute area/shape proxies and seed per incident plus stable generated section/fragment ID, independent of pool ordering. Use relative air velocity against the read-only render wind. Keep the current mass, spawn and scatter caps; limit forces, torques, angular speed, and time steps. Any sinusoidal rocking is explicitly a visual approximation, not the plate model. Apply no aerodynamic feedback to the float64 flight state or damage.

**Rejected / gate.** Do not implement Navier–Stokes per fragment, thousands of rigid fragments, or merely lower global damping before contact qualification. Run same-seed film/foam/chip/engine calm-versus-crosswind cases, near/far, pause/reset, and finite-energy/speed bounds; compare against current motion and test unchanged flight traces at every graphics level. Measure paired native 1080p frame p95/p99, GPU/CPU and allocations with maximum bodies/chips; no new feature is accepted from headless timing alone.
