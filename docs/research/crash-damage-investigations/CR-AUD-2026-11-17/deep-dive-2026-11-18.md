## Assessment

This continuation adds **five separately scoped records, CR-AUD-16 through CR-AUD-20**. They extend the first fifteen records rather than recounting them. Three concern concrete state or coordinate inconsistencies; two demonstrate gaps in mixed-contact arbitration and terminal obstacle aftermath whose desired policy must be made explicit. The most consequential pattern is **publication and lifecycle composition**. ## CR-AUD-16: Delayed rotor release mixes crash-time and current reference frames

### Source and failure mechanism

`HeliWreck._cache_rotor_kinematics` stores main and tail rotor hubs and axes in world coordinates at the initial crash pose. Later, `_rotor_release_velocity` calculates a blade group's center from the **current** core/twin transform, subtracts the cached hub, and crosses that difference with the cached axis. The two operands describe different poses after the core moves. See [the cache and release calculation](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/render/rotor/heli_wreck.gd#L437-L484) and [current-center evaluation](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/render/rotor/heli_wreck.gd#L551-L554).

This matters because release need not be immediate. The shared wreck computes contact-distance delays, and its normal `_process` scheduler activates due groups later. Activation inherits current core motion and then the helicopter adds the rotor contribution. Those paths are visible in [delay assignment](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/render/crash/wreck.gd#L316-L335), [the scheduler](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/render/crash/wreck.gd#L529-L540), [shared activation](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/render/crash/wreck.gd#L862-L879), and [helicopter activation and energy caps](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/render/rotor/heli_wreck.gd#L275-L294).

For a rigid translation `d`, a rotor's spin-relative velocity should remain unchanged when the blade center and hub translate together. The current expression instead adds `omega * axis.cross(d)`. For a rigid rotation, the spin-relative velocity should rotate with the assembly. These are geometric invariants; they do not require a measured failure threshold or a particular restitution coefficient.

### Reproduction and controls

The [probe](evidence/round2/probes/probe_heli_delayed_frames.gd) uses the stock HX visual model and X-Cell context, with two authored main blades and two authored tail blades. It supplies C4 severity to reach delayed release, disables scatter, sets inherited core linear/angular velocity to zero, and uses low shaft speeds of 10 rad/s main and 40 rad/s tail. Ground is placed far below the bodies. No physics frame advances between prescribing the core transform and evaluating release.

Four cases isolate the behavior: unchanged pose; translation by `(1, 0.3, 0.8)` m; rotation by 0.7 rad around the normalized `(1, 0, 1)` axis; and transported geometry with zero shaft spin. The oracle transports the original blade-relative spin vector and separately applies the declared shaft/group energy cap. The probe also invokes the actual delayed scheduler and compares the final body velocity, so the raw-vector discrepancy is not dismissed merely because a later cap exists.

| Observation | Recorded result |
|---|---|
| Unchanged-pose spinning control | Raw and released velocities agree with the oracle |
| Transported zero-spin control | No spurious spin velocity |
| Translation, largest released-velocity discrepancy | **6.29 m/s**, on a main blade |
| Translation, uncapped main-blade extra term | Approximately `(-8, 0, 10)` m/s |
| Rotation | All four spinning blade groups violate the transported-vector expectation |
| Delays actually exercised | Main approximately 0.0361 s; tail approximately 0.0627 and 0.0663 s |
| Checks | **90 total; 16 failures**, comprising paired raw/released comparisons for the eight moved, spinning blade cases |

The [raw log](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/heli-delayed-frames/engine.log), [decoded observations](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/heli-delayed-frames/observations.json), and [checked report](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/heli-delayed-frames/report.json) preserve individual values. The existing helicopter crash-visual suite still passes 33/33; its immediate-release and group-selection coverage does not establish invariance of transported delayed releases.

### Consequence, correction, and limits

Delayed blades can leave in an erroneous direction or with erroneous speed even while the final speed and energy caps are obeyed. A cap bounds magnitude; it cannot repair a coordinate mismatch. This record is distinct from the first audit's general wreck COM/reference issue: the inherited core contribution is zero here, and the extra rotor contribution alone fails.

Store hub/axis data in model coordinates, then transform the hub, axis, and blade center through the **same current aircraft pose** at release. Keep the frozen-phase and shaft-speed policy explicit; this finding does not require introducing free-running rotor dynamics. Apply existing caps after obtaining a coherent vector.

Acceptance must include main and tail blades, translation and rotation separately, zero spin, unchanged pose, delayed activation, current inherited core motion, and cap-bound cases. The repaired vector must be covariant before clipping, and the released velocity must satisfy the chosen budget afterward. A subsequent rendered moving-core scenario should establish visible integration.

This is a presentation-layer counterexample. The fixture deliberately supplies C4 with zero reported impact energy and prescribes a zero-velocity transformed core. It does **not** prove that a naturally computed C4 flight crash follows that exact trajectory, establish occurrence frequency, or validate general rotor energy conservation.

## CR-AUD-17: A crash observer can capture and restore an incomplete quad session boundary

### Source and failure mechanism

The simulation commits its accepted frame and emits `events_located` and `stepped` before `Simulation.step()` returns. The multirotor session's kernel-event handler queues events; `tick_once()` appends the tape and updates `crash` only after that call returns. Its public `checkpoint()` can run during the earlier callback and combines the already committed kernel with not-yet-updated session metadata. See [kernel publication](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/simulation.gd#L691-L704), [session queueing](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/multirotor/session.gd#L386-L388), and [session completion](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/multirotor/session.gd#L593-L624).

The envelope validator checks the kernel and crash object separately. It does not require `MOTOR_CRASHED` to agree with a nonempty crash record. Resume and pilot-facing `crashed` telemetry depend on the session dictionary. These contracts are in [checkpoint/restore](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/multirotor/session.gd#L867-L919), [resume](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/multirotor/session.gd#L549-L559), and [telemetry](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/multirotor/session.gd#L827-L842).

### Reproduction and controls

The [probe](evidence/round2/probes/probe_quad_crash_checkpoint.gd) runs a stock-data quad through the existing strict-mode 3 m disarmed drop with neutral inputs. It captures an **untouched** result of `session.checkpoint()` inside the real crash `events_located` callback. No mode, crash dictionary, kernel frame, tape count, or serialized byte is edited.

The accepted crash is `Hard impact` at **tick 229**. The callback's kernel checkpoint is byte-identical to the kernel in the post-`tick_once` checkpoint. Their enclosing session metadata differs:

| Field | During kernel crash callback | After the session finishes the tick |
|---|---|---|
| Motor mode | `2`, `MOTOR_CRASHED` | `2`, `MOTOR_CRASHED` |
| Cut latch | `1` | `1` |
| Session crash record | Empty | Present |
| Tape ticks | `228` | `229` |
| Restore into a matching fresh session | Accepted | Accepted |
| `telemetry().crashed` after restore | **False** | Crash metadata retained |
| Resume after an explicit pause | **Accepted** | Refused |

A healthy pre-crash checkpoint also restores and resumes correctly. The probe completes **15 checks, with two desired-behavior failures**. [Observations](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/quad-crash-checkpoint/observations.json), [raw log](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/quad-crash-checkpoint/engine.log), and [checked report](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/quad-crash-checkpoint/report.json) retain the evidence.

### Consequence, correction, and limits

A public observer can obtain an envelope that the same public restore API accepts but that disagrees about whether the attempt crashed. **The motor cut remains latched; this test does not restore propulsion.** The defect is inconsistent session state and enforcement of the documented reset-required resume rule. Once crashed, the plant skips re-adjudication, so a later ordinary tick is not guaranteed to reconstruct the missing session crash record.

Choose and document one owner-boundary policy. Either finish session-owned metadata before exposing a session checkpoint at that boundary, or refuse checkpoint acquisition while `tick_once()` is still publishing its enclosing state and provide a documented completed-session signal. Do not solve this by prohibiting all kernel observer reads: the kernel deliberately supports read-only checkpoint observation of a committed frame.

Independently validate cross-field invariants during restore. Distinguish `MOTOR_CRASHED` from `MOTOR_DEPLETED`; an exhausted pack can validly have no crash record. A mismatch must be rejected without changing the live session. Resume and telemetry should not independently reinterpret contradictory terminal metadata.

Acceptance includes healthy, depleted, ground-crashed, and obstacle-crashed envelopes; reads from `events_located`, `stepped`, session crash callbacks, and the completed session tick; rejection/atomicity for inconsistent envelopes; and unchanged motor-cut behavior. The test's current either-refuse-or-return-a-complete-envelope oracle intentionally leaves the implementation choice open.

The stock scene was not shown to save a checkpoint from this raw kernel signal. The finding concerns a supported public observer/checkpoint boundary, demonstrated during a real accepted drop, rather than an observed UI save command or a claim that ordinary post-tick snapshots are broken.

## CR-AUD-18: Scenery arbitration masks a qualifying soft-ground impact

### Source and failure mechanism

With soft hull contacts enabled, fixed-wing dry ground is omitted from the early ground-versus-scenery comparison because continuous hull loads provide ground response. A terminal scenery hit can therefore become `_pending_crash` first. Subsequent hull-terminal checks either require no pending crash or return once a crash exists. See [soft-ground omission and scenery selection](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L3492-L3532), [the sampled-mass guard](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L3459-L3468), and [the session guard](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L3146-L3159).

The distinction is between **ground force evaluation** and **terminal incident arbitration**. Correctly applying a penalty force during RK stages does not itself decide which terminal event should own the resulting incident.

### Reproduction and controls

The [probe](evidence/round2/probes/probe_contact_order.gd) runs the real fixed-wing session and reference `Simulation.step()`. A single datum hull point starts 1 mm above flat ground, with northward speed 8 m/s and downward speed 4 m/s. A synthetic terminal wall starts at north 0.025 m. Production box/bin construction and production `HullContact.loads` are used. Aero, propulsion, gear, prop-disc, gravity, rotor momentum, continuous blocks, and extra substep policies are removed to isolate this composition.

At 240 Hz, the ground-only and combined ground/wall runs record **byte-identical RK stage states and loads** before the post-tick filter. The maximum recorded ground force is **1,043.227 N**, so this is not a no-force endpoint sketch. Independent production calls establish:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Ground terminal predicate at the matched candidate | `impact`, **8 J/kg**, above the declared 2.5 J/kg threshold |
| Reconstructed ground crossing fraction | Approximately **0.067783** |
| Reconstructed wall crossing fraction | Approximately **0.767628** |
| Ground predicate at the wall-contact pose itself | Already terminal; approximately 10.325 mm penetration |
| Ground-only 240 Hz control | `hull_contact` |
| Ground-clear wall-only 240 Hz control | `scenery` |
| Combined 240 Hz case | **`scenery`** |
| Combined finer-step 960 Hz control | `hull_contact` on its first accepted tick |

All ten fixture/control checks pass; the desired ground-selection check fails. The [raw log](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/contact-order/engine.log), [stage-level observations](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/contact-order/observations.json), and [checked report](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/contact-order/report.json) are retained.

### Interpretation, correction, and limits

This is a reproduced **mixed-contact arbitration gap**. The current soft-hull terminal rule is explicitly a tick-boundary rule. The fractions above are interpolation estimates, not exact physical times of impact. The 960 Hz case follows a different numerical trajectory; it is a useful independent ordering control, not dense output from the 240 Hz integration. Consequently, the evidence does not justify claiming an exact ground event at 0.067783 of the RK trajectory.

It does establish that the same accepted-step computation can qualify terminal ground contact, yet the presence of later reconstructed scenery contact changes the incident owner to scenery without comparing those terminal candidates. That can affect cause, witness, contact pose, downstream effects, and any future damage attribution based on the selected incident. This is separate from CR-AUD-04, which concerns mixing a selected witness with another probe's crossing fraction during event serialization.

Define an explicit terminal-candidate policy for soft ground, scenery, lake, track, gear, and prop contacts. If earliest accepted-path evidence is the goal, retain candidate identity and trajectory quality, compare compatible evidence, and report unresolved ordering when the path is insufficient. If a tick-boundary precedence rule is the intended approximation, document and test that rule and avoid presenting the chosen event as a physically established first impact.

The initial implementation gate should preserve the matched-stage controls while resolving this counterexample under the chosen policy. Add equal-time ties, reversed order, survivable ground followed by terminal scenery, dynamic mass, lake banks, rejected ticks, and multiple hull points. A later stock-geometry flown test should qualify practical relevance. Do not blindly enable legacy `touches_ground` for soft contacts: doing so could turn every harmless belly scrape into a terminal event and undo the intended continuous response.

## CR-AUD-19: Crash pause publication precedes the reason and damage commit

### Source and failure mechanism

The fixed-wing `_crash()` assigns the `crash` dictionary, calls `sim.set_paused(true)`, then assigns `pause_reason` and appends the contact event. For a pending scenery incident, `_on_sim_stepped()` applies scenery damage after `_crash()` returns. Meanwhile the real flight logger handles the pause signal synchronously and writes the current reason. See [crash publication](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L3748-L3778), [contact/damage completion](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_session.gd#L2580-L2605), and [logger pause handling](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/sim/flight_log.gd#L322-L332).

This is an observable re-entry boundary even in one thread. A pause listener can run between those assignments. The pinned engine run establishes the actual order; the [official Object signal connection reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_object.html#enum-object-connectflags), checked 18 November 2026, also distinguishes deferred callbacks from immediate delivery.

### Reproduction and controls

The corrected [probe](evidence/round2/probes/probe_crash_pause_log.gd) uses a single synthetic hull point and a terminal wire box. It starts at north -1 m, down -10 m, and body-forward speed 100 m/s, with gravity disabled and ordinary coupled stage loads retained. It uses production broadphase bins, manually advances accepted simulation ticks, and records with the real `FlightLog` implementation. The existing aircraft/continuous-state layout remains intact for valid checkpoint and log serialization.

A manually triggered pause with an explicitly preassigned reason first demonstrates that both the listener and saved log preserve a reason assigned before the signal. The subsequent swept wire hit occurs on **accepted tick 3**. At the crash pause callback:

| Observable | During pause callback | After the accepted step completes |
|---|---|---|
| Crash dictionary | Present | Present |
| Pause reason | **Empty** | `CRASH at 99.5 m/s ... fence impact (wire:probe) ...` |
| Contact-event count | **0** | **1** |
| Scenery-damage count | **0** | **1** |

The completed, readable `.orfl` file contains the correct manual-pause reason and a crash-tick pause event with an **empty detail string**. This is a persisted effect in an existing consumer, not merely a custom listener's observation. The probe executes **15 checks, with three desired-behavior failures** and no checked engine diagnostics. See [observations](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/crash-pause-log/observations.json), [raw log](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/crash-pause-log/engine.log), [checked report](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/runs/probes/crash-pause-log/report.json), and the [actual recording](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/8fdb1be6727e0620cdf04df303f3cff4454d6743/docs/research/crash-damage-investigations/CR-AUD-2026-11-17/evidence/round2/recordings/fixedwing-crash-pause.orfl).

### Correction and limits

Stage and commit the full owner-level incident before publishing the pause notification used by observers. That includes the final reason, terminal contact, simultaneous supported contact facts, and scenery damage. A dedicated session-level completed-crash event with a coherent immutable payload may be preferable if kernel pause cannot change its timing. Update the logger to consume a boundary that carries a complete reason.

Moving only the `pause_reason` assignment ahead of `set_paused()` addresses the missing log detail but leaves the partial contact/damage observation. Changing only the logger to a deferred signal can conceal the ordering issue and may change tick association; it needs explicit lifecycle tests rather than being treated as a complete transaction fix.

Acceptance should cover manual pause, terminal ground, terminal scenery, lake, auto-restart, immediate log stop from a listener, and failure/rollback. Verify the saved event's accepted tick and reason, and that the intended public completed-crash boundary exposes consistent contact/damage data. Do not claim a corrupted fixed-wing checkpoint here: that was not established by this probe.

The first attempt did not hit the wire because the handmade fixture omitted the negative-East broadphase cell. That run and its exported trace are retained as **inconclusive fixture evidence**, excluded from product counts. The correction used the production bin builder. The 100 m/s initial condition is a controlled logging trigger, not a certification of stock high-speed flight or damage severity.

## CR-AUD-20: A terminal quad obstacle strike has no solid-preserving aftermath response

### Source and failure mechanism

In the graded quad frame-obstacle path, a closing speed at or above the class threshold returns `Obstacle strike` **before** the nonterminal push-out and bounce. The endpoint transition marks the motor state crashed and latches the cut. It appropriately avoids the terrain-specific `_settle_impact()` for an airborne obstacle, but it supplies no alternative obstacle response. Later crashed ticks skip obstacle adjudication entirely. See [graded frame response](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/physics/multirotor/controlled_plant.gd#L977-L1021) and [terminal transition](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/physics/multirotor/controlled_plant.gd#L624-L667).

The session continues accepted ticks after a crash. The activity's aftermath advances that session and poses the original quad from its accepted/interpolated state; obstacle effects do not substitute a separately colliding wreck. These integration paths are in [the scene's crash handler](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/vehicles/multirotor/multirotor_scene.gd#L954-L963), [continuing advancement and pose](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/vehicles/multirotor/multirotor_scene.gd#L1096-L1164), and [obstacle effect handling](https://github.com/bultodepapas/OpenRCsimulator/blob/a7de9b35676d891bc2034cc344b910d9e1ef9c61/app/vehicles/multirotor/quad_crash_fx.gd#L138-L157).

### Reproduction and controls

The [probe](evidence/round2/probes/probe_quad_obstacle_aftermath.gd) prepares the real default electric quad with graded impacts, ordinary gravity and aerodynamics, a disarmed start at 30 m altitude, and stopped shafts to isolate frame contact. It commits valid prescribed starts at **3 m/s and 9 m/s** toward a synthetic hard box, plus matched no-obstacle controls. The wall spans north 0.6–0.9 m and is 12 m wide and tall. Each case accepts 120 ticks, or 0.5 s at 240 Hz.

The slower hit at tick 43 has incoming normal speed **2.978410 m/s**. Its contact-point normal velocity becomes **-0.660716 m/s**, the front of the frame is pushed back to north **0.59 m**, and no terminal crash occurs. This demonstrates that the obstacle is installed, detected, and capable of producing a bounded nonterminal response.

### Interpretation, correction, and limits

This is a bounded **terminal-aftermath capability gap**: the body continues through a solid that already caused a terminal frame strike. It is not an undetected-impact claim; detection and motor cut both work. It is also not a request to apply the terrain settlement function to an airborne wall. That function uses a different surface and could move the vehicle incorrectly.

The current documented graded law specifies nonterminal bounce and a terminal classification; it does not promise a measured terminal restitution model. The audit therefore proposes the minimal product invariant that an intact visible frame should not continue fully through a fixed solid after its terminal strike. That invariant is separate from choosing realistic restitution, fracture, or wall damage.

Implement an obstacle-specific terminal response at the accepted contact, or hand off to a declared colliding aftermath body. Separate **one-shot terminal gameplay adjudication** from **continued physical collision response**, so suppressing repeat crash events does not also suppress all later obstacle contact. Carry the contact normal, witness, and world identity into the chosen aftermath path. Keep drive cut and one-shot notifications unchanged.

Acceptance should include head-on and oblique strikes, wall/roof/bar normals, adjacent second obstacles during aftermath, ground contact after a wall strike, thin obstacles, replay/reset, and bounded energy behavior. The 3 m/s control should keep its current nonterminal behavior. A rendered scene check must confirm the visual consequence; this headless probe establishes accepted motion and the source predicts how the scene poses it, but no screenshot-based observation is claimed.
