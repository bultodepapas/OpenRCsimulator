# Vehicle and aircraft opportunities — 22 November 2026

Scope: drone/multirotor, ground cars, fixed-wing flap/camera paths, and selected helicopter/airship presentation guards. The audit worktree application files match main `7940a4081e10994f34c858c4acac3d7a1a271464`. These are source-confirmed work paths, not measured FPS gains. No app, test, or hardware was launched. Findings are distinct from prior Nitro voice/artifact reads, hidden contact-debug clear, quad rotor-attach construction, fixed-wing contact observers, controller scans, and the fixed-tick cap. Priorities below are for discrimination, not measured ranking.

The 2026-11-21 stutter map exercised **three fixed-wing aircraft**, with native physics and muted sound, not a drone or either car (`docs/research/god-test/2026-11-21-stutter-map.md:1-12`). Its loading/menu and GPU observations do not establish the cause or magnitude of any vehicle-specific item below. Earlier 2026-11-12 quad evidence, on one 1080p Windows PC, reports the old full presentation process around 0.45 ms/frame, including about 80 µs telemetry, after its then-current optimizations (`docs/research/drone-racing/M1-M3-2026-11-11/README.md:83-91`). This is useful historical context, not current source or savings evidence.

## T01. Quad telemetry evaluates the entire plant for two electrical bus values

**Reachability and path.** Home's ION R5 and its command-line route select `openrc-fpv5-x1` (`app/vehicles/multirotor/multirotor_catalog.gd:1-11`); its shipped powertrain is electric with damped braking (`app/data/multirotors/openrc-fpv5-x1.json:700-714`). The activity calls `_current_telemetry()` during each process callback (`app/vehicles/multirotor/multirotor_scene.gd:1185-1193`), which calls `Session.telemetry()` when the accepted tick changes (`:1249-1256`). The session obtains the accepted plant frame and invokes plant telemetry (`app/sim/multirotor/session.gd:1015-1033`). For the electric law, `ControlledPlant.telemetry()` invokes `_evaluate()` and then uses only `evaluation.bus` for voltage and pack current (`app/physics/multirotor/controlled_plant.gd:1480-1493`).

**Work.** Before producing that bus, `_evaluate` obtains wind and density, computes airframe and ground contact forces (`:364-379`); it subsequently computes all four rotor loads and coupled shaft/body accelerations (`:417-473`) unless the bus fails. Bus inputs themselves are assembled from charge, held motor command, rotor speed and modes (`:397-416`); `_bus` contains idle, damped regenerative, one-quadrant and fallback cases (`:480-526`). Thus on an ordinary successful electric telemetry evaluation, all body force and derivative results are discarded. The cache normally evaluates once at the first caller observing a new accepted tick, typically a render callback; `_race_phase()` is another caller during reconfiguration (`multirotor_scene.gd:929–932,1259–1266`). It is not intrinsically 240 extra evaluations/s: updates can coalesce and repeated reads of a held tick reuse the cache. Reset/invalidation can also recompute at the same tick. Terrain memoization and the high-altitude contact early return (`:529-545`, `app/sim/multirotor/session.gd:790-814`) already reduce some work.

**Actual backend.** This particular plant is created from `GDScript` (`controlled_plant.gd:160-167`) and installs its `sample_tick` and `stage_sampled` callbacks on the model (`:294-315`, `app/sim/multirotor/session.gd:281-295,331-335`). Race composition wraps and invokes those same callbacks (`app/sim/race/race_model.gd:78-83,118-139,282-290`). A native request or an aircraft-native result is not evidence of native quad execution; observe the actual route. Godot vector/container methods invoked from GDScript may themselves be native engine operations, but the cited control flow is script.

**Smallest candidate.** Extract the exact current charge/command/shaft/mode-to-bus input and solve sequence into a shared helper; call it from the current stage and a bus-only telemetry projection. Do not reuse a result from an RK stage at a different state, lower evaluation frequency, or remove warnings/pack values under an Off OSD. Preserve the disconnected-pack empty bus and failed-bus dictionary/default behavior, operation ordering, damped regeneration/charge acceptance, one-quadrant fallback, rotor health and held inputs. The helper must not change `Simulation`'s transaction, checkpoint, float64 state, 240 Hz clock, or physics laws. A native implementation is an option only behind real admission and parity gates; a newly extracted shared GDScript helper is the smaller first experiment.

**Discriminating check.** Compare old and projected telemetry and full bus dictionaries on detached accepted states covering disarmed, armed, coast, depleted/zero charge, negative shaft speeds, turtle reverse, regenerative acceptance limit, mixed motor active sets, invalid/no-bus and airborne/ground poses. Add reset, pause, race/free-flight and cache tests, including READY/COUNTDOWN. Race telemetry passes the accepted plant frame unchanged; the stage separately suppresses commands in READY/COUNTDOWN (`race_model.gd:167–170,282–290`), so a stage bus cannot replace the telemetry result. Confirm the purity/error contract of injected wind and contact callbacks before removing their telemetry-only invocation. The solver fingerprint hashes physics source (`app/sim/flight_log.gd:420–438`), and multirotor logs bind it; a numerically identical helper can still cause old-source replay to report `solver_changed`. Keep old baselines and separate same-source exact replay from a candidate comparison; use `test_mr_controlled_plant.gd`, `test_mr_pack_charge.gd`, `test_mr_electric_bus.gd`, `test_mr_activity.gd:591-594`, session/race and replay tests as relevant. Because extracting a common helper touches the stage, require numerical parity and fault/rollback gates, not just visual equivalence. Measure component, whole process, physics callback, frame tails and actual selected backend on identical routed flights; do not extrapolate the historical 80 µs.

## T02. Normal car rendering asks the full physics road query for surface data

**Reachability and path.** The default car is electric Bisonte and the selected alternative is MONSTER CAR 2 (`app/vehicles/ground/vehicle_catalog.gd:4-13`). Production uses the airfield rather than the synthetic fixture, with vehicle effects enabled by default (`app/vehicles/ground/ground_scene.gd:90-120,195-207,225-238`). The activity's render road callback is `AirfieldCourse.sample` (`:206-207`). Every process frame it samples the drawn vehicle center for `surface_id` (`:717-737`); when effects are prepared, unpaused, telemetry valid and wheel positions finite, four wheels sample the same full callback at their individually drawn positions (`:744-749`, `app/vehicles/ground/ground_effects.gd:114-159`). Camera terrain and the optional projected-shadow cue can add samples (`ground_scene.gd:215-217,734`, `ground_camera.gd:32-52`). These are **at least five applicable presentation calls per frame**, not five physics calls or five calls in pause/disabled-effects operation.

**Work.** `AirfieldCourse.sample()` composes validated field/lake terrain and the RC overlay, which the visual consumers do need. But it also constructs the normal/friction result and calls `Track.nearby_walls` for the physics `walls` field (`app/physics/ground_vehicle/airfield_course.gd:188-264`; `app/physics/ground_vehicle/airfield_track.gd:334-340`). A nonempty scenery-wall inventory adds binned candidate lookup, possible distance sorting/merging and overflow counting (`airfield_course.gd:265-278`, `scenery_walls.gd:95-114`). Empty scenery bins short-circuit that path. Track wall queries still inspect the full bounded wall list (at most eight) even far from the circuit; only the separate track surface calculation has the distant early-out; the source proves calls and branches, not that each call scans every obstacle or that this dominates FPS.

**Smallest candidate.** Add an explicitly presentation-only surface/height projection sharing the exact existing field, track, lake and surface arithmetic. Route only center, drawn-wheel, camera and shadow calls that require these fields through it; keep the complete physics road callback with the same wall queries and numerical ordering. Distinct interpolated wheel/camera positions are not interchangeable with accepted physics telemetry. Do not drop wetness, dust or terrain work merely because the vehicle is airborne. Note that `AirfieldCourse.sample` increments the global `walls_omitted_total` and emits its diagnostic warning for overflow even on presentation calls (`airfield_course.gd:265-286`): changing the number or meaning of those diagnostics is observable and requires an explicit diagnostics-contract decision and validation. Also, `airfield_course.gd` is part of the course source-artifact manifest and SHA (`:143-183,429-443`), so changing that file changes course identity even when the new function is presentation-only. Preserve the documented checkpoint and recording compatibility/refusal behavior; do not hide identity changes. A presentation wrapper using unchanged helpers may be preferable if it can reproduce the exact terrain/surface composition.

**Validation.** Preserve invalid samples caused by normal, friction and rolling validation even when a render consumer needs only height (`airfield_course.gd:218–245,475–478`). Compare needed outputs and failure/default behavior at surface precedence edges, RC ramps and banks, lake shoreline, wet/mud areas, invalid/finite positions and scenery-heavy bins. Verify dry/wet dust, wading, camera clipping, projected shadows, both vehicles, reset and transitions. Confirm physical trajectory, contacts, replay and diagnostics remain as specified. Measure whole-process and road-query callback timings, calls/frame and frame tails on the default airfield, busy scenery and wet shore, plus effects Off and multiple camera modes. The default matching car field always installs its circuit (`ground_scene.gd:475–485`, `airfield_course.gd:124–129`); the aircraft/quad RC-track preference does not control it (`rc_track_option.gd:1–8`). A no-track comparison requires a nonmatching custom field or an explicit fixture and is a different workload, not a normal car setting A/B. Car `VehicleDynamics` loads a separately admitted GDExtension with receipt/hash checks and falls back when absent; its candidate binary list only admits Windows/Linux (`app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_dynamics.gd:198-226,243-248,738-759`). Confirm actual identity/calls; do not report a requested backend as actual. The road composition remains script, but its near-lake branch can use admitted native `VehicleDynamics.sample_lake_terrain` before falling back (`airfield_course.gd:201–210`). MONSTER CAR 2 retains a scripted Nitro adapter over the optional native body evaluator, with composite backend identity (`nitro_dynamics.gd:1–5,34–52,87–95,125–132`); a native body receipt does not prove native powertrain execution.

## T05. Dry car frames still run wet-pool slot and ripple bookkeeping

**Reachability and work.** With vehicle effects enabled (default), valid unpaused telemetry and all four wheel positions finite, `GroundWetEffects.update_render` parses the accepted environment, computes four wheel activities and calls `_sync_wheel_pool` for each (`app/vehicles/ground/ground_scene.gd:225-238,744-749`; `app/vehicles/ground/ground_wet_effects.gd:70-145`). A nonfinite wheel position skips that wheel's pool update (`ground_wet_effects.gd:107–114`), reducing the count below. In a completely dry environment `VehicleEnvironment.dry()` supplies zero wheel wetness/mud (`app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_environment.gd:22-28`). Even with zero activity, four wheels each enter eight drop iterations and calculate separate water and mud visibility, ages and flight heights; they also visit eight hidden rings and write their zero-alpha/visibility values (`ground_wet_effects.gd:295-349`). That's **32 loop iterations and 64 age evaluations per applicable frame**, plus bounds/count/property updates; it is not 64 draws or a GPU cost claim. No MultiMesh instance transform/color is uploaded in the zero-activity branch. Pools are preallocated and use `visible_instance_count`; pause exits early, and disabling vehicle effects avoids constructing or updating the component (`:43-65,83-90,143-144,182-249`).

**Candidate and check.** After validating telemetry and computing exact wheel activities, skip per-slot work when both activities are exactly zero, and clear only on an active-to-inactive transition. Retain advancing presentation clock, audio cooldown, dirty/invalid-state cleanup, pool bounds when next needed, zero counts/ring state, diagnostic snapshot and exact first wet/mud frame. Do not gate on speed alone: residual splashes, mixed wheels, pause/resume and sound can be observable. Test `app/tests/test_rcv_wet_effects.gd` and `test_rcv_effects_activity.gd` plus dry→wet→dry, mixed mud/water, pause/reset, invalid input and sound enable transitions. Profile dry versus wet frames and feature off on a real car route; avoid promising a meaningful gain before attribution.

## T06. Quad builds the same scenery index three times on entry or rebind

**Reachability and cost.** A quad with a validated, nonempty scenery inventory installs three callables for obstacle clearance, class and prop-disc contact (`app/vehicles/multirotor/multirotor_scene.gd:210-228`; `app/sim/multirotor/session.gd:321-325`). Each factory independently calls `obstacle_index(world, reach)` with the same world and reach (`session.gd:753-754,769-770,874-875`). Each build enumerates all P proxies and constructs cell lists (`:821-843`): three full proxy enumerations and three separate retained bin-map/list structures, not three copies of every proxy or a known 3× total startup time. Live `set_collision_world` on a prepared flight installs another three (`:714-736`). Race-frame boxes are separately iterated, and empty-world/index paths make the candidate cheap. Existing DR-22/R1 already replaced the older broad phase with an exact spatial index (`docs/research/drone-racing/M1-M3-2026-11-11/README.md:83`); this finding is about duplicating that index's construction, not an unindexed query.

**Candidate and check.** Compute a single immutable prepared index per installed world and reach and capture it in all three factories, without a global cross-session cache. Keep gate-frame arrays and query-specific filtering separate; preserve candidate ordering, tie/signed-zero behavior, world replacement isolation and live rebind refusal/recorder semantics. Compare clearance, class and disc witnesses across borders, wide objects, overlap/ties, empty worlds, race gates and replaced inventories. Extend `test_mr_fast_paths.gd`, `test_mr_session.gd`, `test_quad_disc_box_separation.gd` and race/crash replay checks. Attribute index build duration and resident unique allocations on cold/warm activity entry and rebind before asserting loading improvements.

## T07. Default car signal duplicates telemetry unused by its presentation listener

On each successfully accepted unsuppressed tick, the ground session emits `tick_advanced` with `_state.duplicate()` and `_last_telemetry.duplicate(true)` (`app/sim/ground_vehicle/session.gd:471-483,515-556`). At real-time cadence this is up to 240 full-telemetry duplicate **calls** per second; it does not prove 240 deep physical copies of every packed buffer. The application subscriber is `ground_scene._accepted_tick`, which ignores the telemetry parameter and uses state to advance interpolated pose and Nitro crank phase (`app/vehicles/ground/ground_scene.gd:295,710-715`). The accepted telemetry computation and validation itself is required for damage/rollback and must remain (`session.gd:515-527`). External/dynamic signal listeners may still rely on its public payload.

If profiling shows material allocation/callback cost, consider a lightweight pose-only notification for the activity and retain the original detached full-payload signal for its existing observers, avoiding construction only when no full-payload listeners are present. Preserve signal ordering and subscription changes, callback ownership and mutation isolation, failure paths, state publication, rollback and checkpoint/replay. `test_rcv_session.gd`, damage/recording and activity tests should be extended with both simultaneous listener types, mutation attempts, connection changes and rejected ticks. Profile whole-tick time/allocations separately from the physics evaluator; the source does not establish a meaningful FPS effect.

## T04. Fixed-wing flap animation reads a full advisory state

**Path and scope.** During a fixed-wing process frame, `main.gd` calls `_render_pose`; for a session with simulated flaps it calls `session.flap_state()` and passes only `angle_deg` to the visual builder (`app/main.gd:1555-1564,3687-3696`; `app/render/airplane.gd:381-386`). The getter validates the AUX position, then computes `air_data(sim.state)` and separately `air_density(sim.state)` so it can return the equivalent-airspeed overspeed advisory along with angle and target (`app/sim/flight_session.gd:4453-4464`). Spatial weather may make `air_data` enter `WindFlow.stage_air` (`:677-686`). Those advisory calculations occur for flap animation even when the diagnostic HUD and controls guide are hidden, including frozen frames whenever presentation still runs. The actual actuator angle remains an accepted-state value, not the interpolated rigid-body pose. `Flaps.enabled` explicitly requires the capability and module (`app/physics/flaps.gd:8-11`): Avanti, F4U Corsair and P-51 data declare simulated flaps; the default Ugly Stik and GP Extra do not. This is distinct from the previously reported flight-audio wind/air query and does not imply every aircraft or every native stage takes this path.

**Candidate and conditions.** For the animation consumer only, use a validated accepted flap-angle projection that preserves the current unavailable, invalid-AUX and nonfinite fallbacks, without calculating airspeed or density. Keep the full `flap_state` for diagnostics, pilot actions and control-guide overspeed status. Do not reduce physics rate, change actuator interpolation/trajectory, feed render values back to simulation, or cache across reset/restore and model swaps without an identity/lifecycle proof. A small built-in/GDScript projection is a plausible first experiment; a native rewrite requires a measured hotspot and the appropriate actual native admission and parity gates.

**Conditional guide add-on.** When fixed-wing overlays exist, `_overlay_snapshot()` constructs `control_guide.equipment_state.flaps` with a separate `flap_state()` call on each approximately 10 Hz text refresh (`app/main.gd:1607-1612,2477-2493`). Yet the guide reads that payload only when both the overlay layer and controls frame are visible (`app/render/overlays/overlay_manager.gd:418-448`). If controls are hidden, consider omitting only guide-specific equipment/capability production and forcing a fresh value on reveal. Controls are visible in the default Pilot preset (`app/render/overlays/overlay_settings.gd:30-36,47-48`), so this is conditional and cannot be described as the default for all flights. Preserve alert, fuel, battery, flight-metric/statistics and warning sampling; the rest of the snapshot has other consumers. Existing context equality already avoids rebuilding unchanged controls rows (`app/render/overlays/controls_guide.gd:70-82`).

**Validation.** Count calls and time `flap_state` / weather sampling separately from the full render callback for Avanti/Corsair, flap-equipped versus non-equipped aircraft, controls visible/hidden, diagnostics on/off and uniform/spatial weather. Preserve extension/retraction, rejected/malformed AUX, pause, restore, reload and recording/replay; compare accepted angle, built-in visual articulation and full overspeed advisories. Existing seams include `app/tests/test_flaps_session.gd` and `app/tests/test_overlay_manager.gd:620-628`; extend them and test actual native/reference selection. No current timing or FPS gain is established.

## T11. Lower-priority camera finding: duplicate last fly-by clearance

**Path.** Fixed-wing `main.gd` and the helicopter activity pass their scenery clearance adapter to the shared camera rig (`app/main.gd:2465-2466,3711-3713`; `app/helicopter_main.gd:281-283,375-378`). It is used when fly-by is selected, on initial placement, teleport or after the aircraft passes (`app/render/camera/camera_rig.gd:289-295`; `app/render/camera/flyby_view.gd:58-67`). The placement tries at most six candidates: the loop calls `obstacles(aim, position)`, then after the final attempt calls it again with the same arguments for pull-in (`flyby_view.gd:81-107`). The actual adapters call `CameraCollision.free_fraction`, whose local calculation traverses read-only 32 m scenery bins (`app/render/camera/camera_collision.gd:19-56`; `app/scenery/collision_world.gd:16-18`). Therefore, for this production adapter there is one duplicate query per placement, including when the first candidate is clear: **one to six candidate calls plus one repeat**, not one duplicate per process frame. Pilot view is the default and does not exercise the fly-by path.

Reuse the last clearance result for the final pull-in when the callback has the production adapter's read-only semantics; preserve exact random draws, candidate order, ground clamp, six retries and fractional pull-in. Since the public argument is a generic `Callable`, do not silently assume arbitrary external callbacks are pure: define/verify the callback contract or limit the optimization to a proven adapter. Compare clear, blocked, six-fail, no-world, empty callable, zero-length boom, reset and teleport cases with `app/tests/test_camera_collision.gd`, `test_camera_views.gd`, `test_camera_history.gd` and a call-count fixture; then measure placement-frame tails. Historical CAM-09 measurements on an earlier Godot 4.7.2 / llvmpipe setup report roughly 3–9 µs for isolated examples, with 56.6 µs under simultaneous rendering/load (`docs/research/camera-views/CAM-09/README.md:26-36`); neither their source nor platform establishes the current cost or a visible hitch.

## Supplemental coverage: checked guards, not new bottlenecks

- The helicopter's selected fly-by shares the checked camera path above. Its magnifier starts disabled, updates its SubViewport only when shown, and returns before scene-camera work while hidden (`app/render/camera/magnifier.gd:61-72,125-147,211-215`); do not claim a hidden extra render pass. Previously documented F18 covers its accepted-tick rotor/envelope and hidden-HUD work.
- Airship `telemetry_info()` caches the detailed dynamics evaluation by accepted tick (`app/sim/airship/session.gd:525-533`), although the surrounding telemetry dictionary and HUD can still update per process frame; those are already F18. The normal airship visual's legacy emission preparation caches material bindings on the visual root and returns for unchanged mode/gain (`app/vehicles/airship/airship_visual.gd:274-289`; `app/render/world/aircraft_lights.gd:597-637`). Dynamic light-follow work is visibility-gated (`airship_visual.gd:292-296`); a visible kit, its on/off transition and a Workshop lighting rig have separate behavior. No repeated full model scan or hidden-kit GPU draw is established by these calls.
- Airship removes its joypad and controller-selection connections when leaving (`app/vehicles/airship/airship_scene.gd:308-316`). Its development GLB digest is cached by file path/modification time, and the exported missing-raw-GLB path relies on the pin (`app/vehicles/airship/airship_presentation.gd:26-27,212-227`); this does not prove entry is cheap or the metadata cache catches every possible same-time mutation. No extra broad cache or teardown change is recommended without a specific measured cause.
