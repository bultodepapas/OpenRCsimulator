# Fourth pass — ground vehicles and native boundaries

Review input: `OpenRCsimulator` branch `graphics-audit`, HEAD `b0f0bd17fdc2e2e2944d2cda6765c06bc31f6ac9`; application source previously verified against main `7940a4081e10994f34c858c4acac3d7a1a271464`. This is a read-only source investigation: no app, engine, tests, native binary, or hardware was launched. Counts describe source operations under stated conditions; they are not timings, physical allocations, draw calls, GPU work, or FPS savings. The lead owns any probes and integration.

## U01. One successful native body evaluation calculates corner geometry twice

**Path and eligibility.** An unpaused car tick is sent by `app/vehicles/ground/ground_scene.gd:504-515` to `app/sim/ground_vehicle/session.gd:471-515`. Bisonte installs its joint `_stage`, which calls `VehicleDynamics.stage_derivative` (`session.gd:2215-2234`); the selected MONSTER CAR 2 instead executes the scripted `NitroDynamics.step` from the whole-tick policy and calls `VehicleDynamics.step` on a 31-value body (`session.gd:2217-2240`, `app/physics/ground_vehicle/nitro_dynamics.gd:87-141`). Both also evaluate accepted-endpoint telemetry after the step. `VehicleDynamics._evaluate_active` dispatches to the separately admitted native body extension when available; otherwise it uses its GDScript reference (`app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_dynamics.gd:698-759`). The native ground loader is Windows/Linux only and requires compatible artifact/receipt checks (`:198-226,243-248`). The current checkout has no `app/native/ground_vehicle/` installation, so no native execution is established here. Nitro's powertrain remains scripted; native body selection does not make the full vehicle native.

On a successful call, `research/rc-monster-truck/native/src/ground_vehicle_native.cpp:651-694` parses the state/model/controls into C++ values, computes steering acceleration and `corner_geometry`, computes query positions, and calls the road batch. After that callback, `ground_vehicle_kernel.hpp:743-777` recomputes the same acceleration expression and a second `corner_geometry` from those values before solving. This is **two source calls to the four-corner geometry routine in one successful native evaluation**, including endpoint telemetry, not twice the complete stage or a proved runtime factor. A callback failure exits before the second call. Wet and wall work in GDScript are separate and may independently construct geometry (`vehicle_dynamics.gd:721-737,765-801`).

Dry nonzero-derivative ticks normally have four RK body evaluations plus one accepted-endpoint telemetry evaluation; zero derivatives can stop early. Wet-zone selection may split a 240 Hz tick into two pieces, giving up to eight RK evaluations plus endpoint telemetry. The integration policy's checks and early exits determine the real count (`vehicle_dynamics.gd:476-510,559-582`, `app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_environment.gd:15`, `app/physics/integrator.gd:28-35`). A rejected tick may not reach telemetry. Count actual calls instead of treating these as universal.

**Small candidate and boundaries.** Pass the already computed geometry and acceleration into a private native implementation for the *same invocation*, leaving the existing standalone kernel entry's validation and independent callable contract intact. Retain all validity, road-sample, failure and exception checks and their observable order. The parser copies each state scalar, model scalar/vector and control into stack-owned C++ structures before the batch callback; that callback receives coordinates, not those structures (`ground_vehicle_native.cpp:99-206,253-325,666-694`). Nevertheless, the public callable can mutate its own inputs, reenter or alter ambient execution state; do not assume arbitrary callbacks are pure or that floating-point environment can never change without an explicit contract. Verify equivalence or restrict the optimization accordingly. Never reuse geometry across RK stages, endpoint telemetry, reset or ticks. The opaque `callv` boundary and compiler optimization make it necessary to inspect optimized assembly or profile: two source calls do not prove that all instructions execute twice. Preserve float64, strict compiler flags, the 240 Hz atomic tick, solver/refusal and source/binary/receipt identities, rollback, and replay identity. This is distinct from N1 artifact reads, F25/S02 aircraft arithmetic, and T02 presentation road calls.

**Check.** Compare complete native results and failures, callback count/order, mutated/reused road samples, signed zeros and nonfinite/malformed inputs against the original, including callback reentry/failure and floating environment if admitted. Include wet/dry, non-flat road, wall/no-wall, suspension/steering extremes, friction saturation, both car paths, telemetry, exact accepted states, checkpoint/restore and rejected-tick rollback. Use `research/rc-monster-truck/native/parity_harness.gd`, `app/tests/test_rcv_road_batch.gd`, `test_rcv_vehicle_dynamics.gd`, `test_rcv_obstacle_contacts.gd`, `test_rcv_nitro_session.gd`, and native build/package gates. Refresh platform receipts and retain correct old-build replay refusal. Measure isolated native geometry/evaluator and whole-tick/frame tails on matched actual native/reference configurations before prioritizing.

**Additional boundary coverage, not yet a separate recommendation.** Every native `evaluate` reparses 32 required scalar model fields, an optional brake split, five model packed arrays and hull vectors; `read_model` and `read_controls` validate them, while `evaluate` validates the resulting values again (`ground_vehicle_native.cpp:82-206,651-681`, `ground_vehicle_kernel.hpp:185-276,743-760`). Each full result also packages nine float64 arrays and two byte arrays (`ground_vehicle_native.cpp:216-250`), and GDScript validates every native result channel before the stage uses acceleration and steering (`vehicle_dynamics.gd:600-636,747-759,1060-1093`). Accepted telemetry, wall response, malformed-channel rejection and direct native callers require the full contract; no wholesale deletion or unsafe prepared-model cache is proposed. Session model data is copied on preparation and rebuilt for reset/experiments (`session.gd:199-240,316-352,396-397`), while Nitro creates a torque-adjusted body-model dictionary separately for its step and telemetry (`nitro_dynamics.gd:125-132,151-169,224-231`). If profiling shows boundary marshalling dominates, prepare immutable fields at a proved owner boundary and pass dynamic torque separately or design an internal stage projection, but first preserve mutation/refusal semantics, all currently checked channels and public full results. This is not evidence that any full-tick native integration or body state conversion would be safe or worthwhile.

## U02. Nitro visual samplers repeatedly prepare identical axis coordinates

**Path and count.** MONSTER CAR 2 is the selected alternative, not the default Bisonte (`app/vehicles/ground/vehicle_catalog.gd:4-13`). Once its validated asset is built, `ground_scene.gd:717-733` invokes its visual adapter on each process frame, including while paused. `monster_car_2_model.gd:123-148` builds 205 ordinary mesh samplers and four wheel-pivot samplers from `app/assets/vehicles/monster-car-2/visual_rig.json`; `:358-417` invokes all 209 in a successful draw. The 209-track inventory and historical rig-binary hash are already recorded in `docs/research/rc-monster-truck/RCV-35/README.md:7,29`. RCV-37 had already compiled metadata and introduced reusable per-instance scratch (`docs/research/rc-monster-truck/RCV-37/README.md:1-3`); its historical timing is not this experiment. This lead concerns remaining repeated per-axis grid preparation, not discovery of the tracks or prior optimization. `_sample_compiled_track` (`:461-501`) computes clamped grid coordinates, lower/upper indices and fractions separately for every track axis. The pinned manifest has 171 one-axis tracks and 38 two-axis tracks: **247 axis preparations** per successful draw and an upper bound of **494 mask-loop iterations**; zero weights skip the corresponding 12-component decode. Its current five named inputs have identical `(low, step, count)` for all occurrences of each name: `h_FL` 53, `h_FR` 53, `h_RL` 47, `h_RR` 47, `steer` 47. Asset metadata is at `visual_rig.json:49,1064,1172,6377,6448`. These are source-loop counts, not submitted draws. The existing [RCV-37 optimization](../rc-monster-truck/RCV-37/README.md) already precompiled the metadata and scratch arrays; this is a continuation of that work, not a rediscovery or claim that those older costs still exist.

**Small candidate.** During one draw, compute each unique exact `(axis name, low, step, count)` grid mapping once and reuse the low index, high index and fraction for matching samplers; retain generic handling for differing/invalid metadata. A separate candidate gives only the runtime result a `PackedFloat32Array`-typed transform helper; keep the original `Array` helper for manifest correction matrices. The component experiment isolates this boundary from shared coordinates. Do not alter interpolation mask order, bounds checks, correction matrices, input clamping, or the 12-component accumulation through the sampler's `PackedFloat32Array` (`monster_car_2_model.gd:21-31,438-501`). No result needs to persist across frames. Actual mesh setters occur between sampling calls in `draw`; production use also needs a proof that callbacks/reentry cannot change the shared input dictionary or compiled metadata during that interval. The isolated fixture does not establish that ownership contract. The compiled tracks and their mutable sampler buffers already exist, so this is a refinement to current preparation, not a proposal to rebuild or regenerate the rig. The four wheel groups each have nine meshes, and all 36 meshes are also animated tracks. Any more ambitious unchanged-track cache must preserve each track's **unspun** base before applying each frame's wheel rotation: using the prior transformed node as base compounds spin (`:397-417`). Preserve engine phase and bell/wheel distinction (`:419-434`).

**Check.** Extend `app/tests/test_rcv_nitro_visual.gd` with exact old/new per-track transforms, all 209 real manifest tracks, grid endpoints, interior cells, grid ties, out-of-range clamp and malformed data. Exercise all wheels independently, moving steering/suspension with changing spin, repeated identical input, pause/resume, model rebuild and engine/bell phases; compare rendered output. The sparse worktree lacks `visual_rig.bin` and the GLB, but the pinned Git object database contains the exact 3,553,104-byte rig binary; its SHA-256 is `4127dc26b64ed2b4ddacd230cc073ec7bab273bb7a624416587264592bfb573d`, matching the historical receipt. Extract it to an isolated probe, leaving the checkout unchanged. A component witness can call `_compile_track` then `_sample_compiled_track` on those exact bytes, but full-scene profiling with the same captured/recorded input remains necessary. Measure sampler and complete render callback separately; no gain has been measured here.

## U03. Nitro service status repeatedly reconstructs its full state each frame

**Path and count.** For a prepared MONSTER CAR 2, `ground_scene.gd:736-738` calls `session.nitro_service_stationary()` in every process callback, whether the garage is shown or not; the dashboard consumes the resulting status (`app/vehicles/ground/ground_dashboard.gd:589-608`). Bisonte returns before doing this work. In `app/sim/ground_vehicle/session.gd:642-649`, each `_state[index]` runs the property getter (`:69-74`) and `_combined_state()` (`:2165-2168`), which duplicates the 13-value body and appends the 20-value Nitro continuous block. The three `v[i] * v[i]` expressions therefore perform **six** reconstructions; at or below the speed threshold, up to four more arise from wheel checks. A moving frame above threshold makes six calls; a complete stationary check makes ten. Wheel early exits make seven to ten. The count is getter/reconstruction calls, not a verified allocator or CPU-time figure. `sim.state` and `sim.continuous` are plain kernel members (`app/sim/simulation.gd:40-51`); the predicate has no intervening callbacks.

**Small candidate and checks.** Capture `_state` once after the prepared/Nitro guard, and use that same-call local through the existing arithmetic and early returns; do not cache across calls, use an interpolated render state, change mathematical grouping, or alter existing malformed/nonfinite behavior. `service_nitro` also relies on this predicate while paused (`session.gd:585-601`), so preserve the real safety check. Test `app/tests/test_rcv_nitro_service.gd` and activity/recovery replay with speed-squared just below, exactly at and above `0.01`, wheel rates around ±`0.5`, each early exit, pause with moving wheels, service/refuel/restore, invalid state and independent vehicles. Measure whole-callback cost and allocation behavior before claiming a meaningful gain. This differs from T07, which concerns detached telemetry signal payloads; do not casually replace later signal-state reads with a previous local, since recording-limit/interruption callbacks can run before `tick_advanced` (`session.gd:545-558,1295-1310`).

## U04. Zero-grime overlay rendering is a device-measurement hypothesis

**Path and prior evidence.** Vehicle effects are enabled by default (`ground_scene.gd:110-111,225-238`). `GroundEffects.prepare` binds a transparent grime `material_overlay` to every shell mesh and all four wheels, with dirt/wet uniforms initialized to zero (`app/vehicles/ground/ground_effects.gd:62-101,302-333`). The Nitro manifest lists 66 unique shell nodes (`visual_rig.json:6378`); the `Body` alias (`monster_car_2_model.gd:209-217`) is already in this list and is deduplicated (`ground_effects.gd:80-87,348-350`), giving **70 overlay bindings**, not 70 renderer draws. Bisonte has a smaller shell. The shader declares blended, depth-draw-never rendering; zero dirt and wetness yield zero source alpha, although the fragment source still contains pattern/hash expressions (`ground_grime_overlay.gdshader:1-2,27-50`). Compiler/renderer optimizations and auxiliary pass behavior are unknown. The existing RCV-36 report already records 70 overlays and dirty/clean captures (`docs/research/rc-monster-truck/RCV-36/README.md:3-18`); RCV-39 retains all 70 overlays but shares five materials and avoids unchanged uniform writes (`docs/research/rc-monster-truck/RCV-39/README.md:1-15`). The new question is the cost and equivalence of their **zero-amount** state, not the inventory itself. Existing exact frame/amount guards already avoid unchanged uniform writes (`ground_effects.gd:353-378`).

**Experiment and limits.** On a matched scene, compare current clean overlays against an experimental exact-zero-only detach/reattach, retaining vehicle-owned materials and restoring on the first nonzero dirt *or* wet frame. Preserve the zero-to-positive transition, wet wash/drying, clean, pause, moved ancestors, changed poses, two-vehicle resource isolation, source material ownership and teardown. Detaching changes observable node bindings and diagnostics: `test_rcv_ground_effects.gd:77-83,169-182` and `test_rcv36_effects.gd:64-89,118-150` deliberately assert overlay existence and exact uniforms, so any production change needs an explicit contract decision and revised tests rather than treating the change as invisible. Check all render passes and shadow/temporal outputs, not just alpha/color. Measure native GPU and presented-frame costs, transition-frame hitches and image sequences on real hardware with both cars and effects Off as a control. Do not claim the shader actually executes its source, that the overlays cause 70 draws, or that detaching improves FPS without those results. This is distinct from T05 dry wet-pool slot bookkeeping.
