# OpenRC product and platform moonshot

Research snapshot: 2026-10-28. Baseline supplied for this review: `96287766296b3fef6a37dcd4cc3847d7efbe9f19`; the working tree also has unrelated CI/export, F4U, truck-native, and documentation edits. Read-only review; this file is the only assigned write. This is product strategy, not a physics validation or a user-study result.

## Recommendation

Keep the engineering-first kernel, but make the player-facing promise **a free, open RC flying club that helps pilots learn, set personal goals, and share flights**. The code already has unusual foundations for that promise: an RC-focused fixed-wing route, a responsive multilingual shell, exact identity/version contracts, local statistics, bounded flight records, deterministic resolved scenarios, a data-only Workshop, and an open-source license. Today those foundations mostly serve engineering workflows. The visible loop still ends at “choose an aircraft and fly.”

Over the next 12 months, build three compatible layers on the current app:

1. **A first-flight path and practice cards.** Make first use succeed, then give beginners and experienced pilots clear, repeatable goals. Teach the real transmitter and pilot view through short, optional lessons; follow with scored/reflective cards for orientation, circuits, approaches, wind, and emergency handling only where the scenario and aircraft are qualified. Explain results in terms of the logged flight; don’t award “real-world-ready” badges or quietly change physics.
2. **A club loop built around weekly scenarios and ghost flights.** Let people attempt the same frozen setup, compare their own attempts, and later fly against a recorded ghost or join a casual field session. An asynchronous challenge is the low-risk product experiment. Add live multiplayer only after players demonstrate that co-presence is worth operating and moderating.
3. **A creator commons for data-only flight content.** Let people author and exchange scenario/challenge cards first, then compatible Workshop aircraft variants and fields as their contracts mature. Make source identity and usage rights visible. Start with existing stock models and licensed assets; don’t commission a content library just to fill a catalog.

These are hypotheses about what could earn repeat play, not claims that features caused competitors’ sales or that OpenRC’s flight is physically superior. Official product pages are feature evidence; none of the sources below independently measures handling transfer or product preference.

The useful competitive conclusion is narrow: users can already buy sims that advertise large libraries, guided instruction, challenge modes, customization and multiplayer. OpenRC should answer **what will I do in my first session, and why will I come back next week?** with a credible RC-specific loop. Do not answer with more airframes alone, and do not chase generic game progression (currency, artificial unlock grinds) that conflicts with an open tool and real-hobby identity.

## Multiplayer and UGC: a small architecture, not a kernel rewrite

**Start asynchronously.** First ship a frozen practice challenge and local retry/result. Next export/import a challenge record or replay/ghost that identifies the exact scenario, aircraft and model, field, solver/build, timebase, content version and score contract. Use current read-only CLI/replay/tooling paths; ensure normal flight preferences and physical identities never change on import. A checksum proves bytes, not who made them or whether the flight is honest.

**For casual live rooms, network the activity shell around each local simulation.** Godot already provides high-level multiplayer plus ENet, WebRTC and WebSocket peers; its docs explicitly warn that security, cheating and abuse must be handled. [Godot networking](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/networking/high_level_multiplayer.html). Preserve the existing `Simulation`/`VehicleModel` boundary. Each player’s accepted local `FlightSession` advances its own 240 Hz kernel; transmit compact, rate-limited aircraft pose/identity/room state to other clients and interpolate remote visual proxies. The room/service coordinates membership, selected compatible scenario, ready/start/pause, and rate limits. Do not send remote controls into the physics kernel, load room-supplied paths, or require a new vehicle branch. This casual mode is for seeing/flying with people, not anti-cheat rankings.

**Treat official scores as a separate service.** Lockstep/deterministic cross-machine physics is not demonstrated by the current local replay evidence. An honest ranked result would need a server-side replay/re-simulation contract for a specific executable, solver, aircraft bytes, scenario and input tape; otherwise label results personal/unverified/community. This keeps a compromised client from writing a “world record” and avoids claiming comparable results across incompatible physics builds.

**Package rules:**

- Versioned JSON with exact keys, strict parser, explicit schema/capability allowlists, digest and bounded bytes/depth/nodes. Reference known installed `aircraft_id`/`field_id` values and resolved data; no arbitrary executable resources or auto-install paths.
- Freeze versioned scenario and score definitions; explicit incompatible-version and missing-content responses. Keep a local preview/validator and optional import before publishing.
- Store creator, attribution, source URL, content license, app version, physical/content identity and change history as explicit metadata. Hashes and signatures are not a substitute for a lawful license or provenance. Don’t repackage downloaded models, photos, music or simulator files without redistribution permission. The project already has a source-delivery/license index; use it before commissioning or bundling anything.
- Begin with reviewed first-party packs in a static catalog. Later allow community submissions with report/takedown/moderation, contact/author attribution, terms, and compatibility states. A store alone does not provide useful moderation or guarantee rights.
- Keep offline play functional. Do not require telemetry or account registration to fly. Multiplayer needs user-visible privacy controls, mute/block/report, safe display names, disconnect recovery and data-retention policy before a public launch.

## 12-month sequence, player outcomes, and acceptance gates

**First practical slice (weeks 1–6): prove a better first session.** Reuse the current Practice screen. Test first-launch/control setup with novice pilots, then add three short, optional cards around a fixed-wing baseline selected from current human-flight evidence: learn orientation/controls, fly a simple pattern, inspect a result/retry. If no aircraft meets a transfer-claim gate, label these as software practice only. Use current aircraft/field/conditions; no new aircraft or scenery required. Add only enough card metadata to express objective, explanation, compatible model/scenario and result. Keep one “free fly” path visible. Fix the worst launch stall before adding audiovisual production. Do not call it a certification course.

**Months 2–4: repeatable challenges.** Add 6–10 scored scenario cards as resolved-data envelopes, use current statistics and recordings for personal bests and “try again,” and manually publish a weekly first-party challenge on the existing website/release channel. Each card must state the supported build/model, scoring rule, weather and objective. Pilot-review any language that implies real aircraft readiness. A user can finish a meaningful flight in under 10 minutes without reading a developer guide.

**Months 5–8: share/replay and creators.** Add share/import/preview for compatible cards and replay/ghost only when exact replay evidence exists for that build/model. Start with first-party and opt-in pilot content, then accept reviewed data-only user packs. Collect consented playtest observations, not hidden flight telemetry. Add a static catalog only after update/cache/withdrawal and license/provenance flows are defined.

**Months 9–12: social rooms as a gated experiment.** If playtests show demand, build a small 2–8 pilot room for casual circuit/airshow sessions and spectators. Use session-adapter/network-presentation boundaries; preserve local physics and offline mode. Do not add ranked matchmaking until server verification and operating cost are accepted. If a no-cost shared service is required, keep the room optional and prefer existing platform services only after a distribution decision.

Initial acceptance hypotheses (revise after a baseline with 8–12 novices and experienced RC pilots; these numbers are product targets, not current measurements):

- At 1920×1080, at least 8/10 first-time desktop pilots can identify/connect the controller, reach a controllable first flight and understand how to restart/end without facilitator intervention; median first airborne flight within 10 minutes.
- At least 8/10 can complete one first-session practice card within three attempts and describe one actionable improvement from the result. Experienced pilots can find free flight and personal-best challenges without navigating a lesson.
- Core flows are keyboard- and controller-completable, retain visible focus, work at enlarged text, provide non-color-only status, and receive a screen-reader inspection before public “accessible” claims. Add non-English/native-speaker review for any shipped curriculum.
- Every challenge result identifies its exact inputs/content and complete run status. Exact replay must pass on the declared supported configuration; a changed solver, aircraft, scenario, engine or incomplete log becomes “incompatible/unverified,” never silently accepted. Challenge metrics compare behavior; they do not establish physical realism.
- At the named minimum/reference desktops, record native 1080p frame-time distribution, simulation tick pacing, loading latency and resource use for baseline flight, cards, and later rooms. Maintain the project’s primary 1080p priority; 720p-only captures cannot pass acceptance.
- For a live room, test packet loss/latency and disconnect/rejoin, malformed RPCs, host migration or explicit no-host-migration behavior, privacy/reporting, and an independently specified participant ceiling. Do not infer the supported player count from AI companion counts or a happy-path LAN demo.
- Independently pilot-review takeoff, pattern, approach, stall/wind and any emergency task on the specific aircraft before presenting its score as RC-relevant. Preserve the project’s flight-log method and mark missing physical evidence.

## Risks and caveats

- **Engineering can still outpace the player loop.** More aircraft, more weather models and a more elaborate Workshop do not automatically improve first-flight success or repeat play. Measure player outcomes before committing a large roadmap.
- **Progression can imply qualification.** Avoid “ready to solo,” skill grades or transfer claims without appropriate independent evidence. Report the exact model/configuration, task and proof class.
- **UGC turns into a support and rights burden.** Compatibility matrices, stale packages, asset redistribution, moderation, spam and unsafe content are product work. A content author’s license grant must be explicit; platform upload terms are not proof that third-party material is theirs.
- **Networking adds security and operations.** Godot explicitly calls out cheat/exploit risk. A public room list, accounts, voice/text chat, anti-cheat, servers, privacy and incident response are not “just add ENet.” Start with read-only shared flight records, then an opt-in casual room.
- **Replays are not automatically comparable.** The recorder is precise but bounded and source-bound. Cross-platform/cross-build replay equivalence needs its own evidence; a replay or a passing score is not physical validation.
- **Performance and accessibility are conversion features.** Current 1080p-native loading stalls and open screen-reader acceptance should be addressed with actual desktop and assistive-technology trials before a public curriculum/community push.
