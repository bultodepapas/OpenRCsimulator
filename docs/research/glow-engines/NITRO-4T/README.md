## Identity and baseline configuration

The baseline is O.S. FS-64V, product 3AY00, single-cylinder naturally aspirated overhead-valve four-stroke glow engine, 10.46 cm³, 25.0 mm bore × 21.3 mm stroke, type 61T carburetor, Type F plug and stock F-4051 silencer. O.S. publishes **1.14 PS / 1.12 hp at 11,000 RPM**, practical engine speed **2,400–11,000 RPM**, bare engine **442 g**, silencer **44 g**, sport/stunt propellers **12×6–8 or 13×6–7**, and scale propeller **14×6** ([O.S. product specification](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/english/line_up/engine/air/single/catalog/3AY00.html)). Use 1.14 PS × 735.49875 = **838.468575 W** for the manufacturer's rounded metric rating, giving **0.7279 N·m** at exactly 11,000 RPM. The additional 1.12 hp is rounded; do not treat rounded PS and hp as separate test results.

The manufacturer engine-plus-silencer mass is **486 g**. The documented O.S. 2022 flight-review configuration also used the FS-64V and NITRO-X Standard 15-18; it reports easy starting, usable low/high response and half-throttle cruise qualitatively, without RPM, torque, fuel-flow or flight telemetry. It flew a Spacewalker II and the pilot described a 14×6 propeller as turning through the air well. This is useful configuration/pilot-source context, not calibration ([O.S. flight review](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/web_content/202210/index_1.html)).

## Ten-area calibration record

### 1. Torque and power

The current profile's full-admission WOT knots, inferred torque/power, rating identity and local sensitivities appear in `calibration_report.json`. The only FS-64V engine power point used is the official **1.14 PS @ 11,000 RPM**. The rated-point reconstruction residual confirms only that the candidate's generated efficiencies invert to this rating; it is not an empirical fit residual. No measured FS-64V full curve or torque-peak location is available. The candidate has a distinct **manufacturer-recommended maximum of 11,000 RPM** and estimated numerical support to **15,000 RPM** to carry an unloading propeller through flight-model scenarios. The 13,000/15,000 knots are an explicit high-speed inference outside the published practical range, not an O.S.-approved operating envelope; the generated report flags them and searches for accepted-profile peaks only inside 2,400–11,000 RPM.

AIAA 2016-5011 measured a different O.S. four-stroke, **FS-a56, 9.32 cm³**, not the 10.46 cm³ FS-64V. In its hybrid test, the FS-a56 measured about **0.17 N·m at 80 W** and **0.32 N·m at 415 W**; the paper says both tested engines rose toward 0.35 N·m by about 150 W and then remained near that level as RPM increased. The FS-a56's lowest/highest reported volumetric efficiencies were **33% / 44%**, with leanest achieved mixture changing from φ=2.4 at 80 W to φ=2.24 at 415 W. Use these only as adjacent-family plausibility checks: do not scale them into a 64V curve or reduce its speed ceiling. The test was around **78.2 kPa / 291.3 K** (about 10,930 ft density altitude) with alternator-cradle load. No FS-64V residual is computed ([AIAA 2016-5011](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2016-5011)).

### 2. Throttle response

No numeric throttle-command/barrel/RPM transient or servo-separated model was found for the FS-64V. O.S.'s 2022 flight review describes normal start, low/high throttle response and sufficient half-throttle cruise, but gives no synchronized trace. The MAX-61FX first-order 0.25-s estimate is a different 2T engine and is not used as a 4T parameter. Use **0.10–1.00 s** only as a broad exploratory range for charge/mixture response sensitivity; it is an engineering sweep bound, not an FS-64V response measurement or acceptance band. The installed `GlowLifecycle` adds servo, air-admission and fuel-transport lag states. Its constants are inferred settings, not a fitted FS-64V transient model. Actual shaft rise/fall also depends on prop/crank/cam inertia and load balance. The standalone profile remains a steady mean-cycle source model.

### 3. Combustion dynamics

This single-cylinder engine has one nominal opportunity per 720° cycle: `f = RPM/120`, or **20–91.7 Hz** over the O.S. practical 2,400–11,000 RPM band; at rated speed it is **91.7 Hz**. Its inter-opportunity interval falls from **50.0 ms** at 2,400 RPM to **10.9 ms** at 11,000 RPM. The 4T kernel retains a separate cycle angle, volumetric filling, intake depression, pumping and valve-train loss. The mean-cycle source accepts an externally given firing fraction; integrated lifecycle tracks phase/opportunities and applies an assumed smooth firing fraction versus mixture and temperature. No FS-64V misfire curve, cam-phase pressure waveform, cycle-variation or plug-temperature law is available to calibrate those assumptions. A missed fire does not change the engine to a 2T. The O.S. FS64 manual describes mixture-sensitive pickup/idle and over-lean overheating qualitatively, without a numeric failure boundary ([O.S. manual](https://www.os-engines.co.jp/english/line_up/engine/air/single/img/manual/3AY00.pdf)).

### 4. Propeller compatibility

O.S.'s 13×6–7 sport/stunt and 14×6 scale recommendations support the two identified builds above. The APC 13×6 has a direct 1/4-in hub fit; the Master Airscrew K 14×6 is explicitly listed for larger glow engines and has a 1/4-in shaft. APC's 13-in speed limit does not constrain the 11,000-RPM engine band. Applying Master Airscrew's generic 14-in plastic-prop safety formula provisionally yields 11,786 RPM; the engine recommendation remains the lower limit at 11,000 RPM. Confirm that the generic limit covers K Series composite before a physical run. No engine-specific `Ct/Cp`, static thrust/torque, advance-ratio/Reynolds map, or measured rotating inertia has been retrieved for either. O.S.'s flight story is one qualitative 14×6 configuration, not a load data series. Geometric still-air pitch speed at the official rated speed is **62.5 mph** for 13×6 and **62.5 mph** for 14×6 because both have 6-in pitch; those numbers are not aircraft speed or thrust. Use measured prop curves or explicit, bounded surrogates.

The generated model section uses **K=0.075** in its induced-drag term for both 4T installations. This inferred correction was selected so the positive-thrust surrogate satisfies an independent ideal momentum-theory shaft-power lower bound across the audit grid; the sampled minima occur at static conditions: **1.0831×** ideal for K 14×6 and **1.1101×** for APC 13×6. The audit uses $A=\pi D^2/4$, $V_d=(V+\sqrt{V^2+2T/(\rho A)})/2$, and $P_\text{ideal}=T V_d$, following [NASA's propeller-thrust explanation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/propeller-thrust/). It sampled 3,000 RPM/forward-speed combinations per configuration over the full 0–15,000 RPM profile support and 0–30 m/s. This check applies the plan’s 5% engineering margin above the ideal lower bound; points below the ideal are physically impossible, while points between 1.0× and 1.05× are flagged as insufficient margin. It is not measured induced flow, a propeller efficiency/map fit, or a calibration target. All positive-thrust roots below 1.05× are refused and excluded from nominal predictions and sensitivity extrema. This correction and its exact generated inputs are recorded in [`calibration_report.json`](calibration_report.json).

### 5. Fuel consumption

O.S. states the FS-64V manual fuel should be methanol based, with at least **18% lubricant** and **5–20% nitromethane by volume**; its factory adjustment assumes **18% oil / 15% nitro**. By contrast, the candidate uses the AIAA research blend **72/10/18 vol% methanol/nitromethane/castor oil** and an inert-oil lower-energy bound. That candidate is inside the manual's broad nitro/oil constraints but does not match the factory's 15% nitro baseline; physical calibration must freeze one exact fuel. The report converts the configured blend from volume to component mass and lists candidate delivered/burned/unburned flow at every RPM knot.

The AIAA FS-a56 hybrid run weighed fuel during one-minute steady periods and reports a linear fit **4T flow = 0.34x + 27.11 mg/s**, `R²=0.97`, where `x` is ESC electrical power in watts. This fit is based on the sibling FS-a56 and the hybrid electrical-demand axis, not FS-64V shaft output. It is an external scale/plausibility check only. O.S. says rich idle can be smoky/sluggish and lean settings can cut out or overheat; neither source quantifies FS-64V fuel flow through throttle, mixture and duty cycle. There is no FS-64V weighed consumption or endurance result here. Do not apply a 2T half-flow rule.

### 6. Idle and stalling

O.S.'s practical speed floor is **2,400 RPM**, which is not a declared minimum sustainable idle. As an exploratory warm-idle search only, use **2,200–3,200 RPM**, bracketing the O.S. floor and nearby four-stroke analogues. Saito FA-82B's manual describes a 2,000–2,500-RPM idle on a different, 13.47 cm³ engine; it is a comparator only and cannot establish FS64 stability. The O.S. FS-64V flight report records successful starting and responsive throttle, but no idle RPM, duration or restart. The installed lifecycle implements start/stop requests, minimum-start speed, idle admission and mixture/temperature-dependent firing and stall transitions. The candidate WOT curve uses **0.35 N·m at 2,400 RPM and 0.50 N·m at 4,000 RPM** to leave plausible low-speed torque margin for a reduced-admission warm-idle state; this is a synthetic engineering inference, not empirical calibration or an idle measurement. All transition settings remain provisional. There is no warmed-up FS64 idle hold, rich/lean repeat set, stop boundary or stall probability measurement.

### 7. Temperature and cooling

No quantitative FS-64V head/case temperature, airflow, warm-up/cool-down or overheat boundary was retrieved. A separate **0.99 cm³ two-stroke** glow research engine measured about **160–200 °C** cylinder-head temperature at full load; its cycle, scale and measurement setup make it unsuitable as a 4T value. For instrument planning only, a **100–250 °C head-surface search span** is a broad engineering logger/model bracket, not a predicted FS64 temperature range, operating target or failure threshold. Keep sensor location and cooling airflow fixed and report raw measurements before fitting heat capacity/conductance. The installed lifecycle now calls a scalar thermal energy model with combustion heat, mechanical loss, premix evaporation and airspeed-dependent cooling, and uses temperature in its inferred firing law. This family-neutral reduced-order component is integrated in software, but it is not a calibrated FS-64V head/case or installation model.

### 8. Sound and vibration

Nominal single-cylinder 4T exhaust cadence is 0.5/rev: **20–91.7 Hz** over the practical band, or 91.7 Hz at rated speed. A two-blade prop has blade-pass frequency `2×RPM/60`, **80–366.7 Hz** over the same range. These are kinematic event/order values, not measured spectral amplitudes. O.S. describes a deeper/more realistic four-stroke sound and the F-4051 silencer as designed for its characteristic 4T sound, but publishes no FS64 dBA trace or tach-synchronized spectrum. The runtime voice synthesizes separate firing, valve and propeller orders with authored timbre; these are software presentation cues, not recording-derived levels or acoustic validation. NIST IR 75-693 supplies a model-airplane recording method with multiple 1-m positions and a microphone directly below the propeller; it provides measurement protocol, not a numeric sound prior for this engine ([NIST report](https://doi.org/10.6028/NBS.IR.75-693)). The 14×6 flight story is pilot description, not acoustic validation. No physical FS-64V sound/vibration recording exists in this dossier.

### 9. Aircraft dynamics

The only relevant aircraft-level report found is O.S.'s Spacewalker II flight review using FS-64V, NITRO-X 15-18 and a 14×6: it says takeoff was quick, climb was strong in that setup and half throttle maintained level flight. It provides no airframe mass/CG, weather, tachometer, airspeed, takeoff distance, climb rate, fuel trace or matched comparison. It therefore provides a named configuration and qualitative pilot evidence, not a force/performance fit or pilot acceptance. Simulated takeoff, climb, cruise, acceleration, torque reaction, gyroscopic response, P-factor, windmilling and engine-out glide need matched telemetry and propeller data. Do not encode a generic 4T advantage.
