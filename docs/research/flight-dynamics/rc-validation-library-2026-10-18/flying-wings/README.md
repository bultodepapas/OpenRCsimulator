# Flying-wing and tailless-aircraft evidence

Research snapshot: **2026-10-18** (library date); downloads and checks were made on 2026-10-19 UTC. This is a source collection for RC-aircraft physics comparison, not an adopted aerodynamic model or a claim of simulator validation. The [source manifest](sources.json) records 24 primary studies or datasets, including 19 downloaded originals and five useful link-only records.

Original papers and datasets are local-only under `references/rc-validation-library-2026-10-18/flying-wings/`; the byte-identical Ananda 2015 wing paper is reused from the sibling low-Reynolds collection. The manifest records repository-relative paths, direct download URLs, sizes, SHA-256 values, access outcomes, license statements and limitations. Public availability does not imply permission to redistribute a paper; the local files are ignored by Git.

## Strongest evidence for model comparison

| Evidence | What it can support | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Skywalker X8 system-identification flight set](https://doi.org/10.18710/U4TLYV) and the [2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICUAS54217.2022.9836114), [2024](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2024-4653), and [2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13272-025-00816-3) modeling studies | Flight-based coefficients, control response, damping identification, model residuals and separate maneuver validation for the 2.1 m X8 flying wing | The 2024 log set has 17 processed maneuvers and reports strong, vertically varying wind. Aerodynamic angles are estimated, and the 2025 study uses an EKF-based wind/airdata estimate. The 2022 study adds flight-identified damping to reused wind-tunnel statics. |
| [Skywalker X8 icing flight study](https://doi.org/10.1017/aer.2026.10234) and [its flight-data repository](https://doi.org/10.18710/NNHVBP) | Clean-versus-iced changes in measured flight response and fitted coefficients; the repository contains 106 maneuvers and six artificial ice-shape meshes | These are processed, closed-loop flight maneuvers using printed icing shapes. The dataset's precomputed aerodynamic coefficients use the authors' propulsion model; use its model-agnostic channels when testing a different propulsion model. The files warn that sample intervals vary around 20 Hz, so resample absolute timestamps for frequency-domain work. |
| [Zagi 400X wind-tunnel report](https://archive.aoe.vt.edu/mason/Mason_f/FinalMorphRpt02.pdf) | Measured lift, drag and moment trends, full-span elevon effects, winglet/sideslip tests and stall behavior for a 48-inch RC flying wing | A 2002 senior-design report rather than a peer-reviewed paper. Tests were at a stated Re of about 379,000; the report notes VLM elevon predictions disagreed with tunnel results. Use its reported stall and coefficient plots as geometry- and facility-specific evidence. |
| [36-inch Zagi MAV report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20050185598) | Model geometry and a half-scale tunnel comparison for a foam Zagi with MH45 section and elevons | Only longitudinal tunnel loads were measured because the transverse strain gauges did not work. The setup had flexible control surfaces and substantial errors in several predicted coefficients; it does not include an open raw flight log. |
| [X8 wind-tunnel/model study](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICUAS.2018.8453370) and [lateral flight-identification paper](https://doi.org/10.14311/AP.2018.58.0077) | Static whole-airframe coefficient trends and X8 lateral control/stability derivatives | In the 2018 model paper, static tunnel loads were measured, but the stability derivatives were supplied by XFLR5 rather than measured in the tunnel. The lateral-flight paper uses closed-loop PID data and discusses parameter collinearity and prior information. |

For flow-model sensitivity rather than an exact airframe match, [Ananda, Sukumar and Selig (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ast.2014.11.016) measured ten finite wings (AR 2–5, Re 50,000–150,000). The [2017 UiTM elevon study](https://ir.uitm.edu.my/id/eprint/39162/) measured all six force/moment coefficients across 31 deflection arrangements, but used a thin flat-plate blended-wing-body model at fixed incidence. [Shams et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.3390/mi11060553) combine full-scale tunnel measurements and qualitative flight testing on a 200 g Eppler-387 flying-wing MAV; its rate and elevon-control derivatives are calculated rather than directly measured. The [2024 Opterra study](https://doi.org/10.1017/aer.2024.56) is useful for pressure sensing and motion in a dynamic wind tunnel, but reports no outdoor flight-control experiment. These analogues can inform trends and modeling questions; their coefficients should not be transferred directly to an X8 or Zagi.

## Keep measured and modeled quantities separate

- The X8's 2018 wind-tunnel paper reports measured static forces and moments, while some final stability derivatives come from XFLR5. Its 2022 follow-up points out that a very good fit to tunnel data did not guarantee good flight acceleration predictions, then identifies a damping model from flight data.
- Winter's 2019 thesis and the corrected 2019 icing paper use stationary and unsteady CFD-ALE plus software flight simulation. They do not provide measured X8 icing-flight coefficients. The retained conference-paper PDF is explicitly author-corrected: it says the published paper had numerical errors in Figure 5 and Table 8.
- The 2024 and 2025 X8 system-identification papers are the most direct clean-flight modeling sources here. Their states and aerodynamic angles include estimates, and wind, actuation and propulsion-model errors remain part of the residuals.
- The 2026 icing article is measured flight evidence, but its excitation is closed-loop and the icing was made in an icing tunnel, digitized, and reproduced as printed shapes. In its dataset README, use raw/model-agnostic state and motor-speed channels to evaluate alternative propulsion models rather than the supplied propulsion-dependent coefficient fields.
- The 2020 FWMAV study's static coefficients come from tunnel tests, while its rate and elevon derivatives come from computation. Its flight results are qualitative mission/handling observations, not coefficient-identification logs.
- The 2019 X8 icing thesis explicitly says its proposed icing-management results still require flight-test validation. The 2019 corrected CFD study also lists frequency and oscillation-amplitude limits for its reduced-frequency method.

## Additional sources and their role

The [Tailless Mini-UAV wind-tunnel study (2018)](https://ph02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/nkrafa-sct/article/view/159333) compares measured lift/drag with CFD at Re 300,000–400,000. Its companion [airfoil/planform study (2017)](https://ph02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/nkrafa-sct/article/view/142027) is CFD and XFLR5 only. [Hamada et al. (2018)](https://www.athensjournals.gr/technology/2018-5-3-2-Hamada.pdf) describes one design/build/wind-tunnel/flight project, but does not supply a reusable raw flight series. The 2013 [Shields and Mohseni damping study](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2013-3192) is a forced-oscillation experiment on an AR-1 flat plate at Re 75,000: useful for dynamic-derivative mechanisms, not a complete RC aircraft. [Gatto, Bourdin and Friswell (2010)](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.C000251) measured surface pressures around articulated winglets on a swept wing using a Zagi 12 section, not aircraft-level forces or free-flight response. [Li et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1756829316660320) instrumented and recorded a spin of a slender-body delta-wing UAV; it is adjacent evidence for angle-of-attack/sideslip measurement, not a flying-wing aerodynamic model. The 2018 [X-8 distributed flush-airdata flight study](https://doi.org/10.1175/JTECH-D-17-0192.1) is likewise useful for airdata estimation against a multihole-probe reference, not for aerodynamic coefficients.

## Searches, access limits and gaps

Representative queries included `"Skywalker X8" aerodynamics wind tunnel PDF flying wing`, `"Skywalker X-8" aerodynamic model flight test identification`, `RC flying wing Zagi wind tunnel flight test aerodynamic coefficients thesis`, `Skywalker X8 flight test dataset aerodynamic identification raw flight data repository`, `tailless mini UAV wind tunnel elevon coefficients`, `low aspect ratio wing aerodynamic damping derivatives Re 75000 forced oscillation`, and `flying wing Zagi winglet pressure experiment`. Searches prioritized publisher pages, NASA NTRS, NTNU and university repositories, DataverseNO, UIUC, and conference proceedings; search snippets were checked against the paper title and aircraft description.

The IEEE Access PDF request for the propeller-induced-flow paper returned challenge HTML, which was discarded. SAGE's PDF endpoint for the seven-hole-probe spin study returned HTTP 403; it remains link-only. No free institutional full text was found for the AIAA articulated-winglet paper or the AIAA forced-oscillation damping paper, so neither was saved locally. The AMS flush-airdata article's full-text page was readable, but a stable direct PDF was not verified in this pass. None of these failures was bypassed.

Important gaps remain: no openly downloadable raw Zagi flight log, no X8 flight series deliberately covering stall/spin, and no exact Zagi/X8 high-angle-of-attack dynamic-derivative campaign. The 2024 clean X8 archive also has a DOI typo in its embedded README (`10.18710/U4TL`); the canonical DataverseNO page identifies `10.18710/U4TLYV`. For the 2026 icing dataset, DataverseNO metadata states CC0 1.0 while its embedded `0_ReadMe.txt` states CC-BY 4.0; that conflict is preserved in the manifest and should be clarified before relying on reuse rights.

Every retained PDF passed a PDF-signature check and first-page `pdftotext` title/author check; abstracts and selected methods/results sections were read for the comparison above. The two retained ZIPs passed `unzip -tq`; their readme files and file lists were inspected, but time-series values were not reprocessed. Link-only sources received title/abstract/source-page checks, not full-text audits. No coefficients were transcribed into simulator data, and this search does not establish physical validity.
