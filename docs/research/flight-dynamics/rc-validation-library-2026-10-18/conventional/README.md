# Conventional RC-aircraft flight-dynamics evidence

Research snapshot: 2026-10-18 (Bogotá), retrieved 2026-10-19 UTC. This source collection is part of the [RC validation library](../README.md). Its machine-readable records, hashes, byte counts and access outcomes are in [sources.json](sources.json). Third-party originals remain local-only in the ignored references directory; the documentation manifest is the reviewable record.

This pass contains 22 records: 19 with verified local originals and three access-only leads. Originals include reused [Ultra Stick/FASER references](../../rc-aero-data-sources.json). Four X8 PDFs reuse byte-identical flying-wings copies, and Jayanti reuses the existing registry's identical original. The 2025 X8 paper PDF and Dataverse ZIP have different hashes/bytes from sibling versions and are preserved as separate file versions. The X8 evidence is included only as an adjacent fixed-wing comparison, not as conventional-tail or Stik data.

## Closest Stik evidence

The [Urbano Molano paper](https://jci.uniautonoma.edu.co/2011/2011-5.pdf) is the clearest primary publication located that names an UglyStik. It reports a rebuilt example with 1.56 m span, 0.59 m² wing area and 1.38 m length, a NACA 632-015 section as printed, and control throws. Winfoil and AAA are used for geometry and cruise-design estimates. The paper explicitly says it did not create a mathematical flight model, so it is useful for historical geometry and terminology, not flight-dynamics coefficients.

The closest measured-model evidence is the [Giant Big Stik thesis](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/items/f97eed21-9c23-4839-ad10-59634eb03bac) and its related [2019 peer-reviewed article](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/3/2/48). Chapter 5 gives a JSBSim six-degree-of-freedom model, aircraft parameters and a limited comparison with recorded flight. The thesis reports an empty weight of 13 lb, 10.538 ft² wing area and 6.709 ft span. In two selected validation sections, reported Pearson correlations are 0.9304 for roll and 0.60664/0.74044 for pitch. The evidence is fit for a narrow sense-and-avoid use case: takeoff/landing are omitted, rudder is omitted from the maneuver comparison, throttle is treated as constant near 50%, and gain/offset/delay were manually adjusted. Wind and nonmatching initial states complicate replay. This is a Giant Big Stik with a distinct configuration, not the Jensen Ugly Stik 60. The article HTML is public, but the official PDF endpoint returned HTTP 403 during this collection; the thesis PDF is retained locally instead.

No public, verified raw flight log was found for either Stik source. The exact Jensen 60 still lacks a freely accessible complete-airframe wind-tunnel campaign or independent flight-identified six-degree-of-freedom model in this collection.

The later [focused Ugly Stik collection](../ugly-stik/README.md) extends this search with further family-specific research, original handling reports and related-airframe flight data. It keeps the Jensen, Big Stik and Ultra Stick evidence separate.

The [Ultra Stick 25e/120 studies](../../rc-aero-data-sources.json) are already local under references/rc-aero-data and are not copied here. Dorobantu’s 25e identification and dissertation provide flight-derived lateral matrices, modes and measured inertias. Lie provides an Ultra Stick 120 model; the FASER work adds NASA instrumentation and forced-oscillation evidence on a research-modified Ultra Stick 120. These remain the strongest tailed model-aircraft comparison sources in the repository, but geometry, electric propulsion and test configuration differ from the Jensen Stik.

## Adjacent methods and released logs

The Skywalker X8 papers and its Dataverse collection provide a useful, unusually complete example of combining static tunnel results, flight identification and reusable maneuver data. The retained dataset ZIP passed archive CRC checks and contains training/validation maneuver files. The X8 is a flying wing; it cannot validate tail effectiveness, conventional-aircraft yaw coupling or Jensen Stik derivatives. The manifest points to one retained original for each byte-identical PDF. The two 2025 PDFs share an article DOI but differ in pagination/publication lines. The two clean-data ZIP containers differ in hash but contain 36 byte-identical non-directory members, verified by member SHA-256. Both publication/container versions are retained, and the bibliography groups each pair under its shared DOI; neither pair is independent evidence.

[Jayanti et al. (2026)](https://pp.bme.hu/tr/article/view/41190) compare a CFD-based six-degree-of-freedom model with three flight-test sets and explicitly report pitch/airspeed residuals. The paper does not identify the subject as a hobby RC airframe, so it is a model-discrepancy example rather than direct aircraft evidence. A Tiger-Trainer article and a scaled Cessna 182 identification paper are indexed as access-only leads; this pass did not obtain their full texts from a free primary repository. An Aerosonde-related source located in search was a simulation benchmark, not an independently verified real-flight validation source, so its results are not counted as flight evidence here.

## Search, inspection and access record

Representative searches included: “Ugly Stik” “flight dynamics” simulation aircraft; “Ugly Stick” system identification RC aircraft flight test thesis; “Giant Big Stik R/C UAV computer model development”; “Bixler” system identification UAV flight test paper; “small UAV flight dynamics system identification Skywalker Bixler Cub Telemaster”; and “Aerosonde UAV flight dynamics model system identification flight test data”. Discovery focused on publisher pages, university repositories, author laboratory pages and DOI records. Search-result dates were not treated as publication dates. The Bixler and Big Stik sources include Indonesian-language work; English-language summaries here are limited to the claims checked in the original PDFs.

All retained PDFs passed PDF-signature checks, pdfinfo and pdftotext first-page parsing. The X8 ZIP passed unzip integrity testing; its member inventory was inspected without reinterpreting the measurements. The 2019 Big Stik publisher PDF endpoint returned HTTP 403, and the official 2016 Tiger-Trainer publisher page exposed metadata/abstract rather than the full article. No inaccessible or third-party repost was kept in place of an original. The Cessna 182 entry is bibliographic only; no full-text claims are used. Read depth is given per record in the manifest; selected-section checks do not imply a complete reproduction, coefficient audit or physical validation of this simulator.
