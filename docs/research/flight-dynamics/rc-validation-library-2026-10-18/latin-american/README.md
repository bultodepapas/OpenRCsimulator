# Latin American RC and small-aircraft flight-dynamics evidence

Research snapshot: 2026-10-18 (Bogotá), retrieved 2026-10-19 UTC. This set contains nine primary Portuguese- or Spanish-language studies from Brazilian and Colombian institutions. It keeps measured-flight, wind-tunnel, instrumentation-planning and simulation-only evidence distinct. The full metadata and file checksums are in [sources.json](sources.json). Four verified originals are saved in the ignored local `references/rc-validation-library-2026-10-18/latin-american/` directory; the other five entries are metadata-only because their official full-text endpoints could not be retrieved locally.

## Source-specific findings

- **VR-01 RC airplane, measured-flight identification attempt.** Beghelli's UnB thesis describes a 2.4 kg, roughly 1.98 m-span model, planned elevator pulses/doublets, aileron bank-to-bank inputs and rudder doublets, with angular rates, attitude, angle-of-attack, sideslip and airspeed recorded. The abstract reports that N4SID/GREYEST did not produce adequate models for the real airplane, citing gusts, difficulty holding a trim condition, pilot effects and sensor timing/rate issues. This is a valuable negative result, but only the official record and indexed abstract were inspected; the [UnB record and PDF](https://bdm.unb.br/handle/10483/24053) could not be downloaded locally because of its TLS certificate failure.
- **Alpha60 RC trainer, tunnel data used for landing-model development.** Bücker's ITA thesis says the Hangar-9 Alpha60's principal characteristics came from wind-tunnel tests plus CAD/CFD, then evaluates a short-field automatic-landing controller in simulation under wind. The abstract reports simulated landing distances from 235 to 329 m. This is a direct RC-trainer geometry lead, not flight validation; the [ITA thesis record](https://bdtd.ibict.br/vufind/Record/ITA_5682512c19420e1397ad770473210632/Details) was available, while its linked old archive host timed out.
- **Vector-P measured-flight identification and instrumentation.** The BDTD abstract for Santos's 2013 ITA thesis confirms that the Vector-P model was identified from flight-test data and used for autopilot design; the legacy ITA download host timed out, so no coefficient-level claims are made. Fischer et al.'s 2016 CONEM paper is locally saved and fully inspected: it documents cRIO, inertial and air-data sensors, GPS and servo-position logging, then specifies bank-to-bank and rudder doublet maneuvers. Its future-tense text describes a planned campaign, not completed new identification results. The [2013 thesis record](https://bdtd.ibict.br/vufind/Record/ITA_46a92b3b81571077a89f3772c619a21d) and [2016 paper and PDF](https://proceedings.conferentia.org/paper/CON-2016-1265) are separate sources. The 2018 Vector-P lateral-identification paper is already listed in the [older source inventory](../../rc-aero-data-sources.json), so it is not duplicated here.
- **EOLO fixed-wing UAV, simulation-only parameter identification.** Alves describes an 8.87 kg, 4 m-span high-aspect-ratio aircraft and demonstrates maneuver selection, output-error fitting and proof-of-match. The thesis explicitly says its inputs are generated by a simulator and that only measured flight tests can identify EOLO's real aerodynamics. The [UNESP thesis record](https://repositorio.unesp.br/entities/publication/c4ae4b25-8925-48a7-94ef-5f4378926095/full) links to the saved original.
- **Flying wing, CFD checked against prior tunnel measurements.** Marques compares OpenFOAM results for a blended-wing-body configuration against wind-tunnel data from an earlier study. The 0–17° angle-of-attack sweep shows steady-solver oscillation and divergence from experiment above roughly 10°, where the flow separates. It is neither a new tunnel campaign nor a flight test, and the geometry is a research BWB rather than a hobby flying wing. The [USP thesis record](https://teses.usp.br/teses/disponiveis/55/55134/tde-27082019-171649/pt-br.html) carries a private research/teaching and citation notice; the original is saved locally under that restriction.
- **Flying-wing parameter-estimation methods, simulation-only.** Benjumea Giraldo and Vélez Sánchez present genetic-algorithm and particle-swarm estimators for a gray-box flying-wing model. Their longitudinal trials compare estimates of 11 parameters against simulator/theoretical values; the authors state that applying the tool to real flight data is future work. The official [EAFIT bitstream](https://repository.eafit.edu.co/server/api/core/bitstreams/426151df-af02-4fe5-a82a-d8933b4a7e78/content) was indexed, but the local downloader received an anti-bot challenge.
- **AURA fixed-wing UAV, simulation-only Kalman identification.** Ortiz Valencia and Gutiérrez Zea formulate a six-degree-of-freedom model and use an extended Kalman filter with simulated flight tests. It is an algorithm example, not measured-flight coefficient validation. The [ITM journal record and PDF](https://revistas.itm.edu.co/index.php/tecnologicas/article/view/229) were readable through the publisher's indexed text, while direct local transfer timed out.
- **Ryan Navion full-scale flight-data reanalysis.** Vasconcelos estimates 22 of 35 stability/control derivatives by least squares from historical Suit (1972) flight-test data and checks a six-degree-of-freedom reconstruction. This is a substantial measured-data workflow example, but it is not a newly flown RC aircraft; the raw historical log is not bundled with the thesis. The [UFSC thesis record](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/handle/123456789/255906) links to the saved original, retrieved from the repository's direct bitstream URL.

The adjacent [conventional-aircraft collection](../conventional/README.md) already contains the new Ugly Stik and Giant Big Stik sources; this search did not yield another primary study on those exact sport models. This set also did not surface a new comparable RC-airplane stall/spin flight dataset. Simulation-only papers remain included because they document methods and, in the EOLO and EAFIT cases, explicitly distinguish simulated self-consistency from real-aircraft validation.
