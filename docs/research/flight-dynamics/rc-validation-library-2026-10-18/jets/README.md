# Turbine jets and subscale remotely piloted jets

Research snapshot: **2026-10-19 UTC**. The [source manifest](sources.json) contains 20 primary-source records: 16 new local originals, two reused originals already held in `references/rc-aero-data/`, and two metadata-only records. Local copies are ignored by Git under `references/rc-validation-library-2026-10-18/jets/`; the manifest preserves source URLs, exact hashes, byte counts, access status, evidence type and limits. This is source research, not an adopted jet model or a claim of physical validation.

## Strongest comparison evidence

**NASA AirSTAR distinguishes a measured subscale test platform from a hobby sport jet.** Jordan et al. describe a 5.5% dynamically scaled, twin-turbine generic transport with roughly a 7 ft span and high-rate instrumentation ([NASA platform paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060022136)). The separate S-2 article describes a COTS subscale transport surrogate of similar size and wing loading, instrumented for flight research, with a single aft-mounted JetCat P120SE ([NASA S-2 study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20080034480)). It reports static thrust measurements and throttle steps, a first-order engine/RPM/thrust response with time constants that vary from 0.85 to 1.96 seconds, ram-drag identification from flight acceleration, and a held-out yaw-roll doublet prediction around 80 kt. Those measurements are valuable small-turbine response evidence, but they are tied to this installed engine and airframe. The same paper notes that near-stall prediction remained future work.

The T-2/GTM studies provide flight-tested control, maneuver and aerodynamic-identification methods on a 5.5% twin-turbine transport. The reused Morelli sources document six-degree-of-freedom multisine excitation and a flight-derived global force/moment model; their existing originals and selected coefficients are reviewed in the [RC aerodynamic source guide](../../rc-aero-data-sources.md). Later AirSTAR papers report offset-landing and departure-prone/post-stall tests ([Gregory et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110014633)), degraded-stability pilot evaluations, rate-derivative maneuvers on T-2 and BAT-4, and identification in turbulence. These are actual subscale flights, but most papers report methods, plots or fitted models rather than a public raw log. Results depend on their instrumentation, control augmentation, test configuration and maneuver.

**The Generic Future Fighter is the closest documented RC turbine-fighter platform in this set.** Lundström et al. report actual free-flight testing of a 13% GFF with a 2.323 m length, 1.440 m span, 15 kg empty mass and JetCat P160 ([ICAS 2016 paper](https://icas.org/icas_archive/ICAS2016/data/papers/2016_0390_paper.pdf)). To allow open-loop piloting, the model’s CG was moved forward from the normally unstable fighter arrangement. Larsson et al. later use the same GFF in an actual multisine flight-identification campaign with an instrumental-variable frequency-domain method ([2020 article](https://doi.org/10.3390/aerospace7090135)). These studies support RC turbine-fighter instrumentation and maneuver design, not direct transfer of full-size fighter derivatives. The 2020 campaign is a second study on the same airframe, not another independent model.

**The Hawk records separate three different kinds of evidence.** The 2019 Soares paper uses a 3D-printed model at half the size of a BAe Hawk D006 *radio-control aeromodel* for wind-tunnel and AVL/VLM comparisons; it does not use a half-scale full-size Hawk, and its dynamic flight campaign was still planned ([official ABCM record](https://www.abcm.org.br/proceedings/view/COB2019/0137)). Soares’s 2020 ITA thesis abstract reports longitudinal identification of the Hawk demonstrator from flight data, but the official page exposes no full-text file. Carnduff et al. describe a separate 1/12-scale BAe Hawk on a four-degree-of-freedom dynamic wind-tunnel rig; only metadata and abstract were obtainable here. Keep static tunnel slopes, captive-rig dynamic estimates and free-flight identification distinct.

The **Raven** paper documents a radio-controlled, dynamically scaled business-jet demonstrator and its instrumentation/test preparation, not a completed public coefficient dataset. The **F16Capstone** files are the only retained hobby-model telemetry in this branch: a Pixhawk ULog and its companion 2021 plan. The project README identifies a small-scale RC F-16 and actual flight work; the plan describes elevator/aileron/rudder frequency sweeps. The ULog itself was not decoded, so it is a raw-data lead rather than an identified model. The inspected project files do not establish the model scale or whether its propulsion is EDF; no EDF claim is made.

## Engine evidence and reuse

Use [AV-05 turbine dynamics](../../../avanti-s-av05-turbine-dynamics.md) and the [AV-18 source review](../../../turbine-realism/AV-18/README.md) for bench/engine transient evidence on P100, P160 and P220 miniature turbines. Those sources are not duplicated here. Cunningham et al.’s S-2 P120SE measurements add an installed, single-engine subscale-aircraft example; they do not replace the AV-05/AV-18 bench studies or validate an Avanti installation.

Morelli’s 2011 and 2012 AirSTAR originals are reused from `references/rc-aero-data/` with their existing hashes; no aliases or duplicate files were created. The two related Morelli records count as separate published studies, but the records from the same GFF and same AirSTAR aircraft are not separate independent airframes. The sibling [helicopter collection](../helicopters/README.md) covers rotorcraft evidence.

## Access and inspection

Queries included `site:ntrs.nasa.gov/citations AirSTAR T-2 flight test aerodynamic parameter estimation`, `"subscale flight testing" "JetCat P160" generic fighter`, `"BAe Hawk" subscale flight identification thesis`, and exact-title/DOI searches for the Hawk dynamic tunnel study. NASA NTRS originals, ICAS papers, the DiVA article and the direct F16Capstone files were obtained from public source endpoints. A few NTRS requests initially returned temporary 503 responses; the NTRS attachment endpoint succeeded. No login, challenge evasion, paywall bypass or alternate-credential route was used.

The ITA thesis record has an abstract but no public full-text download. Carnduff’s publisher DOI record and indexed abstract were available, but a public full-text original was not obtained. Both are marked metadata-only in `sources.json`; no coefficients are inferred from the abstract. NASA, ICAS, ABCM and GitHub copies are retained locally for reference only unless the manifest states a specific article license. Public access alone does not imply redistribution permission.

All 15 new PDFs were checked for `%PDF-` magic and parsed with `pdftotext`; their titles and topic-specific passages were inspected. The ULog begins with the expected `ULog\x01` signature and its hash is recorded, but its channels were not parsed. There are no ZIP archives in this set, so no ZIP CRC check applies. The two reused AirSTAR PDFs were re-hashed against the existing source registry. No coefficients were imported into production.
