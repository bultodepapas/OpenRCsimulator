# RC jets, turbines and electric ducted fans

Research snapshot: **2026-10-19 UTC**. The [source manifest](sources.json) contains 24 primary-source records: 19 new local originals, two reused originals already held in `references/rc-aero-data/`, and three records without local originals. Local copies are ignored by Git under `references/rc-validation-library-2026-10-18/jets/`; the manifest preserves source URLs, exact hashes, byte counts, access status, evidence type and limits. This is source research, not an adopted jet model or a claim of physical validation.

## Strongest comparison evidence

**NASA AirSTAR distinguishes a measured subscale test platform from a hobby sport jet.** Jordan et al. describe a 5.5% dynamically scaled, twin-turbine generic transport with roughly a 7 ft span and high-rate instrumentation ([NASA platform paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060022136)). The separate S-2 article describes a COTS subscale transport surrogate of similar size and wing loading, instrumented for flight research, with a single aft-mounted JetCat P120SE ([NASA S-2 study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20080034480)). It reports static thrust measurements and throttle steps, a first-order engine/RPM/thrust response with time constants that vary from 0.85 to 1.96 seconds, ram-drag identification from flight acceleration, and a held-out yaw-roll doublet prediction around 80 kt. Those measurements are valuable small-turbine response evidence, but they are tied to this installed engine and airframe. The same paper notes that near-stall prediction remained future work.

The 2009 Gregory L1 controller-design paper is simulation and piloted-simulation evidence only; actual controller flights are reported in the 2010 and 2011 Gregory papers. More broadly, the T-2/GTM studies provide flight-tested control, maneuver and aerodynamic-identification methods on a 5.5% twin-turbine transport. The reused Morelli sources document six-degree-of-freedom multisine excitation and a flight-derived global force/moment model; their existing originals and selected coefficients are reviewed in the [RC aerodynamic source guide](../../rc-aero-data-sources.md). Other AirSTAR flight papers report offset-landing and departure-prone/post-stall tests ([Gregory et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110014633)), degraded-stability pilot evaluations, rate-derivative maneuvers on T-2 and BAT-4, and identification in turbulence. These are actual subscale flights, but most papers report methods, plots or fitted models rather than a public raw log. Results depend on their instrumentation, control augmentation, test configuration and maneuver.

**The Generic Future Fighter is the closest documented RC turbine-fighter platform in this set.** Lundström et al. report actual free-flight testing of a 13% GFF with a 2.323 m length, 1.440 m span, 15 kg empty mass and JetCat P160 ([ICAS 2016 paper](https://icas.org/icas_archive/ICAS2016/data/papers/2016_0390_paper.pdf)). To allow open-loop piloting, the model’s CG was moved forward from the normally unstable fighter arrangement. Larsson et al. later use the same GFF in an actual multisine flight-identification campaign with an instrumental-variable frequency-domain method ([2020 article](https://doi.org/10.3390/aerospace7090135)). These studies support RC turbine-fighter instrumentation and maneuver design, not direct transfer of full-size fighter derivatives. The 2020 campaign is a second study on the same airframe, not another independent model.

**The Hawk records separate three different kinds of evidence.** The 2019 Soares paper uses a 3D-printed model at half the size of a BAe Hawk D006 *radio-control aeromodel* for wind-tunnel and AVL/VLM comparisons; it does not use a half-scale full-size Hawk, and its dynamic flight campaign was still planned ([official ABCM record](https://www.abcm.org.br/proceedings/view/COB2019/0137)). Soares’s 2020 ITA thesis abstract reports longitudinal identification of the Hawk demonstrator from flight data, but the official page exposes no full-text file. Carnduff et al. describe a separate 1/12-scale BAe Hawk on a four-degree-of-freedom dynamic wind-tunnel rig; only metadata and abstract were obtainable here. Keep static tunnel slopes, captive-rig dynamic estimates and free-flight identification distinct.

The **Raven** paper documents a radio-controlled, dynamically scaled business-jet demonstrator and its instrumentation/test preparation, not a completed public coefficient dataset. The **F16Capstone** files are the only retained hobby-model telemetry in this branch: a Pixhawk ULog and its companion 2021 plan. The project README identifies a small-scale RC F-16 and actual flight work; the plan describes elevator/aileron/rudder frequency sweeps. The ULog itself was not decoded, so it is a raw-data lead rather than an identified model. The inspected project files do not establish the model scale or whether its propulsion is EDF; no EDF claim is made.

## Engine evidence and reuse

Use [AV-05 turbine dynamics](../../../avanti-s-av05-turbine-dynamics.md) and the [AV-18 source review](../../../turbine-realism/AV-18/README.md) for bench/engine transient evidence on P100, P160 and P220 miniature turbines. Those sources are not duplicated here. Cunningham et al.’s S-2 P120SE measurements add an installed, single-engine subscale-aircraft example; they do not replace the AV-05/AV-18 bench studies or validate an Avanti installation.

Morelli’s 2011 and 2012 AirSTAR originals are reused from `references/rc-aero-data/` with their existing hashes; no aliases or duplicate files were created. The two related Morelli records count as separate published studies, but the records from the same GFF and same AirSTAR aircraft are not separate independent airframes. The sibling [helicopter collection](../helicopters/README.md) covers rotorcraft evidence.

## Electric ducted-fan evidence

The [INTI 1:28 wind-tunnel study](https://icas.org/icas_archive/ICAS2022/data/preview/ICAS2022_0431.htm) uses two commercial six-blade EDFs described as typical R/C units (70 mm central-duct and 73 mm fan dimensions are reported, requiring clarification before geometry reuse) in a 1.12 m-span box-wing model. It measures forces, electrical power, wake flow and total pressure in BLI and non-BLI installations. This is powered subscale tunnel evidence, not free-flight RC data; its interaction and power findings are specific to the conceptual transport layout.

Two NASA studies characterize a larger COTS EDF. [Weinstein and Simmons (2024)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230017299) test the single 130 mm Jetfan 130 Pro in the Langley 12-Foot Tunnel and identify static and dynamic motor, thrust and power models using designed experiments and held-out validation. [Weinstein et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.C038401) reuse that fan on a 6 ft ADEPT wing, measuring longitudinal force and moment across six fan positions and arrays of one, three and five fans. These data directly inform EDF response and installation effects, but the first study is an isolated fan test and the second a wing-only tunnel test; neither is a hobby-jet flight campaign, and they share the same fan hardware.

Urban et al.’s [50/64 mm EDF study](https://doi.org/10.3390/app13052787) compares static thrust and electrical power across cowling/outlet modifications and single- and two-rotor arrangements. It is the closest small-unit bench comparison here, but does not measure installed-airframe or flight behavior. The full publisher article text was reviewed; its direct PDF endpoint returned 403, so it has no local copy or file hash in the manifest.

## Access and inspection

Queries included `site:ntrs.nasa.gov/citations AirSTAR T-2 flight test aerodynamic parameter estimation`, `"subscale flight testing" "JetCat P160" generic fighter`, `"BAe Hawk" subscale flight identification thesis`, and exact-title/DOI searches for the Hawk dynamic tunnel study. NASA NTRS originals, ICAS papers, the DiVA article and the direct F16Capstone files were obtained from public source endpoints. A few NTRS requests initially returned temporary 503 responses; the NTRS attachment endpoint succeeded. No login, challenge evasion, paywall bypass or alternate-credential route was used.

The ITA thesis record has an abstract but no public full-text download. Carnduff’s publisher DOI record and indexed abstract were available, but a public full-text original was not obtained. Urban et al.’s publisher text was readable, but its direct PDF request returned 403. These three records have no local original in `sources.json`; the manifest distinguishes abstract-only inspection from the full publisher HTML inspected for Urban et al. No coefficients are inferred from abstracts or missing originals. Search also surfaced Long et al.’s [AIAA 2026 ducted-fan blown-lift RC model](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2026-4271), whose indexed author-uploaded text describes a 3 m-span aircraft with ten EDFs and flight plus wind-tunnel testing. Both the AIAA and author-upload endpoints returned 403 on ordinary requests, so no local copy was retained or manifest record added. No challenge or access-control workaround was attempted. NASA, ICAS, ABCM and GitHub copies are retained locally for reference only unless the manifest states a specific article license. Public access alone does not imply redistribution permission.

All 18 new PDFs were checked for `%PDF-` magic and parsed with `pdftotext`; their titles and topic-specific passages were inspected. The ULog begins with the expected `ULog\x01` signature and its hash is recorded, but its channels were not parsed. There are no ZIP archives in this set, so no ZIP CRC check applies. The two reused AirSTAR PDFs were re-hashed against the existing source registry. No coefficients were imported into production.
