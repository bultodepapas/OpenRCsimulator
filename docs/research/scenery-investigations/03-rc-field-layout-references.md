# 03 · RC club field layout: rules, markings, structures and photo references

Date: **2026-10-07**. Step: SC-01 (a), research input for SC-06, SC-07, SC-08 and decisions O-6, O-7 of [SCENERY-PLAN](../../SCENERY-PLAN.md). **Status: research only. Nothing was added to `app/` or `assets/`.**

**Question:** what do official guides, full-scale marking standards and real clubs say about the layout, markings and buildings of a compact club field like the owner's reference photo?

**Method:** web search; official PDFs downloaded to scratch (`/tmp/claude-1000/scenery-research/`, not in the repo), text extracted with `pdftotext`, AMA figure read from a page render; club pages read with a fetch tool; Wikimedia Commons metadata read through its API.

**Evidence tags:** **[V]** read on the page or in the PDF on 2026-10-07 · **[V-fig]** read from a rendered figure · **[sec]** secondary page read (club directory, newspaper) · **[F]** forum post read · **[U]** not verified (search snippet only, or page blocked) · **[est]** estimated here · **[calc]** unit conversion.

## Summary

1. **AMA gives the depth bands; the photo fits them.** Distances behind the safety line (runway edge): barrier 15 ft, pilot line 25 ft, pilot stations 45 ft, pits 65 ft, spectators 80 ft, then parking (2022 table) [V]. The 2010 edition calls the same numbers **minimums**: pilots ≥ 7.6 m, pits ≥ 13.7 m, spectators ≥ 19.8 m, parking ≥ 24.4 m [V]. In the photo, the cars sit about 30–45 m behind the pilots, so they meet these minimums.
2. **The car-park rules disagree.** BMFA asks for ≥ 100 m from the take-off/landing area, "if possible". The text of AMA's own 2010 booklet asks for ≥ 300 ft (91 m) from the landing strip. AMA's 2022 spec gives no such number [V]. O-7 therefore needs per-field rules that carry their source.
3. **A yellow X at each runway end is the FAA "closed runway" mark.** A temporarily closed runway has an X only at each end; a permanently closed one also has one every 1,000 ft [V]. Pilots on a forum read the X on RC strips as "not for full-size aircraft" [F]. No RC body defines it.
4. **The square pads are most likely taxi or start-up pads.** Clubs describe "taxi strips" and "taxiways" from a paved pit area to the runway, and "starting stations on taxiways" with restraints [V/sec]. AMA 2010 defines a "taxi area" with no take-offs or landings [V]. Our reading of the photo is still a hypothesis.
5. **Club structures are simple and similar.** Open pole barns 6 × 15 m with a hip roof of 29-gauge steel [V]. Covered pit rows 3 × 28 m [sec]. Pavilions from 6 × 9 m up to a 27 m shaded pavilion [sec/V]. Concrete pilot stations 1.5 × 1.2 m with 0.9 m barriers [V].
6. **Florida paved RC runways** are 350–600 ft long and 30–60 ft wide (107–183 × 9–18 m) [sec/V]. Our 100 × 12 m runway is at the short end, like Triple Creek's 350 × 35 ft.

**Reading of the AMA 2022 table:** in Figure 2, E, F, G, H and I are nested distances from the safety line to the barrier, the pilot line, the back of the "designated pilot stations", the pit/spectator barrier and the spectator line, with parking behind [V-fig]. The table writes them as ranges ("0' - 45'") and does not say whether they are minimums or maximums. The 2010 edition gives the same values for pilots, pits, spectators and parking and calls them "a minimum of" [V]. We treat them as minimums.

## Markings and pads

**Yellow X**
- **Full size [V]:** the AIM, Chapter 2 §3 says "crosses are placed on the runway only at each end of the runway. The crosses are yellow in color" for a temporarily closed runway. For a permanently closed runway, the yellow crosses go "at each end of the runway and at 1,000 foot intervals". AC 150/5340-1M adds: "only solid yellow 'X' markings are painted (never striated 'X' markings)", and a temporary X can be made of plywood or fabric.
- **RC practice:** pilots on Pilots of America (2016) say "the 'X' on each runway. Presumably so none of us 1:1 scale pilots get any ideas", and "The X's are a giveaway too. 'Don't try to land here if you're a real airplane.'" [F]. AMA, BMFA, MAAC and DMFV give no RC meaning [V: not in their texts].
- **The photo:** each end has an X with a yellow cross-bar, and the runway has no numbers. This matches the "closed to full-size" reading [est].

**Square pads**
- **AMA 2010 [V]:** "Taxi Area: No landings or takeoffs from this area. Provides additional open space between pilots and aircraft … Allows taxi room in front of other pilots".
- **Clubs:**
  - Columbia RC Flying Modelers (TN): "500-foot paved runway, five taxi strips, paved pit area" [V, AMA 2010].
  - Triple Creek (FL): "350-foot by 35-foot paved runway … three taxiways" [sec].
  - Gateway RC (FL): "Concrete starting area with restraints for glow, gas and turbine", with starting stations on the taxiways [V].
  - Bell-Air (WA): "Five 5 x 4-foot concrete pilot stations … 3-foot-high safety barriers on three sides" [V, Model Aviation 2004/03].
- **Interpretation [est]:** the pads sit on the runway side of the fence, one at each fence gap. That fits **taxi or start-up pads**, short paved links from the pits to the runway. The small fenced boxes on the fence line are more likely the pilot stations. No source names the photo's pads, so this stays a hypothesis.

**Centreline:** no RC standard was found. FAA dashes have a 3:2 dash-to-gap ratio [V]. The photo shows dashes about as long as the gaps [est].

## What this means for the plan

- **SC-01:** label the south-side depths from AMA 2010 minimums with 2022 as the cross-check: barrier ≤ 4.6 m, pilot line ≥ 7.6 m, pits ≥ 13.7 m, spectators ≥ 19.8 m, parking ≥ 24.4 m behind the runway's south edge (safety line, y = 9 m in L5). Then check the plan's targets against them:
  - The pilot at 0 m is 9 m behind the safety line: inside the pilot band.
  - The shelters at −10…−20 m are 19–29 m behind: deeper than AMA's pit band, consistent with the photo.
  - The car row at −30…−45 m is 39–54 m behind: above AMA's 24.4 m minimum, below the 91 m of AMA 2010's text and BMFA's 100 m.
- **SC-06:** a car row along trees is BMFA's own advice: "position your car park near some obstacle to flying such as trees or a high hedge" [V]. Bay spacing 2.6 m stays a photo estimate.
- **SC-07:** two open pole-barn shelters, 3–6 m deep and 25–30 m long (Perrine 3 × 28 m, OTOW 6 × 15 m), eaves about 3 m, light 29-gauge steel roof, wooden 6 × 6 in posts. Under them: pit tables with model stands (HHAMS photos) and sloped start-up benches 0.48–0.70 m high.
- **SC-08:** a white pavilion with a hip roof, 6–10 m (20 × 30 ft Perrine, 32 × 16 ft Tri-County). A hip roof and steel sheeting are documented for Florida club builds (OTOW).
- **L10 (landscape track, for information):**
  - pilot stations about 1.5 × 1.2 m on concrete, barriers 0.6–0.9 m high, at fence gaps;
  - AMA suggests non-metal (orange plastic) barriers near transmitters, but the photo shows post-and-wire;
  - F3A poles (4 m high, at 150 m and ±60°) are an optional cue for an aerobatics field.
- **O-6 (paved runway):** supported. Florida clubs commonly pave 350–600 × 30–60 ft, and 100 × 12 m is like Triple Creek. For the second field file:
  - a dashed white centreline (no RC standard; FAA 3:2 dash-to-gap, the photo about 1:1);
  - a solid yellow X at each end, labeled "closed to full-size aircraft (FAA closed-runway convention; RC usage [F])";
  - six square pads on the pit side, labeled "taxi/start-up pads (hypothesis)".
- **O-7:** the sources support the plan's proposal: photo-compact depths for the default field, with the loader's distance rules stored per field as `{value, unit, kind, source}`. Suggested profiles:
  - `ama-2010-min` (7.6 / 13.7 / 19.8 / 24.4 m);
  - `ama-2010-carpark` (91 m);
  - `bmfa` (pits 30 m from the take-off/landing path, car park 100 m).

- No RFAE (Spain) field-layout guidance was found. Only AESA/ENAIRE general rules appeared in search: 120 m AGL, 150 m from residential areas, and 500 m from the field reference point in an ENAIRE template [U].
