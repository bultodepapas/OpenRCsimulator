# Time of day: primary-source findings

Date: 2026-10-10

Status: Research complete; implementation has not started.

Step: TOD-00

## Scope and method

This report checks the current Godot configuration and atmosphere code against primary astronomy sources and the pinned engine documentation/source. It supports the time-of-day implementation plan; it is not a photometric calibration or a rendering experiment. No code or assets were downloaded or copied.

## Current project state

`app/get-godot.sh` pins Godot 4.7.2-stable; [`project.godot`](../../../../app/project.godot) selects the Compatibility renderer. [`render/atmosphere.gd`](../../../../app/render/atmosphere.gd) derives a fixed sun vector from `Spec.ATMOSPHERE` (azimuth 225°, elevation 45°), builds the custom sky, fog, and one DirectionalLight3D together, and uses sky radiance for ambient and reflection. Exposure is fixed at ACES 0.6. The shader is a custom, estimated 45°-sun gradient and has no date, site, or civil-time input. [`app/data/fields/default.json`](../../../../app/data/fields/default.json) defines local field geometry but no latitude, longitude, or time zone. The current aircraft and field therefore cannot yet produce a site-specific solar position. Existing Compatibility color-space and fog findings are retained in [landscape investigation 02](../../landscape-investigations/02-atmosphere-aerial-perspective.md).

The current sky's `PROCESS_MODE_QUALITY` does refresh: the pinned 4.7.2 GLES3/Compatibility source marks radiance dirty when sky material uniforms change, and QUALITY regenerates the radiance cubemap in one frame. `update_clouds()` changes a uniform at most once per simulation second, so it already requests a full-quality radiance update at that cadence. Godot documents INCREMENTAL as the same quality work spread over frames and REALTIME as faster/lower-quality, capped at a 256×256 radiance cubemap. The first INCREMENTAL build still occurs in one frame in the pinned Compatibility implementation. Any sun, moon, sky, or fog uniform update must therefore be budgeted and measured; “quality” does not mean a free or frozen map. See [Godot 4.7 Sky](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_sky.html), [sky shader update rules](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/sky_shader.html), and the pinned [Compatibility source: mode selection and dirty state](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/drivers/gles3/rasterizer_scene_gles3.cpp#L3241-L3285) and [radiance update path](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/drivers/gles3/rasterizer_scene_gles3.cpp#L3657-L3674).

## Astronomy and twilight

Solar direction must be a function of a date-time and the field's geodetic latitude and longitude. A simple 24-hour rotation with fixed tilt cannot reproduce seasonal declination, changing noon altitude, day length, or the solar azimuth at sunrise and sunset. Store one absolute time (UTC is suitable for calculation); derive the displayed field-local date/time from an explicit time zone. Civil time-zone and daylight-saving rules affect the display/input conversion, not the astronomical calculation. The simulation clock and the calendar clock also need an explicit relationship so pause, restart, and accelerated time produce predictable skies.

For a small visual feature, USNO's approximate solar coordinates are a practical independently implemented baseline: about 1 arcminute accuracy within two centuries of 2000, using Julian date; its altitude/azimuth conversion defines azimuth clockwise from true north. This is already far more precise than a rendered sun disc or a twilight color fit. NREL's SPA reports ±0.0003° uncertainty for years −2000 to 6000, but its source-code download has separate license terms and a registration step. Prefer the simpler USNO procedure unless the broader date range or precision is needed; treat SPA code as a separately licensed dependency, not implicitly public-domain code. See [USNO approximate solar coordinates](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/sun_approx), [USNO altitude/azimuth conversion](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/alt_az), [NREL SPA report](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy08osti/34302.pdf), and [SPA source distribution terms](https://midcdmz.nlr.gov/spa/).

Use geometric solar-center altitude as the continuous environmental input. USNO's civil, nautical, and astronomical twilight boundaries are respectively −6°, −12°, and −18°. They are useful named points for review, not exact illuminance thresholds: USNO states that actual visibility also varies with clouds, local atmospheric conditions, and the horizon. The conventional sunrise/sunset time is a different definition: solar-center zenith distance 90.8333° (center 0.8333° below a level horizon), which includes mean atmospheric refraction and the Sun's apparent radius. Do not switch day/night at elevation zero or use the sunrise convention as the twilight curve. High-latitude dates can have continuous daylight, continuous darkness, or twilight lasting through the night, so the implementation must not assume two horizon crossings per day. See [USNO rise, set, and twilight definitions](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/RST_defs).

The existing Hosek-Wilkie reference is explicitly a *daytime* analytic sky model. Its authors say after-sunset conditions were future work because the fitted model could not reproduce the Earth's shadow on the atmosphere. Its good sunset rendering near the horizon does not validate extrapolating it for negative solar elevations. The later Wilkie et al. 2021 model explicitly provides post-sunset sky patterns, but is a larger fitted radiance/attenuation model with data and code that would need Compatibility cost and licensing review. For the first implementation, verify astronomy within the selected algorithm's declared error and treat sky color, twilight radiance, direct-light fade, and night-sky values as art estimates anchored to the USNO altitude points. See the [2012 Hosek-Wilkie paper](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/projects/SkylightModelling/HosekWilkie_SkylightModel_SIGGRAPH2012_Preprint_lowres.pdf) and the authors' [2021 model page](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/publications/skymodel-2021/).

## Moon, night, and aircraft visibility

Moon phase and Moon position are separate inputs. USNO defines phase from the Moon/Sun apparent ecliptic-longitude difference and says the illuminated fraction is geocentric; that fraction does not say whether the Moon is above this field's horizon. The Moon may rise during daytime, leave a moonless night, or have no rise/set event on a given date. A rendered Moon or moonlight therefore needs a date-time and lunar altitude/azimuth, not just a 24-hour solar cycle. Percent illuminated is a disk-area measure, not a linear moonlight lux value. Keep lunar light strength an explicitly estimated art parameter until independently calibrated. See [USNO Moon phases and illumination](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/moon_phases), [USNO daily Sun/Moon altitude and azimuth](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/AltAz), and [USNO rise/set caveats](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/RST_defs).

A moonless night is not simply an all-black sky in nature: starlight, airglow, zodiacal light, artificial skyglow, cloud cover, and ground lights can contribute. Those conditions are not currently modeled or sourced for this field. Treat a star field and a moonless-night horizon as visual estimates; any field lights should be explicit local emitters. Let the aircraft become very dark or disappear when the direct Sun is below the horizon and no modeled light reaches it. Do not add a shared ambient-light floor, aircraft emission, fill light, outline, or exposure gain to rescue the model; those changes would illuminate an unlit aircraft that should be hard to see.

Godot 4.7 confirms that auto exposure is unavailable in Compatibility. The renderer comparison classifies Compatibility as RGBA8 low-dynamic-range color, unlike Forward+'s RGBA16F HDR path. The app's current ACES/exposure settings and light energies are art-space values, not a calibrated observer or luminance model. Keep the current exposure fixed for a first comparison so night visibility is caused by the lighting, and label all light-intensity and sky-radiance curves as estimates. A future eye-adaptation option would be a separate perception feature requiring pilot review; it must not silently change the night baseline. See [Godot CameraAttributes](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_cameraattributes.html), [renderer comparison](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html), and the existing [`Spec.ATMOSPHERE`](../../../../app/spec.gd) / [Landscape plan](../../../LANDSCAPE-PLAN.md).

## Recommendations for the plan

1. Add explicit field latitude/longitude and a UTC-backed calendar instant; define the local-time display zone. Validate date, latitude, and longitude. Do not infer global coordinates from the field's local north/east geometry.
2. Derive one sun azimuth/elevation from date-time/site and feed the sky, directional light, sun disc, fog scatter, and ground haze from that shared result. Validate against USNO golden cases, including equinox/solstice and high latitude.
3. Use twilight angles as reference checkpoints for a continuous, estimated visual transition. Keep the current 45° Hosek fit restricted to sun-above-horizon cases; research the 2021 post-sunset model only if the estimated transition fails the visual gate.
4. Treat a lunar model as a scoped sub-step: date-correct Moon position/phase, moonlight only when it is above the field horizon, and a labeled estimated intensity. A named moonless setting is needed for dark-night evaluation.
5. Preserve the possibility that an unlit airplane is unreadable at night. Evaluate the pilot view and inspection view separately, with capture cases at solar altitude 0°, −6°, −12°, and −18°, plus moonless and full-Moon-above-horizon cases. Keep exposure and any field lighting fixed across airplane/no-airplane comparisons.
6. Probe sky-radiance update cadence/mode on the pinned Compatibility renderer and a target GPU. The source establishes invalidation behavior, not frame cost or visual quality. Avoid parameter changes every render frame; compare QUALITY and INCREMENTAL at the actual time-acceleration rate, and verify that sky ambient, directional lighting, and the horizon do not visibly lag one another.

## Limits and unresolved probes

This research does not select the field's geodetic coordinates or time zone, determine whether night flying is offered for every aircraft, calibrate sunlight/moonlight in Godot energy units, establish a physically measured night sky, or prove that a pilot can control the simulator when the airplane disappears. USNO sunrise/twilight definitions assume a near-sea-level observer and level horizon; weather and local horizon effects remain outside a basic calculation. Compatibility's low dynamic range makes scene appearance an artistic mapping even when the celestial direction is accurate. Required follow-up evidence is a solar-vector comparison, target-renderer update/performance captures, and owner/pilot review of the unlit-aircraft visibility cases.
