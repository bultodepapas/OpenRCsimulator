## Generated loops and references

[`recorded_powertrains.py`](../../../../tools/audio/recorded_powertrains.py) deterministically decodes the pinned previews with NumPy 2.4.6 and miniaudio 1.71. It downmixes to mono, keeps each preview's decoded sample rate (44.1 or 48 kHz), removes DC, trims the listed material, crossfades the loop boundary, applies gentle frequency shaping, and writes signed 16-bit PCM WAV. The two rotor loops also receive periodic 200 ms moving-RMS leveling, bounded to ±3 dB, before final RMS normalization and the −8 dBFS peak ceiling. It does not upsample or synthesize extra detail. The crossfade is correlation-aware: a constant-amplitude curve is used for correlated endpoints and equal-power weights otherwise. The WAV imports set `compress/mode=0`; the `--check` path verifies those settings as well as source identities, generated bytes, and wrap metrics because QOA compression changes sample values used by the seam guard.

RMS and peak columns are linear full-scale values. The normalization target is 0.10 RMS (about −20 dBFS), with a 0.398107 peak ceiling (−8 dBFS); transient-heavy cuts stop at the peak ceiling, so their final RMS is lower. The wrap test measures `abs(x[-1] - x[0])` after PCM16 quantization and requires it to be no more than `max(1.5 * p99(abs(diff(x))), 0.002)`. Every listed loop passes. The output loop duration is source-window duration less the crossfade overlap; short source material is not repeated to inflate a loop. The manifest includes SHA-256, exact sample counts, PCM and wrap measurements for each output.

## Loop quality checks

The selected decoded source windows have zero samples at or above 0.999 full scale; that check does not establish that the original recording chain was never clipped. Source-window peaks range from 0.176 to 0.972. A 100 ms RMS envelope with 10 ms hops has a p95-to-p05 spread of 0.15–6.33 dB across these windows. Within the one-second span centered on each loop wrap, the final 100 ms RMS range is 0.66–2.96 dB. The toy-helicopter source windows remain variable (5.60 and 6.33 dB p95-to-p05), but periodic leveling reduces their output envelope spreads to 0.96 and 2.45 dB and their near-wrap ranges to 2.96 and 1.22 dB. The leveling is bounded to ±3 dB and slows only the source-level contour; it cannot remove Doppler or distinguish source-distance movement from throttle changes. The maximum wrap second-difference divided by interior p99 is 1.00, so the seam's local curvature is comparable to the ordinary high-frequency variation in the source. All exact per-asset measures, applied gain bounds and envelope measurements are in `manifest.json`.

The 6,960 electric reference comes from a strongest spectral line near 232.1 Hz in the selected quadcopter segment, converted as `60 * 232.1 / 2` under a two-blade-pass-events-per-revolution assumption and rounded to 10 RPM. The toy-helicopter segments have strongest lines near 75.5 and 88.2 Hz; the same assumption yields about 2,270 and 2,650 RPM. These are acoustic pitch estimates, not tachometer readings. The gasoline RPM values are low/high runtime mapping anchors only; the source provides no calibrated RPM. The synthetic turbine's 80,000 RPM is also only a runtime mapping anchor.

## Source-fit limits

This bank does not include a verified .55/.61 glow two-stroke or .64 glow four-stroke recording; those profiles should continue using the existing procedural fallback until a source with matching provenance and license is accepted. The 120cc takeoff supports a broad gasoline-class sound only: its page does not identify cycle/fuel, the recording is short, and neither layer is a calibrated steady-RPM bench capture. It is a takeoff recording, not a separate starter/ignition event. The electric source is an onboard quadcopter recording, not a fixed-wing three-blade propeller. The rotor source is a toy helicopter fly-around, not a .60 glow helicopter; it contains some source-motion pitch/level change and no isolated tail-rotor layer. The turbine layer is explicitly designed audio rather than a field recording. These proxies should not be treated as exact engine-model captures.

## Follow-up source search and spectral fit (2026-10-20)

The additional open-source simulator search did not supply a replacement for the missing glow or model-turbine recordings. [CRRCSim's sound directory](https://sourceforge.net/p/crrcsim/code/ci/default/tree/sounds/) contains electric, fan and glider sounds; its [electric-source commit](https://sourceforge.net/p/crrcsim/code/ci/8ecd9a0608ca6d2ef5d06b9852601f6efecc4429/) provides no engine recording provenance. [PicaSim's raw motor files](https://github.com/Rowlhouse/PicaSim/tree/main/data/SystemData/Audio) have suggestive names but no verified asset-specific recording credits or permission suitable for this project; its [current repository license](https://github.com/Rowlhouse/PicaSim/blob/main/LICENSE.txt) is noncommercial. [FlightGear's Rascal RC model](https://github.com/ThunderFly-aerospace/FlightGear-Rascal/blob/master/Rascal110-JSBSim-set.xml) points to shared generic aircraft audio rather than an identified RC engine recording. None was imported. A simulator's use of a sound does not establish that it records the required powertrain.

Targeted Freesound, OpenGameArt and Archive searches found no verified CC0 or CC-BY recording of a glow 2T, glow 4T, model RC turbine, or fixed-wing electric propeller. This describes the search results, not proof that no such recording exists elsewhere. A stronger real RC-helicopter candidate is Nicholas Judy / designerschoice, [“RC Start, Hover, Stop”](https://freesound.org/people/designerschoice/sounds/840045/): 16.619 s WAV, 48 kHz/16-bit/stereo, CC-BY 4.0. It is an explicit RC helicopter recording and is a candidate for a hover segment and actual start transient, but the page does not identify its engine size/cycle or provide a separate tail-rotor recording. The same author’s [50.428 s start/fly/shutoff recording](https://freesound.org/people/designerschoice/sounds/840043/) has the same format/license and includes muffled wind, so it is less suitable as a loop. The download control requires login, and direct requests for the public preview returned HTTP 403 here, so its audio has not been measured; the failed retrieval is recorded under ignored `tmp/audio-aud15/source-candidates/`.

The [rcEyeSoar CC0 pack](https://freesound.org/people/rcEyeSoar/packs/11123/) contains two additional recorded gas RC fly-bys: [176972](https://freesound.org/people/rcEyeSoar/sounds/176972/) (22.889 s) and [176973](https://freesound.org/people/rcEyeSoar/sounds/176973/) (11.611 s), both 44.1 kHz/16-bit/stereo. The pack also includes a [17.817 s failed-start recording](https://freesound.org/people/rcEyeSoar/sounds/176971/); its page describes voices behind the start. None names the engine cycle or RPM, and the fly-bys bake in motion, so these do not establish a clean glow-engine replacement. The closest real model-turbine source found was Sonniss’s [RC Jet](https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/rc-jet/) library: authentic static AJ37 RC-turbine starts/throttle material, 16 files at 96 kHz/24-bit, but it is paid ($19) rather than CC0/CC-BY. It is not included. No standard-license YouTube clips were considered for reuse.

A further Wikimedia Commons and Internet Archive search found no suitable stationary powertrain recording. NASA's public-domain [“Miniature Aces” video](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%22Miniature_Aces%22_NASA%27s_Dale_Reed_Flight_Research_Laboratory_(AFRC-2015-10946).webm) has a directly downloadable original, but its narration, music and unidentified fly-by passages prevent a clean, attributable engine loop. Internet Archive matches were irrelevant or lacked explicit reuse rights. These rejected candidates do not close the source-fit gaps above.

The gasoline layers were compared in the final PCM WAVs with 0.5 s Hann FFT windows, 0.25 s hops, and the strongest line from 40–1,000 Hz. `gasoline_low.wav` yielded 94, 94, 90 and 94 Hz; its repeated 94 Hz line is close to the 93.3 Hz expected at the 2,800 RPM mapping anchor under the assumed two acoustic events per revolution. The superseded 5.00–7.05 s high cut yielded 334, 332, 166, 248, 248 and 248 Hz, so it included a changing tonal/harmonic transition. The current `gasoline_high.wav` cut is 6.00–7.25 s; its three output windows peak at 248, 248 and 250 Hz, close to the 246.7 Hz expected at the 7,400 RPM mapping anchor. At 5,000 RPM, 94 and 248 Hz scale to about 167.9 and 167.6 Hz, so the two steady tonal references align closely.

The selected high-source window's 100 ms RMS-envelope p95-to-p05 spread is 2.46 dB. Its shaped and normalized one-second loop has a 2.01 dB near-wrap RMS range, seam step at 0.64 times interior-difference p99, and wrap second-difference at 0.87 times interior second-difference p99. The peak ceiling remains −8 dBFS. The generator now uses this recipe; the short output is one source slice and is not repeated to extend its length.

The two toy-helicopter loops are now levelled in the generator with a circular 200 ms RMS envelope and a ±3 dB gain bound. For the loop material immediately before and after leveling, the p95-to-p05 RMS spreads fall from 4.36 to 0.96 dB and from 5.86 to 2.45 dB; near-wrap 100 ms RMS ranges are 2.96 and 1.22 dB. This reduces slow fly-around pumping while also flattening some sub-500 ms throttle or distance movement; it cannot remove Doppler. The final global normalization preserves the intended 0.10 RMS where headroom allows, then enforces the −8 dBFS peak ceiling, so the peak-limited low rotor loop ends at 0.0958 RMS. These PCM outputs are reproducible from the recorded previews; no listening or physical-validation claim is made.
