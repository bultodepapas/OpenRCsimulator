# NVIDIA and AMD ray tracing: an OpenRC integration guide

Research snapshot **2026-11-15**. OpenRC application baseline `e488ef7653e8dd0f0a2e7db18e396bb4da0aaccc`; audit branch at dispatch `e92db3654432f81fb3e666e1c0e7f59b94355002`. Official engine pin: Godot `4.8.beta2.official.cafc17a19`, commit [`cafc17a192acf67903910a68aeb3c076c0907a4b`](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/tree/cafc17a192acf67903910a68aeb3c076c0907a4b). The newer main checkpoint `273a7e4c0617fdf4cacc39c360796fd79afa5f2c` is not integrated. This is a source-based recommendation, not a GPU run, SDK adoption, supported-device list, or measured OpenRC improvement. Source descriptions and release states were checked on the snapshot date; mutable web manuals can change.

## Choice: one portable effect before vendor SDKs

The best first candidate is a **controlled, opt-in, opaque directional-sun visibility fixture** on an actually eligible Forward+/Vulkan device. Test the same Vulkan method on available NVIDIA and AMD devices as each is qualified; keep OpenRC's existing raster shadows whenever the requirements fail. This experiment needs **no RTXDI, denoiser, DLSS Ray Reconstruction, or FSR Ray Regeneration SDK**. It is justified only after a reproducible pilot-visible shadow defect and measured 1080p headroom exist. An implementation may require a small engine patch: no public scene-TLAS, selected-sun buffer, or automatic ray-traced Godot effect is assumed.

This guide extends, rather than replaces, the [engine audit](../04-ray-tracing-in-godot.md), [exact scene/light/lifetime design and RT-01–06 tests](../10-reflex-and-ray-integration-design.md#2-direct-sun-ray-tracing-real-seams-and-limits), and [community cases](../community/03-ray-tracing-cases.md). The existing owners and gates remain unchanged. In particular, M0 stays within **five engineering days total**; after admission, make the RT feasibility decision within **two engineering days**, and at the **two-week** checkpoint show a correct, useful opaque fixture with cost and limitations or retain raster/defer. The older 2–4-week controlled-fixture and 6–12+ week flight-integration estimates remain rough, conditional estimates, not guarantees.

Why this order: an outdoor RC pilot generally needs a legible aircraft and a reliable height/contact cue more often than complex many-light illumination. The [High profile](../../../../app/render/high_graphics_profile.gd#L114) defaults to four cascades and a 0.53° solar diameter for contact-hardening PCSS; [shadow settings](../../../../app/render/shadow_settings.gd) can override them and expose hard/soft and native/projected choices. Record the actual effective control. Raster shadows can cover off-screen casters too. A ray-corrected ground shadow may help only when a **reproduced** cascade, bias or caster-coverage defect visibly harms that cue. The hard-ray fixture tests visibility and lighting correctness; it does not establish better softness than existing PCSS. An off-screen aircraft reflection in the lake is another specific candidate, but needs surface shading and moving-scene data; it is not a reason to replace the whole renderer. Compare at normal viewing distance, in motion, against the best applicable native/PCSS plus intentional projected cue, planar or probe option. No effect or vendor advantage is assumed in advance.

## What the first implementation actually requires

1. **Select and prove the device path.** Record actual renderer, backend, OS, physical device, driver, exact editor/templates and Vulkan validation configuration. Check shader compilation, actual RD feature reporting, allocations, a known hit and a miss. Compatibility, headless, Metal and other backends keep their existing paths; the separately patched Metal diagnostic is not stock Vulkan or stock Metal support. Capability bits are a starting point, not proof that tracing works.
2. **Negotiate the API correctly.** Both Vulkan routes require acceleration structures. `VK_KHR_ray_query` plus its SPIR-V/GLSL shader support returns traversal results inside an existing shader. `VK_KHR_ray_tracing_pipeline` supplies dedicated ray-generation/miss/hit stages, dispatch and a shader binding table (SBT). These are independent capabilities; their extension/version dependencies, feature structures, buffer device address, limits and alignment must be available and enabled on device creation, not inferred from a GPU brand. See [Khronos ray-query dependencies](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VK_KHR_ray_query.html), [AS dependencies](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VK_KHR_acceleration_structure.html), and the [RT guide](https://docs.vulkan.org/guide/latest/extensions/ray_tracing.html). Godot owns the real device creation; an extension cannot retroactively enable a missing feature.
3. **Resolve the exact-pin prerequisites and respect the query-only risk.** The later [fixture work order](03-vulkan-fixture-work-order.md#card-0--correct-a-pinned-vulkan-address-mismatch-first) identifies an AS-address contract mismatch, incomplete creation-error propagation and a repeated-build input risk. The positive witness requires a reviewed correction based on the exact pin, with matching editor/templates; a stock hit on one device does not resolve those source findings. Exact beta2's `tlas_build` also requires a nonzero hit-SBT range, while its normal SBT allocation uses a real ray-tracing pipeline. Initially require **both** RD query and pipeline support and a legitimately allocated/initialized SBT. Query-only support is **untested** until an appropriate device is examined; do not invent a range. The inspected front end can admit pipeline creation on a query-only report even though the Vulkan entry point may not be loaded. Never call an unavailable/null entry point. Query-only enablement remains a separate dependency; details and an isolated negative test are in [the handoff](../10-reflex-and-ray-integration-design.md#query-only--sbt-feasibility-a-real-decision-not-a-workaround).
4. **Own explicit, compatible geometry.** Build an owned opaque floor and box/triangle with buffers meeting the pin's device-address and AS-build-input requirements. Build a bottom-level AS (BLAS) for each immutable fixture mesh and a top-level AS (TLAS) for its instances; prove current transform, culling/masks, known distance and hit identity against an independent CPU oracle. Include a caster outside the camera view. The pinned renderer's normal mesh buffers are not established as AS-compatible and reading every surface back every frame is not a scene feed. Rigid motion requires an updated TLAS description and supported rebuild/update; changed mesh topology/LOD or deformation requires a valid changed BLAS and dependent TLAS. The pin's examined Vulkan path uses **BUILD**; update/compaction flags alone do not prove an exposed refit/compaction command. The [engine-source detail](../10-reflex-and-ray-integration-design.md#geometry-materials-and-lifetimes) controls.
5. **Put visibility into the right light.** For the fixture, trace toward the selected sun from the actual receiver using a validated origin/bias, normal, distance and masks. In the beta2 Forward+ shader, an engine-owned seam can select ray visibility in place of that sun's raster visibility, then preserve its shadow opacity/fade and existing cloud policy. Apply it once to that sun's diffuse **and** direct specular light. Leave other lights, ambient/indirect and emission alone. A compositor callback can schedule work but does not supply a ready scene AS or make the selected-sun shader consume its mask; an already-composited frame cannot be darkened correctly. The pre-opaque alternative also needs proven matching depth/normal prepass, viewport and sample conventions. See the [actual render order and candidate seam](../10-reflex-and-ray-integration-design.md#exact-lighting-insertion-point).
6. **Preserve ordering and failure behavior.** Establish the actual render-graph dependencies for geometry writes → AS builds → traversal → selected-sun shading, including scratch reuse and any cross-queue ownership. Keep every referenced generation and resource alive until a proven GPU-safe retirement point. Vulkan barriers provide execution/memory dependencies; a CPU callback or a fixed number of frames is not proof of completion. Do not synchronously read back or wait on the GPU each flight frame. On unavailable capability, incomplete current caster coverage, stale scene/light/viewport, allocation failure, unsupported material or unproven lifetime, use the complete raster result and record why. Disabled means no RT scene scan, allocations, history or dispatch.

The first fixture deliberately excludes wind-deformed vegetation, alpha-cut cards, transparent effects and the whole normal-flight scene. It is a **test fixture**, not a partial RT setting for players. Product integration later needs complete relevant caster and receiver coverage, including off-camera casters, or the player viewport remains raster. Simulation state and the 240 Hz physics/input contracts stay unchanged.

### Why one hard-shadow ray does not automatically need a denoiser

One deterministic ray per receiver sample toward an ideal point/directional sun gives binary visibility, not a Monte Carlo estimate of an area light. Its edge can still alias, flicker with pixel sampling, reveal bias/self-intersection, or disagree with coverage, so inspect raw output and compare existing antialiasing or carefully measured supersampling. A denoiser cannot repair missing geometry, incorrect alpha, wrong light composition or stale transforms.

Soft shadows sample directions over a finite sun disc; sparse samples become stochastic and can benefit from a suitable temporal/spatial **shadow** denoiser. Glossy reflections and indirect light need different signals, hit distances, material/roughness information and often distinct denoisers. Temporal reconstruction adds motion, disocclusion, history reset, ghosting and memory costs. Add one only if a later measured effect needs it, and test raw and reconstructed output independently.

## Where NVIDIA and AMD tools fit

| Candidate | Practical opportunity | Integration decision for OpenRC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shared Vulkan ray query / pipeline** | Hardware traversal can answer an off-camera shadow or reflection query against admitted scene geometry. The API permits both vendor implementations; actual extensions, execution and performance vary. | First controlled fixture. The engine, scene, light and resource contracts above are still required. A brand, model name or generic API claim does not qualify a particular device. |
| **NVIDIA RTXDI / ReSTIR** | Resamples lighting candidates across space/time; DI targets many lights, and the SDK also contains GI/PT algorithms. The [v3.2.0 release](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXDI/releases/tag/v3.2.0) and [sample repository](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXDI) describe Vulkan support through their rendering abstraction and HLSL-to-SPIR-V build. | Defer for one sun: reservoirs, temporal buffers, light/material bridge and a many-light renderer add complexity without a demonstrated need. The [integration guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXDI/blob/v3.2.0/Doc/Integration.md) requires application callbacks for scene, materials, G-buffer and traversal; it is not a Godot plugin. Revisit only for an evidenced many-light/indirect-light scenario, with separate bias, history and cross-device tests. |
| **NVIDIA NRD, including SIGMA** | API-agnostic denoising library; SIGMA targets per-light shadow signals, while ReBLUR/ReLAX address other radiance signals. Its Vulkan integration example demonstrates explicit inputs, formats and dispatch. | A later soft-shadow/GI/reflection experiment may compare it with a correct non-NRD reference. Supply exact normal, depth, motion, signal and reset semantics; account for all passes/history and native shader integration. Source availability does not promise a drop-in renderer or qualified AMD execution. Audit the exact [NRD SDK license](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/NRD/blob/v4.18.0/LICENSE.txt), dependencies and redistribution terms before reuse; it is not simply an MIT license. |
| **NVIDIA DLSS Ray Reconstruction** | The [Streamline 2.14.16 guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/v2.14.16/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS_RR.md) describes NVIDIA-supported DLSS-RR with noisy HDR ray-traced color, depth, dense motion, normals, roughness, diffuse/specular albedo, and reflection motion or hit-distance/matrix inputs. It runs in the DLSS reconstruction path and replaces the ordinary DLSS super-resolution evaluation for that mode. | Much more than a one-sun visibility filter. Consider only for a later noisy RT/path-tracing candidate and matching selected adapter, OS, driver, feature support, SDK binaries and guide inputs. The 2.14.16 guide says its RR path does not support DRS; recheck the exact version if revisited. The existing OpenRC NVIDIA lane pins Streamline **2.11.1**, not 2.14.16; adding or upgrading RR is a separate change and cannot be inferred from Reflex/DLSS presence. |
| **AMD FidelityFX SSSR / Hybrid Reflections** | [SSSR 1.5](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/stochastic-screen-space-reflections/) traverses screen depth, while the [Hybrid Reflections sample](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/samples/hybrid-reflections/) combines screen-space and hardware tracing, classification and denoising. | SSSR alone cannot recover an off-screen aircraft. Only a hybrid implementation with a correctly maintained RT scene and hit shading could address that specific lake case. Sample DX12/Vulkan support does not provide Godot's scene, water composition or motion data. Compare against OpenRC's existing probe/planar options first. |
| **AMD FidelityFX Hybrid Shadows / Denoiser** | [Hybrid Shadows](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/samples/hybrid-shadows/) illustrates shadow-map classification, limited ray work and optional shadow denoising. Classification must not reject rays based on a shadow map whose own missed caster is the defect being fixed. Verify all partitions against a ray/reference image. Samples list Windows and DX12/Vulkan; neither the sample nor SDK is a ready Godot effect. |
| **AMD FSR Ray Regeneration** | AMD's [1.2.1 manual](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/denoising/) describes a separate machine-learning denoiser with multiple signal choices and temporal inputs; it is decoupled from upscaling. | The stated 1.2.1 requirements are **Windows 11, DirectX 12 with Shader Model 6.6, and Radeon RX 9000 series or later**. It is not a Vulkan route in this version, despite the broader FSR SDK containing Vulkan-capable components. OpenRC's separate [Windows NVIDIA-fork build](../../../../tools/graphics/native/build.py) pins FidelityFX **SDK 1.1.4 / FSR upscaler 3.1.4** on Vulkan; this is not Ray Regeneration. The separate SDK 2.4 upscaler candidate is not proof of RR integration either. RR is a separately scoped future backend, not an AMD replacement for the first portable fixture. Check the exact binary/provider license and support query before shipping. |

The vendor examples are techniques to evaluate, not download-and-enable support. AMD describes [FidelityFX SDK v1.1.4](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/) as MIT-licensed; verify the exact component headers, third-party notices, assets and any separately distributed binaries. NVIDIA's SDKs and dependencies have different terms, and an MIT-licensed host framework does not change a bundled component's license. Preserve reproducible revisions and review redistribution before importing code or binaries. Do not upgrade OpenRC's pinned engine or Streamline as a side effect of research.

## Textures, aircraft, water and clouds

| Content | Expected visual gain and engineering cost |
| --- | --- |
| **Aircraft and ground** | Thin wings, control surfaces and the aircraft-ground gap are good A/B cases for shadow detail and height perception. Current [hinged parts](../../../../app/render/airplane.gd) change transforms; the [propeller blur](../../../../app/render/propeller_blur.gd) changes visibility and uses a non-shadow-casting disc. Represent the actual admitted casting policy rather than treating a visual blur as opaque geometry. Test takeoff, low passes and landing, with moving aircraft and camera. |
| **Albedo, normal and roughness textures** | Ray tracing does not create better texture resolution or automatically fix lighting. The first opaque visibility ray only needs correct blocker geometry; regular raster material shading continues. Reflected-hit shading later needs the right mesh/instance/material IDs, UV transforms, textures, normal mapping, roughness/metalness and color spaces. Trace-stage texture LOD needs an explicit, validated ray-footprint/LOD policy; do not assume fragment derivatives are available in a compute or hit shader. Budget descriptor access, residency and missing-resource fallback. |
| **Cutout vegetation** | [Treeline cards](../../../../app/render/treeline.gdshader) and [imported vegetation](../../../../app/scenery/imported_vegetation_wind.gdshader) have wind deformation and alpha rules. Later, create non-opaque geometry, inspect each traversal candidate, interpolate its UV, sample the corresponding alpha/LOD policy and confirm only real blockers; match sidedness, material and current deformed vertices. Treating the rectangle as opaque makes false solid shadows. Unsupported relevant material/deformation must reject the normal-flight RT scene. |
| **Lake** | A correct ray-traced reflection could show a moving aircraft outside screen space, but it also needs correct water normal/roughness, hit material and radiance, sky miss, transparency policy, temporal behavior and current scene geometry. The existing lake probe excludes the moving aircraft, while the optional planar capture is another control. Measure whether the aircraft reflection is visible at normal pilot scale before undertaking this larger effect. |
| **Sky and clouds** | OpenRC's [atmosphere](../../../../app/render/atmosphere.gd) supplies a sky, directional light, cloud transport and cloud-shadow resources; [ground shading](../../../../app/render/ground.gdshader) applies cloud transmission. Preserve that direct-sun transmission exactly once alongside object visibility. For a later reflection, a ray miss may sample a current sky/environment representation, but that does not magically intersect or solve volumetric clouds. A new ray-marched/cloud-volume treatment is a separately costed effect. Visual atmosphere must not alter flight/weather physics. |

## Vendor-specific optimization and measurement

First establish a correct shared path; then profile each actual device independently. On NVIDIA, [Nsight Graphics](https://developer.nvidia.com/nsight-graphics) can inspect AS organization and ray workloads, with GPU Trace for event/timeline attribution. On supported AMD configurations, [Radeon Raytracing Analyzer](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/rra_manual/) exposes AS geometry, overlap and traversal views; [Radeon GPU Profiler](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/rgp_manual/) provides event timing, barriers, occupancy and bottleneck context. These are complementary per-vendor tools, not interchangeable results or a reason to add a new always-on profiler.

For example, compare build versus trace time and static versus moving geometry; identify overly broad/overlapping bounds; reuse unchanged BLASes; select valid masks, ray length and opaque flags; and avoid candidate processing on truly opaque triangles. Measure each change on the named workload. Do not mark alpha-cut geometry opaque for speed, truncate an off-camera caster set without a proven bound, or promise an `ALLOW_UPDATE` refit unsupported by the pin. Async compute is a possible later optimization only with proven queue support, dependencies and total-frame benefit.

[Opacity micromaps](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VK_EXT_opacity_micromap.html) and NVIDIA [shader execution reordering](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/path-tracing-optimization-in-indiana-jones-shader-execution-reordering-and-live-state-reductions/) are optional feature-specific optimizations for later alpha-heavy or divergent workloads, not prerequisites. They require actual extension/feature and engine integration checks, correct fallback, and measured benefit. The cited NVIDIA reordering example concerns a ray-generation path, not a guaranteed speedup for the first inline query. Current Godot RD support for these optional paths is not established here. For AMD Linux captures, the [RRA manual](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/rra_manual/) notes a capture limitation with newer driver packages and documents alternatives; verify the installed driver/tool combination rather than assuming availability.

Use the [existing VQ benchmark protocol](../06-validation-and-benchmark-plan.md#ray-tracing) on a named native device, starting at actual **1920 × 1080**. The proposed review ceilings are **2.0 ms additional GPU p95** and **128 MiB incremental peak allocated GPU resources** for the entire effect, including scene/AS, scratch, build, traversal, composition, histories, in-flight copies and SDK-private allocations where observable. These are planning limits, not measurements or reserved budget; stricter whole-frame headroom wins. Report uncertainty, missing allocation visibility, asset/camera/time-of-day identity, transient peaks, raster control and quality settings. Reject or defer a visually insignificant, incomplete, unmeasurable or over-budget candidate. Higher resolutions follow 1080p; 720p is the later compatibility target.
