# EL1a-R1 — Battery and ESC investigations 04–06

2026-10-10 · **Status:** primary papers and manufacturer documents inspected (web sources accessed 2026-10-10); no pack coefficients or ESC thresholds calibrated. This extends the [EL1a source review](../EL1a/sources.md). Conclusions below separate published behavior from recommendations for the selected 4S Timber-class setup.

## 04 — Battery model order and identifiability

**Question.** What is enough for EL2, and what evidence justifies a polarization state or hysteresis?

[Suti, Di Rito and Mattei (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3390/en15239249), *Development and Experimental Validation of Novel Thevenin-Based Hysteretic Models for Li-Po Battery Packs Employed in Fixed-Wing UAVs*, §§2.3–2.5 and 3.1, pp. 7–13, tested one 6S, 1850 mAh fixed-wing UAV pack. Their HPPC campaign used 0.5, 1 and 1.5 C pulses at 0, 15 and 49 °C, 0.1 s sampling and 30-minute rest periods. They first estimated two RC grids with nonlinear least squares, then modeled hysteresis in the RC capacitances. The rested OCV charge/discharge difference was about 5 mV at 70% SOC; RC relaxation depended on SOC, temperature and current direction. Their validated model stayed below 1% voltage error above 10% SOC, but reached about 10% near empty. Fast-current cold parameters were extrapolated, not measured. EL1a already cited this paper for pack-specific HPPC; full inspection adds that OCV hysteresis was small while RC hysteresis mattered for this particular pack.

**Plan change and fixture.** Retain EL2a's `OCV(SOC)` plus measured pack resistance. Add the planned single RC branch only if synchronized pulse/recovery data show a voltage residual beyond measurement uncertainty and its parameters remain identifiable on held-out traces; do not adopt the study's two branches or coefficients by analogy. For the exact pack SKU, compare zero-, one- and two-RC fits across charge/discharge pulses and recovery at representative SOC and current. Report parameter intervals and held-out voltage error. Add temperature dependence or hysteresis only when the intended flight envelope shows a material residual; no runtime aging or detailed thermal model follows from this source.

## 05 — Usable charge, energy and endurance

**Question.** How should SOC, voltage sag and flight endurance be measured against a pack's nominal capacity and C-rating?

[Di Nisio et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23156937), *Battery Testing and Discharge Model Validation for Electric Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAV)*, §§3.1–3.3 and 5–7, pp. 5–16, define charge as `∫I dt` and validate a three-parameter capacity model on one 4S 10 Ah Li-Po pack. Its 10 C label implies 100 A, but tests used 50–125 W constant power and 2–8 A for resistance (about 0.3–0.9 C at nominal pack voltage). The study therefore does not measure performance at its C-rating. Tests stopped around 55% depth of discharge after preliminary trials reached 3.5 V/cell; its under-0.7% normalized capacity error applies only to that pack and validation profiles. The authors report fitted resistance of 20.68 mΩ with a 95% interval of 20.07–21.29 mΩ. EL1a already cited this paper for variable-load validation; full inspection reveals its partial-discharge and low-rate limits.

**Plan change and fixture.** Keep SOC as coulomb counting from measured *total pack current*. Track charge `∫Ipack dt` separately from energy `∫Vpack·Ipack dt`; voltage sag and cutoff mean nominal Ah or a C-rating cannot establish usable Wh or flight time. For EL5/EL6, test the target pack and actual motor/ESC load profile to the selected cutoff, recording calibrated pack voltage/current and each cell voltage; repeat across packs and conditions and carry sensor, resistance and capacity uncertainty into a held-out flight-time result. Include BEC/servo load in total pack current or explicitly label endurance motor-only. Close against EL6's existing ±15% flight-time criterion; do not extrapolate a nominal C-rating into a resistance or high-rate model.

## 06 — LVC, imbalance, recovery and receiver power

**Question.** Does Avian LVC detect a weak cell, how does it recover, and does the receiver stay powered?

The [Horizon Turbo Timber Evolution B manual](https://www.horizonhobby.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-horizon-master/default/Manuals/EFL105250B-Manual_EN.pdf), updated 10/23 (pp. 10–11, 16, 18), identifies `SPMXAE70E`, says default soft LVC reduces motor power to preserve control reserve, and sets 3.4 V/four cells as a transmitter alarm—not the ESC threshold. The [Spektrum Avian Smart ESC family manual](https://www.spektrumrc.com/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-horizon-master/default/dwb627f043/Manuals/Avian-Manual-EN.pdf), pp. 9–13 and 16, allows 2.8–3.8 V/cell, auto/fixed cell count and soft or hard cutoff (50% over 3 s versus immediate); troubleshooting instead says 60%. It specifies no recovery hysteresis or minimum-cell sensing. Smart-battery data can pass over IC, but LVC use is undocumented. The family BEC table omits 70 A; the [SPMXAE70E product page](https://www.spektrumrc.com/product/spektrum-avian-70-amp-smart-lite-brushless-esc-3s-6s-ic3-ver.-e/SPMXAE70E.html) gives no rating. The family manual (pp. 5–7) says the ESC powers the receiver over the throttle lead and warns against a second receiver battery. Delta from EL1a: exact BNF mapping and alarm/cutoff distinction; recovery and BEC remain unknown.

**Plan change and fixture.** In EL3, store alarm separately from ESC LVC, motor output and BEC. Do not treat 3.4 V as trip or assume cell-level cutoff, recovery logic or BEC rating. Bench the exact ESC with a current-limited programmable pack supply; log trip/recovery, motor output, receiver rail and pack current under servo load. Verify cell sensing separately through matched Smart-pack data; pack terminals alone cannot test weak-cell detection. Include measured BEC draw in EL6 or label endurance motor-only.
