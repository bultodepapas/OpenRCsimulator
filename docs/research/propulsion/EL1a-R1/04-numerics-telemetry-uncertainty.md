# EL1a-R1 — Numerics, telemetry and physical acceptance

2026-10-10 · **Status:** primary-source research complete; proposed integration and measurement checks remain unimplemented. Investigations 10–12 support EL1c–EL6 in the [electric plan](../../../ELECTRIC-PROPULSION-PLAN.md). Sources accessed on 2026-10-10.

## 10 — Can one fixed timestep handle stopping, cutoff and different motors?

**Verified findings.** LLNL distinguishes model defects from numerical errors and warns against changing the solver's input state inside a derivative callback. Its discontinuity guidance separates known-time changes from state-triggered events; root detection may require evaluating a smooth continuation beyond an event. The advice to discard multistep history is specific to those solvers, not an extra operation required by this project's RK4. Separately, Driscoll and Braun show why fixed-step stability depends on the product of timestep and dynamical eigenvalue, not on timestep alone. [LLNL, *SUNDIALS Usage Notes*, “On controlling unphysical solutions and integrating over discontinuities,” A–B](https://computing.llnl.gov/projects/sundials/usage-notes); [Driscoll and Braun, *Fundamentals of Numerical Computation*, §11.3](https://fncbook.com/python/absstab-diffusion/).

**Inference and EL change.** EL1a refuses negative RPM, so EL1d cannot simply run an unconstrained RK stage through a stop and clamp afterward. Define event direction, trial-state domain, simultaneous-event priority and restart behavior. Use event localization with a documented trial continuation or a convergence-tested low-speed regularization; keep accepted-state mutation outside callbacks. Do not import SUNDIALS just for this principle. Distinguish continuous voltage/SOC crossings from sampled ESC timers; a discrete controller's sampling phase is part of its model.

For a fixed-body, smooth scalar shaft fixture, our linearization gives `lambda = (dQsource/domega - dQprop/domega)/Jrot`. A small torque residual identifies an equilibrium; `lambda < 0` establishes local asymptotic stability for this fixture only. Coupled aircraft and battery dynamics need their own timestep evidence. EL1c should reject an unstable branch as a normal powered-trim target, and diagnose it separately from an unbracketed root.

**Proof to add.** Refine 120/240/480/960 Hz across multiple inertia/Kv/resistance configurations; assess smooth trajectories and event times separately. Test a stop between ticks, a command at the stop, near-threshold recovery, simultaneous cutoff/stop, exact replay and whole-state rollback. Do not demand fourth-order convergence at a discontinuity. Unknown: the supported parameter envelope at 240 Hz.

## 11 — Does a telemetry RPM/current sample mean what the model assumes?

**Verified findings.** ArduPilot's ESC documentation explicitly converts electrical RPM using motor pole count, separates requested output from telemetry, and notes that some ESCs omit sensor fields. Its BLHeli-compatible serial example defaults to 10 Hz and has different responsiveness from bidirectional DShot; these are protocol-specific examples, not Spektrum timing data. Its sensor guide separately specifies optical pulses per revolution and electrical commutation scaling. The guide's introductory four-pole example conflicts with its later pole-pair wording, making independent tachometer verification especially important. [ArduPilot, *ESC Telemetry*, setup/logging sections](https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/common-esc-telemetry.html); [ArduPilot, *RPM Measurement*, electrical commutation and optical sensor sections](https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/common-rpm.html).

**Inference and EL change.** EL5's acquisition manifest must identify protocol/firmware, units, measurement plane, scaling, update cadence, timestamp meaning, filtering/delay and missing-data policy. Preserve raw observations. Do not divide an already corrected shaft RPM by the pole-pair count again. Do not convert a command percentage into measured effective duty. Compare transient predictions through a declared observation/filtering model; interpolating a slow signal onto the 240 Hz simulation clock does not recover unobserved dynamics. This measurement contract complements EL1d's separate accepted-endpoint simulation telemetry contract.

**Proof to add.** Synthetic reducer fixtures: 42,000 electrical revolutions/min with seven pole pairs yields 6,000 mechanical RPM; 18,000 optical pulses/min with three markers yields the same value. These conversions apply only when those raw conventions are established. Reject double correction, stale values treated as fresh, dropped samples treated as zero, unexplained time shifts and phase current labeled battery current. Check installed RPM against an independent optical tachometer. Unknown: exact SPMXAE70E telemetry cadence, filtering, resolution and output scaling.

## 12 — What evidence is sufficient for the ±10% and ±15% targets?

**Verified findings.** NIST's uncertainty law includes sensitivity derivatives and covariance, and its measurement model includes calibration corrections and other variability. Expanded uncertainty is `U = k uc`; approximately 95% coverage with `k = 2` requires suitable distribution and uncertainty-estimation conditions. It is not a universal identity. [NIST, TN 1297, 1994 edition, Appendix A, A.1–A.5](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-law-propagation-uncertainty); [NIST, TN 1297, §6](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-6-expanded-uncertainty).

**Inference and EL change.** From the project's coefficient definitions, `Ct = T/(rho n² D⁴)` and, with directly measured shaft torque, `Cp = 2pi Q/(rho n² D⁵)`. First-order relative sensitivities to diameter have magnitudes four and five; those to RPM are both two. Shared diameter, density and RPM errors correlate Ct and Cp. Propagate their covariance; do not count two coefficients derived from the same sensors as independent confirmation. Battery electrical input is not shaft power. Propagate measurement uncertainty separately from parameter-fit uncertainty and model discrepancy; a poor fit must not be excused by inflating sensor uncertainty.

**Project acceptance rule, not a NIST mandate.** Before examining held-out data, define operating bins, reference quantities, absolute tolerances near zero, coverage assumptions and residual uncertainty `Ue`. For a residual `e` and allowed magnitude `L`, pass when `|e| + Ue <= L`; fail when `max(0, |e| - Ue) > L`; otherwise report inconclusive. For a relative target, propagate uncertainty through the normalized residual, including shared inputs. Keep ROADMAP's ±10% current/thrust and ±15% endurance targets; select physical absolute floors and RPM/torque/transient limits with the acquisition protocol before fitting. Preserve all points and report worst-bin errors, not only a pooled mean. Reserve separate operating points and flights for validation.

**Proof to add.** Offline synthetic checks with shared sensor bias, mis-scaled diameter/RPM, zero-thrust crossings and deliberately delayed channels must expose false agreement. Reports must reproduce a pass, fail and inconclusive case and state calibration, repeatability and fit/validation split. Unknown: Timber sensor budget and sufficient independent operating coverage; these require measurements, not more fitted constants.
