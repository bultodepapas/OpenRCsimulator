# EL1a-R1 — Propeller and installed-powertrain validation

2026-10-10 · **Status: primary-source cross-check complete; sources accessed 2026-10-10; no Timber propeller or installation values validated.**

## 07 — RPM and Reynolds-number dependence

**Verified.** UIUC's [Propeller Database](https://m-selig.ae.illinois.edu/props/propDB.html), “Basic Relations” (updated through 2022), defines static Reynolds number using chord and rotational velocity at the 75% blade station. Deters, Ananda, and Selig, [“Reynolds Number Effects on the Performance of Small-Scale Propellers”](https://m-selig.ae.illinois.edu/pubs/DetersAnandaSelig-2014-AIAA-2014-2151.pdf), AIAA 2014-2151 (2014), measured 27 commercial and four printed propellers in static and advancing flow. For the DA4002 example, 2,000–5,000 rpm spans Re 25,300–63,200 at 75% radius (p. 22); thrust and power coefficients change with Re, and the effect on power coefficient changes with advance ratio (pp. 22, 25–26). The conclusion reports higher Re increasing `CT`, decreasing `CP`, or both, with advancing-flow efficiency also changing (pp. 38–39). Thus a fixed `CT(J), CP(J)` curve is not universally valid across RPM. APC's [Performance Data](https://www.apcprop.com/technical-information/performance-data/) page (undated, “APC Propeller Performance Data”) says its files use actual geometry but compute blade loads and performance with a proprietary vortex-theory method; it separately links UIUC's experimental wind-tunnel data. APC tables are computed comparisons, not measured wind-tunnel results.

Dantsker, Deters, and Caccamo, [“Propulsion System Testing for a Long-Endurance Solar-Powered Unmanned Aircraft”](https://aerosyslab.com/papers/conferences/PropTest_AIAA-Aviation-2019.pdf), AIAA Aviation 2019, Sec. IV.A (pp. 10–11), defines `J = V/(nD)`, `CT = T/(ρ n² D⁴)`, and `CP = 2πnQ/(ρ n³D⁵)`. Reynolds number must accompany the map. At `n=0`, `J` is undefined (and diverges as `n→0` for nonzero `V`); the coefficient normalizations also fail at zero RPM.

**EL recommendation (inference).** Measure the selected Timber B three-blade EFL5962 11×7.3 over static RPM and advancing `J` at low, middle, and high RPM. Record diameter, RPM, airspeed, pressure, temperature, thrust, shaft torque, measured 75%-radius chord `c_75` with geometry provenance, and air viscosity; calculate `CT/CP/J/Re_75` under a documented Reynolds-number convention and retain uncertainty. Interpolate only within the measured `J/Re` envelope. Do not substitute a two-blade APC table or infer a three-blade map from nominal diameter and pitch. Use held-out points across `J` and Re to check force and torque predictions against predeclared uncertainty/tolerance before making a Timber physical-accuracy claim. Synthetic or borrowed experimental EL1 integration remains usable with provenance recorded.

## 08 — Stopped and windmilling propeller drag

**Verified.** Hartman, [NACA Report 464, “Negative Thrust and Torque Characteristics of an Adjustable-Pitch Metal Propeller”](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930091538) (cover dated 1933; NASA record dated 1934), Summary and test results, measured negative thrust/torque for a 4-ft model over `V/nD` from zero to infinity, and separately measured a locked propeller over blade angles 0°–90°. The report says the body behind the propeller affects negative thrust and feathering can reduce locked-prop drag. Its adjustable-pitch metal-propeller values are not transferable to an 11-inch fixed-pitch plastic propeller.

Hammer et al., [“Free fall drag estimation of small-scale multirotor unmanned aircraft systems using computational fluid dynamics and wind tunnel experiments”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13272-023-00702-w), *CEAS Aeronautical Journal* 15 (2024), Secs. 2.3 and 3.3, used a six-component wind-tunnel balance on small UAS and compared fixed with free-spinning propellers. Free-spinning propellers changed total drag in their configurations; the rotor stopped as orientation reduced through-flow. These multirotor results are not numeric estimates for a fixed-wing RC installation.

**EL/G1d recommendation (inference).** Measure the exact propeller and nose installation with blades locked at documented azimuths, freely windmilling with the motor/ESC disconnected, ESC connected at zero command with brake disabled, and the same zero-command condition with each available ESC-brake setting. Sweep representative airspeeds and shaft-axis angles; log axial force, torque, RPM, air density, and brake state. Check repeatability within calibrated uncertainty, and whether windmilling equilibrium balances aerodynamic torque against drivetrain drag. At `RPM=0`, retain force by airspeed and azimuth as dimensional stopped-blade drag data; never extrapolate a finite-`J` coefficient map or let a zero-load cutoff imply zero drag. Do not apply either cited study's drag coefficient directly to the Timber.

## 09 — Installed powertrain torque and transient response

**Verified.** Dantsker et al. (AIAA Aviation 2019), Sec. II.B (pp. 3–4), specifies synchronized measurements of motor-propeller thrust, torque, RPM, motor-terminal voltage/current, supply voltage/current, airspeed, pressure, and temperature; the rig targeted 100–400 Hz. Secs. III.B and V.A (pp. 7–8, 12–13) describe torque/thrust sensors and calibration with known forces and moments. The rig tests advancing flow as well as static operation; it is a measurement method, not a transient acceptance result.

Gauthier et al., [“Digital Twin of a DC Brushless Electric Motor-Propeller System with Application to Drone Dynamics”](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2024-2359), AIAA SciTech 2024, Sec. 3.3 and Fig. 3, compare a physics-model prediction with a first-order-lag model tuned to match at half-height; their predicted curves differ on approach to equilibrium. An [accessible full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09981) (arXiv:2312.09981v1, 2023) states the coupled model response is non-exponential due to propeller drag. Sec. 4.2 reports measured *static thrust* after a throttle step (`T=0.34→0.45`) reaching steady state in many tens of milliseconds; Sec. 5 fits `L/J_m`. The experiment measures thrust, not shaft torque, and does not experimentally establish a non-first-order Timber response. The dynamic data identify the ratio `L/J_m`, not both parameters independently (Sec. 3.2).

**EL recommendation (inference).** Test the exact motor, ESC settings, pack, propeller, spinner, and representative nose on a calibrated rigid stand. Synchronize the sampled controller/ESC command, motor and battery voltage/current, RPM, thrust, and torque. Use enough sample rate and sensor bandwidth to resolve rise/decay and controller timing; 100–400 Hz is Dantsker et al.'s rig target, not a universal requirement. State the torque sensor plane and calibrate it with a known moment arm and weights. For a fixed support enclosing the motor and propeller, the signed external-moment balance is `Qsupport + Qair = dH/dt`; internal bearing friction is not another external moment. Separate non-propeller aerodynamic torque and sensor tare when reducing this measurement. Raw transient support reaction is not propeller `Q` for `CP`. If inferring torque from acceleration, independently constrain rotor/prop inertia and aerodynamic load, and characterize ESC command and sensor delay separately rather than absorbing either into fitted inertia. Repeat static points and throttle steps/chops at low, middle, and high commands; restart from rest and windmilling, then test advancing flow. For EL5, hold out operating points, apply the plan's ±10% thrust/current target, and report RPM/torque residuals against sensor uncertainty. A static pass does not close transient or flight validation.
