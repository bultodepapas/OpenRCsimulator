# R05-B: Dynamic mass integration boundary

Status: R05-A provides validated, fixed-datum geometry only. It does not implement resource depletion, mass flux, angular-momentum flux, or flight dynamics. The current turbine policy remains prescribed fuel; the jet authoring data does contain a kerosene inventory item, but its usable fraction, reserve, depletion law, and lifecycle are not validated.

Implementation follow-up: [R05-B1](R05-B-glow-contacts.md) now connects the existing Glow fuel law to hull, scenery and lake consumers and extends the pre-commit transaction with continuous-state endpoints. The review below describes the broader family integration boundary. Event remainder integration and free-rotor impulse coupling remain outstanding; R05-B as a whole is not complete.

## Reusable geometry and family-specific consumers

`app/physics/mass_inventory.gd` evaluates stable-ID components and removable resources about an explicit fixed reference point. It returns total mass, first moment, CG, datum and CG inertia tensors, and the inverse CG tensor. `app/physics/glow/fuel_mass.gd` already reuses its exact rectangular-box-per-unit-mass kernel. This is a suitable geometry boundary for future changing inventories; callers still own the physical interpretation of those resources.

Glow is the existing mass-changing flight consumer: `app/physics/glow/flight.gd` evaluates mass properties from fuel at integration stages and supplies a body derivative using the current tensor. `FuelMass.datum_derivative` in `app/physics/glow/fuel_mass.gd` encodes Glow's stated co-moving-efflux cancellation assumption. It is a family-specific open-system law; it does not establish the correct exhaust, inlet, or angular-momentum flux terms for a turbine or another vehicle.

For ordinary fixed-wing aircraft, `app/physics/dynamics.gd`, trim, `GroundStart` and the static `Simulation` stage path use model mass and inertia. `Simulation.step` can consume a stage `body_derivative` without mutating shared aircraft data. Glow now selects a separate joint contact callback carrying both continuous endpoints and live-mass contact consumers; its current full-fill start/trim policy agrees with wet initialization. A new family must define the complete resource convention before opting into those interfaces. Choose a fixed body datum and consistently translate external moments and CG-relative points, or define complete stage recentering; do not silently move the state origin as CG changes.

Helicopters have a distinct boundary: `app/physics/rotorcraft/flight_dynamics.gd` consumes immutable context mass/inertia in both rigid-body and geared rotor coupling, while helicopter impacts, trim, and ground starts use that context too. Any live properties must reach all of those consumers together. Ground vehicles have their own 31-state integrator and sprung/unsprung generalized mass matrix in `app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_dynamics.gd`; a changing sprung inventory must update that family-specific matrix and contact mobility rather than routing through the airplane rigid-body derivative. Battery state of charge alone does not change vehicle mass.

## State, validation, and migration boundary

Dynamic resource state belongs to the relevant family integrator, not pilot command tapes. Each RK stage must derive properties from the candidate resource state without mutating committed aircraft data. The physics law must define its control volume, datum, momentum and angular-momentum fluxes, and any contact-event sampling convention before a family adopts finite fuel. Use trim and runway initialization with the same explicit fill state as reset.

Flight-session and family-specific checkpoint/recording formats identify state layouts and physics laws (`app/sim/simulation.gd`, `app/sim/flight_session.gd`, `app/sim/helicopter_flight_session.gd`, and `app/sim/ground_vehicle/session.gd`). A historical prescribed-fuel checkpoint has no spent-fuel history to migrate. Preserve its old law for exact replay or reject it explicitly; do not manufacture a fuel state. A finite-fuel version should record the accepted resource state and live mass properties, bump its layout/law identity, and retain exact replay of the old format. Evidence should cover full, half, empty, and off-center resource cases, per-stage properties and flux balance, event-time contacts, rollback/checkpoint continuation, and unchanged legacy replay. Geometry unit tests alone do not establish a depletion law or its physical validation.

## Remaining crash presentation handoff

The R05-B1 follow-up found a separate current consumer in `app/render/crash/director.gd`: `_start` uses wet `sim.mass/inertia`, and `wreck.gd` derives its estimated piece mass from that report. Scenery supplies a live effective-mass scalar, but severity's total-mass input remains wet. Lake-bank presentation uses the generic wet path. `_fuel_kg` is configured from declared inventory, not the current Glow resource. No hidden live-mass override was found in the crash renderer; `_start_water` and `_touch_feedback` do not consume mass. This source review establishes the missing handoff, not a measured error in a real crash.

Complete this with the CR owner through a single immutable impact sample:

1. Publish total mass, CG, inertia, remaining resource identity/amount, datum and sample time from the accepted impact boundary. Scenery/lake must reuse their selected event's stored properties. Soft hull, gear and prop-strike paths need their own explicit timing convention; do not substitute the current endpoint for an earlier collision.
2. Give the crash estimator CG velocity `v_O + omega × c` and the matching contact normal/effective mass. Keep inertial mass separate from the chosen structural damage-capacity reference: burning fuel does not establish a proportional loss of airframe strength. The CR model must identify that normalization explicitly.
3. Feed the resulting report to the wreck estimator and remaining-fuel presentation without modifying flight state. Glow fuel must retain its actual kind and evidence level; kerosene fire assumptions cannot silently become a calibrated methanol/nitromethane model. Missing dynamic samples should be marked unavailable rather than replaced with wet values labeled current.
4. Extend relevant visual/checkpoint identities and validate restoration before publishing effects. Cover full/quarter/empty fuel, shifted CG, scenery and lake-bank normals, late projection rollback, replay continuation and unchanged constant-mass aircraft. Record both the physical impact sample and the separately estimated damage/presentation quantities.

This handoff, free-rotor impulse coupling and event remainder integration are required before treating R05-B as complete. They are not implied by R05-B1's contact-force and incident-energy checks.
