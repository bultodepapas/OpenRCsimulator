# R05-B: Dynamic mass integration boundary

Status: R05-A provides validated, fixed-datum geometry only. It does not implement resource depletion, mass flux, angular-momentum flux, or flight dynamics. The current turbine policy remains prescribed fuel; the jet authoring data does contain a kerosene inventory item, but its usable fraction, reserve, depletion law, and lifecycle are not validated.

## Reusable geometry and family-specific consumers

`app/physics/mass_inventory.gd` evaluates stable-ID components and removable resources about an explicit fixed reference point. It returns total mass, first moment, CG, datum and CG inertia tensors, and the inverse CG tensor. `app/physics/glow/fuel_mass.gd` already reuses its exact rectangular-box-per-unit-mass kernel. This is a suitable geometry boundary for future changing inventories; callers still own the physical interpretation of those resources.

Glow is the existing mass-changing flight consumer: `app/physics/glow/flight.gd` evaluates mass properties from fuel at integration stages and supplies a body derivative using the current tensor. `FuelMass.datum_derivative` in `app/physics/glow/fuel_mass.gd` encodes Glow's stated co-moving-efflux cancellation assumption. It is a family-specific open-system law; it does not establish the correct exhaust, inlet, or angular-momentum flux terms for a turbine or another vehicle.

For fixed-wing aircraft, `app/physics/dynamics.gd` and the ordinary `Simulation` stage path still use static model mass and inertia. `Simulation.step` can consume a stage `body_derivative` without mutating shared aircraft data during RK stages, but fixed-wing trim, `GroundStart`, the cached `HullContact.prepare` coefficients, and scenery/lake collision response also use static properties. The post-step collision callback currently carries body states rather than accepted continuous resource state, so collision-time mass cannot be inferred exactly without extending that transaction boundary. Choose a fixed body datum and consistently translate external moments and CG-relative points, or define a complete stage recentering; do not silently move the state origin as CG changes.

Helicopters have a distinct boundary: `app/physics/rotorcraft/flight_dynamics.gd` consumes immutable context mass/inertia in both rigid-body and geared rotor coupling, while helicopter impacts, trim, and ground starts use that context too. Any live properties must reach all of those consumers together. Ground vehicles have their own 31-state integrator and sprung/unsprung generalized mass matrix in `app/physics/ground_vehicle/vehicle_dynamics.gd`; a changing sprung inventory must update that family-specific matrix and contact mobility rather than routing through the airplane rigid-body derivative. Battery state of charge alone does not change vehicle mass.

## State, validation, and migration boundary

Dynamic resource state belongs to the relevant family integrator, not pilot command tapes. Each RK stage must derive properties from the candidate resource state without mutating committed aircraft data. The physics law must define its control volume, datum, momentum and angular-momentum fluxes, and any contact-event sampling convention before a family adopts finite fuel. Use trim and runway initialization with the same explicit fill state as reset.

Flight-session and family-specific checkpoint/recording formats identify state layouts and physics laws (`app/sim/simulation.gd`, `app/sim/flight_session.gd`, `app/sim/helicopter_flight_session.gd`, and `app/sim/ground_vehicle/session.gd`). A historical prescribed-fuel checkpoint has no spent-fuel history to migrate. Preserve its old law for exact replay or reject it explicitly; do not manufacture a fuel state. A finite-fuel version should record the accepted resource state and live mass properties, bump its layout/law identity, and retain exact replay of the old format. Evidence should cover full, half, empty, and off-center resource cases, per-stage properties and flux balance, event-time contacts, rollback/checkpoint continuation, and unchanged legacy replay. Geometry unit tests alone do not establish a depletion law or its physical validation.
