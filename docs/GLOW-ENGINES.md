# Glow engine installations

The catalog includes experimental Stik installations with **O.S. MAX-55AX two-stroke**, **O.S. FS-64V four-stroke** and **O.S. MAX-61FX two-stroke** engines. The MAX-61FX is the default Stik's own engine; its profile is scaled from the MAX-55AX ([NITRO-61FX dossier](research/glow-engines/NITRO-61FX/README.md)). All use finite methanol/nitromethane premix, independent combustion models and coupled shaft/aircraft dynamics. Engine curves, propeller aerodynamics, transient and thermal coefficients include explicit estimates. These installations have software evidence, not completed physical or pilot acceptance. See the [active plan](plans/RC_NITRO_ENGINE_REALISM_PLAN.md) and [integration evidence](research/glow-engines/NITRO-02/README.md).

## Fly and configure

Select a glow Stik in the aircraft catalog, or launch with `--quick-flight --aircraft=stik-glow-61fx-2t` / `stik-glow-55ax-2t` / `stik-glow-fs64v-4t`. Airborne starts initialize a warm, trimmed engine. Runway starts initialize the warm engine **stopped**; press **F7** to start. **F6** cuts fuel/combustion; **F8/F9** lean/richen the mixture command. Ordinary zero throttle requests idle. The HUD reports family, RPM, fuel, head-temperature proxy and engine status. Opening the throttle does not restart an extinguished engine.

Airborne trim is approximate: the trim solver uses nominal mixture, while initialization settles fuel transport against the actual feed pressure. Fuel consumption then changes mass and balance, so neutral controls do not hold an exact steady trajectory.

Propeller diameter controls the rendered blade and blur radius, aerodynamic loading and mass inventory. Relative shaft inertia includes the entire uniform-box propeller polar inertia plus an explicitly estimated crank contribution. The alternative propellers therefore change acceleration and gyroscopic response as well as thrust. The uniform mass distribution is a surrogate, not measured blade geometry. The Stik airframe and engine appearance are reused; the renderer does not reconstruct manufacturer-specific engine hardware.

Edit the source generators `research/glow-engines/build_profiles.py` and `build_installations.py`, then regenerate. Do not edit generated aircraft/profile JSON. Engine profiles declare family, fuel chemistry, independent charge/loss maps, operating support and provenance. Aircraft `propulsion.engine.glow_installation` references a closed `openrc-glow-installation v1` file, and `capabilities.energy` must be `finite_glow_fuel`. Its named fuel inventory supplies the initial usable mass and tank geometry. Every quantity carries unit, evidence kind and source. Malformed, mismatched or unsupported modules fail before flight.

Exact input bytes identify checkpoints and retained experiments. The targeted `.gitattributes` rules preserve LF for the engine profiles and flight records, and the existing CRLF representation for generated Stik installations/aircraft. The installation generator writes that representation explicitly and accumulates inventory quantities in a fixed order across Python versions. Run `test_installation_line_endings.py` alongside the generator checks when changing this pipeline.

## Runtime model

The 2T model accounts for delivered/trapped charge, scavenging loss and one combustion opportunity per revolution. The 4T model accounts for independent valve intake, pumping and valve-train losses over two revolutions. Rich 2T missed firing remains a two-stroke mechanical cycle. Neither model derives from an RPM-scaled version of the other. Per-profile efficiencies and losses determine consumption; there is no universal torque or efficiency ranking.

Both integrate servo, admitted charge, transported fuel, finite tank mass and a thermal storage balance. Successful-fire counters and phase advance only at accepted tick boundaries. Heat/cooling affects firing stability. Cutoff, stall, exhausted fuel and overheating have explicit causes; cold charge can fail to sustain firing. The simplified warm starter imparts crank momentum with the opposite carrier impulse and consumes the configured priming dose. It does not model an electric starter battery or detailed plug heating, compression strokes or starter duration.

Shaft RPM follows engine torque minus aerodynamic propeller load and carrier acceleration. Blade inflow and the local cooling estimate include the rigid-body velocity at the offset propeller hub, `v_datum + omega_body × r_hub`, under locally uniform wind. The same hub arm sets the thrust moment; moving fuel CG does not move this fixed datum. The `glow-mean-cycle-finite-tank-hub-inflow-v2` law changes prior rotating-aircraft trajectories and refuses checkpoints carrying the previous glow law. A passive annular blade-element surrogate supplies thrust and torque together, including stopped drag and windmilling. It satisfies `QΩ − T V = aerodynamic dissipation ≥ 0`. Positive-thrust forward-flow points must also absorb at least the ideal actuator-disk power `T (V + sqrt(V² + 2T/(ρA))) / 2`, where `A = πD²/4`; inconsistent points are rejected, never repaired by clamping thrust. This additional necessary bound caught two physically impossible 4T estimates that passivity alone missed. The estimated 4T quadratic loss coefficient is now 0.075, constrained to clear the bound with a 5% screening margin for both assumed geometries; the conditional 2T fit retains 0.06. This is a bounded estimate, not measured induced flow. Surrogate chord is an aerodynamic fit parameter, not measured blade geometry. Reverse breakaway is unsupported and refuses the step. Propeller side force, P-factor and tail wake modules are currently refused for these installations; torque reaction, shaft acceleration reaction, axial offset moment and rotor gyroscopic terms are active.

**Fuel feed (NITRO-05).** An installation may declare `fuel_feed`: the carburetor spray-bar position and the driving pressure at idle and full throttle (carburetor suction plus muffler or crankcase pressurization). The delivered flow is the needled flow times `k = sqrt((p_drive + Δp_head)/(p_drive + Δp_head,needled))`, needled in level 1 g flight with a full tank (`physics/glow/fuel_feed.gd`). `Δp_head` is the premix column between the tank's free surface and the spray bar under the apparent gravity `−F_non-gravitational/m` of the same RK stage. Gravity alone accelerates fuel and airframe alike, so what leans the engine is a steady up-line or a full-throttle acceleration (fuel thrown aft of a carburetor ahead of the tank), a hard pull or a low tank. A dive that drag is slowing richens it. The driving pressure grows with the admitted flow squared, so the same head moves the idle mixture several times more than full throttle. All five bundled installations carry estimated values (2T 1.5/6 kPa, 4T 1.0/4 kPa); no pressure was measured. Rotational terms at the tank, slosh and clunk blockage are not modelled. The HUD telemetry reports `fuel_feed` (factor, head, drive, g_eff).

Tank depletion subtracts the distributed fuel mass and inertia. Body position/velocity remain at the **initial wet-CG datum**, while stage-local mass, moving CG and full inertia determine acceleration. Co-moving distributed efflux cancels mass/inertia derivative terms; there is no spurious `−I_dot ω` spin-up. Geometry remains fixed to the aircraft. This uniform-tank reduction omits fuel slosh and exhaust jet thrust. `Simulation.mass/inertia` and corresponding initial metadata retain the wet reference; `glow_state().mass_properties` reports instantaneous values. Since FUEL-04 the law lives in the shared fixed-datum kernels (`MassInventory.evaluate_reference`, `FixedDatumBody.derivative`); the installation prepares the tank reference once and `fuel_mass.gd` is a thin adapter, bit-identical to the original ([evidence](research/fuel-systems/FUEL-04/README.md)).

Hull forces and thresholds, scenery impulses and lake incident energy use current fuel-derived properties with fixed-datum geometry. Swept contacts retain one interpolated fuel sample at their crossing fraction; rejected ticks publish no contact or damage. The separate `fixed-datum-live-mass-contact-linear-fuel-v1` checkpoint/configuration marker refuses older contact semantics. The existing impact response still omits post-impact remainder integration and uses locked-rotor inertia while preserving relative RPM. [Derivation, replay evidence and remaining coupling limits](research/physics-audit-2026-10-14/remediation/R05/R05-B-glow-contacts.md).

The 17 scalar continuous values are RPM, fuel, temperature, servo, air admission, fuel transport, wrapped crank phase, running latch, stop-reason index, opportunity count, successful-fire count, firing accumulator, requested equivalence ratio, shaft work, propeller work, thrust work and burned chemical energy. Optional wheel states follow. Checkpoint v9 binds the state layout, engine/installation hashes and law; failed stages roll back the entire state. Audio is an observer and cannot consume fuel or generate a physics misfire.

At initialization, the validated profile, lifecycle settings and propeller annulus geometry become detached prepared evaluators. Dynamic inputs and outputs are still checked at every stage. The public and prepared routes use the same family equations, and same-stage propeller loads are reused for the body and shaft. This removes repeated static validation and duplicate propeller evaluation without changing the integration law. The prepared object carries no evolving state; reset/reload rebuilds it and an installation-identity mismatch uses the fully validated route.

The [source-excitation observer](research/glow-engines/NITRO-audio/vibration.md) uses manufacturer bore/stroke and explicitly estimated reciprocating mass and connecting-rod geometry. It reports slider-crank inertia forces and signed 1X/2X coefficients without changing flight state. The audio voice reconstructs those orders at audio rate, with one shared authored force-to-sample gain and a bounded layer amplitude. The flight adapter marks its current-RPM estimate as constant-speed; no frame-sampled force or artificial camera shake is applied. Mounted response, combustion-force excitation and physical amplitudes remain unqualified.

## Qualification limits

This is a mean-cycle model: high-frequency crank/compression torque ripple and mount vibration amplitudes are unresolved. The sound has distinct firing, valve and blade orders but authored timbre; it has no tach-synchronized acoustic calibration. The propeller surrogate has no induced-flow iteration or measured Reynolds/Mach coverage. The FS-64V's numerical support extends to an explicitly inferred 15,000 RPM tail to handle unloading; **11,000 RPM remains its manufacturer practical upper recommendation**, and telemetry flags exceeding it. Numerical support is not an operating recommendation.

Independent analytic tests, installed-flight refinement, full-state replay, native audio capture and legacy regressions qualify software behavior. Both families also pass compiled-resource PCK, native Windows and Linux-under-WSL export smoke tests without loose-project fallback. The macOS export passes architecture/signature checks but has not run on a Mac. These checks do not establish real torque/flow/thermal/transient/flight accuracy. The independent [crank-angle oracle](research/glow-engines/NITRO-02/cycle-oracle.md) checks 360°/720° pressure work and conditional averaging with installed inertia. Its pressure assumptions are inferred; differences between its work closure and the runtime maps expose uncertainty, not measured error. Matched measurements, experienced-pilot comparisons, production cycle/load qualification, target-machine performance and complete platform qualification remain gates in the plan.
