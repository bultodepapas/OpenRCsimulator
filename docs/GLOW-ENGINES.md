# Glow engine installations

The catalog includes experimental Stik installations with **O.S. MAX-55AX two-stroke** and **O.S. FS-64V four-stroke** engines. Both use finite methanol/nitromethane premix, independent combustion models and coupled shaft/aircraft dynamics. Engine curves, propeller aerodynamics, transient and thermal coefficients include explicit estimates. These installations have software evidence, not completed physical or pilot acceptance. See the [active plan](plans/RC_NITRO_ENGINE_REALISM_PLAN.md) and [integration evidence](research/glow-engines/NITRO-02/README.md).

## Fly and configure

Select either glow Stik in the aircraft catalog, or launch with `--quick-flight --aircraft=stik-glow-55ax-2t` / `--aircraft=stik-glow-fs64v-4t`. Airborne starts initialize a warm, trimmed engine. Runway starts initialize the warm engine **stopped**; press **F7** to start. **F6** cuts fuel/combustion; **F8/F9** lean/richen the mixture command. Ordinary zero throttle requests idle. The HUD reports family, RPM, fuel, head-temperature proxy and engine status. Opening the throttle does not restart an extinguished engine.

Sources, test conditions and uncertainty belong to the separate [2T](research/glow-engines/NITRO-2T/README.md) and [4T](research/glow-engines/NITRO-4T/README.md) dossiers. The 2T fit assumes reference density and differs from the manufacturer's test fuel; it is not an independently validated thrust/torque map. Alternative files end in `_apc_13x6.json` under `app/data/aircraft/`. They retain the corresponding Stik family ID and renderer; exact input/profile hashes distinguish configurations. Overspeed telemetry uses the lower engine/propeller recommendation: APC Sport `190000/diameter(in)` RPM and Master Airscrew's generic plastic `165000/diameter(in)` RPM. Applying the latter to K Series glass-fiber composite is provisional, not a SKU-specific rating; the FS-64V's lower engine recommendation controls. Sources and this caveat are retained in the generated quantity.

Propeller diameter controls the rendered blade and blur radius, aerodynamic loading and mass inventory. Relative shaft inertia includes the entire uniform-box propeller polar inertia plus an explicitly estimated crank contribution. The alternative propellers therefore change acceleration and gyroscopic response as well as thrust. The uniform mass distribution is a surrogate, not measured blade geometry. The Stik airframe and engine appearance are reused; the renderer does not reconstruct manufacturer-specific engine hardware.

Edit the source generators `research/glow-engines/build_profiles.py` and `build_installations.py`, then regenerate. Do not edit generated aircraft/profile JSON. Engine profiles declare family, fuel chemistry, independent charge/loss maps, operating support and provenance. Aircraft `propulsion.engine.glow_installation` references a closed `openrc-glow-installation v1` file, and `capabilities.energy` must be `finite_glow_fuel`. Its named fuel inventory supplies the initial usable mass and tank geometry. Every quantity carries unit, evidence kind and source. Malformed, mismatched or unsupported modules fail before flight.

## Runtime model

The 2T model accounts for delivered/trapped charge, scavenging loss and one combustion opportunity per revolution. The 4T model accounts for independent valve intake, pumping and valve-train losses over two revolutions. Rich 2T missed firing remains a two-stroke mechanical cycle. Neither model derives from an RPM-scaled version of the other. Per-profile efficiencies and losses determine consumption; there is no universal torque or efficiency ranking.

Both integrate servo, admitted charge, transported fuel, finite tank mass and a thermal storage balance. Successful-fire counters and phase advance only at accepted tick boundaries. Heat/cooling affects firing stability. Cutoff, stall, exhausted fuel and overheating have explicit causes; cold charge can fail to sustain firing. The simplified warm starter imparts crank momentum with the opposite carrier impulse and consumes the configured priming dose. It does not model an electric starter battery or detailed plug heating, compression strokes or starter duration.

Shaft RPM follows engine torque minus aerodynamic propeller load and carrier acceleration. A passive annular blade-element surrogate supplies thrust and torque together, including stopped drag and windmilling. Its identity `QΩ − T V = aerodynamic dissipation ≥ 0` prevents net propeller energy creation. Surrogate chord is an aerodynamic fit parameter, not measured blade geometry. Reverse breakaway is unsupported and refuses the step. Propeller side force, P-factor and tail wake modules are currently refused for these installations; torque reaction, shaft acceleration reaction, axial offset moment and rotor gyroscopic terms are active.

Tank depletion subtracts the distributed fuel mass and inertia. Body position/velocity remain at the **initial wet-CG datum**, while stage-local mass, moving CG and full inertia determine acceleration. Co-moving distributed efflux cancels mass/inertia derivative terms; there is no spurious `−I_dot ω` spin-up. Geometry remains fixed to the aircraft. This uniform-tank reduction omits fuel slosh and exhaust jet thrust. `Simulation.mass/inertia` and corresponding initial metadata retain the wet reference; `glow_state().mass_properties` reports instantaneous values.

The 17 scalar continuous values are RPM, fuel, temperature, servo, air admission, fuel transport, wrapped crank phase, running latch, stop-reason index, opportunity count, successful-fire count, firing accumulator, requested equivalence ratio, shaft work, propeller work, thrust work and burned chemical energy. Optional wheel states follow. Checkpoint v9 binds the state layout, engine/installation hashes and law; failed stages roll back the entire state. Audio is an observer and cannot consume fuel or generate a physics misfire.

At initialization, the validated profile and lifecycle settings become a detached, read-only evaluator. Dynamic inputs and outputs are still checked at every stage. The public and prepared routes use the same family equations, and same-stage propeller loads are reused for the body and shaft. This removes repeated static validation and duplicate propeller evaluation without changing the integration law. The prepared object carries no evolving state; reset/reload rebuilds it and an installation-identity mismatch uses the fully validated route.

## Qualification limits

This is a mean-cycle model: high-frequency crank/compression torque ripple and mount vibration amplitudes are unresolved. The sound has distinct firing, valve and blade orders but authored timbre; it has no tach-synchronized acoustic calibration. The propeller surrogate has no induced-flow iteration or measured Reynolds/Mach coverage. The FS-64V's numerical support extends to an explicitly inferred 15,000 RPM tail to handle unloading; **11,000 RPM remains its manufacturer practical upper recommendation**, and telemetry flags exceeding it. Numerical support is not an operating recommendation.

Independent analytic tests, installed-flight refinement, full-state replay, native audio capture and legacy regressions qualify software behavior. Both families also pass compiled-resource PCK, native Windows and Linux-under-WSL export smoke tests without loose-project fallback. The macOS export passes architecture/signature checks but has not run on a Mac. These checks do not establish real torque/flow/thermal/transient/flight accuracy. Matched measurements, experienced-pilot comparisons, an independently converged crank-angle oracle, target-machine performance and complete platform qualification remain gates in the plan.
