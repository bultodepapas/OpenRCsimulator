What the comparison establishes
--------------------------------
The probe assigns an idealized virtual roll-stick value each tick and passes it
through FlightSession._inputs(). Although input_enabled is false, rates remain
available because no external driver or radio is active. Normal and sport
therefore go through the production rate logic; full bypasses that shaping.
Roll trim is added after shaping and the result is clipped. The CSV field
raw_roll is the assigned virtual target, not a raw physical controller sample;
cmd_roll is the resulting post-profile, post-trim and post-clamp command.

With the stock Extra's zero aileron expo and no nonzero pitch/yaw pilot input,
sport roll and full roll have the same command for the tested +/-1 pulses. The
full-profile +/-0.394705 pulses match normal-profile +/-1, including trim. The
probe asserts equality of sampled dynamics hashes for both signs, with checks
passing. These identities are specific to this setup; they do not establish
that all axes, devices, or aircraft profiles are identical.

The direct Simulation.step() path still runs the production pre-tick, including
servo limiting, and emits the logging signals: the recorder captures each
accepted tick. Direct stepping bypasses Session._physics_process, including
keyboard virtual-stick slew, physical controller polling and conditioning, and
session-level crash/UI handling; it also bypasses normal pause/hold scheduling.
It supports an open-loop, ideal pre-servo stick-step interpretation, not an
end-to-end keyboard/radio latency test, closed-loop piloted maneuver, or claim
about physical aircraft handling. The technical level trims at 15 and 22 m/s
were independently solved with different equilibrium conditions; the nominal
speed is an initial target, not a speed held by feedback.

Servo angle and trim
--------------------
`surface_roll` is the accepted normalized simulated servo position. For this
Extra's symmetric ailerons, `aileron_deg = surface_roll * 17.600665` is the
signed RIGHT aileron trailing-edge angle, positive up; the left angle is its
negative. It is a model-derived angle, not a measured physical servo or sensor
reading. For aircraft with different up/down travel or other surface topology,
use the production surface_angles_deg() mapping instead of this multiplication.
`max_abs_surface_during_pulse_deg` includes the nonzero starting trim; it is not
just the incremental movement induced by the stick.

At 22 m/s the trim is +0.0014370784 of full roll command. Consequently +1 is
clipped to +1.0000000, while -1 becomes -0.9985629; the positive and negative
full servo targets are +17.600665 and -17.575371 degrees, respectively. The
normal targets also differ in absolute angle (about +6.972364 and -6.921777
degrees). Thus differences between right and left pulse outcomes cannot be
ascribed entirely to aerodynamic asymmetry without allowing for this small
input asymmetry and the trimmed initial condition.

Time, response, and coupling
----------------------------
At 240 Hz, the ON interval starts at pre-step tick 48 = 0.2 s and ends at
pre-step tick 168 = 0.7 s. Inputs are applied for 120 steps = 0.5 s. CSV rows
are post-step: the first ON sample is tick 49, the last ON sample is tick 168,
and tick 169 is the first OFF sample. `release_p_deg_s` is sampled at tick 168,
immediately BEFORE the command release has acted; peak_abs_p_during_pulse is
the maximum absolute body roll-axis angular rate over the ON samples, not a
settled or steady roll rate. Body-axis p need not equal Euler roll-angle rate
when other rates and attitudes are present. Flight-log loads are RK4 k1 values
at the interval start even though state/servo are accepted at interval end.

For example, 22 m/s/full/right airspeed during the pulse was 21.9357--22.0123
m/s, while sideslip reached about +6.03 degrees and body pitch and yaw rates
reached +36.47 and +29.23 degrees/s. The airspeed is nearly constant during
that particular pulse, but the response includes coupled uncorrected motion;
its peak is not a pure-axis derivative at a fixed attitude. No physical flight
or pilot validation is implied.

Replay meaning and minor reporting wart
----------------------------------------
Reader.inspect() checks complete ordered log structure and the initial snapshot.
With no candidate supplied, Reader.prepare_replay() refuses mismatched solver
identity, engine, timestep, stage backend, or incompatible checkpoint. The
probe feeds recorded post-conditioning input and interventions to fresh physics
and Reader.disagreement() compares state, loads, inputs, aux, continuous state,
modes, air and mass bytes at all 480 accepted ticks. This establishes exact
replay of those physics fields for these recordings and this solver identity,
not reconstruction of the original controller, UI, or human pilot. Raw/stick
observations and events are not themselves compared by that routine.

The probe's result reads `replay.get("solver_changed", false)`, but successful
Reader.prepare_replay() does not return that key. Its reported false is thus a
default rather than a separately measured field. In this same-solver path the
strict identity check above still enforces the stated equality; this wart does
not invalidate the exact physics replay. Do not represent the default value as
an independent solver-comparison result.
