EXTRA 300S — ROUND THREE
Measured control response and propulsion evidence
2026-11-06

RESULT

This bounded pass establishes no new serious implementation defect and no new
runtime speedup. It adds an executed, exactly replayable control-response
baseline; closes a documented primary-source gap for the O.S. .61FX; identifies
a useful engine-versus-propeller calibration experiment; and quantifies two
model sensitivities without mistaking them for demonstrated physical errors.

The most useful practical result is that input travel affects both the peak
roll response and the time the modeled servo needs to return to trim. The
propulsion result also changes the next diagnostic priority: inspect the
propeller torque demand independently before increasing engine power.

1. EXECUTED ROLL-RESPONSE BASELINE

Each case starts with a fresh stock Extra, full fuel, 100 m initial altitude,
reference density 1.225 kg/m3, and a newly solved level trim. Initial live mass
is 3.8195476 kg. Density is the session's reference-air value, not an assumed
altitude-dependent ISA value. The initial speeds are 15 or 22 m/s. A scripted
roll target is applied from 0.2 to 0.7 seconds; the recording lasts 2 seconds.
Pitch/yaw pilot commands remain zero and throttle remains at the initial trim.
There is no pilot feedback or speed-hold controller.

The probe calls the actual Session input-shaping path and production physics
step. Servo limiting, propulsion, coupled rigid-body motion and the connected
recorder hooks remain active. Direct stepping bypasses device sampling,
keyboard virtual-stick slew, UI scheduling and session-level crash handling.
It is an open-loop pre-servo stick-step experiment, not a controller-latency
test or a coordinated piloted roll.

Peak absolute body-axis roll rate during the 0.5 s pulse, degrees/second:

  Initial speed     Profile       Left         Right
  15 m/s            normal        77.7316      77.1320
  15 m/s            full         227.8530     224.4895
  22 m/s            normal       107.7087     107.8598
  22 m/s            full         283.6908     279.0455
  22 m/s            sport        283.6908     279.0455
  22 m/s            matched      107.7087     107.8598

"Matched" is the full profile with stick amplitude +/-0.394705, instead of
the normal profile's +/-1. These two configurations produce identical
post-profile commands, including trim and clamping.

The four paired physics comparisons are exact across their sampled numeric
arrays: sport versus full for each direction, and matched versus normal for
each direction. Every recording was then replayed in a fresh instance using
the existing reader's strict identity and byte-comparison path: 12 recordings,
480 replayed physics ticks each, 5,760 replayed ticks total, no mismatches.
The complete footer was verified for every recording. This verifies software
reproducibility at this source/engine/backend identity, not physical realism.

The equal-command controls establish the causal scope of the profile effect
for this setup. They do not show that sport and full are interchangeable on
other axes or with other devices. Expo is zero in this stock Extra profile.

The quoted values are pulse peaks, not steady roll rates. Body-axis p is not
always the Euler roll-angle derivative. At 22 m/s/full/right, airspeed stays
within 21.9357--22.0123 m/s during the pulse, but sideslip reaches +6.03 degrees,
and pitch/yaw rates reach 36.47/29.23 degrees/s. The response contains coupled
motion even though its airspeed changes little.

The signed aileron value is the modeled right-aileron trailing-edge angle,
positive up; the symmetric left angle has the opposite sign. It includes
trim. At 22 m/s the roll trim is +0.0014370784 of maximum travel. A positive
full command clips at +1, while a negative full command reaches -0.9985629.
Consequently the small left/right differences do not isolate aerodynamic
asymmetry. Exact command matching is more discriminating than comparing only
the labels "normal" and "full".

Evidence: pulses/results.json, all twelve ORFL/CSV pairs, pulse_analysis.json,
pulse_summary.csv, Extra300_Roll_Response.png, and the accepted control-pulse
receipt. PULSE_REVIEW.txt contains the independent interpretation review.

2. WHY THE STOPPING RESPONSE CHANGES

Stock data selects a constant servo rate corresponding to 0.14 s from neutral
to full throw. Normal roll travel is 0.394705 of full travel. The ideal
continuous-time movement durations are therefore approximately

  normal: 0.394705 * 0.14 = 0.0552587 s
  full:   1.0 * 0.14 = 0.14 s

At 240 Hz, the first accepted sample at the target occurs after 14 ticks
(58.33 ms) for normal and 34 ticks (141.67 ms) for full in these cases. The
return from pulse target to initial trim has the same measured durations.
This is predicted by the implemented rate limiter, not a newly discovered bug.

At 22 m/s/right roll, 100 ms after release the normal case has slowed to
14.78 degrees/s, while full retains 134.60 degrees/s. The full aileron is still
about 5 degrees up at that instant, whereas normal is already back at trim.
The comparison combines different initial roll rates, ongoing servo motion,
aerodynamic damping and coupling. It does not attribute all of the rate
difference to the servo alone.

This is useful for interpreting pilot language such as "doesn't stop crisply".
The actuator can still be commanding a roll after the pilot target returns
to zero. Changing aerodynamic damping before checking that signal chain can
compensate for the wrong mechanism.

A small-disturbance roll equation makes the separation explicit:

  Ixx * dp/dt = qbar*S*b * [Cl_delta*delta_a + Cl_p*p*b/(2V)]

The first term supplies control moment; the second supplies roll damping;
delta_a is the actual surface angle after the rate limiter. For frozen V and
linear coefficients, equilibrium p scales approximately as V*delta_a, whereas
initial control acceleration scales as V^2*delta_a/Ixx. The measured full
pulse additionally contains nonlinear and cross-axis dynamics, so these
relations are diagnostic guides rather than fits to the reported peaks.

Recommendation: preserve the surface trace alongside the stick and body-rate
traces when comparing future tuning. Separate response delay, initial roll
acceleration, peak/settled rate, and release behavior. Do not use one generic
"agility" multiplier to compensate for all of them. Extend the existing
flight-test/logging tools with the paired commands if this becomes a project
gate; do not maintain a second physics implementation.

3. NEW PRIMARY EVIDENCE AND CURRENT SHAFT BALANCE

The original Gierke source was located and its tables and graph visually
checked. The project had already used one secondhand power anchor and marked
the primary text unavailable. The source identity, selected measurements and
page mapping are in propulsion_source.json. The full copyrighted publication
and page images are not included here. The old anchor itself is not claimed
as a new discovery.

The current Extra was evaluated with the production GlowOperating.solve()
path at full throttle, zero axial inflow and total/dry density 1.225 kg/m3.
This assumes nominal mixture, a running engine and full firing. It does not
integrate temperature, fuel transport or fuel-feed equilibrium.

The selected restoring root is 10,288.179431 RPM. Engine and propeller torque
both equal 0.679532236 N m, the residual is zero at printed float64 precision,
shaft power is 732.111472 W and modeled static thrust is 32.137174 N. Eight
checks passed, including a residual sign reversal around the selected root.
These are current software calculations, not an assertion of a measured
aircraft bench result. The old EX-14g value is not substituted for this run.

At the new source's APC operating speed, current component values are:

  Model engine torque                 0.679552242 N m
  Model propeller resisting torque     0.886357119 N m
  Engine minus propeller torque       -0.206804877 N m
  Model engine power                 836.159896 W
  Power needed by modeled prop       1090.624430 W

For a comparison of scale, the published corrected power at that speed has
a torque equivalent of 0.696940053 N m. Current modeled engine power is about
2.49% below that number, while the current propeller power demand is about
27.18% above it. This is not a matched-condition physical error percentage.
It shows that the present equations' operating-point gap cannot be removed
by a small adjustment toward that power point while leaving the prop load
unchanged. It prioritizes inspecting the propeller torque law independently.

Known comparability differences remain material: the aircraft target has a
Pitts-style installation, while the model's installed engine curve is a
generic muffler hypothesis, not measured Pitts data. Model fuel has 18% oil.
The nominal 12x6 propeller section fit was transferred from measured 11x6
data rather than identified from this installed 12x6. Atmospheric reference,
mixture, engine sample and installed exhaust must be reconciled.

Normalization needs care. Applying P=Q*omega to the selected lower-speed
torque point produces 0.979784 mechanical hp; applying the listed correction
factor produces 1.018976 hp, consistent with the plotted corrected power.
This suggests the torque is uncorrected, but the inspected source does not
explicitly declare its normalization. Do not count torque and its derived
power as independent raw observations. The exact correction convention and
target atmosphere must be established before a fit.

The diagnostic quantities and independent arithmetic are retained in
static_propulsion_point.json and propulsion_analysis.json. No production
propeller/engine parameter was changed.

4. THE IDENTIFICATION PROBLEM AND A BETTER CALIBRATION ORDER

The observed shaft speed satisfies

  R(n) = Q_engine(n, throttle, mixture, density) - Q_prop(n, U, density) = 0.

One measured RPM constrains the equality, not the two torque laws separately.
An engine curve and a propeller curve can both be wrong while their crossing
is right. For a parameter theta near a simple root,

  dn/dtheta = -(partial R/partial theta)/(partial R/partial n).

This explains why forcing a target RPM can hide a wrong load model. At fixed
diameter, density and torque coefficient, propeller torque scales as n^2 and
absorbed power as n^3. The new tach reference is about 14.21% above the modeled
root; that would require approximately 48.97% more absorbed power if those
other quantities stayed fixed. This is conditional scaling, not a proposed
power increase.

The useful next implementation sequence is:

1. Update the source record through research/glow-engines/build_profiles.py
   and the owning NITRO/EX-14 provenance, retaining the old interpretation and
   explicitly distinguishing corrected power from raw observations.
2. Make a separate stock-exhaust bench fixture with explicit fuel, mixture,
   atmosphere and correction convention. Constrain engine torque with the
   primary curve before touching the aircraft's installed power scale.
3. Check propeller torque and thrust independently over more than one speed
   and advance ratio. A static RPM does not validate forward-flight thrust.
4. Keep the Pitts installation as a separately supported estimate until
   matched installed measurements exist; do not invent an exhaust penalty.
5. Only then compare one explicit candidate against the preserved flight tape,
   and separately retrim the changed aircraft for equilibrium tests.

A weighted fit should use independent measurements and retain at least one
held-out operating condition. Measurement uncertainties were not supplied by
the inspected excerpt, so this review invents no statistical confidence
interval and declares no calibrated optimum.

5. TWO BOUNDED MODEL SENSITIVITIES

High-angle wing progression. The documented spanwise progression multiplies
the whole strip angle before polar evaluation. At geometric alpha=90 degrees
this leaves small nonzero section CL values because the factors differ from
one. With a constructed 20 m/s normal flow and body roll p=+/-2 rad/s, the
stock-versus-no-progression aerodynamic yaw-moment difference is
+/-0.106960890 N m. Wing-strip moment sums account for it; the paired signs
reverse symmetrically, and the tested alpha=0-degree controls are exact.

The explicit aircraft contract authorizes the full-angle rule, so this is not
an implementation defect. The counterfactual deletes a useful stall-progression
effect and is not an acceptable proposed fix. This extreme fixed-state probe
is not a flown maneuver, a knife-edge trim or an occurrence-rate estimate.
Sixteen new asserted checks passed; existing wing-polar and spatial-continuation
tests passed 126 and 91 checks. Details and source hashes are in aero/HANDOFF.txt.

Cooling. At one stock 22 m/s trimmed airborne state, the production cooling
proxy adds sqrt(T/(2*rho*A)) to hub-speed magnitude. That additional speed is
the static ideal-disk value. For an ideal aligned forward-flow disk,

  T = 2*rho*A*vi*(U+vi)
  vi = 2*v0^2 / [sqrt(U^2 + 4*v0^2) + U], v0=sqrt(T/(2*rho*A)).

Using the same frozen state gives 5.37803 m/s from the present added term and
1.24626 m/s from the ideal forward-flow comparison. With the existing estimated
thermal coefficients, estimated cooling changes by 50.4985 W and head-proxy
temperature rate by 0.360704 K/s. Seven checks passed, including the zero-flow
algebra control. These are instantaneous sensitivities, not a thermal
trajectory. Real head/cowl flow is not necessarily an ideal disk flow, and
the installed thermal law remains uncalibrated. There is no justified repair
or real-engine temperature-error claim. See dynamics/FINDINGS.txt.

6. REPRODUCIBILITY AND EXCLUSIONS

Accepted receipt directories retain the required completion markers and report
ok=true, complete=true, zero engine errors/warnings. The application still
prints its intentional experimental-data notices; those are not physical
qualification. Independent source review checked the pulse's input/replay
boundaries and source identity.

A small reporting wart is preserved transparently: the probe's solver_changed
field defaults to false because prepare_replay does not return that key.
Same-solver identity is actually enforced by prepare_replay before execution;
the default is not a separate solver-comparison measurement. The exact-byte
replay results and complete-recording checks are the evidence used here.

Failed setup attempts are not accepted evidence. The first root smoke run had
missing scenery preloads and an unsupported format specifier; the second had
one remaining missing texture. The final full matrix is separate and clean.
The aero witness initially requested the wrong completion count, and the
first cooling attempt had missing assets plus an out-of-scope negative-flow
control. Retained rejected receipts are labelled under prior_failures/.

This review excludes the previous E300-01..08 defects/optimizations, previous
fuel/firing-counter repairs, previously discussed induced-source weighting,
the normalization cusp, the shaft convention and the already known review
weight/fuel ambiguity. They are not new discoveries or reopened defect counts.

The next concrete gain is better identification: preserve the now-measured
command/surface/rate relationship, and separate engine output from propeller
load using the newly recovered primary reference. The retained experiments
make that next comparison reviewable without claiming a tune is already valid.
