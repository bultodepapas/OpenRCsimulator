EXTRA 300 — SECOND-PASS CODE, MATHEMATICS AND PERFORMANCE AUDIT
============================================================

New actionable results
----------------------
E300-07: An exact-reuse optimization removes two steady-RPM searches from each
finite-glow level-trim finalization. For the Extra's half-power cruise search,
the measured work count falls from 202 to 174 searches. A reviewable candidate
patch preserves all tested output bytes and reduces median solver time in the
isolated Linux benchmark.

E300-08: A successful F5 reload can change the Extra's physical propeller and
collision radius while leaving the visible propeller and blur at the previous
diameter. The real application reproduces the mismatch in both directions;
fresh startup and a rejected inconsistent edit provide discriminating controls.

The performance opportunity and developer reload defect are distinct from the
six first-pass findings. No additional severe flight-dynamics defect was proved
in this bounded second pass. The aerodynamic sensitivities and previously
documented collision convention are retained as research context, not counted
as newly discovered bugs.

E300-07 — Exact RPM reuse in trim finalization
--------------------------------------------
Practical priority: medium; a localized CPU improvement when a glow launch
operating point must actually be solved.

The Newton solver already owns both the accepted six-variable state x and its
steady RPM. In app/physics/trim.gd:142-224, accepted bounded steps commit x,
x_rpm and residual together. A rejected trial leaves the last accepted pair
unchanged. Nevertheless, _result at lines 352-377 drops that RPM, calls
_evaluate without it (one new equilibrium solve), then calls _rpm again for
the reported result (another new equilibrium solve).

The equilibrium is a deterministic root of

    Q_engine(omega, throttle, dry_density) - Q_prop(omega, inflow, density) = 0.

The existing operating-point algorithm keeps the highest restoring root from
an RPM mesh, engine knots and bisection. Every relevant input at final x is
the same as it was for the retained x_rpm. Reusing that exact value preserves
the root-selection law, nonlinear equations and convergence tolerance. It is
not an approximate cache across different Newton iterates or flight stages.

The candidate passes x_rpm into an optional _result argument only on the
bounded finite-glow level path, supplies it to _evaluate, and uses it in the
returned result. Other paths keep their existing fallback. Independent source
review checked all five exits, including failed line searches, and confirmed
that the saved value belongs to the reported iterate.

The measured equilibrium-call counts were:

    Extra level trim at 22 m/s:    19 -> 17, two saved.
    Extra half-power cruise:     202 -> 174, 28 saved over 14 finalizations.
    Excessive-idle refusal:       30 -> 24, six saved over three attempts.
    Stopped-engine glide:          0 -> 0, unchanged control.

The half-power calculation uses the production CruiseTrim search with full
fuel and the reference atmosphere. The final result remains V=32.4591201889649
m/s and 10784.9340656827 RPM. The 13.861386% reduction is an exact source-level
work-count reduction for that case; it is not an application-wide percentage.

E300-08 — F5 changes the physical propeller but keeps stale visual size
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Practical priority: medium; a reproducible developer-workflow correctness bug.
It was not observed on an unchanged stock launch and is not classified as a
new ordinary-flight instability.

Startup in app/main.gd:880-899 applies the validated glow propeller diameter
through AirplaneBuilder.apply_propeller_diameter(). The adjacent comment
explicitly mentions same-ID installation-profile reloads. The actual F5 path
at lines 2137-2140 calls session.reload() and updates only the model CG.
Meanwhile the accepted session rebuilds its physical propeller collision disc.

The witness used the real headless application and real F5 key events through
the repository's debug bridge. It changed both diameter quantities from
0.3048 m (12 inches) to 0.3302 m (13 inches) in a disposable same-ID Extra
aircraft/installation pair. This is a synthetic accepted developer edit,
not a shipped alternative or a calibrated propeller configuration.

Observed states:
- Stock/no-op F5: physical radius 0.1524 m, visual scale 1.0.
- Aircraft-only edit: rejected because installation and aircraft disagree;
  accepted physics and visual state remain unchanged.
- Coherent 13-inch F5: accepted diameter 0.3302 m and collision radius
  0.1651 m, but visual propeller and blur basis scales remain 1.0.
- Fresh 13-inch startup: scales correctly become 1.0833333731.
- Restore stock files in that fresh flight and press F5: physics returns to
  radius 0.1524 m, but the 13-inch visual scale remains.

For a diameter-only change, the necessary scale is absolute:

    scale_xy = accepted_diameter / authored_diameter.

Thus 13/12 = 1.0833333333. It must not be multiplied repeatedly into the
previous scale. The existing setter already follows this rule. The defect is
the missing accepted-reload call.

The blur's measured.radius is local to the propeller, so effective radius is
measured.radius times the world basis scale. After 12-to-13-inch F5, physical
radius is 0.1651 m but effective blur radius remains 0.1524282545 m: a radial
difference of approximately 12.672 mm. The reverse-direction control gives
about 0.1651306 m visible radius against 0.1524 m physical radius. The original
mesh tip is already about 28 micrometres beyond nominal; that small difference
does not explain the reload mismatch. No actual strike at that boundary was
flown or rendered during this audit.

A diagnostic invocation of the existing absolute setter after the reproduced
mismatch returned true and updated both propeller and blur transforms to the
correct 13/12 scale. PropellerBlur.update copies the propeller global transform
before its early return, so a diameter-only repair does not inherently require
rebuilding the blur profile. The recommended code organization is one helper
for applying accepted installation geometry, called from startup and successful
reload. Keep refused reloads unchanged, refresh dependent extents consistently,
and handle any post-commit presentation failure explicitly.

The regression needs the actual accepted F5 path: no-op, both diameter-change
directions, inconsistent-edit refusal, repeated reload idempotency, and blade/
blur/collision geometry observations. A helper-only unit test would miss the
missing wiring. The existing nominal Extra installation test passed 17 checks
but does not exercise F5.

No production F5 patch was applied or qualified. Evidence is the application
witness, source diagnosis, controls and tested setter primitive. Successful
headless runs each emitted 120 landscape UID text-path fallback warnings and
no engine error lines; their reconstructed import environment and complete
transcript are disclosed. This does not qualify pixels, a clean exported app,
or target-hardware rendering.

Evidence: model_lifecycle/FINDINGS.txt, reload_witness.json, bridge_transcript.jsonl,
the two modified fixture JSONs, successful application logs and the 17-check
installation receipt.

Additional investigation: what was and was not established
----------------------------------------------------------
Finite fuel: 23 stock-session checks passed for the four offered fill levels,
independently computed live mass/CG/inertia, runway prime/starter momentum,
short cut/restart replay and reset refueling. The 16-tick tape and exact replay
observations are included. This is useful control evidence, not a new fix or
long-flight/real-tank validation.

Aerodynamics: the active polar's attachment fraction and the induced-source
retention heuristic can differ substantially on the negative-alpha branch.
At -20 degrees and 20 m/s, a diagnostic alternative changed Fz by about
22.366 N. A separate station-speed maximum causes a small roll-rate cusp,
and uniform spanwise velocity changes the same normalization. The controlled
probes attribute these behaviors, but there is no established contract or
measurement proving the alternative wake law correct. They are questions for
model validation and documentation; no generic aerodynamic repair is proposed.

Excluded as covered or unsupported: the six first-pass findings; O4 downwash
reuse; the documented locked-rotor scenery approximation; planned coasting
prop-strike work; the fixed tailwheel-contact approximation; and an apparent
Workshop scale issue that cannot be reached because finite-glow Extra scaling
is refused. The source/sign/unit review found no additional distinct defect.

Suggested implementation order
------------------------------
1. Review and integrate the exact trim reuse as a small independent change.
   Retain failed-trim and non-glow controls; use repository CI for merge gates.
2. Fix accepted F5 visual synchronization using the existing absolute setter
   and a public-path regression. Keep this separate from propulsion tuning.
3. Resolve aerodynamic modeling questions with explicit intended invariants
   and independent data before changing negative-alpha wake behavior.
