# Extra 300S presentation and lifecycle review

## New finding: F5 reload accepts a changed propeller but leaves its visual size stale

At source commit `8e5f2f0ba6aacd44efa1c4ac48c4023f181c47bd`, F5 reloads the selected aircraft data and, when accepted, re-trims and restarts the session. `docs/FIRST-LAUNCH.md:74` documents F5; `app/sim/flight_session.gd:2774-2819` defines its transactional acceptance/refusal. `_commit_aircraft()` at `flight_session.gd:2236-2264` replaces the accepted propulsion and rebuilds the collision disc from the new physical diameter.

The ordinary scene startup applies the validated glow installation diameter to the visual propeller at `app/main.gd:880-899`. The adjacent comment specifically says the absolute setter also handles same-ID installation-profile reloads. But the F5 branch at `main.gd:2137-2140` calls `session.reload()` and updates only `_cg_model`; it never reapplies the diameter to `_airplane.propeller`. The detached `PropellerBlur` copies the propeller's transform in `app/render/propeller_blur.gd:466-475`, so the blur inherits that unchanged scale too. This is a presentation/collision-geometry consistency defect; the accepted physics model and strike disc do update.

### Actual public-path witness

Two isolated, headless application runs used the project's debug bridge, Godot `4.8.beta2.official.cafc17a19`, the actual Extra 300S flight scene, and real F5 key events. The original aircraft and installation files were copied into the disposable application, then restored byte-for-byte. The test's 13-inch (`0.3302 m`) diameter is a **synthetic, accepted same-ID developer edit** in both the aircraft JSON and its existing glow installation JSON. It is not a claim that a 13-inch alternative is a shipped Extra catalog choice or a physically calibrated propeller.

| State and control | Accepted physics diameter | Strike-disc radius | Visual propeller scale | Blur radius and effective scale |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Stock Extra, and no-op F5 | 0.3048 m | 0.1524 m | 1.0 | 0.1524282545 m × 1.0 |
| Edit aircraft diameter only, F5 | Remains 0.3048 m | Remains 0.1524 m | 1.0 | Unchanged; reload refused because installation disagrees |
| Edit both files to 0.3302 m, F5 | 0.3302 m | 0.1651 m | **1.0** | **0.1524282545 m × 1.0** |
| Fresh application started with the same 0.3302 m files | 0.3302 m | 0.1651 m | 1.0833333731 | 0.1524282545 m × 1.0833333731 |
| In that fresh 13-inch app, restore stock files and press F5 | 0.3048 m | 0.1524 m | **1.0833333731** | **0.1524282545 m × 1.0833332539** |

Both accepted F5 cases reported `aircraft reloaded: trimmed at 32 m/s, throttle 50 %`. The one-file mismatch reported `reload failed; previous aircraft and flight retained: aircraft data invalid: Glow installation and aircraft propeller diameters disagree`. A subsequent bridge state for the reverse case showed the flight remained active without a reported fault or crash.

`measured.radius` belongs to the blur's **local propeller frame**. Multiplying it by the observed global basis scale gives the effective radius in this unit-scale flight. After the stock-to-13-inch F5, the physical disc radius is 0.1651 m while the effective blur radius is 0.1524283 m, a 0.0126717 m radial difference. After fresh 13-inch startup followed by stock F5, the effective blur radius is about 0.1651306 m while the physical disc is 0.1524 m. The original mesh's measured tip radius is about 28 µm larger than the nominal 12-inch radius; the reload mismatch is much larger and is not due to that existing small difference.

The physics change is not just a renamed value. At the same constructed 12,000 RPM, axial speed 30 m/s and density 1.225 kg/m³, the real annular `GlowProp.evaluate()` returned thrust/torque 20.2944686 N / 0.7222116 N·m for the stock installation and 25.6877933 N / 0.9365224 N·m for the accepted synthetic 13-inch installation. At the same body air velocity `[30, 3, 0] m/s`, `Propulsion.normal_force()` returned `[0.0040330, -0.1195139, 0.0160902] N` versus `[0.0049072, -0.1454189, 0.0195778] N`. These are software observations for a controlled arbitrary operating point, not independent validation of either configuration.

### Small repair boundary

After the observed stock-to-13-inch F5 mismatch, a diagnostic debug-bridge call to the existing `AirplaneBuilder.apply_propeller_diameter()` returned `true`. The propeller and blur basis scales both became `1.0833333731`; the resulting effective blur radius was about 0.1651306 m, within the pre-existing nominal mesh-tip difference of the 0.1651 m collision disc. This was a diagnostic call after the real F5, **not a production patch**.

The narrow candidate is to reuse the existing absolute diameter setter after a successfully committed reload, before dependent presentation is used, and refresh extents/camera framing as needed, following startup's existing handling. The blur already copies the updated propeller transform on its next process update before its early return, so a diameter-only change does not inherently need a new blur profile. Retain the previous geometry on refused reload and handle setter failure without misrepresenting a committed physical state. Tests should cover no-op F5, both accepted diameter directions, mismatch refusal, actual blade and blur transforms, startup and reset, as well as alternate supported shaft installations. No candidate patch or full graphical/lifecycle qualification has been performed.
