## 1. The blade's modeled leading edge is on the trailing side of positive-RPM rotation

`app/aircraft/extra_300s_model.gd:661-682` constructs the +X blade with its leading edge in the +Y direction from its trailing edge, with an authored cambered front face and fixed pitch. For the Extra's finite-glow engine `app/render/airplane.gd:148-149` selects spin sign `-1`. Positive RPM advances `_prop_angle` in `app/main.gd:1369-1371`, and `main.gd:3175` applies the negative angle about local Z. Thus a point on the +X blade moves toward -Y, so the designated trailing edge advances first. The second blade is the same geometry rotated by pi (`extra_300s_model.gd:646-649`), so the same relative handedness applies.

The check in `runtime/spin_probe.gd` selects the ninth radial station, locates both calculated section-edge positions exactly in the actual generated mesh, and computes `(leading - trailing) · (angular_velocity × midpoint)`. At radius `0.1112777921 m`, chord `0.0215147533 m`, and unit angular velocity about -Z, that dot product is `-0.0023391903378 m²/s`; an opposite-spin control gives `+0.0023391903378`. Successful checked receipt and output: `checks/spin-probe-2fcpk9u6/report.json` and `engine.log`.

This is a model presentation/handedness inconsistency, not evidence that physical thrust or the simulated rotation direction is wrong. The actual appearance and perceptibility at rendered speeds were not assessed. A correction should reconcile leading edge, camber and pitch with the existing physics-consistent spin direction, then check the shaft axis/hub, selected propeller geometry, blur and strike visuals at stopped and moving RPM. Do not just reverse the shared spin sign without reassessing the physical convention and other aircraft.

## 2. Construction-time loft indexing reduces stored geometry, at a CPU cost in this test

`extra_300s_model.gd:71-105` emits repeated triangle corners into every loft and commits an unindexed surface. The minimal experimental patch `extra_loft_index_candidate.patch` adds `st.index()` immediately before the `_loft` commit. It uses Godot's complete vertex attributes, rather than position-only merging, and retains the authored triangle order. It is a disposable experiment, not a production change.

The checked probe builds two separate stock models and the indexed candidate. It visits every `MeshInstance3D` surface and re-expands indices into triangle-corner order, comparing every present vertex attribute, plus relative mesh paths and local transforms, using exact Godot value equality. It repeats this after `procedural_skin.gd:83-151` prepares the Extra for custom paint; both stock and prepared comparisons report `{}` differences. The updated probe explicitly fails if either comparison is nonempty. These are same-engine numeric comparisons, not byte comparisons that distinguish signed zero, full integration tests or rendered-image equivalence. The model has 32 mesh instances, 31 distinct mesh resources (the two blades share one resource within a build), and 19,502 instance triangles before and after. Two independently generated stock builds share no mesh instances with each other.

| Measured quantity | Stock builder | Indexed `_loft` | Scope |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Stored vertices, all 31 distinct meshes | 53,204 | 10,878 | Includes the four distinct cylinder meshes; repeated blade counted once |
| Serialized vertex + attribute + index buffers | 1,317,552 B | 373,176 B | 27 distinct ArrayMesh resources only; 944,376 B or 71.68% reduction |
| The same buffers after custom paint preparation | 1,825,800 B | 1,475,672 B | 350,128 B or 19.18% reduction |

These are `_surfaces` byte-array sizes retrieved in the headless engine, not total process RAM, actual GPU allocation, object overhead or memory savings under a production renderer. Paint preparation expands each painted triangle (`procedural_skin.gd:186-241`), explaining why the gain becomes smaller. Comparing retained source vertex bytes and indices after custom preparation also confirms that the custom adapter does not simply preserve the indexed representation.

On an Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C with the checked debug engine and CPU affinity 0, the two warmed builder scripts were measured in one process using `Time.get_ticks_usec`, 16 alternating AB/BA pairs after three warmups each. Only `builder.build()` was inside the measured interval; freeing, project import, registry validation, file loading and any renderer work were outside. Baseline median was **96,192.5 µs** and indexed median **103,238 µs**. The median of paired candidate changes was **+8.505%** (range -22.802% to +32.249%; candidate was faster in 4/16 pairs). The candidate should therefore be considered a possible geometry-storage tradeoff, not a supported startup-time optimization.

For `PaintAdapter.prepare()` alone on already built matching roots, eight alternating pairs after two warmups per role gave medians 86,330 µs stock versus 76,200 µs indexed and median paired change -10.818% (range -34.215% to +58.586%; 7/8 favor candidate). Those widely varying, few samples do not establish a repeatable full custom-build speedup; they also omit building the model. Raw samples, warmups, exact command and runner verdict: `checks/bench-index-g_b1yxdo/{engine.log,report.json}`. No target-hardware, GPU or frame-speed claim follows.

**Rejected candidate:** indexing meshes after committing and reading them back via `SurfaceTool.create_from()` introduced an extra encode/decode pass. The failed diagnostic in `checks/geometry-stock-taat7mxb/engine.log` reports changed decoded normals and tangents, e.g. up to `0.0001264107` in normal-vector distance and `0.0000703931` in a tangent component. It is not the candidate patch and does not satisfy exact comparison.

**Additional future opportunity, not prototyped:** the builder constructs the same immutable meshes again on every fresh `build()`. Reuse needs a bounded lifetime and fresh node/hinge/gear ownership, stable source and generator identities, and independent material overrides. Existing Home retains up to three complete preview models (`app/ui/home_scene.gd:137-161`); reuse therefore must be evaluated on actual new build paths, not mischaracterized as a missing Home selection cache. Custom paint makes its own mesh per target and installs instance overrides (`procedural_skin.gd:120-150`; `custom_skin.gd:165-220`), but this is not a proof that every other mesh consumer is safe to share.

## 3. Custom-paint preparation produces duplicate shader resources

Stock `extra_300s_finish.gd:127-139` correctly caches **one** shader and five material variants; the stock probe observed exactly those counts. This is not a duplicate-stock-shader finding.

On the Extra custom-skin route, `render/airplane.gd:172-183` invokes `procedural_skin.prepare()`. The latter creates a new prepared `Shader` for every painted mesh surface in `_prepare_material` (`procedural_skin.gd:120-147,302-324`). The stock Extra has 14 such surfaces: the checked headless probe observed 14 prepared material objects and **14 different Shader objects with one identical code string**. `custom_skin.gd:181-215` keys final copies by the prepared material instance ID, so the distinct inputs each go through `paint()`; `procedural_skin.gd:327-364` creates a fresh Shader again. With one representative opaque no-texture paint record, the probe observed **14 final Shader objects with one identical code string**. Production paint records can vary, and final shader-code identity should be grouped by actual generated code/alpha/texture branch rather than assumed across all possible records. The test calls the real adapter preparation and paint functions; it does not import a user `.orskin` or measure compilation or loading latency.

A narrow candidate is to reuse an immutable Shader by exact generated code and relevant variant, while retaining per-material uniform values, instance ownership and transactional failure behavior. Prepare-time and final-paint caches have different generated code and lifetimes; never merge materials merely because the shader source matches. Qualify alpha and textured/untextured variants, multiple liveries/instances, reset and mutable material isolation. No hardware compilation or GPU improvement is established by object counts.
