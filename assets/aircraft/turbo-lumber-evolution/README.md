# Turbo Lumber Evolution ("Timber"): visual geometry and experimental flight

The **visual model** of the fifth catalog aircraft, shown as "Turbo Lumber Evolution" ("Turbo Tronco Evolución" in Spanish): a fictional name for a model inspired by the E-flite Turbo Timber Evolution 1.5 m, drawn by its author from photographs without manufacturer plans or artwork. "Timber" remains the internal codename of the track. The runtime asset is the author's r2 glTF game delivery, imported by Godot's scene importer; nothing is modelled procedurally. Its experimental electric v2 data is generated by `research/timber/tt07/derive_physics.py`; it is selectable and flies with airborne or qualified runway starts. Flaps stay up, assists off and the nominal 4S bus does not deplete. [Data derivation](../../../research/timber/tt07/derivation.md), [playable baseline proof](../../../docs/research/timber-integration/TT-10/README.md). Plan and step status: [TIMBER-INTEGRATION-PLAN](../../../docs/TIMBER-INTEGRATION-PLAN.md).

## Frames and datum

Metres, +X right, +Y up, −Z nose. The origin D0 is the symmetry plane × the wing-root leading-edge station × the thrust-axis height, preserved from the r1 export; `AirplaneBuilder.datum()` is therefore `(0, 0)`. Aircraft-contract positions convert as `[x_aft, y_right, z_up] = [z_model, x_model, y_model]`.

## Builder contract

`build()` returns `{root, propeller, hinges, gear, has_propeller = true, data, aircraft_id}`. `root` is the imported scene root renamed `airplane` (one child, `TurboTimberEvolution`, identity at D0). Hinges (`aileron_left/right`, `elevator`, `rudder`, `flap_left/right`) are the imported pivot nodes with `{node, axis, rest, pivot, range_deg, positive, app_sign}`; a deflection sets `node.basis = Basis(axis, angle) * rest`, so zero restores the import exactly. `apply_surfaces()` takes the app's degrees (trailing edge up for ailerons/elevator, right for the rudder) and applies `app_sign` (−1, −1, +1) because the imported pivots are positive trailing-edge down.

Nodes the app drives through Euler properties get a two-level wrapper (mount with the rest basis, identity child receiving the rotation) because writing `rotation.z` or `rotation.x` replaces the whole basis: `propeller` (child of `propeller_mount` under `thrust_frame`; its +Z is the thrust axis, so `rotation.z = +θ` is clockwise seen from behind, the author's sense) and `gear = {left, right, tail, steering}` (`wheel_*_spin` nodes on the axle centres rotating about the root +X, forward rolling negative as in `render/ground_gear.gd`; `tail_steering` rotating about +Y, positive = wheel trails right). The imported pivots keep their world transforms; no mesh is reparented.

## Current r2 acceptance (TT-19)

The imported model has **42,519 triangles, 21 mesh nodes, 49 material surfaces and 10 shared materials**, with no image textures. This is 33.9% fewer triangles than r1 (64,291), but more surfaces (r1: 36); triangle reduction alone does not prove a frame-time improvement. Native Godot captures and integration checks are recorded in [TT-19](../../../docs/research/timber-integration/TT-19/README.md).

Fixed geometry is authored as `fuselage_core`, `wing_left_fixed`, `wing_right_fixed`, `stabilizer_fixed`, `fin_fixed`, `cowl` and `canopy_glass`. Each mesh and each moving pivot has an `openrc_section` string. The common model manifest uses `gltf_extras`; the wreck consumes eight detachable categories plus the core directly. A wing's fixed panel, aileron and flap share its section; the engine/cowl and propeller remain independent. Thin white closure faces at the wing roots, cowl and tail cover the authored separation seams. The r1 generated triangle map is historical and is not loaded by this builder.

The 13 moving pivots and fixed thrust frame retain their authoring contracts. The app preserves aileron/elevator sign conversion, the rotor rest-frame wrapper, wheel rolling axes and tailwheel steering. Flaps remain visual-only in the current flight configuration; suspension remains static.

The supplied cowl foam intersects some black/red marking shells. The builder clones only those two cowl surface materials per instance and applies 0.8 mm of render-only normal growth, reducing the visible white tearing; mesh vertices, source bytes and physics are unchanged by this correction. The remaining marking-edge artifacts and visible white wing-root closure faces require a source-author revision. Import LOD remains at its original default. Mesh attribute compression is explicitly disabled: a fresh Godot 4.7.2 import then reproduces the accepted r2 measurements exactly, preserving generated geometry and physics. This setting does not resolve the source marking defects.

Current dimensions, surface areas, wheel radii, contacts, control radii and assumptions are generated in [geometry.json](geometry.json). The r2 reduction removes raised paint shells from several surfaces, so measured skin chords and areas change slightly. The old wingtip landmark included a navigation lens; the r2 landmark identifies wing skin. A changed endpoint is not evidence of a changed wing sweep. No uniform scaling or aerodynamic calibration is justified by these differences.

The flight generator retains the manual 2.190 kg reference mass and estimated inventory/motor/propeller evidence; the author's 1.46 kg metadata ledger is explicitly a non-flight placeholder. The model remains experimental. Its approximately 1.116 m overall length still differs from the manual's 1.040 m; physical validation and target-machine performance acceptance remain open.

The delivery verifier reports a wing/flap contact at 40° (0–39° clear), suspension wire/bracket contact beyond about 6° (authoring range 0–12°), and 49 surfaces against the requested soft target of 40. These are retained advisories, not silently corrected control limits. Live flaps-up/static-suspension behavior does not exercise those authored endpoints.

Godot's control-skin checks find no crossings at the tested manual/authored poses, with approximately 0.2–0.3 mm gaps. Whole-leg/fuselage checks include the attachment and report some rest-pose overlaps; they cannot reproduce or qualify the author's isolated wire/bracket boundary. See TT-19 for those explicit diagnostics before implementing suspension motion.

## Authoring and future deliveries

The self-contained [Blender delivery specification](../../../docs/BLENDER-AIRCRAFT-HANDOFF.md) owns coordinates, pivots, editable game source, simplification, section IDs and export requirements. Preserve the accepted r2 package as supplied. To revise it, produce a new delivery, then regenerate the manifest, measurements, geometry, physics, model manifests and Workshop descriptors through their source scripts. Do not hand-edit GLB bytes or generated data. The supplied editable file is `source/aircraft_game.blend`; its export does not need the original large CAD caches.
