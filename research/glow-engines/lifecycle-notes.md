# Glow lifecycle kernel

**Checked:** 2026-10-13

[The lifecycle kernel](../../app/physics/glow/lifecycle.gd) composes the mean-cycle source and thermal kernels for both `glow_2t` and `glow_4t`. It remains standalone; aircraft and shaft integration call it through their own adapter.

`create(profile, settings, fuel_kg, temperature_k, rpm, running)` returns `{ok, state, message}`. The six float64 stage values are `fuel_kg`, `temperature_k`, `servo_fraction`, `air_fraction`, `fuel_transport_kg_s` and `phase_revolutions`. `pack_state` and `with_continuous_state` transfer those values without wrapping phase or clamping event-side fuel. The detached state also carries `running`, `stop_reason`, `opportunity_count`, `fire_count`, `firing_accumulator`, `family` and the profile's mechanical cycle length.

`evaluate(profile, settings, state, rpm, throttle, mixture, dry_density, ambient_k, cooling_airspeed)` returns the state-keyed `rates`, the evaluated `source` and `thermal` results, a mean `firing_fraction` and telemetry. Throttle first moves a servo state; admitted air follows it with a separate lag and an idle admission floor. A third lag tracks premix flow. The source receives an actual mixture derived from that transported mass flow and admitted air. Profiles must support every transient mixture query in their combustion-efficiency curve.

The firing fraction combines the configured rich/lean mixture window with cold and hot head-temperature responses. `temperature_firing_min_k < temperature_firing_full_k < temperature_firing_hot_full_k < temperature_firing_hot_zero_k` bounds that response; these are installation estimates, not universal material limits. Mean shaft work uses that fraction. On accepted mechanical phase crossings, a deterministic sigma-delta accumulator records successful and missed firing opportunities for replay/audio. It does not introduce pulse torque ripple. Mechanical phase advances at `rpm / 60` revolutions per second and wraps after one revolution for a 2T or two revolutions for a 4T. Rich 2T misses therefore keep 2T opportunity cadence and family identity.

`accepted(profile, settings, before, candidate, dt, rpm_before, rpm_after, firing_fraction)` commits an integrated candidate, reports opportunity times within the step, and latches stall, exact fuel exhaustion or overheat. Reaching `temperature_firing_hot_zero_k` shuts the engine down with stop reason `overheat`. It does not integrate rates. A candidate below zero fuel is rejected with a request to split at exhaustion; callers must locate that boundary instead of discarding tank mass. `request_start` accepts a start only above `minimum_start_rpm`, with starter availability, usable fuel and temperature below the overheat shutdown threshold. `cut` stops carburetor flow immediately. A stopped source still evaluates mechanical, pumping and valve losses so the thermal kernel can cool the engine during coast.

Installation settings are explicit and unit-bearing at their source: `idle_admission` and mixture-window ratios are dimensionless; `servo_time_constant_s`, `air_time_constant_s` and `fuel_transport_time_constant_s` are seconds; `stall_rpm` and `minimum_start_rpm` are RPM; firing thresholds are kelvin. The nested `thermal` group supplies the six parameters required by `thermal.gd`. The kernel does not select production defaults. `candidate_settings_for_tests` marks its synthetic values as estimates and must remain a test fixture.

Software evidence: `app/tests/test_glow_lifecycle.gd` passes 47 checks on both candidate profile models, including transport lags, cold/hot thermal feedback, lifecycle events, fuel-boundary rejection and rich-2T/true-4T cadence. These checks do not calibrate either installation or establish physical validation.
